# Chapter 3: Neon and Kinetic Fire

Leo squinted through frost-crusted airslots, scrutinizing the jagged crater-scape surrounding their wrecked enemy craft. Streaks of ionized plasma crisscrossed sickly yellow skies as distant energy weapon fire echoed across the lunar landscape. Geysers fizzled and died against night darkened by dust storms, like incandescent penance lit by Icaria's dying star.

His breath fogged the clear inner visor a ghastly white before dispersing into the chilly vacuum seeping past the breached lock where a ragged chunk of metal was blasted away. He made an involuntary face, nostrils flaring at the acrid reek of scorched plastic and hydraulic fluid carried by those insidious frigid drafts. Sweat trickled down his cheek, turning icicles on contact with chilled glass, and he felt a shiver run through Leo despite his armored suit absorbing most of the frigid onslaught.

"Gotta think...," he muttered, mind racing for options while keeping a watchful eye on the horizon, searching for telltale flashes of heat signatures or particle trails cutting through the thin atmosphere. The Starlight Marauder might be undamaged physically, but it needed power to start engines again—the lifeblood of any decent spacecraft.

Leo shifted his gaze to Astrid, noticing how she methodically examined their own crippled vessel's outer hull, scanning for fissures with practiced intensity. Even crouching in shattered wreckage, back bent at an angle to peep up through viewport slits, there was a predatory grace about her that defied the hellish chaos surrounding them.

"You got ideas?" she asked, not bothering to turn around. It was almost as if she expected Leo to solve this predicament solo, like some trusty sidekick who would know just the right thing to do in a pinch without asking questions.

An edgy amusement flickered through Leo's weary thoughts. This woman sure knew how to push buttons. Still, they were both in deeper shit already—what did a little added pressure matter?

"I can try...initiating emergency external umbilicals first, spin up Marauder's life support to conserve whatever power we have left," he proposed hesitantly, knowing full well just how sketchy a plan that sounded even to his own ears. "From what the nav data showed before our communications went tits-up, there should be a service station about three clicks due east with docking clamps for refugees."

She grunted noncommittally but nodded once, her jaw flexing as she bit down on something no doubt tasting foul with the lingering tang of sulfur and burnt wiring hanging heavy in the destroyed hydroponic chamber air.

"That's our best shot," Astrid conceded dryly, brushing a stray black lock of hair off her forehead only to have it whip back into place courtesy of the howling solar winds rushing across Icaria's fractured crust. "Grab an O2 scrubber for backup and let's move. Just hope our friends aren't fast Learners.'

Together, the unlikely pair crept out of their temporary shelter, hunched against the merciless gales trying to rip their suits apart at seams with every clumsy step they took across pockmarked moon dirt stippled with micro-meteorite splashes the size of peas. Yet even that insignificant danger barely registered compared to the nagging intuition that their survival clock had started ticking far, far faster the moment those cannons opened fire...

As they slogged forward into the teeth of the relentless storm in search of salvation, all Leo could do was pray his patchwork theories and Astrid's guerrilla instincts held true enough to get them out of this orbiting gauntlet alive.

Chapter 3: Neon and Kinetic Fire 

The unsteady cradle of Icaria bucked like a spooked stallion beneath their boots with another colossal impact. A shockwave rumbled in their earpieces as another of Astrid's scavenged warheads detonated amidst the asteroid field, scattering molten rock and fragmented metal across several kilometers of lunar-regional space. For all the good it did; the enemy ships remained ominously quiet, their hungry eyes locked onto their prey while patiently waiting for the perfect opportunity to tear the Starlight Marauder and its stowaway passengers to pieces.

Leo cursed under his breath, the acrid taste of fear mingling unpleasantly with the pre-cycled rations still lingering in his mouth. He risked a glance up at astral navigation suite, silently praying the next predicted firing solution would put enough distance between them... Any shred of separation worked in their favor at this point. The thought of being trapped aboard this flying target, with nothing save thin cerametal plates separating him from the void outside...

He shook himself free of that grim reverie before he could lose focus on the mission. Focus. That was what got people – no, soldiers - through impossible situations intact. Astrid had made that abundantly clear during their frantic briefing earlier.

Astrid pulled ahead as they crossed into the lee side of a small crater, her steps becoming more assured despite the treacherous surface. Leaning so heavily into the wind it seemed her exosuit might be about to drag her face-first through the regolith, she paused at the rim of the depression, peering out at the surrounding landscape with narrowed eyes scanning for threats both mundane and exotic.

"I think we've got some time before they decide to commit again, assuming they're smart enough not to ambush us in the open." She called back to Leo over the howling storm, shouting even though their helmets amplified voice perfectly thanks to sealed audio tubes. "Our goal has changed, mercier - find cover fast while we still can, then lay low for cover of night if needed. We need stealth, not direct confrontation."

Leo scrunched his nose in distaste, unused to thinking strategically without the comforting blanket of rigid orders dictating every action. The military mindset had been pounded into his bones in Tactical Officer school back home, leaving little room for ambiguity or improvisation beyond a few well-rehearsed drills. This was far removed from anything he'd ever faced at Nellis or Edwards Air Force Bases... 

"Pirates work on percentages, commander," Astrid supplied helpfully, seeming to read his concerns straight from his weathered features. Of course; her reputation preceded her. "We don't fight battles. We play geometry."

The words struck Leo like lightning. An instant later, insight began clicking into place amid the maelstrom of chaos unfolding around them. Geometry wasn't just a game for scavengers on the move; it could be their greatest strength - a shield hiding in plain sight among the stars. If he, Commander Leonardo "Mercurio" Mercer, could tap that same instinctive understanding...then perhaps even in the treacherous frontier of 32nd century piracy...

Perhaps alongside a fearsome yet enigmatic heroine like Astrid Vex 'Ravenstar' Ross herself...just maybe, together they could turn the stars themselves into their allies. And ride those celestial currents to victory against any foe or fate thrown in their path.

"The next time you wanna make a point," Leo growled half-amused, "try using something other than obscure analogies to drive your points home, huh, Vex?"

Leo's eyes stung from burning oxygen and the acrid smell of scorched ceramics as smoke curled up from the marauder's ragged hull plating. Searing bolts crisscrossed the nightmarish landscape outside their cockpit bubble, throwing surreal silhouettes across cracked viewscreens—the jagged teeth of some unseen planetoid, flayed bark scabbing over ancient wounds.

With every desperate bank away from the onslaught, inertia pressed him deeper into the flight bucket seat. Outside, stars wheeled wild kaleidoscopes and hurtled past in frozen strobes. Astrid leaned forward beside him, one hand gripping the armrest while the other rapidly slapped commands into the helm console, fingers dancing nimbly across glowing touch interfaces. 

Their conversation from mere seconds before seemed distant already—an eon ago rather than an eyeblink—as her intense concentration absorbed her completely. Sweat beading her forehead caught silver-blue starlight, glittering like dewdrops scattered across fine porcelain.

"This kind of fire—kinetic and neon both, coming from only three ships..." Her lips curved into a wry, unfathomable smile that barely concealed worry lines etched around eyes gleaming with predatory focus.

"It feels like more—their tracking software giving us false targets?" Leo interjected, mind racing to keep pace with this whirlwind of combat and wit.

"That's a decent bet," she said, not looking up. "Those rats are probably relying on auto-targeting. We're faster, slicker on our feet." A sharp laugh cracked from her throat amidst jagged panting. "Speaking of slick..."

Asteroids howled past in searing streaks of emerald and crimson light. As the Starlight lurched again, Leo braced against the harness straps digging into his chest and stomach with bone-jarring force. "So why exactly did you pick me, Captain Vex?" he asked harshly, struggling to maintain traction on the slick canopy glareshield despite the ship’s violent maneuvers. 

"Talent, Mercer...it leaps off you. I was right about hiring you." She spun to gaze at him fully, golden eyes alight beneath angled brows—a mix of fierce gratitude and undeniable camaraderie flashing there before she turned back to the tumultuous battlefield.

"You got something that most pilots out here lack—and that our particular brand of commerce needs."

As if embodying everything she spoke of, Astrid's left arm shot out sharply, striking a glowing sequence of command keys. An ominous hum filled the cockpit as engines cycled through another drastic overhaul—perhaps the twentieth in as many minutes.

Around them, the storm of starfire and meteoric fury intensified, shredding the void into a maelstrom of light and chaos. With a final, triumphant shout of stressed metal, the Marauder's fusion core surged once more, pouring raw energy into its battered drives. The entire ship bucked and roared, straining forward like a colossus bursting free of its bonds...

Now on the brink of their greatest leap since boarding her decks, Leo felt the rush build to crescendo—heavy chest, thundering pulse, lungs sucking in desperation each precious mouthful of tainted air amidst heat and noise and terror.

There, in the midst of such apocalyptic symphony, Astrid turned toward him fully. Their eyes locked in that charged moment—the gulf of centuries and worlds crumbling beneath the sheer will of two strangers forged into an unbreakable team.

"Ready, Mercurio?"

She held out her gloved hand, offering him the controls, the ship, their lives—all riding a precipice where loyalty and love, danger and desire, blurred into an irresistible pull.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo took Astrid's gauntlet with his own trembling fist, the gesture cementing their fated partnership beyond words. As their hands clasped, a stunning vista burst before them like some celestial work of art—a wormhole gateway opening deep within the turbulent nebula. Swirling threads of sapphire, emerald and crimson danced mesmerizingly against an abyssal blackness, pulsating with the cosmic music of creation and annihilation.

"Astral gate... confirmed," Astrid growled softly, voice strained with exhaustion yet alive with manic exhilaration. Her fingers flew across control surfaces, plotting their suicidal passage through the unstable tunnel.

"Velocity threshold hit," Leo reported grimly, monitoring key systems. "We're past point of no return."

The Starlight Marauder was a razor-thin sliver adrift against the riotous backdrop of the nebula—the only sanctuary within these relativistic winds—and they knew in their bones that neither would escape unscathed.

Their grip tightened reflexively. In the eerie quietude of the pressurized vessel, the faint hiss of atmosphere recyclers mingling with the eerie thrum of stressed drive plates created an otherworldly symphony.

"Mains off bypass in three... two..." Astrid counted down, her knuckles white. "...one. Engaging wormhole drive."

Leo watched the essential numbers tick up—thrust levels, inertial dampeners reaching capacity, the ship's skeleton wracked by the savage, rhythmic torqueing of gravity as it pierced the heart of the singularity... 

Then the gateway engulfed them.

Space-time warped into madness as the Marauder was dragged screaming through impossible velocities along paths contravening causality—itself both matter and conduit, a sentient vessel breaching the metaphysical.

Leo's body convulsed wildly, every nerve screaming as the stress wave propagated from bow to stern, threatening to annihilate them both in a catastrophic cascade of exotic energy...

They were flying blind now, hurtling through alien geometries whose rules they could barely grasp at warp speeds leaving reality in the dust...

With each agonizing pulse and shriek of tortured realspace fabric spewing quantum foam, something primal and beautiful died alongside each life they outran as faster-than-light made their souls feel as raw, exposed and vulnerable as if screaming into the eye of a supernova.

As the wormhole threw open its maw, catapulting the Marauder free once more, Leo found himself gasping for air... though their life support still cycled precious molecules in perpetuity within the sterile womb of their ship.

Through the viewport stretched seemingly infinite volumes of galaxies older than memory, stars deadlier than the Hand of God, and mysterious cosmic eddies whispering unspeakable truths...

"...is this... real?" Leo croaked, disbelieving eyes drinking in visions of sublime horror.

"Hold tight, Mercurio," Astrid warned, struggling to bring the ship under control. Even a corsair queen rarely got the chance to dance among the constellations themselves after all...

A sensor beep cut through the stunned silence, flashing repeatedly across the console:

"Unknown intruder detected, bearing 0 mark 28, range... zero."

Leo exchanged a panicked glance with Astrid over the dim blue glow of status displays.

"It seems we've got company," he whispered hoarsely.

"Fitting, really." The corsair chuckled darkly, eyes glinting with unholy excitement even as the hull groaned ominously under the pressure. "Wouldn't be my luck to die alone out here without finding myself a nice little dance partner first..."
