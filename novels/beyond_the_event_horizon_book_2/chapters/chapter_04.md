# Chapter 4: The Scrap Market Barter


Leo Mercer floated weightlessly by the viewport as Astrid gunned the engines, trying to shake off the unknown pursuer. Alarms flashed and klaxons blared in the claustrophobic confines of the bridge, underscoring the desperation of their predicament. Sweat matted Leo's blonde hair against his scalp, clinging films of perspiration glistening on his tanned face. Despite the chill of the cool air cycling through the ventilation, his palms grew slick where they gripped the console. His heart pounded violently in his ribcage, nearly overwhelming the steady thrum of the drives.

"Not so fast!" snarled a harsh voice from behind them.

Leo tensed, muscles coiling like springs ready to snap. He wheeled to face one imposing figure - another woman, her lean frame almost hidden beneath layers of utility webbing that crisscrossed her black, armored bodysuit. Her jet black hair was tied back in a cruel ponytail, studded with metallic talons that gleamed red-hot in the console lights. Scorch marks marred her angular complexion in angry crimson welts, while scars tracing sinuous paths across her body spoke starkly of countless battles with vicious vacuum-dwelling creatures.

"Orlo?" Astrid croaked incredulously, eyes narrowing as she met the warrior-pirate's glacial stare. Those were no mere decorative ornaments jutting stark from the corsair's gloved hands either - wicked edged close-quarters weapons optimized for brutal hand-to-hand fighting in any environment, soft skins to hard armor.

"My condolences about the Loomship," Orlo spat curtly before fixing icy blue eyes full of fury upon Astrid. "You'll pay for what you did."

Astrid just smirked, unflinching despite the gravity of those words. "Guess I should turn my helm to the Magistracy then," the corsair quipped defiantly, "but you know how that plays these days." She shrugged blandly, but Leo saw the brief flicker of genuine concern cross her expression underneath.

That was the moment when Leo realized just how deep down the rabbit hole things had fallen. They couldn't simply flee the Reach anymore – not with Orlo hot on their heels, and certainly not with Kael's secret alliance crumbling around them. If this turned ugly, the Magistracy wouldn't lift a finger to help them. Their only options would be to defend themselves... or let the merciless void take everything.

"I don't want your blood on my conscience," Orlo growled lowly to Leo, her attention shifting briefly to the astrogator. That sent a tremor skating up his spine - the first hints of fear began creeping into the pit of his stomach as realization dawned of just how outnumbered and outgunned they truly were... but that fear also forged clarity into determination. He wasn't going down without a fight.

"You had your chance to stay civil back on the Loom," Astrid shot back, drawing Leo's gaze again to see the ruthless set to her jaw as she continued to wrest control of the thrashing ship from the savage tugs of gravity's embrace pushing them towards the hungry singularity of some distant black hole. "Time's up for pretenses.Orlo's just doing her job... but I'm giving it to her straight-up here. You choose, sweetheart - join me, or take your chances with whatever darkness is waiting to swallow us whole together."

With that ultimatum hanging heavy in the air between them, the warrior-pirate's lip curled into an unforgiving sneer.

Leo braced himself against the tumultuous lurches of the crippled starfreighter, struggling to maintain balance while Astrid fought tooth and nail to yank Starlight Marauder out of the gravity-warping singularity's deadly grip. His heart hammered a chaotic rhythm beneath his torso strap as he locked gazes with the corsair captain once more, her icy blue eyes drilling into him with unrelenting intensity.

"What makes you think I'd ever side with a pirate?" he spat back, attempting to convey the same steely defiant edge she projected so effortlessly. It was harder than he thought, faced with the sheer force of will radiating from this woman who existed in a realm well beyond anything he'd known. But there was no backing down now, not after embracing his role in shaping his fate alongside hers.

Astrid snorted, a humorless sound barely carried over the freighter's distressed creaking and groaning. "Pig-headed determination isn't exactly uncommon in your species. Still, I guess that's part of what got you stranded here in the first place - too blinded by loyalty and honor to see the bigger picture." Her gaze flicked away briefly to the astrogator console, where data streams of impossible angular momentum struggled to make sense of their situation.

The revelation of their extreme disadvantage sank in like cold dread, each passing moment of exposure to the maelstrom sending new jolts of fear racing along Leo's nerves despite his efforts to steel himself. There'd been a time when the daunting array of armaments affixed to the Marauder's exterior looked impressive – all those fearsome missiles, laser cannons, particle beam disruptors...but under these desperate circumstances, their weapons load felt hopelessly inadequate compared to the scale of the danger chasing them across light-years.

That sobering knowledge did little to stem the rising tide of adrenaline coursing through his system though. For a man whose entire adult existence until now had revolved around pushing the envelope of aircraft and spacecraft performance within the limitations imposed by atmospheric drag and Earth's magnetic field, the stark realization of just how constrained they were in vacuum thrust and maneuverability was devastating.

And yet even amidst these calamitous surroundings, Leo found himself drawn inexorably to Astrid, to the aura of ruthless pragmatism she embodied and the raw power simmering beneath her sleek grace. As thrilling as the prospect of escaping into the unknown reaches of the cosmos seemed – perhaps even necessary for any hope of regaining his old life – he couldn't simply abandon this resourceful corsair and her crew to face the abyss alone.

"Honor has flaws, sure," Leo acknowledged finally, voice tinged with a wry edge of self-deprecation, "but it gives people something to stand on, a foundation for fighting for what matters most to 'em. When we're done running, what do you hope to rebuild? What's worth going outlaw for?" He held her stony gaze steadfast, refusing to concede ground even as the freighter lurched wildly once more.

Astrid let out a harsh bark of laughter, her lips twisting into a derisive smirk as she shook her head incredulously at Leo's innocence regarding the true nature of her existence. "Sweetheart, I don't have much use for 'foundation' these days. The only solid ground anymore is whoever controls the flow – of resources, information, or, when push comes to shove, firepower." Her eyes gleamed avidly with a predator's hunger as she added, "In a universe where civilization hangs by threads of precarious balances, survival means making whatever deals and breaking whatever rules necessary to stay above water."

Chapter 4: The Scrap Market Barter

As Vex leaned back in her chair, the ship lurching sharply into another violent roll, she flashed Leo a grin devoid of mirth. The starfighter jockey had clearly never witnessed such brutal orbital piracy firsthand before finding himself aboard her vessel. His wide-eyed naivety was both refreshing and maddening.

"I told you, little hotshot pilot," she repeated with a laugh that bordered on cruel in its mocking undertone, "survival ain't about honor or integrity—especially not around here. It's cash, credits, or...other valuable commodities." Her gaze slid down Leo's athletic form with an unfrosted hunger that had nothing to do with hunger for food or drink. "If someone's got what I need and is willing to part with it, then I'm eager to do business—a simple cause and effect proposition."

Her eyes met his once more, a wild spark leaping forth to briefly illuminate the depths within their amber hues. It stirred something primal in him—an instinctive awareness of danger, yes, but also a dark temptation born of the thrill-seeking adventurer buried beneath layers of 21st century civility and rectitude.

"There's more to survival than just...trading goods and services, isn't there?" Leo ventured quietly, sensing the complex currents beneath her surface ruthlessness. "People, ideals—things you truly care about." Though his query sounded timid, an unspoken challenge hung in the air, daring her to confront this alien notion of moral fiber amidst the gritty pragmatism of scrap-running. "That's how humanity built civilizations, found strength amid adversity. There always has to be..." He trailed off, searching for the right word to bridge the immense cultural chasm between them. "...something deeper at the core of why we go to war, sign up with causes, risk everything—the things that make us human."

"Aww, Leo, you're so adorable when you try to get all philosophical." Vex snorted derisively, shaking her shaggy hair like an unsettled dog. Even humorously dismissive, however, her voice retained a subtle husky warmth that raised the hairs at the nape of his neck. She drummed her fingers thoughtfully against the armrest, leaning forward in a show of mock fascination. "Tell me, idealist, if morality was our guiding light, how'd humanity end up trashing the only home they've ever known?"

The rhetorical question hung in the charged silence that fell abruptly across the command center, punctuated by the distant clamor of guns blazing and screaming thrusters as Vex's crew repelled boarders assaulting the marauder in mid-orbit. It took Leo a moment to collect his fragmented thoughts as the scope of their desperate struggle crystallized anew.

"We were shortsighted, made mistakes," he admitted gruffly, eyes downcast toward his own hands clenched white-knuckled around his chair arms. "Didn't foresee consequences. But we've learned, adapted—that's what defines our species' resilience." Meeting Vex's watchful amber gaze steadily, Leo drew breath to issue a defiant clarion call, only to be cut short by a sickening crunch reverberating deep within the gut of the freighter.

"Tactical alert!" Alarms exploded in a frenzied symphony of flashing lights and piercing tones. Seeding vessels launched from corvette-size interceptors began to fan out around them, cutting off escape routes in a deadly game of cat-and-mouse in null gravity.

Leo scrambled upright, reflexes honed by countless stress simulations surging to the forefront.

Adrenaline surged through Leo's veins as he sprang erect in the command-center chair, instincts forged in test pilot simulations snapping immediately to urgent focus. Tactical displays flickered to life ahead of him - chaotic patterns of searing crimson and ominous indigo tracing interwoven orbits, with menacing vectors of interception coursing like dark lightning among the tangle of seed vessels.

"The hell? How many—"

"Fifty-six fighters, seventy-nine pursuit drones, and sixteen destroyers on approach," Astrid Vex intoned crisply, her attention divided elegantly between the unfolding tactical landscape and the harried expressions of her bridge crew. "Looks like your people sent a welcoming party to escort us out of the scrap market orbit."

As she spoke, the Starlight Marauder rocked hard under them in response to another scorching hit somewhere below decks, shuddering violently enough to rattle cups and consoles alike. Vex cursed colorfully but kept her icy poise, blue eyes narrowed against the cacophony of war horns and emergency sirens wailing incessantly across the command post. Smoke curled lazily from scorched bulkheads while blood-spattered crew members hastily patched damaged systems, desperation evident in the frantic gestures and frayed nerves.

Leo whipped his head around, jaw tight with concern for both women at his flank: First Officer Yara Nasri, whose sleek black hair had come loose during the chaos, now shielding her faceplate with one hand as she worked feverishly to bring an auxiliary shield online; and Lieutenant Maelin Kresh, an unflappable genius linguist and codesmith whose stoic pallor belied the white knuckles gripping the comms console.

"Any breakthroughs on those transmission puzzles, Mael?" he demanded brusquely. The younger officer barely looked up from the holo display still cycling through maddening cryptic sequences—the digital trail left behind by Vex's cunning hack at a heavily-guarded asteroid base mere hours prior. If deciphered, those encrypted files seemed certain to expose criminal activities large enough to attract the most uncompromising forces in the settled galaxy.

The young Ens signs silently, flashing a quick sequence of alphanumeric codes on the side of his hand before turning back to immerse himself once more in the mind-numbing complexity pinging across the console before him. At this rate, it would take months to crack, even for someone as gifted as Mael.

"Doubt we'll find any easy answers," Vex remarked drily, shooting him a sidelong glance threaded with dry amusement beneath the tension coiling her lean features. Her voice softened slightly as she switched attention to Leo. "Looks like somebody's keen to keep these embarrassing revelations safely buried alongside the rest of the empire's dirty laundry."

He grimaced, lips pressed into a thin line. The implications of such ruthlessness were daunting, as unfathomable motivations swirled behind the veiled expressions on battle-hardened faces now focused dead-set on defending their floating fortress against impossible odds.

"So what do you need from me next, Skipper?" Leo asked grimly, shoulders squared against the onslaught closing in rapidly around them like some vengeful cosmic storm front.

A smirk curved briefly at the comers of Vex's mouth, her eyes glinting with cold hunger. "Oh, I reckon our friend Mercer here has talents yet untapped. We're goin' fishin', see if we can reel in a few of those pesky interceptors while the boys patch us up. Time to put some paid on the table, ace."
