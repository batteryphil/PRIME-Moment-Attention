# Chapter 17: The First Touch


The warm afterglow of passion ebbed gently through Astrid's system, leaving trails of sparkling satisfaction and contentment in its wake. Her fingers lazily circled the intricate tattoo decorating Leo's collarbone, a visual reminder of the intimate connection forged tonight. The captain smiled softly, basking in the rare comfort and vulnerability she'd allowed herself. Perhaps it was the ethereal beauty of the nebula still bathing their quarters, or more likely the unguarded tenderness she felt for this curious relic of the past, but Astrid found herself opening up unprecedented depths within herself.

"So tell me more about your old world...and all these notions you have about us pirates," she prodded lightly, shifting to face him as he settled more comfortably beside her. The silken sheets of the sleeping pod rustled ever so slightly with their movements, releasing a faint whiff of their combined scent - a mingling of ozone from their earlier flight suits, leather from his jacket, and something uniquely Astrid that clung to her skin.

Leo glanced at the shimmering expanse visible through the polarized viewport, where distant stars burned faintly against the nebula's sapphire canvas. Then his intense gaze switched to meet hers, full of curiosity and a dash of trepidation at the intimacy growing between them.

"It's funny, I spent years testing cutting-edge craft, always knowing how and why things were engineered a certain way...but never truly appreciating the artistry behind it until I saw your ship," he began slowly, one hand instinctively reaching to trace the fine curves of the captain's lips. She let out a soft hum of encouragement, fingers interlacing with his through the thin fabric covering his chest.

"You put so much style and creativity into every inch of Starlight Marauder... it's an extension of who you are, a living testament to the innovation and passion needed to survive outside the fold," he continued, eyes searching hers intently as if seeking agreement. "That's the spirit of piracy, isn't it? Embracing the unknown, taking calculated risks, and trusting in the expertise honed by countless challenges?"

Astrid nodded slowly, studying his intent expression. "Yes, that is us. Adaptation and resilience - those are the core talents needed to thrive on the fringes of civilization."

Her voice carried a note of melancholy edged by pride, and Leo couldn't help but notice how those same traits were evident in himself nowadays. Forced onto an unfamiliar trajectory after being pulled from his own timeline, he now rode a similar edge of existence - always looking over his shoulder, relying on ingenuity to scrape together resources enough to stay one step ahead of their pursuers.

"What about your ideals though?" Leo asked cautiously, hesitant to tread on sensitive emotional territory, yet compelled by a rising sense of affinity towards his enigmatic captain. "The stuff you mentioned before, about helping others...does that come into play here too? With your pirating activities?"

Astral's emerald irises narrowed slightly as memories long buried rose to the surface. The bitter taste of loss and regret mingled with anger - righteous outrage fueling her relentless pursuit of those who exploited and oppressed.

"Ah, our noble crusade..." Her dry chuckle contained no mirth whatsoever. With a small shake of her head, the intense focus returned to her features. "We strike against those who wield power without accountability...like corporations with bottom-line ethics as black as space, or corrupt governments hiding behind the flags of their colonies".

Leaning back against the cushions, Astrid wrapped arms around Leo's form, tucking her head beneath his chin.

With Astrid curled snugly against him, Leo felt the warmth of her slim body penetrating his leather jacket like a brand. Their heartbeats synchronized, a primitive rhythm mirroring the ship drifting through the nebula's kaleidoscope depths beyond the viewport. Out there, the roiling gas clouds sculpted fantastical shapes that danced on the edges of reality, an eternal expanse dwarfing even the Starlight's imposing silhouette.

Leo rested a heavy hand on Astrid's shoulder, marveling at the solidness despite her lithe frame beneath. "So the pirates," he said slowly, "are actually...more than just thieves. You're using your fleet to correct some serious wrongs out there."

Astrid gave a wry hum, her cheek pressing firmer against his chest as she nodded. "Sometimes it requires a dose of chaos to spark change. And if I can plunder enough credits doing it to fund our next mission—"

"Funding comes first," Leo interpreted quietly. "Then justice."

A smirk played across her lips as she looked up at him through her lashes, but there was an underlying sadness in that piercing green gaze. "It used to be the other way around, back when I believed all that idealistic hogwash." She sighed, then tilted her face forward, her breath tickling Leo's throat. "I got burnt, Leo. Badly. They killed my crewmates, framed me for treason—it's why I went rogue, why I took to piracy as a means of survival. That old Astrid died along with them."

Her words hung heavy in the enveloping calm of the cabin, weighted with decades-old grief mixed with seething resentment. Leo gazed down at her, silently acknowledging the pain reflected in her eyes, even as a new realization struck him like a comet's tail burning up atmosphericEntry.

"Pirates...like the ones your empire would've sent after us?" Dread seeped into his voice.

The irony seemed lost on Astrid, consumed by her bitter past. But Leo understood now—the scale of what they had stumbled into, the complexity of Astrid's motives that ran deeper than simplistic rebellion against any single force.

"They'd stop at nothing to claim this ship," he murmured, stroking her hair as a gesture of solidarity.

She tensed briefly, perhaps sensing the shift in tone. Then she nuzzled into his touch, exhaling softly. In the stillness,Leo registeredthe subtle thump-thump of the ship's hull as it absorbed stray meteoroid impacts, a constant reminder of the unforgiving environment just outside their sanctuary.

Slowly, imperceptibly, he shifted his hand to wrap fully around Astrid's shoulder, feeling the resilience of muscle beneath downy skin. His thumb brushed the nape of her neck, sending a faint tremor through her. It was a gentle, almost unconscious gesture—a bridge spanning two vastly different worlds colliding within their shared vessel.

Their gazes met once more—Ivory and Emerald locked in a silent question, a fragile alliance forged against forces seeking to tear them apart again. As Leo held his breath, so did Astrid, her chest rising and falling with measured precision. 

In that moment, suspended between cosmos and steel hull—a nebula's heartbeat underpinningtheir suspended animation—neither spoke of trust gained, emotions unveiled, nor the precarious tightrope each stood upon. This was merely the beginning of understanding, tentative footsteps onto unstable ground. Suffice to say, amidst stars born of violent supernovae and dying light-years fading into cold inky nothingness—their own universe had just expanded dramatically.

Commander Leo Mercer froze at the first alarming vibrations jarring through the Starlight Marauder. They rippled up his spine like icy fingers, drawing back to curl protectively over his instrument panel. "What the—" his voice clipped off as the ominous tremors intensified, shaking the very foundation of the sleek corvette.

His gut screamed impending catastrophe as instruments began glitching and plunging toward systemic failure. Alarms screeched like tortured gossamer threads, flashing a garish strobe across console displays. Leo's heart stuttered then raced ahead, pounding a frantic staccato in his ears.

Beside him, Astrid Vex snapped to attention, her emerald eyes narrowing to slits as they converged on the data streams spewing chaos before them. "Engine distress!" she barked, already lunging for the maintenance compartment. "Pulse injectors malfunctioning! We've lost primary coolant—vent and divert excess heat!"

Mercer scrambled after her, his boots clanging hollowly on each step. He barely registered the unsettling silence of vacuum as they burst through the access hatch, revealing the engine chamber's hellish furnace. Rivulets of superheated steam poured from ruptured lines, hissing and splattering like a furious geysershot across scorched bulkheads. 

Astrid worked with ruthless efficiency, her gloved hands streaked with soot and grime as she disconnected damaged components, re-routing bypass conduits. Her breath came in ragged snorts between muttered curses and sharp gasps as the air siphoned away, pressure gauge plummeting swiftly toward catastrophic.

Leo seized an opportunity amidst the pandemonium to grasp one of her wrists, holding firm as she frantically fiddled with a recalcitrant spark gap generator. Their eyes met in a fleeting, intense glare - warning, reassurance, trust—before he wrenched his gaze back to the critical tasks before them. 

The reality of their desperate bind slammed home—he was utterly dependent on this deadly pirate captain now. No protocol existed for this dire scenario. Only instinct and a shared imperative to survive propelled them onward.

With a triumphant curse, Astrid jammed the repaired component back into place, sealed connections and slapped controls shut. Oxygen levels began a halting climb while temperature gauges stabilized ever so slightly.

Mercer released her wrist for only long enough to key open an external comm channel as the starfield filled their windows—ominous tachyon bursts heralding nearby hostile vessels bearing down on the crippled Marauder.

"Astrid!" he shouted over the renewed battering of alarm klaxons. "We need to set up for emergency FTL jumps—our only hope." His voice cracked with strain but resolve tightened his jaw.

Vex nodded curtly, her face grim as she tapped sequences into the mainframe. "I'm prepping the compensators. We'll have one shot to get clear before those bloodhounds realize what's going on."

Leo felt the familiar surge of adrenaline mixed with something new—something startlingly akin to affection for the fiery pirate at his side. Amidst the maelstrom of death and destruction swirling without, amidst the thundering panic of their own predicament—the silence between them crackled with the potential for something transformative.

But there wasn't time for introspection. Survival demanded they merge as seamless a team as possible...which meant no more distance, no more guardrails separating them either physically or emotionally.

In a lightning-fast motion devoid of hesitation, Leo seized Astrid's chin and pulled her mouth to meet his in a fierce, desperate kiss.

Their lips met in bruising force, a brutal clash of breathlessness and desperation. Leo's hands cradled Vex's jaw, angling her head, claiming the heated slope of her cheeks. Her fingers dug fiercely into his sleeves as if trying to anchor herself against the chaos.

For a pivotal heartbeat nothing else in the universe mattered—no screaming alarms, no lurching ship, none of the hellish vortex of enemies closing in. Just the electric zing of skin on skin, tongues tangling, years of pent-up tension detonating like a plasma burst behind their eyes.

When at last they parted for air, the afterglow still smoldered hot enough to scorch. Eyes locked, they gasped in tandem, chests heaving. Leo saw a kaleidoscope of emotions flicker across Vex's face: fear, triumph, raw need, and beneath it all, a profound sense of connection. Of understanding each other.

It was fleeting—a momentary interlude before reality came raging back. "Compensators are online," Vex rasped, voice coarse with arousal and urgency. She gestured to the viewscreen where stars had coalesced into brilliant streaks of color and light. Gas giants loomed large, casting ethereal shadows across smaller rocky worlds.

Leo's gaze followed hers, transfixed by the staggering vista of celestial dance—and then by the tiny, menacing symbols denoting unrelenting pursuit by the pursuers. A shiver ran down his spine as the full implication sank in.

"We're not going to outrun them," he said, disgust dripping from every word as he glared at the readouts. "Those compensators won't cut it past the edge of normal space."

Vex looked away, lips curling in bitter acknowledgment. "Which means we gamble everything on making a jump point before—" She killed the sentence with clenched teeth.

"—before they catch us cold," Mercer finished grimly. Sweat trickled down his temples as the awful certainty settled over him. This could be it—it really could end here, far beyond the comforting bounds of human space, at the cruel caprice of unfathomable cosmic forces.

The air grew heavy, thickening with the weight of their shared fate. Not even the desperate heat of their bodies, pressed together in stolen intimacy, could dispel the chill crawling up Vex's spine.

They exchanged a glance fraught with unspoken regret and longing, and knowing that precious seconds ticked away with each passing moment. Whatever might have been, whatever fragile bonds had almost been forged, would remain forever out of reach.

Because in the end, there were just two choices left, and neither offered any respite from the abyss about to swallow them whole—

"Plot the nearest jump point," Mercer ordered hoarsely, ripping his attention back to survival. "And pray to whatever gods might hear us that this is our lucky break."
