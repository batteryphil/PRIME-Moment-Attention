# Chapter 14: Whispers from the Golden Age

Leo Mercer shifted uncomfortably against the frigid hull of the derelict dreadnought, a grim reminder of the starry chasm that had engulfed Sol centuries ago. The airlock hissed shut with an ominous clang behind him, sealing them within the long-abandoned battleship's guts. A soft crimson glow emanated from the readouts lining the mainframe's console - each blinking digit a stark contrast against the looming darkness pressing at the viewport.

"We're in," Astrid muttered, eyes intent upon the pulsating information scrolling across the ancient display. Her slender fingers danced over the worn keyboard with practiced ease, coaxing secrets from the relic's tortured systems. The metallic tang of rust and ozone hung heavy in the air, mingling with the faintest whiff of something... else. Something eerie and decaying, left behind in the wake of catastrophe.

"Hellions take me," the corsair captain breathed, her voice barely audible above the soft thrum of residual power. "These logs predate Sol's quarantine... Which means Helix Dawn sailed the system waters right before everything turned to shit." Her gaze snapped up from the screen to lock onto Leone's, a haunted glimmer lurking deep within their amber depths. "Whatever happened, it looks even deadlier than we first imagined, kid."

The dreadnought's cold, featureless bulk seemed to close in around them with each passing second, the very walls seeming to press against him with an unrelenting force. A shiver crawled down Leone's spine despite the layers of protective tech layered beneath his fatigues, as if the dreadnought held some sort of malevolent presence, watching them with the unblinking gaze of a predator.

Astrid's fingers flew across the keyboard again with renewed urgency, the readout spools spinning faster to meet her demands. "I'm going to hack the navigation database... maybe we can find—"

A sudden, ear-splitting shriek pierced the air, making Leone's heart jump into his throat. The lights flickered wildly before settling into a chaotic dance of red and blue, casting wild shadows across the metal floor as gravity anchors surged to life.

"What in the seven hells?" Astrid snarled, one hand instinctively lashing out to grab the edge of a console as the ship lurched violently through space. The force pressed Leone hard against the padded wall, a sickening crunch radiating from his left shoulder where it slammed into the hard plastic. He gasped, the pain searing through numbing shock as his body struggled to adapt to the unexpected wrench backward into inertia.

Panic began to rise, cold tendrils winding their way up Leo's spine as the derelict roared around them, pitching and yawing without any semblance of order. Vacuum rapped hungrily at the breached hull seams, a chilling soundless breeze buffeting him relentlessly no matter how tightly he huddled within the cramped confines of the mainframe room.

"The dreadnought!" Leone cried out, fingers scrabbling against the buckled decking for traction as his stomach lurched viciously from the violent maneuvering. "Something triggered its self-defense protocols – or hell, the damned thing could've been booby-trapped!"

"I think you might be right." Astrid's face was pale, teeth gritted as she fought to maintain her footing amidst the madness. "Those logs have got us stalling too bloody long, Mercer! We're either trapped onboard this meat-grinder or—"

Her words cut short as another seismic jolt rocked the dreadnought sideways with terrible force, sending Leo sprawling halfway across the room.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo crashed heavily onto the scorched, buckled floorplate, the air rushed from his lungs in a panicked whoosh. Fractures of jagged metal glittered from torn openings in the ceiling above, casting eerie shadows against the darkened expanse as another convulsion ripped through the derelict.

"Astrid?" Leone cried out, heart hammering against his ribs like a frightened bird taking flight. He scrambled to his knees, wincing as something ground painfully beneath his palms. Peering blindly past an arm cocked over his squinted eyes, he caught a glimpse of the marauder captain just before another jolt slammed him back against the walls once more.

"Screw my neck loose..." Leo groaned, head spinning, fingers gingerly exploring a tender lump swelling near the base of his skull. Something hot and tingling snaked down his spine, warning flags waved red in his mind. Adrenaline surged as instinct kicked in, muscles tensing in preparation to spring clear if that blast opened the seam wider.

"You alright, merc?!"

Astrid's voice pierced the noise and chaos, sharp with concern even as she held fast against the battering of the ship. He felt her nearness then, looming close enough that she probably couldn't help but breathe in some of the caustic haze churning out from underfoot.

"Barely," Leone managed to choke out, swallowing heavily. He tried to push upright again, bracing one hand against the wall behind them while scanning frantically for their escape route. The sight his eyes landed on made blood drain from his cheeks.

"Oh fuck..."

The gaping rent in the hull had spread alarmingly during those frantic seconds, already gushing forth streams of frigid starlight laced with glittering ice shards. Beyond the open maw stretched nothing but the merciless cold of space, stars blazing away like diamond pinpricks against the midnight blue void.

"Merc... there ain't shit left to salvage but parts worth stripping."

Astrid delivered her grim assessment calmly, almost resigned, which only ramped up Leo's burgeoning unease. He'd faced countless dangers in active duty, suffered injuries he never thought would heal properly, spent years haunted by dreams of comrades lost along the razor's edge of war. But this - drowning in an alien vessel crippled beyond repair, potentially stranded lightyears from home without a functional FTL drive, surrounded by no telling what kinds of unforgiving hazards... No, his nerves simply weren't equipped to handle a situation like this.

"A-Vac suit..." came drifting back from somewhere distant, Leone realizing dimly that he was the one whose voice sounded so distant, echoing eerily off bulkheads streaked black with fuel residue. His gaze snapped to Astrid once more, noting sharply how she was pulling herself toward the gap, fingers reaching out with practiced ease to hook a grip about twisted lengths of girder protruding from the breach.

"I don't know if this derelict even has suits compatible with your systems," she cautioned him bluntly, not slowing her movements in the slightest. With a grunt, Astrid hauled herself through the widening sliver, leaving Leo to trail after her, awkward hands scrabbling ineffectually at the cold edge until his knuckles cracked painfully.

"I'll figure it out!" he shouted after her, breath misting as he stumbled forward and wedged himself through beside Astrid. She barely seemed fazed by his entrance despite the brutal angle they sprawled across in a tangle of limbs.

✦ ✦ ✦

Chaos reigned within the gaping void as Leo spilled into the alien ship alongside Astrid, both bodies contorting wildly against the uneven surface. He landed with a heavy thud, crunching knees into exposed wiring while his arms flailed for purchase among the jagged metal teeth rent open in their wake. Shards of broken circuit boards pierced the air as if in mocking greeting.

"What – ugh!—happened?" Leone gasped out, straining eyes to pierce the thick gloom surrounding them. The flickering emergency lights aboard neither vessel mingled into an eerie strobe effect, casting the scene in hallucinatory shadows. Acrid scents of fried electronics and scorched insulation seared his nostrils.

"Thrusters malfunctioned during reentry!" Astrid called over her shoulder, her silhouette ducking to slip beneath exposed decking that creaked ominously. "Couldn't control descent velocity." That was all the explanation she gave before resurfacing into the main passage ahead, bootsoles scraping against mangled conduit covers.

Leo struggled upright, ribs protesting from the violent impact. Sweat trickled down his cheek regardless of the chilly air and adrenaline pulsing through his veins. He watched in awe as Astrid navigated with deadly grace, boots clattering against the twisted framework leading deeper into the derelict. Every clench of her gloved hand against some jutting fragment sent tremors rippling up her sinewy arms.

"No signs of stabilizing yet," Astrid noted coolly, jerking her head back toward the breach. "This bucket could collapse around us any second."

The dire warning struck a chord, urging Leo's legs into reluctant motion as he fought past the queasy sensation of floating free in microgravity. As he moved further away from the gap, the darkness began to coalesce into definable shapes – viewscreen wreckage scattered across the floor plates, console components torn from mounts, shattered stasis pods leaking frost and wisps of frozen blood.

Leone staggered to a halt as the scope of destruction slowly registered. What kind of cataclysmic event had ravaged this once-proud ship? Astrid halted just inches from a massive tear in the outer hull where a chunk of superstructure lay embedded amidst glittering shards of ceramic-hulled armor plating.

"It looks like something tore clean through from outside, maybe some debris from the original battle," Astrid mused grimly, tracing a finger along the ragged lip before hauling herself up onto whatever fragments might offer structural support.

Leo followed suit more cautiously, wincing as grit and dust ground against every scrap of exposed skin. Perching gingerly on his haunches, he peered down into the yawning chasm below. Far beneath them, an opaque void gaped where atmosphere and gravity should prevail.

"One wrong move and we plummet into who-knows-what," Leone muttered, eyeing the precipice warily.

"If your thruster fix holds, I'll worry about that later." Astrid’s attention snapped to Leo, gaze intensified under the harsh sidelight of the tear. "Right now, the FTL might be toasted. We need alternatives if we hope to escape this tomb before it goes nova around us." Her eyes narrowed, voice taking on a challenge as only Astrid Vex could muster. "Think you can work some miracles, hotshot?"

The brusque dismissal notwithstanding, a spark of competitive heat licked life into Leo’s sluggish thoughts. They both knew full well his reputation from back on Earth – but he'd faced challenges vastly unlike this gauntlet, pitting centuries-old tech against threats from hell itself.

✦ ✦ ✦

With a deep breath of scorching air reeking of ionized plasma and spent propellant, Leo stood atop the jagged edge of the torn starship hull. A bitter taste of burned alloys coated his tongue. Far beneath their precarious perch, an abyssal darkness yawned, where atmosphere and gravity should have been - replaced by a chilling void that seemed to whisper eternal cold.

"Careful not to take another tumble," Leone advised gruffly, shooting his companion a wary glance. "This isn't exactly the most stable foundation."

Astrid gave him a clipped nod, her focus already drifting toward Leo's makeshift workshop cluttered across one side of the fissure. She traced her finger along the rough edges of the breach thoughtfully. Fragments of molten matter still dripped from overhead like viscous honey, leaving livid trails against the scorched metal substrate. "There might be intact sections of propulsion system left clean through from outside, maybe some debris from the original battle," she mused grimly, mind calculating odds so slim they bordered on lunacy.

Leo had already begun assessing what lay within his reach, wincing as gritty particles grated abrasively against exposed skin. He perched awkwardly on his haunches, eyes piercing the gloom for usable components amidst the devastation. If luck hadn't turned its back entirely, they might salvage enough parts to cobble together a crude FTL drive off the cuff.

But that was tomorrow's headache. Today's priority revolved around escaping the dying starship before it detonated into a cataclysmic fireball right on top of them. A desperate plan coalesced in his head - if the FTL engines were indeed toasted, they needed alternatives. Fast ones.

His thoughts jolted back to the present as Astrid fixed him with a hawk-like stare, the intensity of which he felt all too keenly despite the layers of grime coating their faces. "If your thruster fix holds, I'll worry about that later," she bit out, her words heavy with unspoken urgency. Beneath the rough bravado, the sheer scale of their predicament threatened to overwhelm even that fierce will.

Leo squared his shoulders, steeling himself against the gut-wrenching knowledge that all he'd ever known was worlds away now. Yet something within his core refused to yield to despair. This, after all, was what pilots lived for - facing impossible challenges and pushing beyond perceived limits.

Astrid's sharp barb pierced his reverie, challenging him once more. "Think you can work some miracles, hotshot?"

Ember-green flames danced within her irises, casting a mesmerizing glow amidst the ruins. And Leo found himself grinning - a defiant, challenging smile carved by desperation mingled with reckless optimism. In defiance of good sense and personal stakes, a thrill kindled in his chest at the prospect of defying the very fabric of interstellar commerce and piracy. Of showing this legend of the Marauder that one ace couldn't cut it in the 32nd century.

He met Astrid's penetrating gaze squarely, the heat of her glare mirroring the fire burning bright within his own rebellious soul. No amount of bruises, cracked skulls or smoldering wreckage would dent his resolve. "Miracles aren't required. Just need the right tools and a solid plan," he flashed back confidently, the familiar cadence of fighter craft lingo rolling off his tongue like second nature.

Below, the starboard cargo bay loomed dark and ominous across the breached fuselage - a gaping chasm beckoning, promising freedom... or an agonizing plunge into the abyss.
