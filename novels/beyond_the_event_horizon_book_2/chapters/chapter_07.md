# Chapter 7: The Hangar Bay Escape

Marauder 's colossal engines shrieked defiance in unison, seismic dreadnotes of power that shook Vex down to the molecular level. Unrelenting, merciless, they demanded sacrifice to unleash acceleration that clawed ruthlessly at the quantum fabric of the cosmos.

G-forces slammed down upon Vex with biblical fury, flattening her against the contoured seat and squeezing lungs shriveled in place until every labored breath was forced through clenched teeth. Rivulets of acid-stung sweat soaked her flight suit, stinging eyes made bloodshot from squinted gaze glued to data feeds scrolling across holo displays etched in frosty blue.

Every tremor, every shudder in the ship's battle-frame reverberated up the spinal column in feedback loops of grinding agony—agonized sympathetic resonance as the Marauder 's ancient skeletal structure screamed revolt against the crushing assault delivered by her own potent engines. Yet Astrid held firm, knees locked tight, gloved hand knuckled white against control yokes. No surrender, no submission.

Beyond the viewscreen pane (once glass, remade from recycled alloys reformed and fused under arc-weld purples), constellations streaked through starlight torn apart and reassembled, galaxies careened by at surreal velocities. Frigid superheated plasma blossomed in Marauder 's massive exit trails like gaseous sunbursts, blooming into searing corona flares that temporarily eclipsed stars themselves. Radiant jets pulsed rhythmically along symmetrical flanks as steering thrusters worked in tandem, fighting the gravitational momentum and torques trying to wrench the ship off track or spin out of control.

But even gods in their vastness could not deflect an asteroid fortress's gunslinging precision from finding its mark. Autocannons bellowed thunderclap volleys down the docking spine where Marauder lay entombed, cratering duracrete walls with explosive force. Debris and sparks flew, ricochets tearing through conduit banks trailing flames—yet the Marauder survived thus far due only to her sleek armored shape providing scant purchase for those projectiles meant for softer targets within.

Vex dared spare a glance aft, the panoramic dome above her station framing the hellish panorama unfolding in the hangar bay's depths. Infernal light bathed everything in a lurid orange glare, throwing long shadows stark against bulkheads slick with hydraulic fluid and lubricants. Asteroid fragments, crushed and molten, danced in tortured pirouettes amidst the fiery melee, briefly illuminated before being consumed once more by churning chaos.

Amidst the apocalyptic backdrop loomed the imposing black silhouette of Sovereign Dreadnought Phobos—its armor-crested wedge piercing the conflagration like a fang driven through bloody meat. Turrets traversed fluidly along the main hull, hammering at Marauder 's hiding spot again and again. Massive energy weapons painted scorching red lines through vacuum mere thousandths away as capacitors discharged crackling waves of ionized potential.

Time compressed into endless microseconds of agonized suspended animation—that single breath when falling through a waterfall or staring impaled on a spearpoint—before Captain and ship lunged forth together in reckless abandon and glorious triumph, surging upward through narrow silo tunnels designed for cargo and personnel in both directions with minimal clearance in between.

There was no room to maneuver, not enough mass for any decent evasion. They would have to punch through like a blunt instrument, rely on armor and speed to overcome any obstacles thrown in their path.

Leo grabbed the nearest handle as Vex expertly spun the grav-craft within the confines of the cylindrical corridor they were hurtling up at near-terminal velocity. The metal grated beneath their boots with each desperate rotation that sent them careening narrowly past massive freight containers packed with mysterious crates and barrels. The hiss of tortured atmosphere sealed within some and the occasional clatter of loosed contents hinted at the precarious nature of their transit.

"Their turrets will get a shot off," Leo gritted out over the howling gale rushing past. Sweat streaming down his face made visibility difficult as his eyes squinted against the onslaught. "We've got to—

"I mean it." Vex cut him off without hesitation, her tone razor-edged. "If you're even thinking about trying some dumb plan A rescue op, ditch it NOW. You don't know what we're up against. Our intel sucks harder than your Navy's tail end of budget cuts."

Leo felt a surge of indignation but bit back any response, aware she had a point given the mangled remains surrounding them courtesy of whatever had birthed that catastrophe. He gripped the seat straps tighter, knuckles white, jaw clenched as he fought not to focus on the growing heat emanating from phasers slicing thin red lines across the bulkhead, each one barely missing their spinning craft.

"You seem to forget, ma'am," he managed to force out through gritted teeth, ignoring the searing pain as a stray plasma blast struck something close behind them, "I spent half my post-adolescent years strapped to a couch of death with nothing but a keyboard and pedal bars separating me from a kiloton of destructive fury built entirely around calculated risk and precision timing." 

"Yeah, sure." Vex's laughter was short and mirthless amidst the screaming wind ripping past the canopy. "A simulator with all the immersion of a VR hump-and-go romcom set on Neptune while you're sipping hazelnut lattes back at Vandenberg."

The comparison stung more than Leo cared to admit as images flashed through his mind – simulated starfields stretching endlessly above the gleaming chrome and carbon fiber fuselage, virtual enemy fighters streaking across azure skies lit by twin suns of vibrant crimson and gold amid glittering nebulae, his focus laser-sharp within the pressurized, climate-controlled cocoon of a cockpit untroubled by cold, darkness, or the harsh realities waiting beyond the safety of Earth orbit. 

But that world was gone, shattered by currents beyond his comprehension and depositing him in this new reality where everything seemed to move faster and hit harder. Where the lines between fact and fear blurred until they became one and the same.

"This isn't some game," Leo said quietly, the adrenaline haze starting to clear from his mind. "Whatever came through that gate... it tore holes in the fabric of our reality. And from the mess we just flew through?"

"At the very least," Vex confirmed grimly as her eyes narrowed to dark slits below heavy lids, "we've got ourselves a genuine alien civilization bearing gifts none of us asked for. Or can deal with," her tone suggested with brutal finality.

Leo nodded slowly, already feeling the contours shift in his mind-set as he accepted the truth lurking beneath the surface of what they faced. "Then our only play may be offense. Draw them out, learn what we can in the open instead of trying to fortify a defensive stance in unknown territory."

"Precisely which is why I called the cavalry earlier." A wry smile tweaked the corner of Vex's lips.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo followed the corsair's gaze down to the hangar bay decks, his own breath catching in surprise at the sudden influx of crew rushing to their stations. Scrambling, shouting voices filled the air as marines armed themselves with weapons both frighteningly advanced and strangely familiar - energy rifles, railguns, and exotic munitions whose purpose he couldn't guess. Amidst it all, sleek, armored shuttles with hulking VTOL thrusters cycled through pre-flight checks, their ferocious growls echoing off bulkheads.

"Hell of a reaction time you Corvus have," Leo called out over the din, nodding approvingly. "Did someone hack into our tactical AI to goose the alert response?"

A snort erupted from Vex before she turned fully towards him. Her grin remained thin but something about the glint in her eye felt almost mischievous, like sharing a dangerous secret only hinted at behind veiled words.

"Might've had a little help," she allowed, tapping a clawed fingert tips against the armrest controlling the piloting interfaces surrounding her chair. "Between my techs and your genius, it didn't take much convincing to get my point across to our shipmind. Between you and me though..." Her voice trailed off meaningfully.

Leo raised a quizzical eyebrow, curiosity piqued. The tumult of activity around them offered cover for idle chatter, while still allowing both to surveil their surroundings without drawing attention.

"What aren't you telling me, Captain? You don't usually bother with such covert operations unless --"

His words dissolved into stunned silence as warning lights flashed crimson across the Starlight Marauder's command center. Alarms shrieked in harsh electronic tones devoid of pitch or cadence, piercing through the background clamor. Leo's stomach twisted as tactical displays switched from friendly presence indicators to dire threats closing fast:

Unknown Hostile 1 
Orbital Altitude: 15 km
Projected Intercept Time: 120 seconds
Velocity: 4 kps
Course: Converging with target vessel

"And shit!"

He cursed under his breath, grabbing for the shock-corded harness securing him to the seatframe as the artificial gravity went to near-null to facilitate rapid reorientation. Across from him, Vex lunged with feral speed, her hand clamped around the edge of a console while the rumble grew to a thunderous roar.

"You hear that?" she yelled over the din, face lit in stark whites and bloody reds by the emergency alerts. Leaning out over the armrest, she pointed a gloved finger straight up toward the deck above. "We're not getting airlifted out of this one. Shutting off the artificial spin to prepare the launch bays!"

As if echoing her declaration, a deck plate shuddered violently, then groaned audibly, swinging open with a protesting squeal like ungreased hinges rending apart under strain. Even in zero-gravity environments, the opening hatch's massive shape threw shadows that shifted and danced across the Starlight Marauder's control surfaces.

The shuttle's primary thrusters kicked in with a teeth-rattling screech, vibrations rippling through Leo's core. He braced his shoulders against the chair back, fighting to keep upright amidst the chaos while stars streaked past the viewport's edge. Flashes of motion raced along the exterior hull, strobing glimpses of starfighters swooping in low, like killer hawks diving upon helpless prey.

With a sharp yank, Vex pulled them to attention as the Starlight Marauder began its desperate burn.

✦ ✦ ✦

The rumbling growl transformed into a cacophonous symphony of roaring engines and straining metal, shaking the hull with brutal force. Thrust mounted to a shrieking crescendo as the Corsair frantically accelerated away from the collapsing hangar bay. Stars wheeled past the viewport at alarming speed, blazing blue, crimson, and white threads piercing the black canvas of spacetime.

Leo's seat canted violently to port as he struggled to maintain an unstable perch, grip tightening on the crash web until his knuckles burned. A maelstrom of disorienting images swirled before him – the mad dash through atmosphere, clouds streaking by like ripped cotton sheets; the precipitous fall into orbit, stars resolving into glittering points of light; now, hurtling headlong through the void once more, the world outside reduced to a dizzying blur of colors and shapes he could hardly process.

Through it all, Vex remained perfectly poised behind her command throne, eyes locked on forward sensors, a furious glint burning in their depths despite the grim set of her jaw. She exuded a palpable aura of focused intensity, radiating determination like heat from a forge. The scent of ozone and scorched metal hung heavy in the air, mingling with the faint tang of sweat wafting from strained crew members clinging to whatever handholds they could find.

Leo spared a glance to starboard, where two of their crew fought their way across the cramped bridge, securing themselves to support cables and harnesses while tending to damaged equipment. Ensign Alvin's usually cherubic features were wracked in pain as he clutched his left forearm, blood seeping through his gloves. The medic, Lieutenant Zara Chavan, worked feverishly to stabilize a burst ruptured artery with makeshift field bandages. Her dark eyes flashed with worry as she met Leo's gaze for a fleeting instant, before returning to her patient.

A shrill warning ping drew both men's attention to the tactical display. A trio of hostile Starforce fighters had closed, laser turrets already flashing ruby bursts in their wake. But those were mere pinpricks compared to the main threat.

Leo gulped as his eyes widened at the approaching specter on the viewscreen – a gargantuan mass of boiling plasma and twisted matter, visible only due to its incandescence against the surrounding darkness. The colossal vortex seemed to pulse malevolently, as if alive and hungry.

"What in God's name—"

"A wormhole," Vex interrupted curtly, voice tight with urgency. "And we just flew right into its mouth." She leaned back in her chair, hands flying across her station, fingers dancing over screens and sliders. "Get ready for everything to get a hell of a lot weirder real fast."

As if to underscore her ominous proclamation, the Starlight Marauder began to twist and distort in ways defying rational comprehension. The cosmos itself warped, stars warping into eldritch spirals and loops that smeared the viewport's surface like liquid paint. G-forces rode the edge of tolerance as the ship warped nimbly through the turbulent tunnel of distorted spacetime.

In this realm beyond sane geometry, quantum fluctuations whispered maddening echoes, and the very fabric of reality throbbed with unrestrained energy. For a few heartbeats – or perhaps an eternity condensed into instants – the bridge was cocooned in pure, raw chaos, impenetrable to linear time or spatial logic.

Then, abruptly, the Starlight Marauder burst free of the wormhole's gaping maw, spewing out into a nightmarish vista devoid of hope.
