# Chapter 15: Breached Containment


The hull groaned, protesting the strain. A rivulet of steaming hydraulic fluid oozed past Astro's boots, carving a glistening path down the canted surface plate. Her gaze flickered perpetually from the torn starfield outside to Leo crammed beside her in the narrow airlock compartment.

"The engines-" she began gruffly yet lacked the nerve to finish the sentence.

Leo supplied the gruesome detail unbidden. "Overheating, going critical." The admission hung starkly in the air they shared, thickened by tension and dread. Out there, beyond the thin, scarred skin of their refuge, an inferno built exponentially with each passing second.

She nodded curtly at Leo's words of confirmation. The close proximity intensified the physical impact of Astrid's curt movements - her shoulders tensed against her harness as if bracing for a blow, or readying for battle. Though they labored simultaneously toward survival, old wariness remained ever-present, an almost palpable force binding them together amid this chaos.

"We don't have long. Let's move, for Helios' sake." Urgency painted each word in vivid strokes, underscoring the hairpin fragility of their situation.

Astrid grabbed two maneuvering lines - webbed vests with tethers attached to sturdy cables snaking out of the airlock - and hurled one to Leo with practiced precision. The vest whipped around his torso cinching tight, clips securing to either side of his chest armor. Then, she clipped her own safety line to Astro's exterior wall anchorpoint and tested its strength with a confident tug.

With that critical task done, true panic could begin blooming in their chests. Panic to fuel their desperate bid for escape, praying it wouldn't arrive too late.

"Ava," Astrid barked into the comms unit. "Open external access for our exit. Now." The AI responded instantly, voice unnaturally calm amidst the turmoil:

External locker and ramp will open on your mark. Prepare for ejection in three...two...

"One!" As if orchestrated by a choreographer of disaster, both Corsair and pilot launched themselves bodily down the length of the air lock, propelled out into nothingness by the slamming hatch doors behind them. 

Air rushed searingly past their faces, stripping eyes and mouth of moisture even as their ears popped to equalize pressure. Even with the protective shell of their suits and flight gear, the temperature plummeted - dropping precipitously until breathing vapor became a visible plume against the endless black backdrop, punctuated by glittering stars and distant systems.

Leo, hand clasping tightly the tether line linking them, flailed a bit to adjust his footing - or rather, his lack thereof in the utter absence of gravity. Beyond the initial shock, the strange weightlessness proved surprisingly intuitive to navigate soon enough. Even so, the alien sensation played havoc with vestibular balance.

"Savor the view while we're at it, huh kid?" Astrid quipped with a wry grimace behind her helmet faceplate. Humor often masked fear this way, especially when such levity felt almost obscene given the circumstances. Still, the attempt helped keep their synapses firing clearly. Survival depended on staying razor-sharp despite the paralyzing terror churning in their guts.

No chance for rest or respite now. They faced no fewer than half a dozen potential threats: hypothermia and dehydration; hostile alien patrols; catastrophic decompression; engine failure leading to drift into a planetary atmosphere; and perhaps worst of all - being overtaken by their pursuers in the short sprint back to civilization before help could arrive.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo's gaze lingered across the star-strewn abyss, mesmerized as much by its infinite expanse as the treacherous situation it framed. The chilling wind howled silently past him and Astrid, both effects amplified to oppressive degrees in the vacuum's merciless grip. Frost rimed the rims of their helmets, breath mist swirling in ethereal haloes around them.

"Penny for your thoughts, Major Mercer," Astrid called, voice tinged with frostiness to match the frigid environment. Her boots scraped against the rough metallic surface as she adjusted her stance to regain stability amidst the unsettling zero gravity.

Leo's fingers tightened reflexively around the tether. The very fact they were still connected, a single fragile strand between them and certain doom, made every nerve scream with heightened awareness. "Just... trying not to think too hard right now, Vex."

Astrid snorted derisively, but there was a nervous undertone to it that even their comlink pickups might have missed. "Well, focus up. If you start to hyperventilate on me, that'll just make things worse." A brief pause, then: "We need to discuss our options once those shuttles get close enough for a serious talk."

"You mean before our pursuers decide we're easy pickings out here in the blue yonder?"

She sighed, frustration evident between the static-laced words. "Keep the wisecracks to yourself for two minutes, will ya? We've got enough immediate risks to deal with without arguing our postures."

"I wasn't—"

"Don't play dumb. I saw your exchange with Colonel Chen."

 Leo bristled, instinctively clenching his jaw. So Vex had witnessed that little heart-to-heart about protocol, loyalty, and honorable retreat versus suicidal defiance in the face of overwhelming odds. Just peachy.

"Oh, look who grew a spine courtesy of a couple days adrift in the cosmos."

Astrid scoffed, but beneath that steel edge, he detected a flicker of something more poignant—concern, maybe, or a tinge of regret that their conversation had gone sour. The corsair captain clearly didn't want to be the one ordering a strategic withdrawal, yet Leo couldn't abide the prospect either. His duty, ingrained from decades of service, rebelled against fleeing like rabbits before foxhounds.

"We'll do what needs doing to survive," he maintained stiffly, meeting her glare head-on within the confines of their visors. Though the chasm between them seemed impossibly wide—their roles, ideologies, worldviews clashing like colliding galaxies—they dare not let doubts or divisions splinter their sole lifeline in the darkness.

After what felt an eternity of strained silence, Astrid finally broke the tense impasse. "Time's wasting." With a nod toward the approaching rescue ships, she jerked her chin to signal toward their craft.

"Let's get moving. Before we miss our free ride home."

✦ ✦ ✦

Commander Leo Mercer mirrored Astrid's movements as they strode purposefully back to the battered Starlight Marauder. Each heavy footfall resonated on the metallic grate, like hammer blows echoing through a hollow cylinder. The harsh red-orange glow cast elongated shadows across their armored suits, accentuating every tension line across stern faces. In the stark silence of high orbit, the very stillness mocked their desperation, their hopes for respite tempered by the knowledge that escape would come at a steep price if it came at all.

"It's gonna be rough," Leon murmured, voice pitched low against the comlink as they drew near the deranged silhouette of their ship. The once sleek frigate now bore the scars of a brutal battle – a twisted latticework of bent metal, dented plating, fused energy armor. Patches of scorched insulation hung like macabre streamers; warning runes glowed cherry-red over ruptured fuel lines and crippled weapon mounts. No mere repair could mend such carnage – this vessel clung to life by a thread of sheer determination.

"You think I don't friggin' know that?" Astrid bit out, her own gloved fists bunching reflexively at her sides. Even at this distance, Leo sensed her brittle control, the desperate need to keep it together masked by snappish deflection. He admired that tenacity, even as it grated – another clash of temperaments, worldviews locked in a deadly pas de deux. They'd come so far together in just a few perilous days, forged under the unforgiving glare of the void toward a wary, grudging partnership built upon respect and a shared will to live. Yet cracks beginning to show, trust fraying at the edges.

As they boarded the ravaged airlock, gravity's gentle tug snapped into nothingness, sending both officers drifting weightlessly on their tethering lines until they could secure themselves. Alarms shrieked an earsplitting wail, a cacophony of failing systems as internal pressure plummeted. Gritting teeth, Leo scrambled to override sequence protocols while Astrid worked feverishly to rig external salvage gear – a hasty patchwork of salvaged segments cobbled from the wreckage of other vessels.

"Vex...we can't compensate enough for this kind of damage," Leo panted urgently over comms as he wrestled with the air recycling unit. The acrid reek of ozone stung his nostrils, tinged with the coppery tang of blood from countless injuries sustained by the Starlight Marauder's crew and alien captives during their ordeal. Amidst the nightmare chaos, he recalled Vex's earlier words when describing her first taste of combat - that familiar, intoxicating elixir of adrenaline mixed with the gut-wrenching terror of staring death down. Christ, as a fighter pilot fresh out of Flight School, he'd felt that same vertiginous giddy-apex-rush back before he learned there were fates worse than drowning in enemy fire...

"No choice anymore," Astrid ground out through clenched teeth as she struggled to secure a stabilizing tow-beam, knotted silver cables slithering end-over-end in lazy arcs. "Just hope to Hell these old parts don't decide to quit on us mid-burn."

Leo swallowed a bitter curse at the thought; mid-course correction failures had accounted for roughly two-thirdsof recorded hyperspace disasters past. The dread certainty curdled in his stomach as he worked the thrumming console, inputting coordinates to match the civilian beacon's frequency.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo's grip tightened around the console support as he powered down systems, each soft click a knife to his racing heart. They were stripping the Marauder bare, leaving only life support and propulsion online. He half-expected to see Vex rip open her own engines if it meant the sliver of a chance for escape.

"The umbilicals are clear!" Her hoarse voice scratched static in his ear. "Casting off now...Brace yourselves!"

A seismic lurch threw him sideways, nearly sending him tumbling into the wall as the last of the Starlight Marauder's makeshift docking attachments pulled free to swivel violently in her wake. Leo fought to right himself, pulse pounding in time with the thundering whine of ion thrusters flaring to full burn just beneath their feet.

"Steady," Astrid muttered through gritted teeth, her voice tight with concentration. Even crippled, the battered vessel's hull creaked around them like an ancient timber-framed house settling under snow load as they accelerated into turbulent darkness.

Around the curve of the battered viewscreen, light began to coalesce, intensifying to a seething maelstrom within seconds. An accreting binary pulsar system loomed ahead, its twin dead stars locked in apsidal orbit around a swirling black hole at their common focus. Gas and plasma spiralled along magnetic field lines toward those ravenous voids, igniting as they struck the roiling vortex at relativistic speed – incandescent shock-front filaments painting the dark cosmos with furious brushstrokes of ruby red and citrine gold.

"Astrid, check our acceleration profile – can we—"

Her warning cut Leo's question short as multiple alerts flashed across the instrument panel in rapid succession. A klaxon wailed an urgent series of pulses, vibrating the thin metal deckplates beneath their boots:

Sensor Contact! High Priority Signal Analyzing... 

Leo straightened, eyes glued to the flickering data stream. That particular pattern of pulsed modulation could mean only one thing:

Another ship was coming their way...
