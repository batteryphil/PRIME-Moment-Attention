# Chapter 2: Bounty on an Ancient Ghost

The proximity alert burst into life, a piercing trill that seized every crew member's attention like an electrified finger jabbing into nerve endings. In sync, indicators began strobing on Astrid's console, data streams flooding across the screens. Her gaze shot to the main display as if magnetically drawn.

Leo stumbled forward to peer over the edge of the raised platform framing the captain's seat. Together they beheld a small, rundown vessel limping into visual range—a fragile husk of a ship barely clinging to integrity. Dust and faded paint marred its hull, while gaping tears revealed skeletal frame and exposed wiring. The thing seemed to drift aimlessly on a crippled arc, devoid of even basic propulsion.

"Why...the hell?" Leo muttered incredulously beside Astrid. They exchanged swift, assessing glances—his wide-eyed consternation mirroring her calculating scrutiny. No signs of damage could explain the derelict's listing drift. There was only one explanation:

"It's being pushed," Astrid declared starkly, fingers flying across the control cluster as she grasped the implications. Already starfighters were materializing from the Marauder's flank, tiny armored deltas flitting toward the crippled transport with deadly intent.

"This isn't just a cripple," she continued with grim satisfaction. "It's bait—and our unwanted herald. Syndicate scouts must have discovered signs of the pilot's survival and sent out a lure to draw us in."

"How many?" Leo asked urgently, already anticipating the tactical complexities swirling behind Vex's focus.

"Dozens. They're planning a full-scale ambush, hoping to take us down or scatter us at least, giving them an opening to capture our prize aboard that toy." Astrid cocked her head toward the vulnerable-looking freighter.

"But why risk direct engagement?" Confusion creased Leo's brow as his strategic mind grappled with the puzzle piecing together before him.

"For the fun of it, Commander," the pirate shot back with a twisted smirk. "They get paid either way—kill us, claim the pilot, or let us leave empty-handed after wasting assets chasing shadows. Besides, who would suspect those 'disabled' spycraft hide lethal armaments?"

Alerts flashed across the bridge as automated defensive systems came online, the Marauder humming with potent readiness coiled beneath her gleaming armor. Leo swallowed heavily, the metallic tang of fear and adrenaline pricking at the back of his throat as the gravity of their situation crystallized.

This was more than just combat—their lives hung by the precarious thread of technological superiority and pure, calculated luck. And God help them both if they were wrong about the motivations driving these shadowy forces into open confrontation.

"Bunker down!" Astrid growled above the rising cacophony of chimes and shrills. As the marines flooded onto the bridge, locking shields in place and closing blast-door hatches, the corsair captain leaned deeply into her station controls. In an almost hypnotic rhythm, she worked through initial countermeasures, hands dancing across keypads at breakneck pace.

Leo tensed alongside her, skin prickling with anticipation as the first wave of attacking ships closed the distance. Soon, the battlefront would unfold with apocalyptic ferocity, painting the pristine heavens in fire-streaked fury as humanity's best fought to survive amidst the unforgiving void.

And yet, perversely, amidst the dread, a spark of exhilarating joy suffused Leo's psyche. This baptism by fire rekindled old flames of passion, transforming mere duty into thrilling, life-or-death stakes.

"Astrid!" The piercing scream of proximity alarm tore through Bridge speakers, echoing Leo's gut-dropping shock. He locked eyes with Vex as the horizon of stars rippled like molten metal, reality bending under the onslaught of hostile vessels diving out of interstellar blackout. Her face transformed - fury, focus, an unmistakable predatory grin slashing across features carved from obsidian and star-studded steel.

"Contact confirmed," Jax barked through gritted teeth, fingers flying over sensor feeds. A searing crimson blazed across readouts as incoming capital ships disgorged swarms of point and fighter batteries. "Looks like our shadow is showing its true colors, Captain."

"Glad we won't be leaving any presents behind..." Astrid muttered without taking her gaze off the storming expanse.

Leo's breath caught as the scale of the assault coalesced into an abyssal pit of despair - they faced an overwhelming force backed by cold, ruthless resolve. Yet despite the bleakness pressing against their hulls like a living nightmare, he caught the glint of excitement sparkling in Vex's eye, heard the hoarse edge of triumph coloring her voice. She thrived on this—confronting an impossible challenge head-on, trusting her ship and wits alone against a tide of destruction.

"What's your play?" he asked gruffly, forcing order onto the roiling knot of panic and pride he felt watching her work.

Astrid's teeth flashed white against the ominous backdrop. "We don't run. We fight dirty." Her gloved hand shot out, slamming a series point-defense array overrides, and the hull lurched brutally as inertial dampers strove to compensate. The jolt threw Leo against his restraints as the superstructure groaned ominously. "Hull integrity breached," Jace intoned tersely. Sweat gleamed on his flushed cheeks.

"Not much we can do about that right now," Astrid cut him off with merciless efficiency, eyes sweeping critical systems. The Starlight's engines whined in protest as main reactors fed a desperate surge of power to shield generators.

Leo glanced around the Bridge, where his comrades clung to stations, faces drawn taut with concentration and terror. Despite the deadly calm Astrid exuded, palpable waves of anxiety reverberated inside the cramped confines, each crew member fighting to maintain composure even as their world shattered around them. Only the captain remained seemingly impervious, driven forward by singular purpose and an icy control born of countless battles fought.

"You're not seriously planning a stand against this—" But Leo never finished the question. Astrid whipped around, a look capable of flaying him alive searing through the chaos.

"I see the fear in you, Leo," she said through gritted teeth, her full attention pinning him. No quarter asked or given this time—only stark observation, unfiltered and brutal. "I get it. We're outgunned. Outnumbered. This ship wasn't built for war."

"We may be outranked," Leo retorted, matching her intensity, "but I'll be damned if anyone says we lay down easy." Anguish edged his words, but beneath that—pride. Loyalty. An unwilling acceptance of what had been forced upon them.

"Then hold on." Her warning came out more a prayer than an order as she punched the main drive past redline and the Starlight surged ahead recklessly, bolting toward the vanguard of the attackers with suicidal abandon. Leo braced for impact...

✦ ✦ ✦

The starboard viewport dissolved in a cacophony of splintering steel and shattering glass. Merciless crimson light assaulted their eyes as the Starlight corkscrewed away from its executioner—a massive heavy-laser ship pouring hellacious energy through its maw in pursuit. Screaming klaxons shrieked in vain protest while the bridge trembled violently, plates squealing in agonized submission as they warped and deformed.

Leo clung to his station like a man repelling a ravaging beast, vision blurred by stinging debris particles. Blood trickled from a shallow cut across his cheekbone where shattered polymers had gouged skin. Nearby, his copilot Ensign Vega convulsed atop her console, strangled screams silenced when a jagged shard pierced her throat.

A grim resignation settled over Leo as he sawed his helmet free of the wreckage—a cruel reminder of the Starlight's inadequacy for combat when every second counted. All he could do was wait, numbly accepting the inevitable until the piranha-like cruisers swarmed around them, hungry for their prey's fragility...

"I hate running..." Astrid declared, her voice rising above the symphony of destruction. The captain stood stock-still by the central controls, expression inscrutable as she watched raw chaos take shape beyond their shields.

Despite everything crashing down around him, Leo couldn't help but laugh darkly. What choice did they have when outnumbered twelve to one? "Join the club, Captain."

Her lips quirked in the barest hint of amusement—rapidly suppressed as renewed growls emanated from the heavy laser cruiser now looming dead center within targeting acquisition range. "We keep talking strategy, but we need to stay alive to execute any of it. Time to get creative with our options—"

That's when disaster struck with vicious precision. Catastrophic failures ripped the shields off three simultaneous attacks, scalding plasma slicing through their armor like hot knives.

Leo's world exploded into a thunderous detonation, the ship's gravity core imploding in slow motion as his consciousness tumbled end over end through the vacuum of space alongside his former home. Stars blazed brighter than nuclear explosions, a blinding kaleidoscope spinning dizzily against the infinite blackness.

Pain seared through his skull as his broken body slammed against the inner hull of an exposed airlock far from where he'd last remembered being seated. Stunned thoughts wrestled their way clear, realizing with a sinking feeling that only the quick reflexes of their proximity suits were keeping him intact after that unholy blast wave. A thin film of frozen blood coated his faceplate now—his own or someone else's made no difference at this point.

Crawling along the buckled deckplates, Leo clawed his way toward the lock controls, dragging himself against the irresistible pull of the void that beckoned hungrily from the gaping rents in their ship's hull. His suit's life support was strained even before the ordeal began—if he didn't find shelter soon, the lack of breathable atmosphere alone would claim him.

"Fucking...help me..." Leo gasped when he finally reached the hatch, his labored breathing visible as clouds of fog condensed in frustrated desperation before dissipating uselessly in the hard, frigid vacuum.

Behind him—a deafening groan, metal flexing with near-incredible strength, protesting the force behind a looming presence bearing down remorselessly—

Mercifully, everything went dark.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo Mercer's consciousness stumbled back to the cruel light of awareness with a throat full of scorched cotton, lungs heaving ragged gasps for an elusive lungful of air not promised to come. Something clattered hollowly in his shattered helmet at every jarring movement as he tried to get his bearings. Through the milky haze of his cracked visor, the first thing he saw blurred into focus was a hellish expanse of molten crimson stretching almost to infinity above and below. Eruptive lava flows crisscrossed by impossible lightning bolts lit up the jagged obsidian peaks jutting spines in the inky night. This could have passed for the heart of Hades itself if he hadn't known better—Icaria IV had never looked so nightmarishly beautiful, nor so deadly.

"Christ, what the fuck happened?" He croaked out hoarsely, the raspy query lost to the hissing static bleeding from speakers he feared were dead, despite their steady heartbeat indicator blinking faithfully. Even battered as he must be, somehow they still pulled duty in his abused ear canal like a grim, relentless drumbeat reminding him of existence...one that he doubted much longer given the sheer devastation encircling them like some ghastly lunar eclipse. By all rights it should've been the fiery netherworld of a hell planet consuming the Marauder stone by tortured, shrieking stone.

Stumbling forward on numb legs, he managed to drag himself into cover just before a thunderous crash shook the star-scorched soil like an angry god pummeling her playthings. Bits of debris fell around him as he huddled beneath a twisted scrap of metal, flinching at the eerie acoustics in vacuum—and the gut-wrenching certainty of what those noises meant: fragments of wreckage colliding into each other; ships breaking apart; life slipping away—

"MERCER!" Astrid's voice cut sharply through the howling silence, barely recognizable over shortwave static. She sounded shaken but strangely calm amidst the chaos, which scared him because that icy focus usually heralded her most reckless moments. "Keep the comms open. We're still alive—you're lucky you got your bitch-ass luck knocked into you." Her mocking tone couldn't fully mask the underlying worry when she added a bit unsteadily, "For the record...that was too close. You're going to pay me back for that stunt eventually.'

"Aghhh..." Leo winced, rubbing gingerly at his sore ribs and head, but her words pierced through his grogginess clearer now: they survived, amazingly so given the brutal ferocity of that near-miss engagement with Icaria's surface-defense platforms. At least that’s what he gathered—his memory was one jumbled mess of explosive light shows, screaming turbines, and bone-rattling collisions. Likely a miracle more than any skill his piloting prowess had instigated, he thought wryly.

Scanning their surroundings via the limited view slots carved into his helmet, Leo picked out snaking lines of destruction radiating outward from where they lay prone on the smoking battlefield—the charred fuselage of some enemy vessel, a handful of asteroids caught unawares by the violence, fields of stunted crystalline geysers spurting steam into the frosty vacuum. But mercifully, Leo couldn't spot any sign of major damage to either of their craft—and thank the void for small favors, because their battered Marauder seemed to still be holding together—just! Not that either pilot owed the universe much by way of gratitude right now...

"...Any sign of pursuit yet?"
