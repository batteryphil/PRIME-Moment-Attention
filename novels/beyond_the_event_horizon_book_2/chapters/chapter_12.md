# Chapter 12: Boarding the Ghost Fleet


Astrid's steely resolve brought stabilizing order back to the Marauder's decks. But beyond the familiar contours of his captain's faceplate, beyond even their shared connection floating weightless among wreckage-strewn void, there waited unimaginable horrors yet to come.

Leo knew it in his gut, a chilling certainty resonating deep within his bones as he struggled to free a tether from the debris-scrambled flight deck. The silence was absolute, underscored only by the hiss of his suit's life support system - a rhythmic reminder he might well be next. His gloved hands fumbled awkwardly, clumsy in the microgravity environment. Each slight motion sent loose bits of twisted hull dancing around them.

As much as possible, Astrid had retained the sleek design heritage of the Valykyrie class frigates. Clean lines and curves flowed elegantly throughout the vessel's form. But here, now, amid this apocalyptic tableau, such elegance faded beneath the cruel kiss of annihilation. Charred chunks of superstructure tumbled past, some still streaked with glowing embers that flickered out only moments later, leaving cold darkness in their wake.

Carefully, mercifully, Leo freed himself completely from the splintered wreck's vice-like tangles. Every movement felt stiff and labored, his joints creaking with each deliberate maneuver. He rose slowly, kicking off stray morsels of plastic and composite until finally drifting alongside Astrid's motionless figure. Their helmets came together, a mere handspan apart in the endless expanse between.

"Captain?" His whispered question hung suspended in the chill emptiness. "Can we...?" Even framing the next words brought home just how hopeless their situation truly was.

"We could try," Astrid replied after a long pause, her voice barely audible over her own breathing. "The Ghost Fleet isn't far. If any ship within can help us...

She trailed off, not needing to state the obvious unspoken risks. This close to the cataclysmic heart of whatever terror had destroyed the fleet in the first place, their odds were already perilously low. Yet they both understood failure meant swift, agonizing oblivion in these frozen wastes - suffocated by the implacable grip of cold dead vacuum as surely and mercilessly as the Valykyrie herself.

Leo took a deep breath, feeling the faint tickle of stale recirculated air in his throat. Then, without another word between them, they slowly set about readying the Marauder for boarding, the silence punctuated only by occasional grunts of exertion and the soft clinks of manual latches engaging seal after precarious seal. Once secure on the other side, the true horrors awaited. But first, they would face down their deepest fears by diving courageously into the maw of a thousand-nameless nightmares waiting patiently shrouded in nebular darkness...

Leo stepped up to the starboard hatch in heavy boots, gloves stuffed into his jacket pockets, his body heat radiating outward to warm his chilled skin. The interior of the Ghost Fleet derelict loomed before him, darkened corridors stretching out into eerie silence like the gaping maw of some slumbering void-dweller. Every fiber of his being screamed at him to pull back, to flee this tomb of unknown horrors as fast as his lungs and legs could carry him. Forcing himself to stand tall, he cast a stern look at Astrid, who leaned against the bulkhead beside him, arms crossed over her chest in a posture of guarded anticipation.

"We ready to do this?"

She nodded curtly. "Let's get it over with."

A flick of her wrist sent the boarding party—Mercer, Vex, and three suited marauders—suiling through the gap between the Starlight Marauder and its derelict cargo hold. Leo pulled his helmet visor shut with an hiss, the world shrinking to a close-focus view encased in matte-black polymer and quartz. Inside his suit's life support pod the air remained cool and crisp despite his pounding heart. A few meters distant the Marauder drifted beyond reach, sealed from outside contact once more.

Tiptoeing across the smooth hull, Leo navigated by the faint bioluminescent signs etched into the decking—the last vestiges of forgotten maintenance guides—and the dull sheen of frozen fluid leaked from countless ruptured pipes and vents. The acrid scent of charred circuit boards and ozone clung to his filters as he and Vex led the team toward what should be the vessel's core command center.

"I cannot stress enough how unwise this course of action is," Leo said, trying to keep the edge out of his voice—the futile urge to lecture fading rapidly under the crushing weight of their impossible plight. They needed those ships' intact systems as much as their lives depended on finding shelter from the deadly cold and the unseen threats within the dying star. But even that desperate logic couldn't dispel the creeping dread that stole over him with every step.

"This place has been dead meat for God knows how long." Astrid gestured around at the darkened, silent husks of structures looming overhead. "Any life left in there's going to want our insides for supper."

"The life support systems might still be functioning, minimally," he countered, peering into the gloom for any sign of the promised central hub. "And maybe, just maybe—"

"Don't push it, flyer-boy," she cut him off with a wry grin, visible only through her reflective faceplate. "Poking the dead things never ends well."

Leo held his tongue, unable to deny her observation. Despite the grimness of their situation, he drew strength from her presence—not merely her skill and deadly cunning as a fighter, but also the resilience burning bright behind those piercing eyes, an indomitable will to survive no matter what horrors awaited them.

As if sensing his thoughts, Astrid glanced aside toward him, then back up the dim passageway ahead. For a heartbeat she let go of her habitual sarcasm, showing him the tired, weary lines around her mouth and forehead that went beyond mere weariness to something deeper—a glimpse of a hardened survivor past her swaggering front.

"You got that right, captain," he said softly, meeting her gaze through the veil of frost-carved helmets. "Whatever's in there...we'll face it together."

With that vow spoken, astride fear's precipice, they pressed forward into the depths of the haunted Ghost Fleet.

Chapter 12: Boarding the Ghost Fleet - Scene 3

Leo's boots clanged leadenly against chilled durasteel plates as they trudged deeper into the ancient derelict's hollow corpse, twin shadows cast by flickering emergency lights strung along the walls. Each step echoed menacingly, punctuated by creaks and groans that sounded less like the workings of a long-dormant vessel than the sighs of some eldritch, slumbering beast stirring from torpor.

The oppressive atmosphere grew more suffocating with every meter they advanced, until even breathing became a labored effort amidst the recirculated air reeking of decay and rust. Astrid, ever vigilant, kept pace beside Leo, her hand resting lightly on the grip of a plasma cutter slung ready across her chest. A tense coiled spring beneath her skin betrayed the weapon's very existence—and her unspoken readiness to unleash its wrath at the first sign of real trouble.

"How much farther?" Leo murmured, swiping a grime-caked sleeve across his brow, only vaguely registering the chill seeping in from contact with the ship's skin. 

"Not much," came Astrid's voice from above, her words clipped due to speaking over her comms link while keeping both hands free. "I think I see the central hub up ahead—looks mostly intact judging by the exterior scans. But we're really pushing our luck stumbling into active defenses with each step."

"Bearing in mind you have the skills to handle anything short of capital-grade armaments—" Leo began, drawing a gruff chuckle from her. They slowed, approaching the dark bulkhead beyond which lay the heart of this decaying realm.

"If it moves or shoots, I can disable it," the pirate corrected, "but nothing lasts forever against dedicated engineering or sheer numbers. Besides, this whole gutting-a-zombie operation would've been simpler if my spiffy holo-projectors worked properly. Never did like relying on jury-rigged duct tape anyway."

Ahead of them, the sealed hatch at the center of the hallway suddenly bucked in their direction with an agonized screech, its heavy locking mechanism whirling open. A gout of foul-smelling gases blasted out, stinging their lungs and eyes in a miasma of ammonia and decay before dissipating harmlessly into the passage.

Silence fell, broken only by thudding heartbeats and shallow panting. Both turned warily as one toward the dark entrance, equal parts dread and morbid curiosity warring within them.

"Well, looks like whatever rot was setting down in here finally gave way," Astrid conceded, raising her laser pistol cautiously. "Time to find out what other surprises our deceased friends have left in store for us..."

Leo nodded, readjusting the strap of his own weapon. As they crept forward hand-in-hand into the unknown, a cold lump settled at the pit of his stomach—the thrill of discovery mingling uneasily with an ominous sense that the true challenge lay not in technological boobytraps, but in confronting the specters that lurked beneath this cursed hive's cold metal skin...

Without warning, Astrid halted abruptly, head cocked to one side as if trying to pinpoint some subtle change in their environment. After a few tense beats she jerked a thumb backward. "Mercer, watch your step!" 

Leo obeyed reflexively, freezing mid-stride just barely in time. Below him, where he'd almost set foot, a narrow groove ran along the floor, gleaming metallic green—a telltale indicator of an uncharged energy field.

Astrid's sharp intake of breath drew Leo's attention to her gaping eyes, which were fixed intently on something past his shoulder. His instincts screamed warning even before her finger shot out toward him like an arrow. By pure instinct, Leo skidded to a halt, heels scraping against the durasteel. He spun around just enough to see – the field wasn't just a tripwire sentinel, it encircled something... or someone lying crumpled amidst the wreckage.

"Oh hell," Leo breathed, feeling the sweat beading at the nape of his neck. Whatever had torn through this derelict ship like a comet still prowled unnamed horrors within its rust-streaked hull. 

Astrid knelt beside the vaguely humanoid form, reaching out with hesitant fingers to brush aside a snarl of wires and tubing. Even half-obscured, Leo recognized the pale blue irises, the finely sculpted nose and full lips that curved into an unmistakable pout – Zara, first officer of the infamous privateer vessel Whisper's Bane.

"You're alive," Astrid declared softly, her tone tinged with incredulity mixed with cautious hope.

Leo watched, stunned, as Astrid carefully peeled away more debris and tethers until there was nothing visible but bare skin – and an unsettling vista of burned muscle and blackened bone peeking through charred flesh. Despite it all, flickers of consciousness lit those sapphire eyes, coalescing into a single pleading word...

"Help..."

Leo felt his composure fracturing, the sickening reality of exposure and desolation reasserting itself. He glanced back along the passage and saw only darkness pressing in – the endless void of space waiting to reclaim the fragments of a doomed civilization. In that moment, the scale and cruelty of the cosmos became brutally, heartrendingly clear.

Zara's gaze shifted anxiously from Astrid to Leo, desperation twisting her face. In a whisper-thin voice that barely carried over the ambient hiss, she pleaded—

"This isn't over... They could come back..."

Astroids shared a heavy look with her companion before turning sternly back to Zara. "They aren't comin', sweetheart. We already found most of their crew... And trust me, nothin's gonna make 'em want to get their tentacles near anything smokin'. What happened here was some kinda cataclysmic event, so unless the universe holds more nasty surprises in store we can't imagine–"

Her words cut off as both hers and Leo's gazes snapped upwards simultaneously, transfixed by the blinding violet strobe painting across the cavernous ceiling above – a warning beacon, pulsing wildly, emanating a signal so powerful it pierced the hull's shielding and shook the very air with its electromagnetic resonance:

DANGER... DANGER... DANGER...
