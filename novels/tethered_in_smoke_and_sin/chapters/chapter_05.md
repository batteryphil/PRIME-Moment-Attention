# Chapter 5: The Court of Raven Mirrors

The gown Vaelen had left on the marble bench was not an apology; it was an act of war.

It was spun from liquid silk the color of midnight over deep water, heavy and cool against Elena’s skin. The bodice was structured with hidden stays of flexible dragon-bone that cinched her waist and pushed up the swell of her breasts, framed by an off-the-shoulder neckline trimmed in delicate, smoke-colored lace. Slits on either side of the flowing skirt revealed glimpses of her thighs with every step, and along the collarbone and cuffs, gossamer threads of spun silver formed subtle, protective wards that shimmered in the torchlight.

Elena stood before the floor-length obsidian mirror in Vaelen’s bedchamber, fastening a pair of silver drop earrings she’d found on the vanity. Her auburn hair had dried in loose, fragrant waves that tumbled over her bare shoulders, still smelling of the crushed jasmine bath salts.

"You look less like a Chicago museum rat," a voice purred from the doorway. "And more like an empress who eats lesser gods for supper."

Elena turned.

Vaelen Thorne leaned against the doorframe, and for the second time that evening, the breath abandoned her lungs.

He had bathed and changed into his formal commander’s regalia. A doublet of black velvet, embroidered with dark silver vines along the lapels, was clasped over an armored gorget of dark, burnished steel. A cloak of heavy charcoal wool, lined with silver fox fur, was pinned to his left shoulder by an obsidian brooch carved into a roaring dragon’s skull. His raven hair was brushed back from his angular face, highlighting the aristocratic sharpness of his cheekbones and the lethal, molten silver of his eyes.

He didn't look like an executioner anymore. He looked like an emperor.

His gaze swept down the length of her gown, lingering on the exposed expanse of her throat and the curve of her waist. A dark heat flared in his silver irises—raw, possessive, and undisguised.

"Is the mortal comfortable in my silk?" he asked softly, stepping into the room.

"It's tight," Elena replied, keeping her voice even as he closed the distance between them. "And I don't recall agreeing to be your doll."

"The stays are reinforced with dragon-silk," Vaelen murmured, stopping mere inches before her. His long fingers reached out, his knuckles brushing against the sensitive bare skin of her collarbone as he adjusted the lace neckline. A jolt of electric heat sparked between their skins, making her shudder. "It will stop an assassin’s poisoned stiletto from piercing your lung. You can thank me later."

"How reassuring."

"The court of High King Malakor is a nest of vipers, Elena," Vaelen said, his voice dropping into a grave, deadly seriousness. His eyes held hers captive. "They have not seen a living mortal in three hundred years. To them, your blood smells sweeter than the finest vintage, and your fragility is an invitation to play. Stay within two paces of my left side. Do not eat, do not drink, and do not accept a gift from any lord, no matter how sweetly they smile."

"And if someone touches me?"

A terrifying, razor-thin smile touched Vaelen’s lips. He reached to his hip and loosened the broadsword in its scabbard with a sharp *clack*.

"Then they lose the hand. Shall we?"

He offered her his arm. Elena looked at the heavy velvet of his sleeve, hesitated for half a heartbeat, and slid her hand into the crook of his elbow.

The moment her fingers rested against his arm, the golden runes on her wrist hummed warmly, syncing their heartbeats into a steady, resonant rhythm. A wave of calm confidence washed over her, bleeding through the tether from his unshakeable core into hers.

They descended the grand spiral staircase of the Nightglass Citadel into the belly of the palace.

The Throne Room of Raven Mirrors was a monument to gothic decadence.

A hundred feet wide and three times as long, the hall was paved in reflective black obsidian so clear it mirrored the vaulted ceiling above, creating the illusion of walking through star-dusted space. Thousands of silver mirrors lined the stone pillars, each angled to catch the violet flames of massive iron chandeliers hanging from chains of blackened steel.

Hundreds of immortal shadow-fae courtiers filled the hall, draped in velvet, spun-glass lace, and feathered capes. Ghostly glass harps played an eerie, haunting melody from a raised gallery, the notes crystalline and sorrowful.

As the heavy double doors groaned open and the royal herald struck his iron staff against the marble floor, the murmur of hundreds of voices died.

The silence was sudden, absolute, and suffocating.

"Commander Vaelen Thorne," the herald's voice boomed across the vast hall. "Lord of the Nightglass Citadel, High Commander of the Umbral Guard. And companion."

Elena held her head high, her fingers resting lightly on Vaelen’s arm. She felt hundreds of predatory eyes lock onto her. Immortals with hair of spun silver and skin of pale moonlight stared in stunned, ravenous shock. Whispers rippled through the crowd like wind through dry autumn leaves.

*"A mortal..."*
*"Look at the color of her skin... she bleeds warm..."*
*"How does she draw breath in the Veil?"*
*"Her scent... by the Void, she smells of living summer..."*

Vaelen did not spare the whispering nobles a single glance. He marched down the center aisle with arrogant, unhurried grace, his heavy boots clicking rhythmically against the obsidian floor. Elena matched his stride, feeling the protective shroud of his shadow magic wrapping around them like an invisible armor.

At the far end of the hall, seated upon a colossal throne carved from a single meteor of weeping black iron, was High King Malakor.

The King was ancient, his silver hair cascading over a mantle of midnight raven feathers. His skin was smooth—preserved by centuries of stolen youth—but his eyes were sunken pits of ancient, diseased yellow. In his withered hand, he held a goblet of hammered gold.

To his right stood Lady Lysandra, draped in emerald silk, her sharp, predatory gaze dissecting Elena with calculating curiosity.

Vaelen halted five paces from the foot of the throne dais. He offered a crisp, formal nod of his head—not a bow, but the bare minimum courtesy of a warlord to his sovereign.

"Your Majesty," Vaelen said, his baritone filling the silent hall.

King Malakor leaned forward, his yellow eyes fixing onto Elena with unmasked, covetous hunger. A slow, repulsive smile stretched across his thin lips.

"Vaelen," the King hissed softly. "My most lethal commander. The whispers in the corridors were true. You bring a mortal into the heart of the Obsidian Veil. A creature of warm flesh and beating blood."

Elena felt Malakor’s gaze crawling over her skin like venomous spiders. Beside her, Vaelen’s arm tightened beneath her hand, the muscles turning to solid steel.

"She is my captive, Majesty," Vaelen said coldly. "She was pulled through the Rift during an attempted breach at the Western sally-port."

"A captive?" Malakor purred, standing slowly from his throne. "Or a prize? Step forward, mortal. Let me see the color of your eyes."

Elena felt the sudden spike of alarm through the Blood-Tether.

Before she could take half a step, Vaelen shifted his weight, stepping directly into Malakor’s line of sight, shielding Elena completely behind the broad wall of his shoulders.

"The mortal stays beside me, King Malakor," Vaelen said. His voice was calm, but underneath it lay the subsonic growl of a dragon daring an intruder to step near its hoard.

The courtiers in the hall gasped. Several lords took two hurried steps back.

Malakor’s smile vanished, replaced by an arctic glare. "You forget your place, Commander Thorne. Everything within the Veil belongs to the throne. Including the stray beasts you drag from the dark."

He gestured to a kneeling cupbearer. The servant hurried forward, offering Elena a chalice of engraved silver brimming with dark, sweet-smelling wine.

"Drink, child," Malakor commanded, his voice dripping with false warmth. "A toast of welcome to the court of Aethelgard. Swear fealty to the Black Sun, and you shall know neither cold nor pain."

Elena looked down at the silver chalice. The wine smelled heavily of crushed almonds and winterberries—the unmistakable scent of cyanide and distilled void-bane.

Before Elena could speak, Vaelen’s arm shot out.

His gloved hand caught the silver chalice, snatching it from the servant’s trembling grip.

With casual, insulting indifference, Vaelen tipped his wrist.

The dark wine spilled across the black marble at the foot of the King's dais. Where the liquid touched the stone, the marble hissed, smoked, and pitted, dissolving into black acid before the horrified eyes of the court.

"My captive does not drink venom, Malakor," Vaelen said softly, his silver eyes flashing with murderous intent.

The King’s jaw twitched. "You dare insult the royal hospitality?"

"I dare protect what is mine," Vaelen replied, taking Elena by the hand, lacing his fingers through hers so that the golden brand on her wrist was visible to every lord in the room. "She is bound to House Thorne. Touch her, King Malakor, and the Umbral Guard will march on this palace before the moons set."

The silence in the hall was deafening.

Malakor stared at the linked hands, seeing the undeniable glow of the Blood-Tether. A cold, furious calculation hardened in the old King's yellow eyes. He knew he could not execute Vaelen here without inciting a full civil war among the northern garrisons.

"Take your mortal pet to your tower, Commander," Malakor hissed through clenched teeth. "We shall see how long your shadow shields her when the winter comes."

"Longer than your crown will sit upon your brow," Vaelen murmured beneath his breath.

Without waiting for the King’s dismissal, Vaelen turned on his heel, keeping Elena tucked against his side, and marched out of the hall as hundreds of courtiers parted before them in terrified reverence.


As they strode through the grand colonnade of the Throne Room of Raven Mirrors, the silence of the court felt heavier than an avalanche.

Nobles in velvet doublets and ladies draped in diamond-dusted mantles pressed back against the obsidian pillars, their eyes darting between Vaelen’s hand clamped over Elena’s and the smoking, hissing puddle of void-bane acid eating into the floor at the King’s feet.

Lady Lysandra stepped forward from the shadows of the royal dais, a delicate glass wine chalice held between her pale, ringed fingers.

"Always so dramatic, Commander Thorne," Lysandra murmured, her voice like liquid poison sliding over silk. She stopped two paces from Elena, her emerald eyes raking over the mortal girl from her auburn curls down to the hem of her midnight gown. "You bring a mortal into our midst and spill the royal vintage on the marble. Are you certain you wish to play this game? The stakes are rather... permanent."

Elena did not wait for Vaelen to answer.

She held Lysandra’s gaze, her chin lifted, her hazel eyes flashing with an unmistakable, golden glint of dawn-fire.

"I don't play games, Lady Lysandra," Elena said, her voice carrying across the hushed hall with cool, razor-sharp precision. "And from what I've seen of this court, your King’s hospitality is as cheap as his wine."

A collective, stunned gasp rippled through the assembled lords. Several courtiers covered their mouths with lace handkerchiefs.

Lysandra blinked in genuine shock, then a slow, dangerous smile curved her red lips. "Fascinating. A mortal with claws. Keep her close, Vaelen. The wolves in this court have teeth."

"My shadow has teeth as well, Lysandra," Vaelen replied, his silver eyes flashing with icy warning. "Tell the King that if he wishes to speak with me again, he may summon me to the northern bastions—under full arms."

With that final declaration, Vaelen escorted Elena through the towering bronze doors of the Great Hall, leaving the trembling courtiers and the humiliated monarch in their wake.


As they walked back through the vaulted corridors of the palace toward the private spire, Elena’s fingers remained loosely curled around Vaelen’s arm.

She could feel the cold, lethal rhythm of his magic slowly relaxing, retreating from the surface of his skin back into the deep conduits of his soul.

"You took a tremendous risk back there," she murmured, looking up at his sharp profile.

Vaelen glanced down, his silver eyes flashing with a faint, dangerous smirk.

"A calculated one, my archivist," he replied, his hand covering hers where it rested on his forearm. "Malakor is a tyrant who rules through fear and hesitation. If you show him a single ounce of deference, he will dissect you on his altar. But when you look him in the eye and remind him that his throat bleeds the same as any mortal’s... he hesitates. And in war, hesitation is fatal."

Elena laughed softly under her breath. "Remind me never to get on your bad side, Commander."

"You are already under my skin, Elena," he whispered, his thumb lightly stroking her wrist where the golden brand hummed. "There is no bad side left for you."
