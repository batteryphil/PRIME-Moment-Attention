# Chapter 7: The Midnight Ambush

The kiss had burned like a star collapsing inside her chest, but the explosion of black glass shattered the illusion of safety in a microsecond.

*CRASH.*

The vaulted stained-glass lancet above the balcony detonated in a shower of black crystal shards that rained down across the Persian rugs like razor-sharp hail. The freezing night air of the high crags rushed into the bedchamber, howling like a pack of wolves and extinguishing half the candles on the mantle.

In a fraction of a heartbeat, Vaelen transformed from an intoxicated lover into an engine of pure, lethal destruction.

His arm whipped around Elena’s waist, violently shoving her behind the heavy granite pillar of the dressing vanity as four dark, armored figures plummeted through the shattered window frame, landing in low, predatory crouches upon the fur-strewn floor.

"Assassins," Vaelen snarled, his voice dropping into an unholy, subterranean roar that shook the iron chandeliers.

The intruders wore form-fitting armor of woven shadow-serpent scales that absorbed the ambient firelight. Their faces were obscured by blank, polished masks of carved humanoid bone, devoid of eyes or mouths save for two narrow slits glowing with eerie phosphorescent green luminescence. In their hands, twin curved daggers dripped with a viscous, emerald venom that hissed as it splattered against the dark oak floorboards, eating through the wood in plumes of acrid, choking smoke.

"Black Inquisitors," Vaelen growled, stepping into the center of the room. "Malakor didn't even wait for the moons to set."

The killers did not utter a sound. They moved with the synchronized, fluid lethality of a royal execution squad bred strictly for regicide.

Two of the assassins lunged at Vaelen simultaneously, their blades flashing in scissor-like arcs aimed at his throat and his femoral artery.

Vaelen didn't bother diving for the heavy broadsword resting against the hearth. He didn't need to.

He thrust both hands outward, his fingers hooking like claws. The shadows pooling in the corners of the room tore free from the stone, erupting into massive, razor-sharp scythes of solidified darkness that whistled through the air with the speed of whip-cracks.

The first assassin’s daggers struck Vaelen’s shadow-shield in a blinding shower of violet sparks. Vaelen stepped cleanly into the blow, his steel-plated left forearm striking the assassin across the throat with bone-shattering force. Before the second killer could exploit the opening, Vaelen’s shadow-scythe whipped around behind him, severing the second assassin’s torso from his waist in a clean, spraying arc of black immortal blood.

Elena pressed her back against the solid granite vanity, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird.

Through the Blood-Tether, she felt Vaelen’s battle-rage—a cold, calculated, exhilarating storm of adrenaline that flooded her own veins, sharpening her eyesight until every falling shard of glass seemed to tumble in slow motion. Her breathing came fast, but there was no terror—only the primal, electric certainty that they were in this fight together.

*The third assassin.*

Elena’s gaze whipped across the room.

The third masked killer had not engaged Vaelen. He had slithered along the perimeter wall, moving with soundless, terrifying speed, his cold green eye-slits locked directly onto Elena behind the vanity.

He knew. The court knew.

*Kill the mortal girl, and the invincible Commander Thorne drops dead on the floor.*

The assassin lunged, his poisoned blade thrusting straight toward Elena’s chest.

There was no time to scream for help. Vaelen was locked in furious combat with the fourth assassin and a fifth climbing over the balcony railing, parrying strikes with blinding speed.

Elena didn't freeze. She had spent five years surviving late-night Chicago commutes on the Red Line; she had once broken a mugger’s nose with a heavy reference volume of ancient Greek verbs, and she was not about to die cowering in a silk gown on an alien floor.

As the assassin closed the final two paces, Elena grabbed the heavy, solid-bronze five-branch candelabra from the vanity and swung it with both hands with all the fury in her body.

*CLANG.*

The heavy metal struck the side of the assassin’s bone mask with a sickening crack. The mask shattered into bone splinters, sending dark blood flying as the force of the blow knocked the killer off balance. His poisoned blade sliced harmlessly through the velvet bed-hangings behind her, sizzling against the down mattress.

The assassin snarled—a wet, animalistic sound—and drew a backup stiletto from his boot.

Elena dove to the right, rolling across the Persian rug. Her fingers slapped against the low table where Vaelen’s weapons lay. Her hand closed around the hilt of a ceremonial hunting dagger—a foot of dark, ridged steel inlaid with silver runes.

The assassin tackled her, his armored weight driving the breath from her lungs as they hit the floorboards.

His left hand clamped around her throat, choking off her air, while his right raised the venom-coated stiletto above her eye.

"Die, mortal whore," a dry, raspy voice hissed beneath the shattered bone mask.

Elena felt the cold prickle of death against her eyelashes.

And then, deep in the center of her chest, something snapped.

It was not fear. It was the sleeping, ancient fire that had brought her across the dimensions.

*No,* her mind roared. *Not today!*

Golden light—blinding, incandescent, and scalding hot—erupted from Elena’s palms.

The solar flame washed over the assassin’s hand holding her throat. The shadow-serpent scales of his armor didn't just burn; they dissolved into white-hot slag. The assassin shrieked in agony as his skin charred to black bone.

With a feral cry, Elena thrust the hunting dagger upward with both hands.

The steel blade drove straight through the soft hollow beneath his jaw, piercing into his brain.

The assassin stiffened, convulsion racking his frame, and collapsed sideways onto the floor, motionless.

Elena scrambled out from beneath the corpse, gasping for air, clutching the bloody dagger with shaking hands. Her palms were still smoking with golden embers of dawn-fire.

Across the room, Vaelen decapitated the final assassin with a vicious backhand cleave of his broadsword. The head tumbled across the floor, coming to rest near the hearth.

Silence descended upon the ruined chamber, broken only by the crackle of the burning mattress and their synchronized, ragged breathing.

Vaelen stood in the center of the carnage, his chest heaving, his silver eyes wild and feral. He looked down at the assassin lying dead at Elena’s feet, then looked at the smoking dagger in her bloody hands and the golden embers still swirling around her fingertips.

For two heartbeats, he just stared at her in stunned, unadulterated awe.

Then he crossed the room in three strides.

He dropped to his knees in front of her, pulling the dagger from her numb fingers and tossing it aside. His hands swept over her face, her neck, checking for cuts, checking for the green venom.

"Are you whole?" he demanded, his voice cracking with raw, terrified emotion. "Did the poison touch your skin?"

"I'm... I'm okay," Elena panted, her hands clinging to the front of his torn tunic. "I killed him, Vaelen. The fire... it came out of my hands."

Vaelen stared into her hazel eyes, where tiny flecks of gold were still dancing.

"Dawn-fire," he whispered, his thumbs wiping a smear of black blood from her cheek. "True celestial dawn-fire. You didn't just survive the Crossing, Elena. The magic of your ancestors chose you."

The heavy oak doors of the chamber burst open.

Kaelen rushed in at the head of twenty armed guards, broadswords drawn, bows notched.

"Commander!" Kaelen gasped, taking in the five dead Inquisitors, the shattered window, and the scorched floorboards. "The palace bells are tolling! Malakor has declared House Thorne traitors to the crown! Three regiments of the Royal Guard are marching on the Citadel gates right now!"

Vaelen stood up smoothly, pulling Elena to her feet and keeping her tucked securely against his side.

The tender lover from moments ago vanished, replaced by the ruthless, brilliant high commander of the northern armies.

"How many men can we trust, Kaelen?" Vaelen asked, his voice cold as arctic steel.

"Fifty veteran scouts are holding the lower courtyard," Kaelen replied swiftly. "The rest of the garrison is divided. If we stay here, Malakor’s siege engines will level the spire by sunrise."

Vaelen’s jaw clenched. He looked toward the northern windows, where the jagged silhouettes of the mountains disappeared into the dark.

"Sound the retreat to the northern redoubts," Vaelen commanded. "Have the mounts brought to the subterranean sally-port. We take the shadow-striders through the valley."

"Where are we going?" Elena asked, looking at the two men.

Vaelen looked down at her, his silver eyes dark with dangerous resolve.

"Into the Wilds," he said. "Past the reach of Malakor's crown. If the King wants a war... we will bring him one that burns his kingdom to ash."


While Kaelen and the veteran scouts barricaded the heavy iron sally-port doors of the tower, Elena knelt beside the washbasin, hurriedly scrubbing the black blood of the Inquisitor from her hands.

The water turned dark and oily, smelling of burned copper and venom. Her hands were still trembling, not with terror, but with the residual, electric hum of the dawn-fire that had surged through her palms. When she looked in the silvered glass above the basin, she saw tiny sparks of gold swirling in the depths of her hazel pupils.

Vaelen stepped up behind her, his broad chest pressing against her back.

In his hands, he held a pendant of dark star-stone on a braided cord of dragon-leather. The stone was smooth, cool, and pulsed with a faint violet luminescence that matched the runes along his neck.

"Take this," he murmured, lifting the cord over her head and settling the pendant against her breastbone.

The moment the stone touched her skin, a soothing, icy calm washed over her racing pulse, anchoring her frayed nerves.

"What is it?" Elena asked, her fingers tracing the carved stone.

"The Heart of the Nightglass," Vaelen said quietly, his hands resting on her shoulders. "It was forged from the same meteorite as the throne. It dampens your spiritual signature. As long as you wear it, Malakor’s scrying orbs cannot track your location beyond five miles. You will be invisible to the royal drakes."

"And you?"

"I have lived in the dark for four centuries, Elena," he said, a grim, confident smirk touching his lips. "They know where my shadow walks. But they will never catch us in the Wilds."

He turned to the table, sweeping several rolled vellum charts into an oilskin tube, along with two vials of botanical healing tinctures and a pouch of gold sun-coins.

"The horses are ready at the subterranean gates," Kaelen called out from the doorway, strapping on a second pair of daggers. "Commander, the royal siege towers are already crossing the lower bridge. We must move now."

Vaelen looked at Elena, extending his hand.

"Are you ready, my archivist?"

Elena took his hand, feeling the fierce, unbreakable resonance of their shared tether humming in her blood.

"Lead the way, Lord Thorne."


Before they stepped onto the secret spiral staircase leading to the sally-port, Vaelen stopped her at the threshold.

He reached into the pocket of his leather cuirass and retrieved a pair of small, silver-handled throwing stilettos in a supple leather sheath. He knelt before her, strapping the sheath securely to the inside of her left boot calf.

"A backup blade," he murmured, his fingers smoothing the leather strap against her trousers. "If you are ever separated from your dagger, reach for your boot. Never let an enemy believe you are unarmed."

Elena looked down at him, her heart softening at the fierce, unyielding protectiveness radiating through their bond.

She reached down, her fingers lightly brushing the dark hair from his brow.

"I know how to use it, Vaelen," she promised softly.

Vaelen looked up, his molten silver eyes meeting hers in the dim torchlight. For a moment, the war, the alarm bells, and the coming siege faded into background noise.

"I know you do, Elena," he said, rising to his full height. "Now let us go show Malakor what happens when he hunts a lion in his own den."
