# Chapter 8: Into the Twilight Fen

The subterranean sally-port beneath the Nightglass Citadel smelled of damp stone, horse manure, and cold iron.

Elena had shed the torn midnight-blue silk gown in favor of dark riding leathers that Vaelen’s quartermaster had dug out of an ancient chest—supple black buckskin trousers, knee-high boots of waterproof shadow-serpent leather, and a heavy shearling-lined coat that swallowed her frame. At her hip, she had buckled the ceremonial hunting dagger she’d used to kill the Inquisitor, its dark blade cleaned and freshly oiled.

Across the vaulted stables, two shadow-striders were being saddled by silent northern grooms.

Elena stared at the creatures in morbid fascination. They were not horses. They stood seventeen hands high, with the sleek, muscular build of thoroughbreds, but their coats were covered in fine, velvety charcoal scales that absorbed the light. Their eyes burned with intelligent violet embers, and instead of hooves, their feet split into four broad, leather-padded talons capable of running silently across boggy peat, vertical ice, and jagged scree without losing traction.

"Can you ride?" Vaelen asked, striding over with a pair of heavy saddlebags slung across his shoulder. He had donned a light brigandine cuirass of blackened steel plates over his leather tunic, his broadsword strapped across his back.

"I took equestrian lessons at summer camp in Wisconsin when I was twelve," Elena said dryly. "On an arthritic pony named Buttercup. Does that count?"

A faint, wry smile touched Vaelen’s lips. "Not in the slightest."

He tossed the saddlebags over the larger of the two beasts—a massive stallion whose breath billowed in silver plumes—and stepped in front of Elena. Without asking for permission, he reached down, caught her by the waist, and lifted her into the high leather saddle as effortlessly as if she were made of thistle-down.

Before she could adjust her boots in the stirrups, Vaelen swung his long leg over the cantle behind her.

He settled into the saddle flush against her back.

Elena gasped softly as the sudden, overwhelming heat of his body pressed into her spine. His long legs bracketed her thighs, his broad chest formed a solid wall behind her shoulders, and his arms reached around her waist to gather the thick braided reins in his gloved hands.

"Hold the pommel," he murmured into her ear, his breath hot against the chill of the underground air. "These beasts do not trot. When they hit the open gorge, they run like thunder."

"Are we sharing one mount?" Elena asked, trying to ignore the delicious, heavy pressure of his groin against her lower back.

"We are bound by fifty paces, Elena," Vaelen said, his voice dropping into that familiar, raspy purr. "If our mounts were separated on a steep mountain path by a landslide or an ambush, the severance would kill us both before our beasts could turn around. You ride with me."

"Understood," she breathed, her fingers tightening around the polished brass pommel.

Kaelen rode up beside them on the second shadow-strider, leading thirty mounted scout veterans in dark cloaks.

"The southern gates have fallen to the Royal Guard, Commander," Kaelen reported in a low, urgent voice. "Malakor has released the Black Wyrms from the aerie. We have less than an hour before their aerial scouts sweep the northern passes."

Vaelen’s jaw hardened. "Open the portcullis. We ride for the Twilight Fen."

The massive iron grate groaned upward into the rock ceiling.

Vaelen squeezed his thighs against the stallion's flanks. The shadow-strider surged forward with explosive power.

They burst out of the subterranean tunnel into the freezing mountain night.

Elena’s breath was torn from her lungs by the sheer velocity. The wind screamed through her hair, biting at her cheeks with needles of frost. Below them, the capital city was a cauldron of fire and sounding alarm bells. Columns of royal cavalry carrying flaming torches were snaking through the lower avenues toward the Citadel, but Vaelen guided their mount along a narrow, treacherous goat track that skirted the edge of a five-hundred-foot abyss.

The shadow-strider didn't stumble once. Its talons gripped the wet granite with supernatural precision, bounding over boulders and leaping across yawning chasms with fluid, terrifying grace.

Behind them, high in the bruised-plum sky, a piercing, unearthly shriek echoed across the peaks.

Elena glanced over her shoulder.

Circling the highest spire of the royal palace was a winged nightmare: a reptilian horror forty feet long, with leathery black wings and eyes that burned like sickly green coals. Mounted upon its neck was a royal wyrm-rider, holding an iron lance tipped with alchemical fire.

"Wyrm!" Elena shouted over the roaring wind.

"I see it," Vaelen growled behind her.

He didn't slow the mount. Instead, he pulled the left rein, veering the shadow-strider down a steep scree slope toward a massive, ancient stone archway that spanned the mountain pass: the Ward of the Broken Spine.

The archway was carved from a single block of petrified black bone, inscribed with glowing azure runes that had stood for a thousand years.

"Brace yourself!" Vaelen commanded, wrapping his left arm around Elena’s ribs, pulling her hard against his chest.

The shadow-striders thundered beneath the bone archway.

Elena felt a strange, elastic resistance—as if riding through a thick membrane of water and ozone—followed by a sharp *crack* that echoed through her teeth. The azure runes flared brilliantly, and then snapped shut behind them in a wall of opaque, blinding silver mist.

High above, the pursuing wyrm shrieked in frustration, wheeling away as its master realized the royal wards had sealed the pass.

The sound of the capital, the bells, and the pursuing army vanished in an instant.

Silence swallowed them whole.

***

They rode for another three hours, descending from the jagged granite crags into the lowlands, until the air grew thick, humid, and heavy with the scent of damp moss, stagnant water, and decaying leaves.

The Twilight Fen spread out before them like an emerald and violet dream.

It was an endless wetland of black water channels and towering, weeping willows with bark like polished ebony. Feathery moss draped from every branch like silver lace, glowing with faint, phosphorescent turquoise light that illuminated the water’s surface in ghostly splendor. Giant water-lilies with petals of deep amethyst floated in the shallows, opening their blooms to the night air, releasing a sweet, narcotic perfume of crushed vanilla and mint.

The war-beasts slowed their pace to a gentle, swaying walk, their taloned feet hissing softly through the duckweed and shallow black water.

Elena leaned back against Vaelen’s chest, completely spent. Every muscle in her legs and back screamed with fatigue from the hours of bracing against the saddle, but the steady, warm cadence of Vaelen’s breathing against her cheek kept her grounded.

"How are your ribs?" Vaelen murmured, his lips brushing the crown of her ear.

Elena tilted her head back, looking up into his silver eyes. "Aching. But alive."

"You fought well in the tower, Elena," he said quietly, his tone devoid of his usual arrogance. His hand rested over hers on the pommel, his thumb absentmindedly stroking the back of her leather glove. "Most mortal warriors would have frozen when the bone masks dropped. You cracked an inquisitor’s skull and burned his flesh with your bare hands."

Elena smiled faintly, watching the turquoise light dance across the dark water. "Back in Chicago, I used to read historical accounts of medieval sieges and Viking raids. I always wondered what I would do if the world went mad and violence came through the door. I thought I'd probably hide in a closet and cry."

"And now?"

"Now I know," she whispered. "When someone tries to hurt the people I care about... I burn them."

Vaelen went still behind her.

Through the Blood-Tether, she felt the sudden, deep resonance of his reaction—a wave of warmth, respect, and profound emotion that made the air between them grow heavy.

*The people I care about.*

She hadn't said *myself*. She had said *the people I care about*.

Vaelen tightened his arm around her waist, pulling her flush against his chest, tucking her head beneath his chin as the shadow-strider waded deeper into the ancient, glowing heart of the wild fen.


By the fourth hour of their flight into the wild wetland, the dense willow canopy thickened until the two crescent moons were visible only as fractured slivers of amethyst and ice.

The shadow-striders were breathing in heavy, rhythmic huffs, their talons sinking into spongy banks of luminous violet moss.

Vaelen raised a hand, signaling Kaelen and the thirty scouts to halt.

"We rest the mounts for one hour," Vaelen ordered in a low, carrying whisper. "Establish a three-point perimeter ward on the higher knoll. No open fires. Use shadow-crystals only."

The scouts dismounted silently, moving like wraiths into the mist.

Vaelen slid from the saddle first, his boots squelching softly in the damp moss. He reached up, catching Elena around the waist, lifting her down from the high leather seat. The moment her boots touched the ground, her knees gave way—her muscles stiff and numb from five straight hours of violent galloping.

Vaelen caught her before she could stumble, his arms wrapping securely around her waist, supporting her against his chest.

"Easy," he murmured, his voice soft against her ear. "The saddle takes a heavy toll on mortal hips."

"My hips are fine," Elena grunted, wincing as she tried to flex her legs. "It's my spine that feels like it’s been run over by an eighteen-wheeler."

"A what?"

"A very large, noisy machine on Earth that carries freight," she sighed, leaning her forehead against the cool steel of his brigandine cuirass. "Don't worry about it."

Vaelen led her to the base of an ancient, gnarled willow whose roots formed a dry, sheltered hollow carpeted in soft moss. He unrolled a thick wool blanket, gesturing for her to sit, before pulling a leather waterskin and a packet of dried provisions from his saddlebag.

He sat beside her, uncorking the skin and handing it to her.

The liquid was cool, spiced with wild mint and elderberries, washing the sour taste of fear and ash from her throat. She passed it back to him, watching as his throat bobbed with three long swallows.

"How far behind us are the royal hounds?" Elena asked, looking out into the swirling silver fog of the fen.

"At least thirty miles," Vaelen replied, breaking off a piece of smoked venison flatbread and pressing it into her hand. "The Ward of the Broken Spine severed their ground pursuit. Wyrms cannot navigate the fen at night; the phosphorescent mist blinds their thermal vision. We are safe until daybreak."

Elena chewed the hearty bread, the warmth of the food reviving her flagging energy. Beside her, Vaelen reached out, his bare fingers gently lifting a damp curl of auburn hair from her cheek, tucking it behind her ear.

His touch was so uncharacteristically tender, so completely at odds with the terrifying warlord who had cleaved men in two hours earlier, that Elena felt a lump form in her throat.

"Why didn't you let them take me in the throne room, Vaelen?" she asked quietly, her hazel eyes meeting his molten silver gaze. "If you had handed me over to Malakor, you would still have your citadel. You would still be the High Commander. You wouldn't be hunted in a swamp with a handful of rebel scouts."

Vaelen went silent for a long moment, looking down at his scarred, battle-worn hands.

"For four hundred years, I thought honor was loyalty to the crown," he said, his voice quiet and heavy as lead. "I watched Malakor drain thousands of mortals in the Great Purge. I told myself it was for the stability of the realm. I became his blade. His butcher. And every year, a little more of my soul turned to ash, until I couldn't remember what it felt like to care whether the sun rose or not."

He turned to look at her, his silver eyes luminous in the turquoise glow of the fen-moss.

"And then you arrived, Elena," he whispered. "You looked at my sword and told me to shove it. You bled fire. You made me remember that a life without love, without something worth dying for, is just a slower way of being dead. I would rather be hunted across every swamp in Aethelgard with you at my side than sit on an empire of ash alone."

Elena felt tears prick her lashes.

She reached out, cupping his jaw in both hands, her thumbs stroking his high cheekbones.

"You're not alone anymore, Vaelen," she whispered.

He closed his eyes, leaning his face into her palms, inhaling her scent like a man dying of thirst finding an oasis in the desert.
