# Chapter 6: Whispers in the Dark

The climb back to the apex of the Nightglass Spire was conducted in suffocating, electrified silence.

Neither of them spoke as they ascended the narrow, winding spiral stairwell carved into the obsidian heart of the tower. Vaelen kept one broad, leather-clad hand firmly on the small of Elena’s back, guiding her steps along the slick granite risers. His body radiated an intense, dry heat that practically burned through the liquid silk of her court gown, while his silver gaze flickered incessantly toward every shadowy alcove and arrow-slit they passed, as if expecting Malakor’s executioners to materialize from the damp masonry.

Elena could feel the tempest churning behind his ribs through the Blood-Tether—a dark, roiling vortex of homicidal fury, cold tactical calculation, and something raw, terrifying, and fiercely protective that made her knees feel dangerously weak.

When they crossed the threshold into his private chambers, Vaelen slammed the heavy iron-banded oak door shut with a violent *thud* that rattled the decorative shields mounted on the wall.

He stripped off his heavy charcoal-wool mantle, tossing it onto the long council table with enough force to overturn a silver wine flagon. The dregs of dark blackberry vintage pooled against a leather-bound battle ledger.

"Insane," Elena whispered, her adrenaline finally crashing, leaving her trembling from head to toe as the cold night air of the high altitude seeped into the chamber. "You are completely, certifiably insane. You just poured poisoned wine onto the King’s floor in front of three hundred armed nobles!"

Vaelen turned on his heel, his silver eyes blazing with unearthly, feral luminescence in the firelit gloom.

"He offered you void-bane, Elena!" Vaelen snapped, stalking toward her until his towering frame blocked out the warm amber light from the roaring hearth. "Do you know what that poison does to mortal organs? It doesn't kill you cleanly. It liquefies the lining of your stomach, turns your blood to black acid, and leaves you screaming on the floor for twelve hours while your soul is siphoned into the King’s phylactery!"

"I know what cyanide smells like, Vaelen! I wasn't going to drink it!"

"You shouldn't have even had to look at it!" he roared, stopping barely a foot from her, his hands balling into tight fists at his sides as if fighting the violent urge to tear the stone walls down with his bare fingers. "Malakor knows you are the key. He saw the golden brand on your wrist. He knows the ancient prophecy of the Dawn Tether. From this night forward, every cup you touch, every shadow in the hall, every whisper in the dark will be an assassin’s blade aimed at your throat."

Elena looked up at him, her chest heaving against the tight stays of her bodice. Up close, in the flickering light of the cedar logs, the terrifying warlord looked exhausted. The razor-sharp lines around his eyes were etched with four centuries of war, court treachery, and relentless vigilance.

And yet, when the moment had come, he had stepped between her and an immortal tyrant without a single heartbeat of hesitation.

The anger drained out of her, replaced by a sudden, overwhelming ache in her chest that had nothing to do with magic and everything to do with the man standing before her.

"You protected me," she said softly, her voice barely louder than the crackle of the hearth.

Vaelen went completely still.

His pupils contracted, the molten silver of his eyes searching hers with sudden, piercing intensity.

"I protected my life," he said coldly, though the lie tasted sour even on his own tongue. "The tether—"

"Don't give me the tether excuse, Thorne," Elena whispered, taking half a step closer until the silk of her skirts brushed his slate-gray breeches. "I felt you through the bond. When that cupbearer stepped near me, it wasn't fear for your own skin that made your magic flare. It was rage. You were ready to burn the palace down."

Vaelen swallowed hard, his throat working as the violet runes along his neck flickered softly in the dim light.

"You speak too much, mortal," he murmured, his voice dropping into that low, dangerous baritone that made her skin prickle with heat.

"Then shut me up," Elena whispered.

A ragged breath tore from Vaelen’s chest.

His control snapped.

He reached out, his large, calloused hands coming up to cup her face, his long fingers tangling into the thick, fragrant auburn curls at the nape of her neck. He tilted her face upward, his thumbs stroking the soft curve of her cheekbones, his touch searing hot against her cool skin.

"You have no idea what you do to me," he growled against her lips, his breath smelling of dark spiced wine and cedar smoke. "Four hundred years I have walked this realm as an empty vessel of blade and bone. And then you fall from the sky... soft, defiant, and smelling of summer... and you make me want to burn the world to keep you warm."

Elena’s breath hitched. "Vaelen..."

"Turn around," he commanded softly, his voice thick with a hunger that made her pulse thunder against her ribs.

Elena turned, her back to him, facing the dark expanse of the chamber.

She heard the soft scrape of his boots on the Persian rug as he stepped right behind her. His chest pressed against her shoulder blades—a solid, unyielding wall of muscle and heat.

His fingers found the silk cords laced through the back of her bodice.

One by one, with agonizing, deliberate slowness, Vaelen loosened the stays.

With each pull of the golden lace, the suffocating constriction around Elena’s ribs fell away, allowing her lungs to expand in deep, shuddering gasps. But as the dress loosened, the heat of his hands through the thin chemise beneath grew unbearable. His knuckles brushed against the sensitive skin along her spine, sending shivers of molten electricity cascading down to her thighs.

"Tell me about your world, Elena," he murmured against the shell of her ear, his lips grazing the delicate curve of her lobe as he unlaced the final row of eyelets.

"My world?" Elena whispered, her head tilting back against his broad chest, her eyes fluttering shut.

"Aye. The city of iron towers you spoke of. Chicago."

Elena smiled faintly, lost in the sensory contrast between his lethal hands and her distant, ordinary memories. "It’s loud. There are millions of people, but you can go months without anyone truly seeing you. We build towers of glass that reach into the clouds, but we don't have magic. We have science. We have electricity. We have trains that run on steel rails and coffee shops on every corner where people sit with their machines and pretend they aren't lonely."

Vaelen’s hands slid around to the front of her waist, resting over the loosened silk of her gown, anchoring her against his hips.

"Were you lonely, Elena?" he asked quietly.

Elena opened her eyes, looking down at the dark, powerful hands resting over her belly.

"I didn't know I was," she admitted softly. "Until I came here. Until I realized I would rather face poisoned chalices with you than go back to a safe, quiet life where nobody cares if I wake up in the morning."

Vaelen groaned—a low, pained sound deep in his chest.

He spun her around in his arms, his back hitting the heavy wooden mantle of the fireplace as he pulled her against him. His hands framed her jaw, his silver eyes dark and ravenous in the amber glow.

"Do not say such things to a monster, Elena," he rasped, his face inches from hers. "Unless you mean them."

"I mean every word, Vaelen Thorne," she breathed, reaching up to press her palm against his chest, right over his pounding heart.

The golden runes on her wrist flared with brilliant light, matching the violet runes glowing beneath his linen shirt.

"You asked about mortals earlier," Vaelen murmured, his voice softening as his thumb traced her lower lip. "In the First Age, mortals and the Shadow-Fae walked together. Your people carried the solar resonance—the dawn-fire that kept the night from turning poisonous. But King Malakor grew obsessed with escaping his own mortality. He discovered that by bleeding mortal souls into obsidian mirrors, he could anchor his lifespan into the bedrock of the world. Three centuries ago, he orchestrated the Great Purge. Fifty thousand mortal men, women, and children were herded into the sunken basins of the capital and drained to fuel his throne."

Elena felt a cold horror clutch her throat. "That's why you hate him."

"I was his commander," Vaelen said, his voice bitter with ancient self-loathing. "I was sworn by blood-oaths to defend the realm. By the time I realized what he was doing, the rifts were sealed, the mirrors were shattered, and your people were extinct from Aethelgard. I have spent three hundred years waiting for a weapon, a chance, a spark to slit his throat."

He looked down into her hazel eyes, his gaze burning with unvarnished reverence.

"And then you tumbled through the glass, carrying the dawn in your veins."

"I'm not a weapon, Vaelen," she whispered, her fingers sliding into his dark hair.

"No," he agreed, leaning down until his lips brushed hers. "You are my salvation."

The kiss was slow, opulent, and breathtakingly tender. It tasted of dark blackberry wine, cedar smoke, and four hundred years of starvation. His mouth moved over hers with ravenous restraint, parting her lips with smooth confidence, teasing her until a soft, needy whimper rose in her throat.

Elena arched into his hard body, wrapping her arms around his neck, pulling him down until there was no space left between them. The Blood-Tether between them hummed like a plucked harp string, flooding her nervous system with his intoxicating heat.

Vaelen’s hands slid down to her hips, lifting her onto the edge of the low oak table, stepping between her parted thighs. He buried his face in the crook of her neck, inhaling her jasmine scent, his teeth gently grazing the pulse point beneath her jaw.

"Elena..." he growled, his breathing ragged. "If I don't stop now, I will take you on this table until dawn."

"Who said I wanted you to stop?" she breathed into his hair.

Vaelen lifted his head, his silver eyes blazing with feral triumph. He reached for the neckline of her shift—



Vaelen led her over to the colossal bay window overlooking the sleeping capital. 

He pointed upward into the bruised-plum sky, toward a dense cluster of silver stars that formed the silhouette of an arched bow.

"That is the Archer of the Northern Void," Vaelen whispered, his chin resting against her temple. "For four centuries, my ancestors looked to those stars when guiding our war-striders through the passes. We believed that as long as the Archer stood watch, House Thorne would never fall from grace."

Elena leaned back into his solid warmth, looking up at the foreign constellations. 

"On Earth, we have the Big Dipper and Orion," she said softly. "My father used to take me camping in the Upper Peninsula of Michigan when I was a girl. We would lie on an old wool blanket on the hood of his truck, drinking hot chocolate from a thermos, watching shooting stars streak across Lake Superior. I thought the sky was the biggest thing in the universe."

"And now?" Vaelen murmured, his arm tightening around her waist.

Elena turned her head slightly, looking into his silver eyes, where flecks of violet starlight danced in the dark.

"Now I think the universe is terrifyingly small," she whispered. "Because no matter where I look, all I see is you."

Vaelen’s breath hitched. His fingers tightened into her hip, pulling her flush against his hard length.

"Elena..." he rasped, his voice dropping into that deep, dangerous register. "You are playing with an inferno."

"Then let me burn, Commander," she breathed, reaching up to press her lips to his.

*CRASH.*

Above the balcony fifty feet in the air, the vaulted stained-glass window exploded inward in a shower of black crystal shards.
