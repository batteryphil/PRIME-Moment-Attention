# Chapter 13: The River of Souls

The morning mist did not lift; in Aethelgard, dawn was not a herald of sunlight, but merely a subtle pale violet curdling in the charcoal sky.

Elena awoke with her head pillowed on Vaelen's bare chest. His left arm was wrapped firmly around her waist, anchoring her against the hardened curve of his ribs, while his right hand rested over her hip, possessive even in sleep. Beneath her ear, his heart beat in steady, rhythmic perfection—mirroring her own pulse with frightening synchronicity. 

When she stirred, his arm tightened instinctively. A low rumble vibrated in his throat, and silver eyes fluttered open, heavy with residual sleep and smoldering heat.

"Good morning, my archivist," he murmured, his voice rough and raspy from the night before. His lips brushed against her temple, inhaling deeply. "You smell of jasmine and cedar smoke."

"And you smell like trouble," Elena whispered, smiling up at him as she traced the edge of the violet tattoo curling over his collarbone. The mark felt different now. Where it had once throbbed with the raw, bruising friction of an unwanted brand, it now hummed with a warm, steady resonance that sank deep into the marrow of her bones. "How far to the Mist-Fens?"

"Two hours by shadow-strider," Vaelen said, his expression sobering as the harsh reality of their rebellion returned. He sat up smoothly, muscles rippling across his sculpted back as he reached for his tunics and leather bracers. "The River of Souls lies at the basin of the weeping cliffs. If we are to forge a weapon capable of executing Malakor, we must hear the Nightglass Oracle’s prophecy. Without her sight, we walk blind into Iron Keep."

***

By mid-afternoon, the pine forests gave way to a vast, silent expanse of black water and rising silver vapor: the Stygian Fens.

Gnarled cypress-like trees with bark like petrified bone rose from the stagnant, mirrored surface of the mere. Luminescent water-lilies glowed with a faint, ghostly turquoise luminescence, their petals closed tight against the cold. The war-beasts' hooves hissed softly as they trod through the shallow, mossy shallows, their breath billowing in white plumes.

"Do not look too deeply into the water," Vaelen warned in a low voice, riding alongside Elena so close their stirrups brushed. "The fens remember every soul Malakor has fed to the obsidian spire over three centuries. If they see mortal eyes that hold life and fire, they will reach for you."

Elena gripped the reins of her mount, her hazel eyes darting across the water's glassy surface. Beneath the dark ripples, she could see them: pale, translucent silhouettes drifting like sunken autumn leaves. Hollow eye sockets, open mouths frozen in soundless cries of anguish. A cold shiver rippled down her spine, but before the chill could settle, warmth surged through her veins—Vaelen’s shadow magic intertwining with her dawn-fire, forming a protective aura around her saddle.

"I feel them," Elena murmured, her throat tight. "They aren't just dead, Vaelen. They're starving. Malakor drained their light until only the husks remained."

"Which is why he cannot be allowed another century," Vaelen replied coldly, his jaw clenched. "His immortality is paid for in tears and stolen youth."

Ahead, rising out of the mist like the ribcage of an ancient leviathan, stood the Sunken Temple of the Nightglass.

Massive pillars of black basalt leaned at treacherous angles, half-swallowed by the black waters. Spiraling stone staircases vanished into the depths of a cavernous vault carved directly into the bedrock of the marsh.

Vaelen dismounted first, wading through knee-deep water before extending his arms up to Elena. He caught her by the waist, lifting her effortlessly from the saddle and lowering her boots onto the dry, slick flagstones of the temple landing. For a second, he held her close against his chest, his silver eyes drinking in her face.

"Stay within arm's reach," he said quietly. "The Oracle is ancient. She does not welcome visitors, even those of blood."

"I survived your throne room, Lord Thorne," Elena countered, raising an eyebrow. "I think I can handle a cranky fortune teller."

A ghost of a smirk touched Vaelen's lips. "Do not say I failed to warn you."

They passed beneath an archway carved with writhing serpents and weeping moons. Inside, the cavern smelled of crushed belladonna, dried lotus root, and ozone. In the center of the hall rested a circular pool of liquid black glass, completely motionless.

Sitting cross-legged on a stone slab beside the pool was a figure shrouded in ragged robes of woven spider-silk. Her hair was white as hoarfrost, cascading to the stone floor, and across her eyes was tied a strip of blackened linen soaked in dried blood.

"The butcher of the Umbral Guard," a dry, raspy voice echoed through the cavern, vibrating with eerie resonance. "And with him... the daughter of the sun. The spark that survived the Great Crossing."

Elena took half a step forward, the dawn-fire tingling in the tips of her fingers. "You know who I am?"

The Oracle turned her blindfolded face toward Elena, tilting her head as though listening to a melody only she could hear. "I knew your coming before your mother's mother drew her first mortal breath, child of Earth. You bring the scent of gasoline, iron towers, and printed paper into a land choked on sorcery and ash."

Vaelen stepped beside Elena, his hand naturally finding the curve of her hip. "We did not come for poetry, Morveth. Malakor has dispatched the Inquisitors. He knows the tether exists. What must we do to shatter his black crown?"

The old woman let out a sound that was half-laugh, half-cough. She dipped a withered, claw-like finger into the liquid obsidian pool. The mirror-smooth surface rippled, sending concentric rings of purple and gold light cascading against the cavern ceiling.

"You seek to kill a god-king who has anchored his heart into the bedrock of the world," Morveth whispered. "Normal steel shatters against his skin. Normal fire merely feeds his shadow. To pierce his chest, you require the Eclipse Blade."

"The star-metal dagger," Vaelen said, his eyes narrowing. "It was locked in the subterranean vaults of Iron Keep during the First War."

"Aye," Morveth rasped. "The raw celestial ore fallen from the shattered moon. But the ore alone is useless, Vaelen Thorne. A cold rock cannot kill an immortal."

She turned her head squarely toward Elena.

"The blade must be forged in the confluence of your blood," the Oracle said, each word heavy as an iron anvil. "Dawn-fire to temper the core; umbral void to quench the edge. You two are the twin halves of an ancient prophecy known as the Eclipse Bond. When two souls bound by the Blood-Tether surrender wholly to love rather than subjugation, their magic ceases to be two rivers and becomes an ocean."

Elena felt Vaelen's fingers tighten against her waist. She looked up at him, seeing the fierce, unyielding intensity in his silver gaze.

"And the price?" Elena asked, knowing from her years studying myths that no prophecy came without a cost. "What is the catch, Morveth?"

The Oracle's lips twisted into a grim smile. "The catch, sweet mortal, is that the tether will demand its final payment. Once the Eclipse Blade drinks Malakor's blood, the dimensional rift between your world and ours will permanently collapse. The mirror will shatter forever."

Elena's breath caught in her throat. Her chest constricted.

*The mirror will shatter forever.*

Chicago. The Field Museum. Her apartment on Lincoln Avenue. Her morning commute on the train with coffee in hand, the smell of rain on asphalt, her books, her quiet, solitary life.

If the rift was destroyed, she would never go home. She would belong to Aethelgard. To this world of endless twilight, bloody politics, and monstrous kings.

To Vaelen.

Beside her, she felt the sudden, agonizing drop in Vaelen's emotional state through the tether. A cold, suffocating wave of despair and dread washed over the bond. He looked away from her, his jaw rigid as stone, his knuckles white against the pommel of his broadsword. He believed she would choose to leave him. He believed that once Malakor fell, she would run back to her safe, modern world and leave him behind in the dark.

"Is that all, Morveth?" Vaelen's voice was arctic, devoid of any warmth.

"One more warning, Lord of Thorne," the Oracle whispered, her voice dropping to a sinister hiss. "Beware the gates of Iron Keep. Your foster-brother General Brand has already sold his soul to the highest bidder. Silver coins buy many blades, but royal pardon buys a coward's loyalty. You walk into a cage."

"Brand was my sworn brother," Vaelen said coldly. "He would not dare."

"Go then, Shadow Warlord," Morveth laughed, sinking back into the shadows of her silk shroud as the ripples in the pool settled into absolute stillness. "Go, and let the blood flow."

***

Outside the sunken temple, the cold fen wind blew through Elena's hair.

Vaelen did not speak as they mounted their beasts. He kept his face turned toward the black horizon, his posture stiff and remote, the wall of ice he had worn when they first met slamming back into place.

Elena could feel the turmoil boiling behind his chest through the tether—a chaotic tempest of possessive hunger, grief, and terror.

She spurred her mount forward, cutting across his path until her war-beast forced his to halt with a snort of steam.

"Look at me," Elena commanded.

Vaelen's jaw twitched, but he refused to lift his gaze. "We must make haste, Elena. If Brand is compromised—"

"I said look at me, Vaelen!"

With a sharp intake of breath, he snapped his head up. His silver eyes were burning with raw, turbulent emotion, the pupils dilated until barely a ring of molten steel was visible.

"What do you want me to say?" he snarled, his voice trembling with barely restrained fury and pain. "That I didn't hear what she said? That once Malakor is dead, you'll be forced to choose between your world and mine? You have family there. You have peace. You have a world where warlords don't brand you with blood-tethers and drag you through swamps while assassins hunt you down!"

Elena dismounted in one fluid motion, dropping down into the shallow moss. She strode right to his stirrup, reaching up to seize the lapels of his leather armor, pulling him down until their faces were inches apart.

"Do you think I survived four years of graduate school, museum politics, and a city of nine million people just to let a blind oracle dictate my heart?" she demanded, her hazel eyes flashing with brilliant gold dawn-fire. "Look into my mind through this damned tether! Do you see fear? Do you see regret?"

Vaelen froze. He opened the sensory channel between them, and the flood of her emotions slammed into him: fierce devotion, unyielding desire, and the unshakable certainty that she would rather burn in Aethelgard at his side than live a thousand safe, hollow lifetimes without him.

"You idiot," she whispered, her voice softening as her thumbs traced his high, sharp cheekbones. "I spent my whole life feeling like a ghost among the living back on Earth. I was cataloging other people's histories because I had no life of my own. I never felt alive until you dragged me off that throne."

A ragged exhale tore from Vaelen's throat. Before she could say another word, he reached down, caught her beneath her arms, and hauled her up onto the saddle in front of him.

He crushed his mouth to hers.

The kiss was desperate, ravenous, and tasted of wild fen-wind and pure hunger. His tongue invaded her mouth, claiming her with possessive fury, while his hands tangled into her auburn hair, tilting her head back to deepen the angle. Elena gasped against his lips, wrapping her arms around his neck, arching into the hard heat of his body.

"You are mine, Elena," he growled against her bruised lips, his forehead resting against hers, his breath coming in jagged hitches. "If you try to go back through that mirror, I will tear down the sky to follow you."

"Good," she panted, her fingers digging into his hair. "Because I don't plan on letting you go."

He pulled her tight against his chest, kicking his beast into a gallop across the dark waters, heading toward the brooding stone ramparts of Iron Keep on the jagged northern cliffs.
