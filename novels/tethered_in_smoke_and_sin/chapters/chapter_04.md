# Chapter 4: The Bath of Ash and Silk

The private chambers of the High Commander occupied the entire crown of the Nightglass Citadel, and they were, in every sense of the word, a fortress built for an emperor of predators.

Massive beams of petrified black ironwood arched overhead, converging into a vaulted ceiling of dark glass that offered an unobstructed view of the bruised-plum sky and the two crescent moons. A roaring hearth dominated the eastern wall, crackling with violet flame that cast long, dancing shadows across thick wolf-fur pelts, antique battle standards from forgotten wars, and bookshelves heavy with leather-bound grimoires in languages Elena couldn’t begin to decipher.

In the center of the room sat a bed that could have easily accommodated four grown men—carved from dark volcanic stone, piled high with black velvet blankets, silver fox furs, and pillows of slate-colored silk.

"You smell of wet mud, fear, and mortality," Vaelen said bluntly, stripping off his heavy steel cuirass with an efficient, practiced shrug of his broad shoulders.

He tossed the armor onto a heavy oak bench, where the metal clattered with a dull, resonant ring. Underneath, he wore a simple tunic of charcoal linen that clung tightly to the powerful expanse of his chest and the tapered line of his waist. Without the armor, he was even more intimidating—raw, lethal muscle packed onto a tall, graceful frame, moving with the casual arrogance of a creature that knew no other living thing could best him in a fight.

Elena crossed her arms over her chest, trying to ignore the way the firelight caught the dark runes curling up the column of his throat, shifting like living smoke beneath his skin.

"Pardon me, your lordship," she shot back, her voice dripping with dry Chicago sarcasm. "I didn't realize that being violently sucked through a demonic portal into a bloodbath was supposed to leave me smelling like Chanel No. 5."

Vaelen paused, one dark eyebrow arching. "I have no idea what that word means, but your tongue remains dangerously sharp for someone whose neck snaps with three pounds of pressure."

"Try it and see what happens to your pulse, commander."

A low, wicked chuckle vibrated in his throat. He pointed a leather-braced forearm toward an arched doorway on the northern wall.

"Through there. The thermal springs feed directly from the caldera beneath the mountain. Wash yourself. The High King holds evening court in two hours, and I will not present my newly tethered companion looking like a gutter rat."

"I am not your companion," Elena snapped, but her body betrayed her. Her shoulder was throbbing, her muscles ached from the violent fall, and the scent drifting from the open archway—warm steam, crushed lavender, and night-blooming jasmine—was overwhelmingly seductive.

"Remember the distance," Vaelen warned, leaning against the heavy oak table and pouring a goblet of deep garnet wine. "Fifty paces. If you try to slip through the balcony or find a secret stair, your lungs will collapse before you reach the outer landing."

"Don't worry," Elena muttered, glaring at him as she backed toward the bathchamber. "I have zero desire to feel your heart exploding in my chest again."

She turned and slipped through the archway, closing the heavy wooden door behind her with a solid thud.

The bathchamber was larger than her entire apartment on Lincoln Avenue.

Carved entirely from a single vein of polished black granite, the room was dominated by a circular, sunken pool fed by two sculpted dragon heads that poured steaming, crystal-clear water from their open jaws. Warm mist hung in the air like a veil of spun silk. Around the perimeter of the pool, glass globes filled with bioluminescent violet moss cast a soft, ethereal glow over stacks of fluffy charcoal towels, crystal decanters filled with golden bath oils, and slabs of fragrant, milled soap.

Elena leaned her back against the door, closing her eyes as she exhaled a breath she felt like she’d been holding since two in the morning.

"Okay," she whispered, her voice trembling slightly in the quiet mist. "You're alive. You're in some kind of gothic fever dream with a murderous, six-foot-four supermodel warlord who shares your heartbeat. Just... one step at a time."

She peeled off her dirty gray cardigan, letting it drop to the stone floor. Next came her sneakers and socks, then her denim jeans. Her fingers hesitated at the hem of her cotton t-shirt. She looked down at her right wrist.

The golden runes were still there, glowing with a soft, persistent warmth just beneath the skin. When she touched them, she could feel a faint, rhythmic hum—a physical echo of Vaelen's calm, measured heartbeat filtering into her nervous system.

It was terrifying. And intoxicating.

Elena pulled off her shirt and undergarments, leaving the remnants of the modern world in a heap on the floor, and stepped toward the pool.

Her toes dipped into the water. It was perfectly, exquisitely hot.

She walked down the smooth granite steps until the water rose over her hips, her waist, and enveloped her shoulders. A long, shuddering sigh escaped her lips as the heat sank into her bruised muscles, drawing out the bone-deep chill of the crossing. She leaned her head back against the edge of the rim, closed her eyes, and let the fragrant steam envelop her senses.

For ten blissful minutes, there was only the sound of cascading water and the steady, comforting beat of two hearts sharing a rhythm. She closed her eyes and let her mind drift, washing away the dirt of the Chicago museum floor and the blood of the dead assassin.

Then, the heavy latch on the chamber door clicked.

Elena’s eyes snapped open.

The door swung inward, and Vaelen Thorne stepped into the bathchamber.

He had removed his boots and his shirt, wearing only loose, slate-gray linen trousers that hung low on his hips. In his hand, he carried a small ceramic jar of dark blue glass.

Elena gasped, immediately sinking lower into the water until only her collarbones and head were visible, her arms wrapping protectively across her chest.

"What the hell do you think you're doing?" she demanded, her cheeks flaming hotter than the thermal water. "Get out! Have you ever heard of knocking?"

Vaelen didn't blink. His silver eyes swept across the steaming surface of the pool, lingering on her bare shoulders, the droplets of water glistening on her throat, and the dark auburn waves floating like silk around her chest.

He knelt on the edge of the granite pool, unscrewing the lid of the blue jar. The scent of wintergreen, camphor, and crushed lotus drifted into the steam.

"Come here," he commanded softly.

Elena didn't move. She glared at him through the rising mist. "I can put on my own cream, thank you."

Vaelen sighed—a sound of supreme, aristocratic impatience. "You cannot reach your shoulder blade without dislocating your arm, Vance. And the star-balm must be worked into the muscle with pressure. Come to the edge, or I will step into this water and drag you to it."

The intensity in his molten silver eyes left no doubt that he meant every single syllable.

"This will sting for half a heartbeat," he murmured.

Elena’s head tilted forward, a breathy moan slipping past her lips before she could stop it.

Behind her, she felt Vaelen’s breathing hitch.

His hand drifted slowly downward, tracing the curve of her spine, down past the bruised shoulder, coming to rest at the hollow of her lower back just at the waterline.

Elena turned her head slowly, looking over her shoulder.

Their faces were inches apart.

His silver eyes were dilated, dark and ravenous, looking at her lips as if he were starving and she were a banquet. She could see the tiny flecks of violet in his irises, could count the individual dark lashes, could see the pulse hammering against the dark runes along his neck.

"Dry yourself," he said, his voice flat, though his chest still rose and fell in rapid breaths. "I left a gown on the dressing table. We leave for the Great Court in twenty minutes."

He turned on his heel and strode from the chamber, the heavy door clicking shut behind him.

Elena sank back into the hot water, burying her face in her hands, her skin still burning where his fingers had touched her.

She was in so much trouble.
