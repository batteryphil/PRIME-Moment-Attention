# Chapter 15: Severance

Pain was not a concept Elena had ever truly understood until now.

She had broken a wrist tumbling down the bleachers in middle school; she had suffered through three-day migraines in graduate school, staring into microfilm readers under humming fluorescent bulbs. But this was not physical trauma. This was spiritual amputation.

The Blood-Tether was pulling at the very fibers of her soul.

Every second that stretched between her and Vaelen felt like barbed copper wire winding tighter and tighter around her heart, threatening to saw through muscle and bone. Her lungs burned with every breath, each inhalation tasting of copper and salt. Black spots danced before her eyes, and a persistent, high-pitched whine rang in her ears, vibrating to the frantic, dying frequency of Vaelen’s distant heartbeat.

*Thump... thump... thump...*

It was sluggish. Weak. Straining against poison and blunt force.

"Hold on, Vaelen," Elena whispered through blood-flecked teeth, her forehead pressed against the freezing, slime-coated granite wall. "Don't you dare die on me, you stubborn bastard."

She was suspended in the Drowned Cells—the lowest tier of Iron Keep, carved deep into the submerged sea crags below sea level. Freezing, brackish water swirled around her thighs, rising and falling with the swell of the northern tides outside. Heavy alchemical silver cuffs bound her wrists to thick iron eyebolts sunk into the stone above her head.

The silver burned like dry ice against her skin, stifling her dawn-fire into dull embers that flickered weakly beneath her fingernails.

Two guards stood on the raised wooden catwalk fifteen feet above the water level, drinking from horn flasks and watching her with bored indifference.

"Quiet down, mortal," one shouted down, his voice echoing off the dripping stone arches. "Brand’s courier rode out for the capital an hour ago. Malakor’s royal transport will be here by midnight. Enjoy the sea water while you can. Once you hit the Black Spire, they'll drain every drop of your juice into the King's chalice."

Elena didn't answer. She kept her head down, allowing her tangled auburn hair to curtain her face, concealing the frantic work of her fingers.

She was not just a captive; she was an antiquities archivist who had spent five years restoring delicate Renaissance lockboxes and ancient Babylonian gear-works with picks thinner than pine needles.

Tucked into the thick weave of her hair braid was a three-inch steel hair-pin—forged from modern, carbon-treated Chicago steel. Not Aethelgard iron, not enchanted silver. Just hard, resilient, tempered alloy.

Her fingers were numb from the frigid salt water and trembling from the crushing pull of the tether, but she forced them to obey. Sliding the steel pin into the keyway of the left silver cuff, she felt the internal brass tumblers.

*Click.*

One.

The tether seized in her chest, a violent spasm that nearly made her vomit sea water. Blood welled in her throat, spilling over her bottom lip. She swallowed hard, locking her teeth together, refusing to scream. She had to stay silent.

Through the tether, she closed her eyes and pushed her consciousness along the straining, agonizing thread of violet smoke.

*I am here, Vaelen,* she sent into the void of the bond, pouring every shred of her defiance, every ounce of her love, and the memory of his lips against her skin into the connection. *I am alive. Come and get me.*

***

Three levels above, in the blood-smeared banquet hall, General Brand wiped the spatter of Vaelen's blood from his cheek with a linen napkin.

Vaelen lay face down in the center of the hall, motionless. A pool of dark, viscous blood spread outward from beneath his chest, soaking into the cracks between the flagstones. The heavy broadsword lay shattered beside his outstretched right hand.

Four guards stood over him with drawn iron-tipped spears, their faces pale despite their victory.

"Is the warlord dead?" one soldier whispered, his spear trembling.

"He took enough null-bane to drop a shadow-drake," Brand scoffed, nudging Vaelen’s limp shoulder with the steel toe of his boot. "And with the girl locked two hundred paces below in the drowned vault, the severance alone would hemorrhage the brain of an elder fae. Bind his limbs in null-chains. Malakor wants his brother's head preserved in honey."

A guard knelt with heavy iron manacles, reaching for Vaelen’s limp wrist.

The guard’s fingers never made contact.

A voice—soft, clear, and vibrating directly inside the marrow of Vaelen's skull—shattered the suffocating blackness of the poison.

*I am alive. Come and get me.*

The blood pool on the floor stopped spreading.

It began to ripple.

Then, the liquid black blood began to rise, defying gravity, floating in small, suspended spheres of dark starlight.

"What in the hells..." the kneeling guard stammered, scrambling backward on his elbows.

The torch flames around the perimeter of the hall turned from orange to an eerie, spectral violet. The heat in the room vanished in a fraction of a second, replaced by an arctic, abyssal chill so intense that frost bloomed instantly across the banquet table and frozen breath puffed from the soldiers’ mouths.

Brand took two hurried steps back, his eyes widening in primal horror. "Spears! Spear him through the spine! Now!"

The guards lunged forward, thrusting four heavy iron spears down into Vaelen’s unarmored back.

The steel blades struck... and dissolved into mist.

Vaelen’s body did not bleed. It melted into a swirling vortex of pitch-black shadows and roaring, ravenous darkness.

From the heart of the vortex, a hand shot out—pale, covered in glowing violet runes that burned with the fury of a dying star. The hand caught the throat of the nearest soldier. With a sickening crunch that echoed off the stone rafters, the man's neck snapped like dry kindling.

Vaelen rose.

He did not look like a man anymore. He looked like the primordial nightmare the ancient poets had warned mortals about.

His raven hair whipped around his head in an invisible gale. His silver eyes were completely eclipsed by liquid obsidian, weeping streams of pure, condensed shadow down his razor-sharp cheekbones. The null-bane in his veins had been completely incinerated, burned away by the raw, apocalyptic surge of the Eclipse Bond.

"You touched her," Vaelen spoke.

The sound of his voice did not come from his mouth; it reverberated from the stone walls, the ceiling, the floor, and the shadows pooling in the corners of their eyes.

"Vaelen... wait!" Brand stammered, raising his broadsword with two shaking hands. "We were brothers! In the trenches of the Black River!"

"You touched my Queen," Vaelen whispered.

The shadows exploded outward.

Tentacles of living darkness, sharper than obsidian scythes, erupted from the walls. The mirror-enameled plates on the soldiers’ armor did not reflect their faces; they reflected grinning demons that reached through the glass and dragged three screaming guards bodily into the mirrors, shattering the frames in showers of bloody silver shards.

A dozen men screamed in unison as the shadows flayed them where they stood. Armor crumpled like parchment; iron weapons shattered like brittle ice. In less than twenty seconds, the great hall was reduced to a slaughterhouse of severed steel and silent crimson ruin.

Brand fell to his knees, his broadsword clattering to the floor, his face pale as curdled milk.

Vaelen stepped through the carnage, his boots not making a sound on the bloodstained floor. In his right hand, a blade of solidified shadow hummed with terrifying, high-frequency energy.

He stopped in front of Brand, towering over the cowering general. With his left hand, Vaelen reached down, catching Brand by the throat and lifting the two-hundred-pound warrior three feet off the ground with a single arm.

"Where is the star-metal?" Vaelen’s voice was an arctic blade against Brand’s eardrum.

"The... the lower vault..." Brand choked out, his heels drumming helplessly against Vaelen’s armored chest. "Behind the drowned cells... the key is on my belt..."

"And Elena?"

"Alive... she's still alive... by the Black Sun, Vaelen, spare me!"

Vaelen’s obsidian eyes stared into Brand's soul without a flicker of mercy.

"You should have chosen the sun, brother," Vaelen said softly.

With a flick of his wrist, the shadow blade plunged through Brand’s throat, pinning him to the stone pillar behind him.

Vaelen did not watch the man die. He ripped the heavy iron ring of keys from Brand’s belt, scooped up a fallen broadsword from the floor, and turned his gaze toward the dark, yawning stairwell leading down into the abyss.

His chest burned with the agonizing pull of the tether, but the pain was no longer a weakness.

It was a beacon.

He plunged down into the darkness, moving at the speed of smoke, slaughtering anything that dared stand between him and the woman who owned his soul.


In the freezing depths of the drowned cell, the seawater continued to rise, creeping past Elena’s hips to lap against the curve of her ribs.

Her teeth were chattering so violently that she bit her tongue, tasting copper. The agony of the severed tether was a living, ravenous monster inside her chest, gnawing at her lungs with every ragged breath she drew. The distance was tearing at the very fabric of her soul, threatening to unravel her consciousness into the dark.

To keep herself from fainting—to keep from letting the freezing water drag her down into unconsciousness—she began to sing.

It was a soft, trembling melody: an ancient Czech folk song her grandmother had sung to her when she had scarlet fever as a child in Chicago. A song about a golden sparrow that flew across three oceans to find the mountain of the sleeping king.

*"Zasviť mi, ty slunko zlaté..."* she whispered through blue, shivering lips. *"Shine for me, golden sun... lead my lover through the dark..."*

With every verse, she focused all her willpower into her numb, bleeding fingers, guiding the steel hairpin into the second silver tumbler.

*Click.*

Above her, the foundation stones of the castle groaned.

Far in the distance, through the stone vaults, she heard the muffled, concussive detonation of iron bulkheads being torn apart, followed by the terrifying, unearthly screams of dying soldiers.

Elena smiled through her tears, blood dripping from her split lip into the rising sea brine.

"He's coming," she whispered to the shadows. "He's coming to kill you all."


As Vaelen stalked through the lower corridors of Iron Keep, the stone walls seemed to part before his living shadow.

Every door, every iron gate that barred his passage was shattered by scythes of pure, condensed darkness. Traitorous guards who dared raise a pike against him were disarmed and hurled across the flagstones before they could even draw breath to scream.

His senses were locked with absolute precision onto the golden thread of the Blood-Tether vibrating in his chest.

With every footstep that brought him closer to the drowned cells, the suffocating agony in his lungs lessened, replaced by a roaring, hungry adrenaline that fueled his speed. He could hear her heartbeat. He could feel her shivering in the freezing seawater. He could hear the soft, haunting melody of the Czech lullaby she was whispering through her bruised lips to keep herself conscious.

*"Zasviť mi, ty slunko zlaté..."*

The sound of her voice echoing inside his skull broke what little mercy remained in his soul.

"I am coming, Elena," he growled aloud, his silver eyes blazing with terrifying fury as he kicked the final iron bulkhead off its hinges. "Hold on to me!"


The subterranean corridors shook as Vaelen’s wrath carved through the final line of defense.

He did not pause to wipe the blood from his brow. His silver eyes burned with the singular, unshakeable directive of his soul: reach Elena, tear down the cage, and burn anyone who dared stand in their way.

The reckoning had arrived.
