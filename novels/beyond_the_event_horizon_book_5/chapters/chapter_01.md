# Chapter 1: The Dead Star System


Leo Mercer groaned as the sudden transition slammed him back against his seat harness. Stars whipped by the cockpit canopy in a kaleidoscope of white light—then steadied into the familiar constellations of home. "Sol," he breathed.

But something was wrong. The system felt... lifeless. No terrestrial planets glowed softly in orbit nor did asteroid fields clutter the path. Only black void stretched out as far as his viewports reached—and not a single other ship or station, abandoned relic or working orbital platform, broke the sterile expanse.

"Astrid?" His call for confirmation to Captain Vex Ross came out more raspy than intended after the hyperjump stresses. Shifting uncomfortably, he peeled his face away from the co-pilot's stick, sweat beading his forehead despite the chilled confines of the Marauder's bridge. A wave of disorientation lingered even as his vision acclimated from the stroboscopic glare of re-entry.

"We're here," her deep voice crackled over comms, echoing slightly as if filtered through hullmetal. "Deadspace ahead."

The ship drifted slowly toward that ominous term, each kilometer measured as excruciating minutes beneath the unforgiving gaze of the crimson stars. Outside, Leo watched his past fade into the distance - Earth an unassuming blue dot lost among the inky darkness of Sol's outer reaches.

His gut clenched. This operation, this one shot at redemption, hung by a thread over an uncaring cosmos. He'd been tasked with piloting salvaged Century Drive technology to Astrid and her raider crew for the promise of a hefty payday and a chance to reclaim scientific honor besmirched by decades of military cover-ups. So far, nothing suggested they were going to get any closer to those goals nestled in their battered starship.

"What's the holdup?" Leo asked, trying to keep his frustration at bay. "Thrusters show nominal."

"Dunno yet." Astrid's tone brooded. "The drive's still stabilizing. Can't risk pushing too hard and blowing a sealshaft again."

Leo bit back a sharp retort about how a few hours ago she'd happily cheer as they coasted down Saturn's rings like a madman. Now their progress lagged what could only be called a crawl, hurtling toward certain ruin at a glacial pace.

In the cramped silence that followed, mere seconds crept by like eternities. The air thickened around the tiny flightdeck as claustrophobia seeped in. No gentle whispers of ion wind caressed the hull, no comforting hum of fusion reactors thrummed through Leo's bones—the normally reassuring rhythms absent, leaving only stately emptiness punctuated by static hiss on the comm.

"Okay." Astrid spoke slowly, her grip tightening on her command chair armrests. "Let's spin up active sensors. See what we're facing..."

Her hand fluttered toward the data console, then froze mid-motion. Eyes widening, a feral grin stretching across her face turned to razor claws behind her scarred helmet visor.

"It's there," she said, almost purring, the word dripping with excitement and dark menace. Inwardly, he flinched but kept his emotions masked, studying her reaction closely.

An icy tremor rippled down Leo's spine as Astrid leaned forward, peering intently at the scanner feeds. They might have escaped detection during re-entry, but whatever monstrous alien artifact—or weapon—inhabited the dead zone waited, patiently.

The static hiss on the comm speakers grated against Leo's eardrums, an unnerving echo in the eerie silence of the Starlight Marauder's crippled flightdeck. He watched warily as Astrid's feral grin stretched her lips back, baring gleaming white teeth amidst the chiseled features framed by her scarred helmet. 

A shiver crept up Leo's spine at the unhinged glee behind Vex's masked eyes. This wasn't the focused intensity he was accustomed to from military ops commanders - this was something closer to a predator sensing fresh meat crossing its path.

"Whoa, hold up," Leo intervened, reaching out urgently to grasp one of Astrid's armored arms before she could react impulsively. His calloused palms pressed into the flexible material, fingers digging in firmly against the steel beneath. She jerked slightly at his sudden contact, turning to him with an arctic glare. 

Leo held firm, meeting her gaze unflinchingly. Even beneath multiple layers of Kevlar-alloy composite and environmental shielding, his fingertips detected the underlying tension - muscles coiled, ready to strike at the first excuse.

"No grandstanding needed," he maintained, voice low and steady as he carefully released Astrid's arm, taking a step back. "We both know where it ends if we blunder into the kill zone blind. So let's talk strategy."

Across from him, Astrid cocked her head, considering his words. A soft hiss escaped her lungs, echoing the stale breathable atmosphere. Finally, the pirate captain leaned back, arms draping over the command chair's padded rests. A sly smile played across her features — all business and lethal calculation now.

"Don't get me wrong, Commando. I'm just as eager to get our hands on some... new playthings. But we need a clearer picture before leaping in guns blazing — even for you types."

She eyed him critically, taking note of his posture braced against the ship's violent potential and assessing whether his expression gave away any sign of faltering resolve under the ominous circumstances.

"You stick with running my scans," Astrid decreed, pointing at the sensor console. "If anything slips through that, it'll be on your watch, pal. But trust me, once I've plotted our optimal vector to grab whatever tech treasures those ancient hussies left behind..." 

Her smile sharpened, hungry anticipation kindling like wildfire behind an expanse of scar tissue. "...we might actually take home enough gear to outfit half of Corvus Cluster before dealing with would-be collectors breathing down our necks."

That sent a jolt down Leo's vertebrae. Piracy was a brutal game when lives weren't on the line, but he'd be lying if admitting how enticing it sounded as a way to secure their salvation, their future - especially given some of the shady backroom deals done within legitimate corporations. 

"We can discuss the details later," he said smoothly, waving off the implications for now.

"Now, I think your crew's earned some downtime while we figure things out? Maybe you've got some old Earth tapes stashed away?"

In this confined space hemmed in by an apocalyptic dead star system — teetering between salvation or certain annihilation — every scrap of levity shouldered heavy responsibilities. Still, Leo couldn't deny the flickers of camaraderie he felt towards Astrid 'Vex' Ross and her ragtag group of space scoundrels - perhaps because they were all in dire enough straits to make petty differences irrelevant.

✦ ✦ ✦

Captain Vex slumped back into her throne, arms crossed atop the navigation console, eyes narrowing at the stars streaming past outside the curved viewport. "It's too quiet," she grumbled against her shoulder-mounted comm mic. The comms officer, Rynne Quasar, just gave Vex an unamused look over her shoulder. The corsair captain snorted. "Not... you," she clarified, voice wry. "The universe. Should be riddled with picket ships, marines swooping down to 'save the day'. Fucking corporate bullshit."

Rynne made a derisive noise, rolling her eyes heavenward before focusing again on the various readouts scrolling across her own station screens. Vex turned toward where Commander Leo Mercer loafed sprawled across an acceleration couch in the corner, feet propped up against the wall beside him. One knee absently bounced, betraying barely contained energy. She raised an eyebrow at his antics.

"Can't sit still either?" she teased, lips curling into a smirk.

Mercer rolled onto his side and fixed her with a grin. "Too much adrenaline still pumping after our little 'expedition'." His smile was lopsided, tired, but genuine. There was something about the way his dark eyelashes framed bloodshot eyes that made Vex feel strangely soft.

Just then, Chirag Suresh, their chief engineer, stumbled into the room, nearly crashing into a support strut and cursing the limited headspace. "Cap'n –" he panted, looking frazzled. "Systems check reveals significant issues with the hull plating around the fusion engines. Sustainin' supercruise velocities too long risks catastrophic meltdowns anytime soon."

"And that means our glorious treasure hunt is puttin' us in the red zones fast," Vex stated bluntly, shoulders tensing. "Leo, come with me and Chirag to assess our options for repairs."

Nodding, Mercer leapt lithely to his feet before sauntering over to join them near the central lift. They stepped inside together in silence once the others cleared out to get to work on other priorities. When the doors slid shut, leaving them alone in an intimate carriage, Vex pivoted to face her companion.

"There's gonna be tough decisions ahead," she said, gauging his reaction intently. "Maybe even ones nobody wants to hear, and sure as hell don't want us to do."

"What sort of choices?" Leo probed warily, one hand rising to rest on the cold metal railing running along the walls opposite them as the elevator began its descent toward engineering decks.

"We could turn south, head for the nearest safe docks to patch us up proper, maybe find new backers for some legit salvage operations..." Vex's voice trailed off, eyes distant, contemplating the paths not taken. Then she focused sharply back on Mercer, expression stern. "Or we play it risky – try to pilfer what resources we need during a quick turnaround somewhere the corporations won't dare enforce their property rights."

There was an unmistakable implication hanging in the air: the choice was life or freedom. To opt for repairs meant surrendering Starlight Marauder's ability to operate independently, potentially throwing Vex's livelihood and the lives of her crew into the jaws of corporate greed. Pivoting in the opposite direction could mean dodging bullets and avoiding legal entanglements, but risked putting them squarely in the crosshairs of rival pirates aiming to exploit the lucrative black market.

"What will it be, Commander?" she asked quietly. "Honor the code of corporate contract law..."

✦ ✦ ✦

The elevator car lurched to a halt with a decisive hydraulic thud, silencing the hum of servos and hydraulics momentarily. Outside the viewport, segmented ledgers of thickened graphite gave way to gleaming steel plating - they were crossing into Engineering now, the pulsing heart of the Starlight Marauder. Vex stepped out first, her boots clacking on the compartment's ceramic floor tiles, the stench of burnt circuitry and scorched insulation tangling with the acrid reek of ozone in the cramped corridor. 

Leo followed close behind, his nostrils flared against the harsh industrial scent assaulting them both. He paused a moment to let his eyes adjust to the dimmer lighting, the only other occupants being several motionless maintenance drones crouched like mechanical geckos over scattered electronics components laid out on workbenches.

"So, Commander," Vex repeated her earlier query, sidelong glance darting between him and the console displaying schematics of the damaged propulsion system, "what's this gonna cost us? In terms of credits, and also..." Her pause carried freighted undertones. "...morality?"

Leo let out a humorless chuckle at that. "You're really testing my philosophical mettle today, aren't you Captain? Alright, let me lay it out. Legally, this isn't just criminal piracy; it's full-fledged treason if word gets out the Starlight Marauder attacked Enkei Industries' heavy cruiser above Orion's Gate."

Vex snorted derisively, hands resting on the hips of her tactical suit. "Details, details..."

"Details that could get me executed faster than you can say 'pirate bounty'," Leo retorted dryly. He paced over to examine the diagrams more closely, tracing calloused fingers over projected stress lines etched across the reactor containment fields like gossamer fractures in a diamond. "As for the price tag...replacing that entire shield array alone, assuming I can even scrounge parts, will require at minimum double-digit millions. That doesn't even address the labor costs."

"We'll make do," Vex said curtly. "My guys are as talented with salvaging parts as hitting targets. Just gotta hustle harder, that's all."

Leo looked back at her skeptically. "And avoid getting your ship blown out of the sky every five klicks? Yeah, no, I think my estimate stands."

Her lips curled into a menacing half-smile. "Watch it, spaceman. You ain't seen real hustle until you've been marooned on a desert moon without hope for months on end. My people have grown creative with making do."

Leo held up his hands in mock surrender. "Alright, alright! No further disrespect intended regarding your gritty underdog crew's resourcefulness!"

"Well see to it you keep that promise," Vex warned gravely. She was already striding away down the catwalk, boots echoing loudly against the metallic decking as she headed for the armory to gear up for rounds inspecting the wounded vessel.

Leo sighed heavily, shoulders sagging under the weight of conflicting loyalties and looming realities of survival in this unforgiving cosmos. Forcing a final grin onto his chiseled face, he fell in step beside Vex, matching her purposeful pace. He'd tackle one problem at a time. First things first, he needed to convince Astrid Vex Ross that walking the line between legitimate salvage and outright theft wasn't the best path forward for either of them...or for the battered Starlight Marauder currently drifting precariously through the void between repair options and outlaw territories waiting to swallow them whole.
