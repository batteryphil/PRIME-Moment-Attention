# Chapter 2: Ruins of the Orbital Ring

As they strode along the damaged spines of Mars' orbital elevators, the sickening crunch of pulverized alloy beneath each footstep echoed in the near-silence of vacuum. Leo's gloved hand brushed Vex's shoulder in passing, sparking a fleeting jolt of static electricity that sent a blue arc dancing along their contact points before dissipating into the chill metal air rushing past.

"Commander?" A faint hiss of atmosphere leaked into the life support suit, betraying the presence of an unseen spacer hovering nearby amidst the jagged debris field.

"Yeah?" Leo replied gruffly.

"We've got some bad news about the foremast plasma vents. They're breached, leaking containment gas, and superheating the surrounding hull plating. We need to reroute coolant immediately or those vents'll blow entirely and rip open a hole big enough to suck in Taurids' entire asteroid belt."

Leo's brow furrowed beneath his helmet's visor. "How long before we start seeing pressure drops and primary seals fail?"

The spacer glanced at his chronometer. "Forty-eight minutes. Tops."

"You've got twenty-four hours to get those repairs done," Vex called over the comms system without breaking stride. "If not, we dump what cargo we can't replace and cut our losses—sell the Marauder for scrap if worse comes to worst."

"No deals yet!" Leo protested, scowling at her profile. "I had that cargo appraised before it went to auction. It's worth far more than whatever these rust bucket yards want for her hunk o' junk."

"Then raise the price, spaceman," Vex retorted coldly. "Or find us a buyer who'll front the cash we need for some proper downtime in civilized space so our techs can actually breathe while patching these holes and calibrating thrusters."

"I'm working on it," Leo snapped irritably.

They rounded a twisted girder archway, where a sprawling mottled plain of crumpled armor plating stretched off towards a massive, crater-pitted asteroid wheel hung in orbit like some ancient spaceborne megalith. Beyond lay vast, star-studded tracts of nothingness punctuated only by the distant orange dot of Earth.

Leo squinted at the horizon, tracing their course across the ruined ring system using landmarks he'd mentally charted during the desperate blind flight in which they managed to leap-frog from falling chunks of Mars's defunct megascale satellite complex when the rest of it went catastrophically to pieces. 

Their damaged freighter hung motionless within targeting locks installed by the AI overseeing its automated defense grid, a laser battery built to blast chunks of rogue asteroids into smaller, harmless bits designed to rain harmlessly onto low-orbit firebreak lanes serving as natural conduits for human civilization. But the Starlight Marauder didn't rate high on any sentient or AI radar, a mere fleck lost amidst endless light-minutes of emptiness.

With each rotation of the dreadnaught-sized battlestation orbiting lazily in the distance ahead, another opportunity slipped by for them to dock, refill fuel tanks, swap out critical components, and perhaps renegotiate some modicum of legitimacy amongst the loose coalition of factions operating out here beyond the reaches of the U.N.-controlled outer Rim.

But this time around, the Marauder would require significant alterations to her compromised structure just to stabilize in geosync and await processing procedures—albeit much cheaper relative to other planetary ports where safety protocols kept prices artificially high for pirates running on extremely tight margins.

Leo craned his neck to gaze past Vex at the vast, glinting metal ring unfolding before them. A ghostly gossamer filigree anchored by pockmarked asteroid masses swarmed with activity—a testament to humanity's relentless drive to expand and settle the stars. His fingers tightened reflexively on the armrests, muscles coiling like spring-loaded catapults beneath lean bulk.

"Not exactly what I had in mind when I agreed to hitch a ride out here," he remarked dryly, forcing the tightness from his jaw to maintain the veneer of detachment.

Vex snorted derisively without turning, one bronzed hand idly rubbing the stubbled cheek that now marked her visage. "Save your breath, Leo. Not like you had beaucoup options after that lovely fireworks display back at Mars." Her voice held a note of biting satisfaction, reminding him she wasn't about to let the near-death experience go unbanked. 

He bristled but bit back a retort, knowing full well the precarious position they were in. As their ship limped towards repair and resupply opportunities that now felt tantalizingly within reach, he found himself torn between his ingrained code—where duty rigidly superseded self-preservation—and Vex's more flexible approach forged in the unforgiving crucible of constant danger.

The ring grew imperceptibly larger, promising answers to myriad questions. What was their current trajectory within this network of stations, habitats, and satellites? How long until they could effect repairs and bolster their defenses against threats lurking unseen beneath the guise of legitimate trade and research? More urgently, how did Vex intend to monetize their new position while minimizing risks to themselves?

"What's our play?" Leo asked bluntly, finally breaking the prolonged silence.

Vex turned, eyes glittering with predatory intensity. She rose and strode across the cramped bridge deck, hips swaying fluidly even in the gravity of the crippled ship. Pausing before him, hands braced on polished durasteel, she leaned in close enough that he could feel the heat radiating off her skin. For a moment, her closeness hijacked his train of thought entirely.

"We'll slip into the shadow of Ymir IV as cover," she murmured, her hot breath ghosting his ear. "Then we're going hunting."

Before he could respond, she pivoted away and resumed her seat, leaving him fumbling to regain traction on the conversation.

"Hunting?" He frowned, struggling to keep pace with her tactics. "What do you mean?"

"With scrap, mostly"—she waved a dismissive hand—"and maybe a juicy cargo transport ripe for the picking. Whatever we can scrounge up without drawing attention our way too soon. Once we've got fresh munitions, a few parts to get us moving again...then we make the hit on the big prize."

"The 'big prize'?" Suspicion and curiosity battled for dominance in his expression. Vex's cryptic hints usually preceded some audacious scheme neither of them fully understood when they embarked upon it, relying on adrenaline and sheer cunning to carry them through.

She smiled enigmatically, black lips parting to reveal the sharp points of her teeth—a wolf baring fangs to a potential meal. "Let's just say I have a buyer looking to acquire certain unique artifacts rumored to be stashed on one of these baby moonlets." With a flourish of her wrist, she conjured an overlay projecting a garish holo-map filled with various celestial bodies orbiting a gas giant. Needle-thin lines snaked out indicating potential vectors.

✦ ✦ ✦

As the Starlight Marauder dipped toward its quarry, an old abandoned station lurking like a ghost among debris-laden rings, Vex's fingers danced over her console, calibrating the ship's sensors in concert with her intuition cultivated by years navigating the ever-shifting ballet of politics and plunder in far frontier regions of human civilization's reach.

"This place looks a mess," Mercer commented, peering at the holographic display with a critical eye. His gaze flicked to the navigation data flashing back to Vex, then focused critically on their projected trajectory. "Any reason we need to be this reckless? We look more harebrained than hungry if anyone's watching remotely..."

"The thrill of the hunt, my friend," Vex replied, a devilish glint sparking in her eyes even as she maintained a steady grip on the control yoke. "Besides, nobody seems to care much about garbage stations these days unless there's something valuable packed inside the rustbin. We catch 'em mapping, we slip through quick, grab what we want—"

"And run like hell before someone decides to ask questions." Mercer finished the sentence dryly, still not sold on Vex's plan but well aware he had less input than when he first signed onto her crew. His eyes drifted to the gunwales, where maintenance suits and salvaged gear tumbled in the wake like unwanted offspring, occasionally reflecting a stray starlight before disappearing back into the void.

Vex ignored his skepticism for the moment, too engrossed examining scan data streaming back from probes deployed by Marauder's bow mount scanners. Something caught her attention—the faint trace signature she'd hoped to uncover in the derelict's innards...

"Evasive," she snapped aloud just as a shrill alert tone burst through the compartment's speakers. The ship reacted instantaneously, a thrum running through its alloy bones as grav-plates kicked to full tilt, pulling the vessel sharply upward and sideways into a corkscrew maneuver meant to confuse rather than outrun whatever closing weapons had put them in their predicament.

Overhead lights strobed, shadows shifting as metal groaned beneath the stress of evasive action while air hissed past sealed hull panels. For one suspended heartbeat, the universe narrowed for Mercer to the frantic dance of instruments across consoles surrounding them like a mechanical mandala: temperature and vibration gauges straining redline, inertial dampeners cycling unevenly due to unseen damage. But Vex's hands remained steady atop her own controls.

"I don't think whoever shot at us gave a damn about subtlety," Mercer grunted as another klaxon wailed, painting his face with harsh blue-green light. He couldn't see the would-be assassin clearly—just a streak of white, expanding rapidly within the viewport's circle—but that didn't stop him from feeling a visceral unease pooling in his gut mixed with anger at their brazen attacker...

The world seemed to snap around them as the Starlight Marauder lunged violently to starboard, centrifugal force tugging commandingly at limbs and guts alike despite the inertial compensators. Metal screamed, protesting the brutal maneuvers they undertook to escape the deadly pursuit – or so Mercer prayed was the case. A glance at Vex told him nothing. Her eyes were fixed intently on the flickering displays casting eerie neon hues across her ashen features.

Suddenly, instruments began shedding data points like fallen dominoes and Vex cursed a litany of expletives only the most ill-mannered courtesans might employ. "Looks like our new friends managed a lucky hit!" She gestured wildly at several shattered screens with a gloved hand. "Portside fusion core suffered a minor breach! We're bleeding plasma –"

"But we'll make it?" Mercer interjected through gritted teeth, struggling against the unyielding grip of his harness as he sought to reach forward, to somehow staunch what she feared was a mortal wound.

Vex barked out an inelegant laugh. A spark of defiance in her eyes, she turned to lean in close, voice low and urgent. "Never fear, my dear mercenary – this husk has known worse." Leaning back again, she surveyed the chaos blossoming across control interfaces like a painter wielding wild abandon. "Besides, I know these systems better'n most priests know their faith."

With a whine of stressed servos, the ship leveled out briefly before diving steeply into what Mercer assumed was a desperate deceleration burn. The gee-forces hammered cruelly into them, driving blood down toward their cores and leaching oxygen from the chamber. His vision darkened at the edges, stars bursting into glittering swarms behind his eyes as consciousness threatened to fold under the oppressive press of acceleration. Through the haze he glimpsed Vex hunched over her console, her hair a windswept wild halo framing the taut lines of concentration twisting her face – every muscle coiled tight as a spring ready to launch.

The silence following, blessed and profound, held no consolation. Not yet having breathed in the acrid tang of scorched circuits nor heard the sickening wet belch of a compromised atmosphere sealing itself away ever after, but something deeper lurked – fear of what lay beyond that event horizon of velocity and velocity alone could part. The quiet stretched into an agonizing eternity during which Mercer lost himself pondering if death waited patiently for them mere nanoseconds hence when suddenly, without transition, he was slapped awake by the staccato rasp of proximity alarms demanding notice. Blinking rapidly to adjust to the cacophony of urgent warnings, he struggled to orient himself amidst the chaotic storm swirling beyond the ship's shields.

"What the hell..." Mercer trailed off mutely, mouth agape as Vex gasped in shock beside him even as her hands flew nimbly over her panel. Whatever threat they raced to avoid or pursue, whatever the nature of the enemy force seeking their demise, none compared in sheer scale and audacity to this.

It began some thousands of kilometers distant – far beyond range of the most ambitious fighters but growing rapidly as they probed closer. Emerging from a swirling nebula like a cancerous growth metastasizing across a fragile galaxy, titanic, armored spires flanked a colossal, serpentine bulk. And at its heart, pulsating incandescent, sat something monstrous, an eye that burned with cold, unfathomable light – a gateway to infinity carved through normalcy and anchored in the very fabric of spacetime.
