# Chapter 15: The Blind Flank

Leo Mercer felt his teeth rattle before the jolt even hit, a warning tremor echoing down spine and sinew to prepare him for the brutal crush sure to come. The sudden deceleration slammed his helmet face-first into the console—stars swam in his gaze as his forehead bounced off and snapped back again—and sent his stomach flying up into his lungs like an unruly balloon seeking escape.

He barely registered hearing his own strangled groan drowned out by the cacophony. Screams cut through the howling feedback that scoured eardrums, each voice pitched uniquely by stress and anguish and simple, terrified reflex—as if screaming somehow counteracted the merciless forces reshaping their lives molecule by brutal, bone-shaking molecule.

Amid this chaos, Leo's hand found purchase solely because Vex's iron grip refused release. He'd swear to Odin it was a miracle his fingers retained any dexterity whatsoever when his entire body moved as though encased in quick-set concrete, each twitch a Herculean effort.

A violent shake yanked him back from the console, nearly wrenching his shoulder out of its socket, as starlight skewed and strobed across his display. The cruiser careened wildly, inertial compensators straining to blunt the effects of the brutal Gs—Mercer clung white-knuckled to survival as the decking heaved beneath him like the surface of a storm-tossed sea. 

Through it all, Captain Ross never relinquished her hold on him. Though he couldn't turn his head to look, he sensed hers eyes glued to the displays. Calculating—the same detached focus that had served her so well thus far warred relentlessly with the telltale signs of concern creasing her features whenever her attention flicked back to him.

"We survived," she breathed when, mercifully, the Gs faded back into their usual easy drift. "But I won't thank you for that quite yet, Commander."

Leo managed a pained laugh and swallowed against the lingering ache in his jaw. "Glad... we're in concordance on that one."

Reaching up gingerly, Mercer rubbed at his forehead, the impact bruisingly sore despite the layers shielding him. A trickle of blood leaked from a minuscule abrasion, making its sluggish way past the seal where visor joined faceplate.

"You're bleeding."

"Yeah, I noticed." He gave her a crooked half-smile and winced. "That's what happens when a former test pilot tries to crash-test a capital ship during celestial evasive maneuvers."

"This isn't over," Vex said curtly, even as she guided his hand away from the wound with an almost maternal instinct. A gesture both gentle and firm, acknowledging his pain without letting it divert her attention from the matter at hand—a stance he admired greatly under present circumstances. "We may have thrown those Imperial warships off our tail for now, but we still can't risk entering the Karmara system undetected."

The way she phrased it made Leo chuckle, low and mirthless. "Bold plan you've got, Cap, sneaking us right into the heart of Imperial territory under the aegis of Jupiter's massive magnetic field. Why bother disabling that blind flank when you could just aim straight for the lion's den?"

Vex shot him a sidelong look tempered with a hint of grudging respect. "It's not about being bold—it's about leveraging physics and tactics cleverly. At 155k, we can emerge safely from Jupiter's shadow...completely outside your dreadnought's sensor range along the far edge of their projected firing envelope."

A strid pulled up the tactical display, biting back a curse as static flared across its surface from the last hail burst. The Emperor's children may have gotten a taste of her guns, but they were far from driven off – a nagging truth as tangible as the sticky plasma residue smeared across her gauntlets.

"Leo, you know our odds of making the jump alive if pursued? Slim."

Mercer harrumphed, shifting in his seat despite the lack of gravity. Not an ounce of fear in that chiseled jawline – only determination and hot poker frustration at getting outmaneuvered yet again before his first sip of coffee this century.

"Caffeine-deprived and bloodied though I am, Vex, my probability assessments weren't quite as optimistic as yours. If I had to guestimate, chances are we'd either get vaporized crossing Imperial borders or ripped to shreds by those warships' point defenses while popping out in local space with our pants around our ankles."

The swarthy blonde pirate captain raised a dark eyebrow, fighting back an involuntary smirk at the colorfully graphic imagery Mercer wrought forth with such casual facility. She should be seething, chastising his impertinence—yet instead, something about the man unsettled her, like being scrutinized beneath a magnifying glass by an equal in cunning rather than mere rank.

"Your blunt candor never ceases to enliven conversation, Commander. However..."
She let the pregnant pause hang in the air like an unspoken challenge, leaning back in her command throne to regard Mercer stern-eyed.

"...if we don't play it smart—and I mean deadly smart—we won't make out of this system alive long enough for you to indulge another piping hot cup from that imaginary jar. Jupiter's shielding might buy us some time, but we dance on a knife's edge nonetheless."

Mercer grunted assent, rubbing his chin thoughtfully. "Fair points, Cap'n. Even presuming those Imperials peel off once they lose line-of-sight, there's risk to entering-system limbo via a slingshot. My models suggest a marginally lower delta-V cost for bypassing Jupiter altogether...though it would leave us outside their optimal detection arc."

"And I suppose that tactically astute maneuvering suits your puritanical military sensibilities better?" Vex retorted acidly, flashing a grin of molten gold against porcelain teeth as she baited the commander. She took sadistic pleasure watching others squirm under her verbal barbs – it kept them on their toes. "You're so eager to play clean-cut cowboy gunning for the bad guys in a solar corral, why not fly solo back to Saturn Station and leave me to mine our profits?"

"If I recalled you correctly, the term was 'bold plan', not foolhardy caper," Leo riposted, annoyance evident in the set of his shoulders—a rare show of vulnerability in front of this hardened pirate queen. "Even if you'll deny any inkling of strategy or calculated risk-taking inherent in your proposal..." He paused, considering her closely, hunting for a shred of softness behind that impenetrable exterior. "...do try to trust I'm not the sort to abandon allies mid-layover. Loyalty's more than just brass medals to me, no matter how much I crave a steaming mug and a stiff upper lip come mission's end."

Vex snorted, tossing back her head in raucous laughter that echoed through the bridge's steel corridors like artillery fire.

Leo clenched his jaw against further exchanges, recognizing the futility of appealing to her mercenary sensibilities alone. They danced around something deeper, an intimate understanding born of shared trials no amount of bravado could mask. 

He watched her throw back her head, spilling raven tresses down slender shoulders as she let out that wild, unbridled laugh that made him ache to hold her close and soothe the tempest within. His gaze drifted across her cheekbones, highlighting angular planes of bone beneath porcelain skin, before settling on the seductive curve of her lips—the same lips that curved into a scathing sneer one moment, promising kisses that lingered sweet and hot the next.

Their banter stung with raw honesty, laying bare both their vulnerabilities amidst the bravado. When emotions overflowed without outlet, old habits died harder...and sometimes, people hurt those they cared about most.

Suddenly, alarms shrieked to life, piercing the air and demanding attention. Status displays flashed red, warning of approaching hostile warships, heavily armed and armored—clear outliers even among the galaxy's cutthroat slavers.

"Helm to battlestations!" Command snapped Leo into action as chaos erupted around them. Crew members scrambled to man duty posts while Vex barked orders, her eyes riveted to main viewer windows depicting their precarious predicament.

Leo spun past navigation stations, taking in the grim numbers scrolling across displays. Their relative velocity put them at a disadvantage in open space—and closing fast would only ensure a swift surrender. Even his legendary piloting prowess couldn't shave off enough delta v to outrun these leviathans.

"We're outgunned and outclasped by a considerable margin," growled Leo as he arrived beside Vex at the command chair. Her amber-eyed focus never wavered from the screen. "Trying to engage will leave us crippled, possibly boarded."

"Balls," spat Vex, her voice a low growl of frustration. Her fingers flew across the armrest control panel, cycling through holo projections and tactical schematics. Flickers of white-hot light danced in her hair as the ship itself absorbed the energy surge, readjusting its camouflage fields.

"The Marauder needs another few system runs before we can boost power output effectively," warned Leo, studying primary systems and plotting alternatives. They needed a fighting chance, which meant—

His brain stuttered at the realization. The ship's asymmetrical design, once a source of pride, presented a solution now. By venturing away from center mass toward the engines, Leo could utilize available heatshields and angle control thrusters to perform an emergency roll, putting her exposed flank out of harm's way.

But even with recalculated vectors...they were still racing headlong towards the waiting arsenal. If his gambit failed...

Leo locked eyes with Vex through the pandemonium surrounding them. Fear didn't factor as he saw an unspoken mirroring of intent—she grasped what must be done.

"No rescue coming, boss," Vex intoned grimly, voice laced with undertones of resignation. A flicker of somber accord passed between them. No promises, no regrets...only acknowledging the gravity of their situation.

"Alright then—let's send these bastards eating dust instead," declared Leo firmly, the resolve to preserve the crew etched across his chiseled features. Vex nodded curtly, amber gaze locking onto his own.

In perfect synchrony, they raised weapons.

The first salvo thundered toward the Marauder, searing lines of plasma fire streaking across the black canvas—but the rogue ship had already begun her lethal pirouette, evasive maneuvers.

Asteroid belt debris pelted the hull like a deadly hail storm as Leo fought to realign course, heart pounding in his chest. "Systems report?" he barked, fingers flying across control panels with frantic precision.

" Engines on line at thirty percent capacity," Ava replied immediately from her post beside him. Her voice betrayed the slightest tremor beneath its typically stoic tone. "Thruster damage severe, but still operational through redundant channels."

Leo's breath hitched. Thirty percent wasn't nearly enough for the next phase, especially considering the pursuit closing rapidly behind them in the rear view displays—a menacing, jagged formation of onrushing projectiles. "Patch me through to engineering."

"What can I do for you, Command--"

"No platitudes," Leo cut in harshly. "I need to know the status of those damned asymmetry shielding panels. Are we clear to—"

"We're clear," Dr. Kavita interrupted from elsewhere on the bridge, her cadence clipped and efficient. "The structural integrity fields are stable enough for your proposed maneuver."

Not ideal, but it would have to suffice. Leo's gut tightened as he contemplated the gamble ahead—rolling the Marauder on her side by directing available thrust away from the enemy to expose her underbelly, essentially winging a blind dive for the relative safety of her engine array while praying it deterred the pursuit long enough to set up a favorable counterattack.

"Okay." He drew a shaky breath, his mind racing through worst-case scenarios even as he spoke aloud to ground himself. "Here's how this goes down—Ava, plot the new trajectory assuming an eighty-five degree pitch offset. Target the vector right down our spinal axis. And get ready to dump excess heat through those exposed conduits once we start the maneuver," he instructed, watching the data streams scroll across the display screens, each second ticking toward their precarious future.

"Got it," Ava confirmed crisply, fingers dancing over her console. Dark circles rimmed the oval of her face, shadows reflecting the stark worry in her eyes.

Leo turned to face Vex, who'd drifted to stand at the bridge rail overlooking the main chamber, arms crossed tightly over her bosom. An uneasy stillness pervaded her normally fluid grace—the taut cords of her neck, the slight flare of her nostrils—all attested to her own anxiety. Just beyond her shoulder, he spotted Jael hovering by the armory, hands flexing restlessly near his pulse blaster, his angular face impassive yet conveying unsaid fears for his loved ones back on Horizon Station.

Leo spared only a glance for his bridge crew before resuming eye contact with Vex. Their gazes met in a moment of shared understanding, a compact bond forged in the crucible of danger and the solemn imperative to protect all of them, no matter the cost.

"If things go sideways," he said quietly, overriding the hum of machinery and distant impacts, "we don't waste time trying to outrun them again. We fight back every way we can—and then we take our chances."

Vex's lips curled into a wry half-smile, though the gleam in her amber stare remained predatory. "Never doubted it, flyboy. Now let's kick some celestial ass."

Her words sparked an ignitable synergy within him—a surge of reckless determination mixing with cold calculation as together, they faced the merciless uncertainty awaiting them against the star-splattered velvet of the void...
