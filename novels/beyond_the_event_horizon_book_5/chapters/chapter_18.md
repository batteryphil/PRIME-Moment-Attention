# Chapter 18: Setting Foot on Earth

Leo's world tipped violently on its axis as the crash survivors splashed down through atmosphere in a controlled descent. Astrid gripped the arms of her chair, teeth bared against the pressure as thermal shockwaves rippled outward from the shuttle's scorched skin. Leo felt the heat seep in despite the insulation - every surface screamed protest at the brutal stress. 

For a fleeting instant while still accelerating downward, the view from the portholes was dominated by an inferno burning across the sky -- flames leaping toward them like eager serpents. When they finally leveled out for re-entry burn, that blazing maelstrom receded steadily into the distance. But only until replaced by an equally mesmerizing horror.

Darkness enveloped the Earth, save for the sickly, glowing blotches spreading like malignant tumors across the landscape below. Wherefore? Some cataclysmic assault perhaps, though the scope defied comprehension from their height. Perhaps that was deliberate, these oozing patches mere canny markers for something far worse waiting in ambush among those shadowy continents sprawled beneath them.

As the shuttle began to descend further under Astrid's skillful hand, Leo found his own focus straying to other things - to the woman beside him with her fierce set jaw, to how her fingers dug talons into the armrests and the tension that thrummed visibly in her stance. How that same tension reverberated in the way she spoke when she broke the heavy silence.

"We're targeting a remote coastal area near the edge of one of those tainted regions. Should keep us well cleared for landing, in theory." Her lips twitched wryly, attempting humor. "Of course, assuming we actually make it."

No matter her nonchalant tone, Leo watched as she checked systems anxiously over and over. A mix of relief and trepidation fought for dominance within him. They'd made it past what might have proven a lethal welcome. Yet now faced an altogether different peril in this beleaguered planet of theirs, torn asunder by forces both human and divine. But he steeled himself for whatever challenges lay ahead, knowing in this moment Astrid needed a stable constant amidst the chaos.

Seconds ticked by agonizing stretches until finally the shuttle commenced an unimpeded powered descent. Even so, Leo grit his teeth in anticipation for impact. When that inevitable hit came, it jolted them both forward brutally in their seats, throwing heads back even as the vehicle absorbed the force with struts groaning protest.

As the shuttle shuddered once, twice, then came to rest with grinding finality, Leo dragged air into his lungs carefully. That first breath filled his nose with something incongruously earthy--the faint scent of sand mixed with seaweed? The salty tang of ocean air flooded through the sealed breach in the fuselage and streamed in through broken windows. As quickly, Astrid was out of her seat, muscles coiled spring-tight beneath her flight suit even as she strode purposefully toward the damaged hatch.

Chapter 18: 
Setting Foot on Earth

The sudden slam of landing gear compressing sent shockwaves crackling up Leo's spine, making sweat bead faster down his face, even though the environmental controls kept the interior temperate and fresh. He gripped the armrests, knuckles white, watching Astri slip into action like liquid steel unfolding. Her pace was economical, every step fluid and intent, shoulders squared under the battered gray flightsuit stretched drum-tight across her toned form. Each movement spoke volumes - practical elegance forged in crucibles of war and survival.

Leo exhaled slowly as his body released its pent-up tension in degrees, one muscle group at a time. The initial adrenaline rush receded enough for the gravity's gentle weightlessness to settle onto his bones, subtly reminding that this world, after eons lost among stars, still bore the imprint of its terrestrial origins intact. Not that such trivialities counted right now. Reality pivoted sharply around a single, critical question: were they truly safe?

Astri halted before the gaping hull, her expression inscrutable behind mirrored lenses that reflected only dim dashboard light spilling outside. With deliberate, gloved hands, she reached out to grasp the crumpled edge of the breached composite canopy, halting just short of plunging through the ragged rent exposing cerulean sky... and air rich with foreign scents that sparked primal instincts still etched deep in their DNA - salt, sun-heated earth, living greenery.

"Hold your breath," she murmured low and flat.

Then leapt out sideways and disappeared into the swirling dust-storm billowing where the Starlight Marauder had crashed an eternity ago yesterday. Leo scrambled into motion a hairsbreadth slower, mind racing against time and gut churning through multiple interpretations of her parting words. Was she seriously suggesting they wade into those eddies unfettered? There was no telling what debris lurked hidden beneath that whirlwind of displaced atmosphere. And if there wasn't... what hellish unknown awaited exposed on that blasted landscape beyond?

In that instant, Leo made a decision - a lifeline extending across the yawning chasm in understanding between 21st and 32nd centuries’ value systems, bridged by the visceral, unspoken kinship born from countless grueling hours pushing metal machines and fragile fleshy bodies to extremes side by side. With her characteristic blend of raw intuition and battle-hardened pragmatism as anchor points, he knew where to find Astrid should worst come to worst. Now, he simply prayed he could keep pace.

Swallowing hard, Leo approached the shattered view-port and peered out warily across their barren surroundings - an alien tableau where rust-red dunes rolled and undulated to meet sky painted a pale blue unlike any on Keppler-62f. The air shimmered and pulsed along the horizon, a heat haze that refracted light into colors smeared across the celestial canvas like the last, fleeting wisps of sunset on some primeval Terra. Amidst that eerie stillness, the crash site appeared stark as skeletal fingers - twisted wreckage half-buried in shifting sands that had not yet claimed it.

Just visible through that churning maelstrom, sprawled near the heart of the vortex, was the dark shape of the Xeridia Starlight Voyager, half-retrieved from oblivion's clutches only minutes ago. Astrid stood at its crumpled flank, one hand braced against a punctured fuel tank to steady herself in the buffeting wind while scanning the blasted terrain intently through her optics.

The raging whirlwind howled around them like a tormented soul, spewing shards of debris and sucking men toward the abyss. Leo's grip on the battered Voyager's hull dug divots in his gloved palm as he fought to stay upright, feet grounded amid a churning chaos of wind-driven dust and shrapnel.

"Goddamn it, Vess," he screamed above the tempest over at the hovering mech, "We need shelter now, before something tears loose!"

His ears popped as another violent gust slammed into them, threatening to upend Astrid too if she wasn't careful. She must've sensed his panic because she flashed him a reassuring nod over her shoulder, her copper-dark hair whipping wildly.

"Ain't going nowhere fast, Mercs," she called back, voice loud but steady despite the infernal racket. In slow motion, she drew her grav-sling, slung it over one shoulder, then activated her backpack's stabilizers with a practiced flick of a finger. "But I'll get us something solid soon enough. These winds just ain't ready to surrender yet..."

As an afterthought, she jerked a thumb over her own head back towards the battered marauder still orbiting them above the swirling dunes. An order given, not asked.

Leo's blood seemed to thicken with relief. Grinning ferociously at her, he punched his comms unit twice in rapid succession - accepting without question. He squatted down against the sleek fuselage's curve for balance and began to haul himself up. One leg at a time, he worked his way higher until he could wrap an arm around the mech's waist, grasping firm hold of a structural seam.

With a grunt that barely competed with the hurricane raging around them, Leo hoisted himself upward to wedge firmly into the Vessel's narrow cockpit, wedging limbs askew among controls and servos for purchase. His pounding heart nearly matched the hammering of the exposed systems as the mech swooped down.

From this close vantage point, he watched Astrid become a blur of motion against the gray metal skin — a dark figure clad head-to-toe in black tactical gear, lit solely by eerie xenon-blue strobes embedded in her helmet and utility belt and the dying glow leaking from the crippled ship.

"Hold on, you crazy bitch!" he yelled just as the first tentacles of darkness began wrapping around her boots. The Starlight's lights flared brighter, casting wild shadows that chased dancing motes of falling sand around the clearing chaos. For a few precious seconds, those brilliant beams pinned and illuminated her—high cheekbones, full lips set in a resolute line, eyes narrowed behind polarized lenses, and raven-black eyebrows etched in concentration.

Their gazes met, holding and unbreakable through the screaming gale separating them. It felt more real than air or ground beneath them at that moment—the connection forged in fire and desperation, born of countless shared struggles across unfathomable distances and harsh alien reaches.

And then the darkness yanked her away with a jarring violence that knocked the wind from Leo once more. He strained forward frantically as Astrid plummeted, almost losing her footing on the hover-drone's upper lip before regaining equilibrium. Her armor absorbed an impact that would have crushed bone; the repulsor field of the mech barely gave her slack, whiplashing her across uneven rubble.

With a snarl of defiance, she recovered her footing again, shaking off the blow with an audible metallic clank.

Leo hung over the battered hover-drone's edge, arms wrapped in a death grip around its rusting frame, boots scrabbling for purchase against the undulating sand. Sand everywhere – spinning in miniature cyclones above their heads, spilling in sheets from the Starlight's gouged flank, seeping into every seam of his suit under shifting currents of air. Even the blood oozing from a shard of Plexiglas lodged alongside his elbow left a faint crimson stain upon the desert floor, slowly being washed away by the relentless tide.

He craned his neck upward to follow her descent, jaw clamped to keep a cry from escaping, heart stammering out each ragged beat as if desperately trying to communicate some final urgent message he ignored in favor of simply watching – watching as she dropped like a stone into an abyss, lost instantly to roaring tempests within the voided husk of Leviathan-IV.

The horror was instant, visceral as a sledgehammer strike to the gut. One moment she thrashed against impossible forces; the next her body was merely a blur streaking toward nothingness at Mach ten. No signs to indicate she'd landed somewhere inside the maelstrom, only a dreadful, sinking knowledge that the abyssal hellscape had already swallowed her whole.

Just as the cruel panic was rearing up to claim him, a sudden flare cut through his view, blinding white against the star-darkened heavens as stars dimmed before the unleashed light. In the wake of that brief strobe, two pinpricks of radiance gleamed in the distance, swiftly growing larger against the black.

With a flick and creak, his glove relaxed its grip. Something scraped rough metal against rusty paint, a sound both painfully familiar yet profoundly out of place amidst this desolate sea of rock, sand, and dead stars.

"Astrid..." Her name left his breath-less mouth a whispered prayer, echoing back to him hollowly off the curve of the void. When it came time to believe, faith crystallized in the certainty that she would be waiting on the other side of whatever marvel was approaching to pluck her from the jaws of extinction - a savior emerging from the cosmos itself, ready to take up where humanity had forsaken her among cold stars centuries past.

Yet even that comforting conviction wasn't enough to still the frenzied pounding of Leo's veins or the adrenaline pulsing hot through his veins as the lights continued drawing closer, eventually resolving into the unmistakable shape of an approaching spacecraft. 

This vessel, unlike theirs, glided smoothly through the air with nary a waver as its descent slanted toward the earthward trajectory. Despite never having seen the interior layout, Leo recognized Vex's Starlight without question, knowing the shape, proportion, and subtle imperfections embedded in muscle memory through countless harrowing adventures they faced together battling against the uncaring vastness that tried to devour them whenever they dared poke fingers of humanity above the parapet.

As the distant dots grew ever-larger, Leo's grip tightened anew to the extent the metal allowed before ceasing lest he try to wrest open sealed armor joints and bleed out further through gaping wounds – an act far easier said than done.

The ship settled onto the earthy expanse, repulsor engines dying down into quiet standby hums and vibration dying away. Its hull darkened as retractable landing gear deployed, metal segments unfolding with a low groan to form wide stabilizing feet. Dust devils swirled around its edges as the craft made contact, rapidly settling after initial disturbances.
