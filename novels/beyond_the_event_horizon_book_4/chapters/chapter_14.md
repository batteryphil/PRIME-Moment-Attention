# Chapter 14: No Time for Tears

Leo froze as the monstrous shape erupted into view, a nightmarish apparition caged in a halo of flickering light reflections. Bulging, malnourished haunches propelled the grotesque amalgamation forward on an elongated, misshapen gait that seemed to defy logic. Its scabrous mass of gray skin stretched over bulging, asymmetrical limbs, knotted tendon and sinew visible beneath the mottled epidermis like a dissected anatomy chart come to life. Pulsing crimson orbs dominated the creature's skull, fixating on its new quarry with a primal fervor devoid of intelligence. Twin horns protruded at precarious angles from the macabre cranium, hooked like daggers intended to disembowel and tear.

The beast's hoarse, agonized panting reverberated along the metal passageway—a chilling symphony announcing its arrival. Leo felt a rivulet of cold sweat trickle down his spine despite the frigid chill of the abandoned station. This wasn't some lab rat or genetically engineered critter run amok. Whatever unnatural abomination pursued them had evolved or been birthed under the harsh, adverse conditions prevalent here.

"Holy shit," Leo breathed involuntarily, fighting a sudden wave of queasiness as the creature's fetid stench wafted down the corridor, reeking of rotten meat, ammonia, and charred bone.

Astrid exhaled slowly through gritted teeth as if reining in a temperamental animal. She tightened her grip on the pulse carbine, aiming past the monstrosity toward the dark recesses where more like it could lurk. "We need shelter and fast. If they've got numbers..."

She didn't need to finish the thought. They both knew their meager weapons and minimal shielding wouldn't stand a chance against a large pack of such formidable, vicious specimens. The creatures would overwhelm them quickly should they be cornered.

Leo nodded curtly, adrenaline spiking as he flicked his carbine's power setting to concentrated disruptor pulses rather than scattering rounds to deter but not harm potential survivors. He'd take any opportunity to disable these beasts, limiting the threat to themselves.

"Ahead on the portside—there's a maintenance room we can barricade." Astrid pointed with her gun barrel. "Stay close and watch my six."

Without waiting for a response, the pirate captain charged forward, leading them on a perilous dash past their one-time terror through the labyrinthine passages leading to the storage compartment. Leo followed doggedly, keeping the creature's bulk near their rear as a buffer against further attacks. His heart hammered against his rib cage, pulse throbbing in his ears as they pressed onward through the dank, echoing confines of the derelict moonbase. Even in the gloom of the corridors, the acrid stench clung to him like a noxious wet blanket—making it difficult to focus on anything other than his racing breath and the metallic taste of dread saturating his mouth. Every instinct cried out for them to run faster, but Leo reined in the impulse, forcing himself to maintain pace alongside Astrid. Rushing blind could lead to a dead end, traps, or ambushes he couldn't defend against effectively.

As they skidded into the maintenance room, Leo slammed and locked the reinforced hatch behind them. Astrid wasted no time surveying the dimly lit chamber—its sterile linoleum floors gleaming with an otherworldly sheen in the scattered emergency lighting. Metal workbenches lined the periphery, littered with rusting diagnostic tools and discarded components of long-abandoned equipment.

"I've got enough scrap for repairs—this'll do as shelter for a few hours," Astrid declared, already stripping the useless machinery to salvage parts. Sweat beaded her brow, glistening in the emergency lights casting long, eerie shadows across the metal floor. Kneeling to rummage through a pile of discarded circuit boards, she glanced up at Leo sidelong, a hint of accusation in her gaze.

"What now, hero?" The amusement in her voice had a sharper edge of sarcasm hanging above it like barbed wire. "Got any bright ideas saving the day?"

Leo bristled at her tone—it was the first crack in her normally tightlipped composure since they'd escaped. Stepping closer, he fought to keep his anger simmering beneath the surface rather than erupting. "My aim isn't to rescue a damsel-in-distress, Captain Ross... nor is winning your admiration my primary motivation." He crouched down beside her, scrutinizing the array of components strewn about the floor. 

"The question is simply where we can expect pursuit next. Tracking this facility has likely taken more resources than our quarry anticipated—which means they might reassess priorities now that they've got your ship in custody. Could be seeking some leverage play to regain her mobility before giving chase again..."

Astrid snorted, waving a hand dismissively as a flickering glance crossed her face. "Speculating won't bring Zara to heel. She's made her intentions clear—a pawn in whatever dirty game between the guilds—until one of you plays by their rules. Or she drops us in the nearest dustbin." Her lips twisted bitterly.

"Well, Z didn't grow the Starlight's reputation with patience." The captain rose fluidly, muscles coiling underneath her fitted leather wear as she began selecting component parts from their haphazard piles. "We either find a way to retake control outright or slip free under their noses while they're cocky, thinkin' I'm outgunned and helpless without her."

"I get what drives you, Vex—rekindling the ghost of your old rebellion; having the upper hand again—but that ain't strategy when you're outnumbered." Leo stood too, folding his arms over his chest as he faced her steadily. He refused to engage with her aggression head-on. No matter how many daggers flashed in her eyes, he could show none in return. "Clever pirates evolve, adapt—your approach worked when the odds were better aligned, but throwing dice in a high-stakes casino isn't leadership. It's recklessness bordering on suicidal."

The hiss of Astrid's exhale sounded suspiciously like a startled gasp—the fire in her gaze briefly banks, replaced by stunned incomprehension before flickering back to full flame. "So now you're counseling me on command?" Venom dripped heavily off each word. "Funny—I could've used guidance from someone with half your arrogance when the ODF was ripping my crew apart...but you know what they say: pride comes before defeat."

"That's not what I meant!" Leo raised his hands in exasperation, searching for the right words amidst the escalating standoff. But every attempt felt like pouring gasoline on this burning powder keg. "I understand the rage fueling your vengeance; I'm just trying to inject some semblance of a plan here! We both want the same thing: freedom and reclaiming our vessels, though we differ—"

"Differ?" Astrid's laugh cut across him, raw and humorless. "Hell, that's one way to put it."

Leo's protest died as it hit the wall of Astrid's glare, frozen mid-air between them like a challenging gauntlet. Her fists clenched at her sides, knuckles dragging white against the black synth-leather of her flightsuit. Tendrils of heat haze eddied around her in the cramped confines of the cockpit. Sweat beaded at her temples and trickled down her jawline in rivulets, shining like tears she refused to shed.

The Starlight Marauder bucked beneath their feet as another barrage shook the hull plates, threatening to throw them off balance entirely. Metal groaned and acrid smoke bit at their lungs, carried in on the turbulent air current from the breached compartments.

Astrid's boots scrabbled for purchase on the wet decking as another volley lit up the viewport behind her, searing blue-white streaks etching across the inky void. She wrenched her gaze free of Leo's, squinting through the sting of salt in her eyes to fix on the looming form of Arcturus II ahead.

"We're going to get through this," she forced out, voice wavering between determination and ragged self-doubt. "I didn't come so far just to—"

Her words broke off in a choked snarl as the bridge display erupted into warnings, klaxons blaring in urgent protest. Alarms flooded every console—a devastating concatenation of engine overload, failing thrusters, crippled life support, and crippling radiation leaks, all converging to paint a stark picture of impending doom.

Astrid lunged forward, grabbing frantic handfuls of hair in desperation as panic clawed its way up her throat. She yanked out a comm-link, shouting over the cacophony. "Ross to engineer! Get us stabil—"

Silence fell as suddenly as the chaos had burst forth, leaving only heavy breathing and the ominous creak of strained metal in its wake. Astrid collapsed back into her chair, shoulders slumped in defeat—except those arms still gripped tight onto her skull, nails scraping against scalp, face twisted in anguish beyond words. The realization dawned in agonizing slow motion, horror twisting her features.

Their window had slammed shut. Arcturus II already filled the viewport like an oppressive demon made flesh, looming dark and malevolent against the glittering stars. There would be no reprieve, no chance to make amends, reforge alliances, or outrun the storm. Only the cold embrace of oblivion waiting to consume them all.

Astrid rocked back and forth, small broken sounds escaping her lips like lost sobs in the vacuum. And then came Leo's bootfalls thudding deliberately along the catwalk to halt before her. He bent at the waist to scoop her up from her meltdown, cradling Astrid against his chest like she was precious cargo, not the fearsome battle-hardened commander who'd commandeered his ship less than a stellar cycle ago.

"What kind of fool does this to themselves?" he ground out, incredulity shading into something deeper, almost gentle, as he held her trembling form against his solid heat. His scent enveloped her—fresh rain, old leather, and that spark of electric danger she couldn't seem to quit. For once, Astrid met his gaze without challenge or deflection, surrender written in the vulnerability pooling in her mismatched eyes.

"No mercy," she whispered, the admission ripped from her like confessional bleeding. "Maybe death's mercy will be sweeter. Let's just..."

The weight of their world hung heavy in each other's arms as they stood there, isolated by fate yet more intimately connected than any ally or lover could ever be. Above them, the vast panorama of nebulas and distant civilizations wheeled on unseen axes, a celestial indifferent to their insignificance. Silent as a tomb, their spaceships drifted ghostly amidst the starry expanse like spectral reminders of everything they were swiftly losing.

It was ironic that within mere lightminutes they possessed technology to breach the fabric of space and time itself. Quantum warps were humanity's greatest invention and darkest horror - an alchemical marriage of gravity and exotic energy. It granted near instantaneously access to every corner of the Cosmos but it did so at a terrible cost. Any vessel utilizing such power left a scar upon its reality – a wormhole that unraveled and regurgitated fragments of unprocessed spacetime. The further the quantum leap, the more unstable those rips became till finally, inevitably, they imploded, snuffing the ship and crew into oblivion along with countless parsecs of extraneous matter and information.

That cosmic trapdoor had yawned open right beneath Luna Nine and dragged them screaming down its light years-long throat. As if fate's cruel fist was twisting the knife, their very survival depended on outrunning those annihilating rifts before they closed forever behind them. Problem was nobody knew where those destructive whirlpools appeared or could predict with certainty when...or where...the final black hole of destruction would materialize to devour the fleeing remnants of their civilization.

Leo clutched Astrid tighter as the awful truth echoed through him, every bone chilled, insides lurching like a riptide pulling a swimmer down into frigid depths. If Luna Nine succumbed before reaching the nearest safe haven...they'd be reduced to subatomic particles that even light wouldn't carry far. Their individual consciousnesses scattered across the universe like flecks of stardust would vanish into irrelevance while still conscious long enough to comprehend forever-witness the void swallowing home worlds whole.

"What are we going to do, Vex?" his raw voice crackled through helmet comms but the question wasn't really meant for her. It was an appeal to a higher authority, a plea to stars now nothing more than distant pinpricks against eternity's bleak canvas.

In response, a frantic sensor alert flared red across the helm displays - unexpected astrogational data flickering onto several scopes simultaneously like frantic static whispers through subspace radio antennae. Leo squinted at the screens, trying to decipher the anomaly until he snapped head-whirling about to face Astrid whose expression mirrored his own shock and trepidation:

"...is that..." he gulped, struggling to speak clearly past the dryness blooming fast in his throat "...an artificial gravity well? In system...but impossible...those signatures don't match any known technology..."

Astrid set to work checking and cross-checking with urgency mounting in each movement while behind them the dying sun bleached whatever remaining light remained on the hull from the Starlight Marauder to ghostly blue.

"It can't be," the commander muttered, shaking her head. "Something's feeding those readings false data. That's how wormholes appear – pseudogravitational lenses masking the true extent and instability."

She spun to face back towards the viewscreen which now displayed a sickening distortion – space itself seemingly churning and belching chaos as if vomiting back something...somebeing from the deepest recesses of void.
