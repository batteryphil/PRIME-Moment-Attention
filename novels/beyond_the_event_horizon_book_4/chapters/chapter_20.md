# Chapter 20: Jump to Sol

Commander Leo Mercer's boots scraped against the chilled alloy grating as he hustled down the Starlight Marauder's cramped, labyrinthine corridors. Bare skin pricked by every brush against the rough surface, his muscles protested the unfamiliar strain, but he pressed on undeterred. In his earpiece, Captain Astrodynamic Units conversion rates. Astrid's clipped voice cut through the static. "Comm arrays online, stand by for frequency sync."

Zero-G greasepaint peeling at the corners of his eyes, Leo ducked past a maintenance airlock and dodged a trio of busy machinists wrestling with a fusion plasma injector. Thermal shields snapped open as he approached, revealing searing embers dancing within violet flame. He waved acknowledgment above his head before continuing on his mission.

Two minutes. Then the combined might of the armada would begin their desperate push back toward Terra orbit—two planets apart by light-second, each step a gut-wrenching reminder of their tiny, insignificant scale amidst unfathomable cosmic expanses.

Leo burst through a final hatch, shouldering his way into the dimly lit bridge. Tactical displays blinked green as systems asserted online status while others continued spinning up, etching glowing trails through the starry blackness stretching seamlessly from viewport to viewport. At the center pedestal, Vex leaned forward, shoulders strained beneath the weight of her armored helm, eyes riveted on projected tactical maps.

"Tactical to bridge," she called, voice barely above a murmur to be heard over the soft hiss of life support and the faint hum of drives transitioning to maneuver mode. "All divisions report ready for transit."

Leo skirted the curve of her chair to reach the comm station tucked away in a nook. Cable-tightened consoles rose like a skeletal forest around him, casting long shadows across the floor. He worked swiftly, his touch sure despite years away from this interface. Each key press and menu selection echoed with reassuring familiarity, grounding him once more amid the eerie sterility and potential chaos of outer space operations.

In under ten seconds, he was signing off from Starlight's command deck, transmitting a brief synchronicity check and standing by for further instructions per standard procedure. A sigh escaped his parted lips as relief crept in—little victories counted when lives depended on such minutiae. Next on the agenda were preliminary fleetwide communications tests...

As Mercury passed out of range, Leo's gaze drifted to viewport three. Outside hung the starfall – countless glittering lights tracing elliptical paths through the firmament, some trailing comet tails, others leaving behind wispy clouds of ionized gas as the hypergolic drive plasma cooled. The beauty of those trails, dancing amidst constellations old and new, rekindled pride kindling alongside the ache in his gut. It really had been so long...

He shook himself free of reverie, the harsh clank of metal on metal jolting him back to urgency. Crew movements turned deliberate, each action precise to avoid accidental misfires within the charged atmosphere. Tactical displays refreshed, feeding updates into projected holospace. Vex straightened at her station, surveying data streams with cold focus.

"All divisions report ready for transit?" She arched a skeptical brow at Leo crossing back toward her, questioning everything unsaid.

"No hitches that I caught, Captain."

One side of her mouth curled in a half smile, a rare hint of approval beneath battle weariness. "Satisfied with your assessment? Because we're just getting started here, Mr. Pilot."

A muscle ticked in his jaw at her tone, but he met her gaze evenly. "If there's reason to suspect issues, you'll know before we slip the wormhole. In the meantime–"

In a blur, she lunged across the space separating them, hand cracking against his jaw with enough force to send him stumbling back. Alarms shrilled in protest as safety harnesses cinched tight, jerking them upright against acceleration seats.

"Cuff yourself to the frame!" Vex barked, already pivoting on her own security line. "And keep quiet until I give orders."

Leo obeyed automatically, seething as he locked himself in place and forced stillness rather than challenging her authority. Through the web of interconnected viewscreens, he watched Starlight's crew scrambling for battle stations: pirates, cutthroats, and rogues whose pasts were steeped in violence – except perhaps Vex herself, who operated without remorse, yet carried scars nonetheless.

The thought nagged; he couldn't quite put name to it, not yet, amidst the maelstrom of sensations swamping his raw nerves. One thing was certain: being trapped in Vex Ross's orbit stoked a cauldron of discord and desire, its edges growing sharper by the minute. Whether that turbulence reflected inner turmoil or external threat remained to be seen.

But one truth stood clear - the fate of billions rested solely upon the precision of their actions, the cohesion of tactics employed, and the willpower to withstand a thousand unseen stresses straining the boundaries of sanity. Command demanded unwavering resolve, and neither Mercer norRoss could afford doubt. Not now, not ever.

As Starlight prepared to vanish down the gullet of a subspace rupture, neither commander held much hope for serenity or trust within their ragtag fleet, or for the future beyond the reaches of the wormhole...let alone the treacherous politics waiting in Sol's neighborhood. All that mattered was reaching their destination intact, weapons primed, and a strategy forged in the crucible of adversity.

There would be blood shed before this reckoning. He knew it with grim certainty.

Yet even as battle-lust anticipation sang in every fiber of Leo's being, something else stirred beneath the tumult – an unfamiliar heat coiling in his chest that transcended mere combat exhilaration. Awareness kindled regarding another woman, a pirate captain he neither comprehended nor fully controlled, whose presence alone set his skin alight and scrambled rational thought.

✦ ✦ ✦

As if sensing Mercer's disquiet, Captain Vex turned from the helm, her piercing amethyst eyes locking gazes with him across the cramped bridge. For a moment, no words were necessary. In the stark silence broken only by the steady thrum of the fusion engines and soft hiss of life support, two kindred spirits recognized the depth of shared struggle etched in each other's faces—a camaraderie honed by hardship that cut deeper than any bond of rank or authority.

Mercer's gaze slid involuntarily to those full, parted lips, noting the faint smudge of engine grime that seemed oddly appealing against the pale canvas of Astrid's skin. The way she wore chaos like armor, defying command with a fierce beauty that still managed to stir something primal within him...

Captain Ross noted the direction of his prolonged glance and raised an eyebrow, a wry, knowing smile playing about her mouth. "Something on your mind, Earthman?" Her voice carried a hint of playful taunt mixed with genuine curiosity beneath its rough edges.

McCoy interrupted the charged moment, his tone laced with worry as he addressed them over ship-wide comms. "...Engineering reports catastrophic failure of our last antimatter containment field! Power output stabilizing, but for how long? Recommend orbit and repairs ASAP."

The gravity of these life-critical issues snapped Mercer back to attention, the heat of their near-interlude instantly cooled by ice water reality. "Aye, McCoy." He turned to Captain Ross. "Prepare course corrections for a stable lunar orbit. We'll need time to effect emergency repairs before-

He broke off abruptly as Starlight shook with an impact that slammed everyone's feet against the decking in unison—the unmistakable tremors signaling an enemy engagement outside.

Alert lights flashed red along the status panel while klaxons wailed urgently in the background, drowning out all conversation.

On the forward main screen, blazing projectiles streaked toward the Starlight at incredible velocity—to Mercer's seasoned eye, mass-drivers or high-yield kinetic warheads—and their own point defenses responded with a burst firings of laser turrets and miniature grav-projectors. Alarms screamed louder than conscious thought as laser blasts and energetic slugs crisscrossed the void around them in a lethal geometric dance.

Captain Ross sprang into action with deadly grace, both hands flying over the helm console to reconfigure their evasive maneuvering. Behind them, McCoy yelled orders into comms, coordinating defensive countermeasures.

Leo braced himself against a bulkhead, adrenaline surging as shockwaves rippled through the hull with each near-miss, threatening to knock him off his feet. Sweat poured down his face, stinging the corners of his eyes, but he hardly noticed the discomfort. His entire focus fixated on the chaotic screen—Tracking algorithms struggled to keep pace with the frenzied onslaught, throwing up increasingly wild predictive firepaths that flickered red and white.

Suddenly, a massive kinetic strike slammed into the port flank with the force of an overpowered freighter crashing into them head-on. Blazing plasma engulfed the nearby sections as compartment integrity alarms shrieked a dire warning of breached hull sections and venting atmosphere.

Mercer stumbled sideways as the violent shudder nearly sent him tumbling off his feet. "Report!" he roared over the cacophony, scanning wildly for the source of this new catastrophic hit.

"Multiple breaches starboard-Portside sectors one through four!" exclaimed McCoy, his voice tight with panic. "We're venting...the cold won't wait us out—"

Astrid silenced him abruptly via direct channel, her tone low and focused.

Leo stumbled, grabbing at handholds as the massive impact rocked the Dauntless on its axis. Compartment walls groaned and buckled outward under the explosive force, spitting plumes of frosty gas into the darkness. Gravity still clung stubbornly to the core hab-sections where they crouched, making every motion feel clumsy and exaggerated as if performed underwater by slow-moving fish.

McCoy's panicked voice cut through the din of structural distress alarms and escaping pressurized fluid. "Venting! We can't—"

Astrid seized control, cutting him off with an iron grip on her helmet's communication circuit. Her words dripped with laser-sharp focus, devoid of emotion save for a steely intent. "Patch me through to engineering. I need schematics and damage assessment now, not excuses."

Leaning heavily against the wall, Leo forced himself to process the escalating crisis. The Dauntless had already taken far more than they were designed to withstand, let alone recover from—and another direct hit would probably destroy them outright when the freezing vacuum outside stripped them bare, muscle and sinew alike.

Stars streaked past their viewscreens in dizzying succession as they careened off course under the relentless barrage, pursued relentlessly by the swarm of ruthless pirate slavers hellbent on turning them into ornaments for some depraved potentate's pleasure dome.

The Dauntless shuddered once more, a jarring aftershock from the most recent onslaught, making it difficult to maintain balance without anchoring oneself. As Astro's voice continued to issue crisp directives to the bridge crew and desperate entreaties to engineering for aid, one glance down the corridor toward the central habitat brought an involuntary choke of revulsion.

Puddles of icy blood glistened black as onyx against the polished metal floor, growing rapidly as severed limbs and torn bodies floated sluggishly along the gentle currents of atmospheric bleed. Mercer looked away before his stomach could fully comprehend the horror unfolding mere steps away.

"Captain," he managed hoarsely, swallowing back nausea, "we need to adjust our heading. Try for an alternate jump point if possible—just until we can assess the state of our engines and—"

"Never mind," Astrid interrupted tersely, eyes glued to her displays even as she braced herself against a shaking console. "This little joyride ain't about getting anywhere safely. But just maybe—" She paused, gaze narrowing with concentration as flickering data streams spun across her screens. A beat later, her breath caught in something very close to surprise despite her weathered façade. "Looks like old Ironhand finally got his act together."

Leo frowned, struggling to understand why this latest development could elicit such an unusual reaction from his enigmatic commander—especially given the life-or-death straits they found themselves in. "How so?" he asked warily.

"We're in range to drop back for a Sol-bound jump in the system's near side," Astrid said quietly. The faintest glimmer of hope sparked somewhere in her stoic expression, though her tone remained measured rather than triumphant. "Should be able to clear the pirate fleet's outer formation. Just pray those engines hold together long enough—" She tensed, gripping the armrests almost hard enough to leave bruises.

Out by the rimward edge of the solar system, something monstrous was taking shape—starlight reflecting from hundreds of gleaming hulls, countless smaller craft darting among their imposing mass like clouds of swarming insects. This juggernaut, assembled from every corner of known space and powered by who-knew-what unfathomable alien machinations or stolen advanced tech, was all too clearly bent.
