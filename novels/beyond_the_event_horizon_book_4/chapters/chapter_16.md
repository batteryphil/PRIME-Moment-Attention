# Chapter 16: Atmospheric Skim

Leo fought gravity every step of the way back to the cockpit, the battered derelict's skeletal frame offering precarious handholds. The distant thunder of the corsair's engines swelled ever louder as Astrodyne drifted nearer, tantalizing glimpses of chrome-and-steel hull painting herself against starscapes.

A stray flash illuminated Astrid awaiting his return, teeth bared in a savage grin as she tracked some unseen menace. Her eyes widened as he clambered in beside her and slammed the hatch shut behind a deft kick. Without preamble, Leviathan strained against tether lines, thrusters roaring defiantly as he fed them everything Solar Wind could muster.

"Hold on!" Mercer bellowed above the racket. Astrid merely flashed a crazed thumbs-up. As Leviathan leapt forward, they surged across starlit darkness toward the waiting derelict's shadow.

Kaelus' thick hydrogen-helium atmosphere began making itself known - the airglow intensified from wispy tendrils to blazing aurorae dancing across the canopy. Radiant curtains swept past, casting iridescent flickers on faces pinched resolutely against impending doom.

"Dive, dive, stay down--" The command came breathless amidst growls of strain as thrusters screamed in protest, spewing smoke trails that bloomed and faded like short-lived constellations.

The corsair's missile lock warnings shrieked insistently atop a cacophony of klaxons and flashing amber lights. A moment later, the night was turned to day as a volley of flares erupted around them, searing vision and filling screens with strobing light.

"Beautifully painful," Astrid quipped acidly, squinting through narrowed eyelids as another flare detonated mere meters away. For the merest heartbeat, she imagined it engulfed them whole, leaving behind naught but drifting, crisped corpses...

Her stomach lurched cruelly as instinct screamed: Fall! Though gravity dragged mercilessly at their weightless forms inside the capsule, Astrid grabbed the release mechanism with numb fingers – no time for hesitation – yanking the lever to sever Leviathan from the tether lines.

The sudden abandonment left them plummeting into Kaelus like a rock, the very atmosphere it seemed cruel to so abruptly deny them now whipping up in a frenzy of buffeting winds and screaming pressure.

Mercer clung to the grip handles like a man possessed, feeling the g-forces slowly bleeding out as they pierced deeper into the unyielding blue depths. Outside the viewport, indistinct shapes blurred into streaks racing past in dizzying shades of azure and scarlet.

"Damn, Leo, what was that?"

"We ditched the tether to get under their radar--"

"So we're just gonna—

–HANG ON!!"

Leviathan struck a turbulent pocket of wind at terminal velocity and bucked violently, throwing both occupants against restraints. Astrid heard something crack ominously in the cabin, pain shooting through her wrist as impact shock rebounded numbingly through the metal alloy shell.

For a fraught eternity, they careered uncontrollably within a churning maelstrom of wind shear, a flimsy husk tossed at the mercy of elements far more violent than even the ravages of war.

Finally, Solar Wind managed to tame their descent somewhat – the brutal jolts giving way to gentle retardation as atmospheric heat glowed fiercely along Leviathan's blistered skin.

Tumbling lazily, merciful gravity once again claimed them as the last wisps of atmosphere sloughed away, exposing starlight to the pitted, scorched hull.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leviathan drifted like a wounded bird, silhouetted starkly against the velvet blackness. Shaken but intact, it bobbed to a stop at a geosynchronous altitude above its target planet—a bruised and livid orb wracked by cyclical storms.

As Astro's head cleared and a throbbing ache pulsed through her dislocated shoulder, she squinted at her copilot's profile through the gloom.

"Humor me, Test Pilot," Vex rasped, struggling upright. Clumsily readjusting her harness, she rotated to face him. The ship pitched gently in response to the correction. "Why in Hades didn't you strap yourself down during that wild ride?"

Mercer braced himself against the console for balance. "I wanted to stay ready in case we needed to eject or make evasive maneuvers." He fixed her with a pointed look. "Your plan relied on the speed to outrun whatever defenses might have detected us—speed I couldn't deliver harnessed up tighter than a corpse."

Astro bit back a wry observation about corpses before forcing out a brittle chuckle. "Fair point." She glanced around at the scattered tools and torn instrumentation. "However... maybe we could spring for some sturdier gear next time instead of scrounging odds 'n ends?"

The pilot smirked. "Not exactly the priority budget can afford right now." His expression sobered rapidly as he surveyed the damage in earnest. "Speaking of which..." Drawing out his comms link, he punched in a coded frequency. "Mercer to Ares," he called crisply. 

Silence stretched uncomfortably, punctuated only by distant thrumming echoes of engines winding up elsewhere in orbit.

After a full minute, he tried again—and received no reply. A chill of foreboding crept across Mercer's nape. This wasn't good.

Vex tracked his griming expression as if sensing it without his voice. Whatever the omens portended, they faced it together within the confined space of command. No matter how much history divided them, right now Astro and the pilot shared an uneasy truce forged of equal parts necessity and distrust.

"You worryin'?" she ventured, leaning close enough to share breathable warmth inside his helmet.

He nodded once, curt and affirmative.

"Well shit, Leo, so am I."

With a low, bitter laugh, Mercer flicked off the comm link. As starlight painted his features somber, Astrid knew this fragile equilibrium between duty and desire was on shaky ground indeed.

"Try to put it out your mind," she suggested mildly, though her voice trembled with uncertain reassurance. "Whatever news Ares has... we'll handle it."

A heavy sigh escaped through his visor. "Just keep telling yourself that, Vex. Meanwhile..." Frowning, Mercer began running diagnostics on Leviathan's wounded systems. The storm battered mercilessly outside as the unlikely duo braced themselves on opposite sides of the void within. Only one thing was certain:

Their greatest challenges lay ahead, each waiting on the wings of tomorrow or beyond in the infinite expanse of space, where a single misstep could mean eternal annihilation amidst the uncaring stars.

✦ ✦ ✦

Mercer struggled against the relentless drumbeat reverberating through Leviathan's bowels - the ship's lifeblood, thrumming machinery, screeching alloy stressed to its breaking point, groaning struts, and distant detonations rippling their way like thunderclaps across a shattered cityscape. Every shock resounded through his boots and the metal deckplates beneath, making the task at hand both physically demanding and mentally grueling.

With each passing heartbeat, uncertainty seeped deeper into the marrow of his bones, until even the cold metallic chill of his suit couldn't quell the insidious dread slithering through him unbidden. Not for nothing had he honed himself into a finely-tuned instrument of precision and control during years spent honing pilot skills at Edwards Air Force Base. Yet in this brutal crucible only moments ago, all those painstaking repetitions were swallowed by an abyssan nightmare he'd never imagined possible.

And now? Now mere survival hung precariously in balance against the tempest raging without - each gust threatening catastrophic overload to their already damaged propulsion. His fingers flew across the controls in the cramped, featureless flight compartment, working frantically along with Naomi to troubleshoot the mounting list of red herrings flashing through the console displays. If either the AI's vast processing power or Mercer's decades-honed instincts failed them, the result would be more than just an inconvenient delay.

It meant oblivion.

Their voices rose as tensions spiked, punctuated by curses aimed squarely at the malfunctioning nacelles and temperamental fusion reactors refusing to obey commands. Time lost all meaning as Mercer focused entirely on problem-solving.

Then came that singular instant, indistinguishable from countless others yet somehow utterly disparate. In the chaos of flickering screens and screaming gasket leaks, a blinding jolt of awareness pulsed down Mercury's sinewy spine - a gut-level certainty of Astrid standing beside him, just steps away despite the distance separating pilot and copilot.

He glanced up to find icy blue eyes fixed intently upon his features. Close enough for her breath to ghost across his face shield like stolen summer mist, yet worlds apart emotionally. There, suspended within the maelstrom, stood the mercurial heart of their partnership - an unspoken accord rooted in trust tempered by wariness, desire tempered by pragmatism.

Without a word, their hands instinctively met, clenching solid around each other's wrist as if to anchor against the torrent of impending doom. A spark leaped across contact, electrifying nerve endings and short-circuiting rational thought.

In that moment, the vast galaxy receded until only two people existed among unfathomable cosmos - two souls bound by circumstance and common purpose, drawn inexorably towards one another like magnets repelling yet yearning for cohesion.

Astrid's gloved fingers tightened, her touch sending heat blossoming beneath armored layers of skin and synthetic tissues. Mercy, he thought hazily, his pulse racing, his body responding to hers even as his brain shrieked warnings about propriety and professional boundaries.

"I've got you," Vex breathed, an unspoken vow hanging heavy in the air as unseen energies crackled and danced between them unchecked. And in that timeless instant, neither mercenary nor spook truly comprehended how the boundaries between duty and debauchery blurred, until they faded altogether like mist before a merciless sun.

There seemed no end to what they might learn about each other amidst the din of catastrophe, their secrets unfolding in snatches between crises - tales of past loves and bitter heartbreaks, of dreams brutally crushed and defiant remembrances carved in scar tissue and steeled resolve.

✦ ✦ ✦

The fragile electric connection between them snapped without warning, plunging them both back into grim reality at once. Vortex winds slammed the crippled starship out of its doomed orbit, the Marauder careening wildly toward the swirling vortex rim of a colossal black hole. Against the backdrop of hungry event horizon and accreting matter, the ship was little more than a bug hurtling into the maw of a predator.

Leo gripped the console tight enough to put white knuckles against the matte-black surface. His eyes darted frantically across readouts as the vessel shuddered under torquing accelerations. Overhead displays flickered out then flared back on, painting the curved ceiling in strobing curtains of emergency code.

"This can't be happening," Astrid's voice cut through the chaos. She braced a hand against the cockpit bulkhead, her gaze riveted to the holographic navigation schematic. A latticework of projected vectors and trajectory lines dissolved into madness as the ship's predicted path diverged from any sane escape plan.

"We're going to die on that doorstep of nothingness, ain't we?" she muttered darkly. But there was no defeat in her tone, only cold acceptance of a scenario both impossible and imminent. Her eyes met Leo's, sparks of steel-gray ice shining within even as fear and adrenaline coursed through veins and quickened pulses. They clung to each other in shared certainty of their fate, bound by desperate trust forged in infernal crucibles.

Outside, impossibly vast swirls of dark energy seethed just beyond visible light. Gravitational waves rippled outward in symphony with hellish aurora discharges and charged plasma that swirled chaotically on the brink of the black hole's abyss. Stars glinted distant jewels lost in limitless heavens as Vortex's titanic angular momentum hurled them toward oblivion at speeds measurable in fractions of lightspeed.

Then, piercing the cacophonous din, came an unearthly siren cry echoing across subspace on multiple frequencies - alien and ancient, a transmission both primal and encoded beyond human understanding excepting maybe for Astrid herself, the lone surviving operative of Sector Nine's most clandestine division.

Recognition dawned on her face, features etched simultaneously by shock and rapture. At her hip, the silver pendant amulet clicked open with a soft hush, revealing micro-textured circuitry within as it pulsed in tandem to the alien signal. An enigmatic smile played briefly across her lips, her eyes fever-bright beneath lowered lids, before she turned to Leo with decision etched on lines graven by countless battles and hardships faced.

"We have exactly eight minutes till impact. Get ready to die, hotshot. Because according to this ghost from the void, Sector Nine ain't done messing with either our lives just yet."
