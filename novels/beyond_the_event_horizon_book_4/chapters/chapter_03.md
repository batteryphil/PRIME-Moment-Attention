# Chapter 3: Scavenger Infiltration


Astrid 'Vex' Ross leaned forward over the tactical displays arrayed before her commander, eyes intent upon his face for updates to the enigmatic threat unfolding around them. Outside the armored hull plates of the Starlight Marauder, the chill of vacuum had seeped into the compartment air, carrying the metallic tang of spent capacitors and ozone.

"Captain, the unregistered inbound is closing fast," warned Navvie 'Slipstream', her synthesized voice level yet urgent. "If it keeps this vector, it'll collide with our decoy freighter in-system transit lanes within five minutes."

Mercer's gaze flickered up, silver eyes glinting against the pale skin drawn taut across his skull by relentless G-forces. "How accurate are those velocity estimates?"

"Highly confident, sir. It's maintaining constant acceleration, no trajectory correction maneuver thus far—either oblivious to potential collisions, or anticipating something we don't yet grasp about this sector's orbital dynamics." As she spoke, a new graphic blossomed across her terminals portraying a dozen hypothetical scenarios, each collision trajectory stark red against black space.

Vex snorted, a sound akin to rustling parchment muffled by her envirus-mesh mask. "Unlucky streak continues, eh Merc? Yet again, we find ourselves playing catch-up amidst some hidden agendas out here..."

He met her gaze steadily over the brim of his own visor. "Your hunches often prove worth more than any fleet intel briefing."

Her lips curled in an ironic smile. "'Out of context' might just become my new favorite catchphrase." She checked the power conduits feeding their cover disguise—hulls plated with cheap alloy and faded livery meant to resemble centuries-dated ore cargo ships rather than a State-of-the-Art stealth raider like the true Marauder concealed beneath.

"Well then, I think today's odds of walking away from some 'friendly fire mishap' or worse have just hit a new record low. Prepare the crew, command authority likely doesn't know what kind of wild beasts are stalking these celestial ruins. If it doesn't recognize us as transports, that means its AI probably doesn't differentiate between friend or foe in the proximity alert chain to make corrections."

"Understood," replied Slipstream coolly, fingers dancing over console keys. "Initiating 'dead-man switch.' Autopilot take control if I'm lost to hostile sensors or impact within three thousand klicks."

"Soon enough to worry about..." grumbled Mercer. He turned his attention back to plotting navigation tracks—searching for a way out of this increasingly precarious situation without revealing a scrap of their actual capabilities to whatever entity sought their destruction.

On the other side of starship, in the cramped engine room, the pulsating heat of fusion flames cast eerie shadows on the duranium walls. Piping groaned under stress, pressure gauges quivered perilously close to the 'Alert' threshold...and below decks, down among miles of storage compartments filled to bursting with salvaged ordnance, the Marauder's gun crews prepped explosive weapons with grim anticipation of coming battle. 

Meanwhile, on the bridge, Captain Vex Ross drew in a deep breath shuddering against the confines of her envirosuit, the stale recycled air heavy with tension radiating from every member of the flight crew like a tangible presence.

"All hands! We're making final preparations," her crackling voice boomed out over the ship's PA system, resonating against the insulated helmets sealing around the heads of every Marauder crew member on duty or standby.

✦ ✦ ✦

The bridge of the Starlight Marauder was a hive of focused fury and raw adrenaline, each crewmember intuitively sensing the unfolding confrontation with their shadowy pursuer. Even the ship herself seemed to hum with barely restrained energy as Astrid Ross stalked across the command deck, her eyes locked onto the mainviewport where the approaching threat loomed like a specter of doom.

Astrid halted abruptly before Commander Mercer, who had just finished fine-tuning their evasive maneuvers. Her gaze bored into him, searching for any sign of weakness or doubt. "Well, Mercer?" she demanded, her voice husky and controlled. "What've you got?"

Her question hung heavy between them - a loaded challenge laced with subtle threat that left little room for misinterpretation. Every Marauder crewman watched the exchange with rapt attention, wondering how the outsider would respond to being so openly tested by their volatile captain.

Leo met Astrid's piercing gaze unflinchingly, a thin line etched between his brows in concentration rather than aggression or anxiety. His response was direct, chosen carefully to acknowledge her dominance while asserting his own capabilities in equal measure.

"We've maneuvered into a reasonable position," he said evenly, gestures concise and deliberate as he worked the nav console. "We should be able to outrun our shadow - given everything we know about it. But we may lose our edge quickly if we don't get off this trajectory soon."

Behind him, Slipstream nodded curtly in agreement. "Agreed, Leo. Time's ticking." Her attention flicked to Astrid, her dark eyes gleaming with competitive spirit. "Sounds like Captain Ross is eager to test these theoretical estimates in practice."

A subtle curl of Astrid's lips acknowledged Slipstream's jab. She turned back to face Leo, her stance open but unyielding. "Let's hear your plan then, Commander. Give me something worth following."

Leo swallowed once, his jaw tightening in resolve. This was not the calculated risks and precise calculations of his former world - out here on the razor's edge, there were no margins for error. The only constants were cunning, resourcefulness, and sheer tenacity of will.

He met Astrid's gaze head-on, his words dropping into the charged atmosphere like a challenge thrown down. "I believe we can make a break for that open corridor of gas and cosmic debris five AU to portside. It'll put some serious distance between us and force the hunter to come after us directly, reducing the advantage of the unknown."

As he began sketching out the proposed trajectory on the display, Astrid listened intently, hands clasped behind her back. When he finished, she let out a slow exhale, brow furrowed in thought. "I see what you're getting at," she murmured finally, nodding slightly. "It might work..."

"And," Leo added briskly, already anticipating counterarguments, "it sets up a good potential ambush point ahead, assuming we can pinpoint our quarry's vectors closely enough. With the right ordnance deployed at the optimal strike window, we could inflict sufficient damage to make pursuit hazardous, or even convince our stalker to retreat altogether."

A flicker of intrigue sparked in Astrid's eyes at the boldness of the plan, though her expression stayed neutral. "If our calculations hold true," she allowed, "you might just salvage our hide with some fancy tactics, Mercer."

Leo inclined his head in acknowledgement of her begrudging praise, the corner of his mouth twitching upward.

✦ ✦ ✦

Their plans hatched, the unlikely duo fell into sync as the Starlight Marauder pivoted toward their gambled escape route. Astrid monitored velocity curves as she initiated a carefully plotted series of small thruster firings, nudging them away from the approaching pursuer incrementally further each second. The vessel jerked forward with rhythmic thumps that rattled the control panels around Astrid and reverberated through the skeletal metal decking beneath their feet as if shaking loose millennia of accumulated grime and neglect.

"Hate these old clunkers," Astrid muttered lowly, more to herself than to Leo. "Reliability's always suspect unless you're within shouting distance of a docking bay." She shot him a wry glance, her fingers dancing across the main console's controls with practiced ease, but the corners of her mouth quirked in a hint of mirth anyway. There was no room for leisurely conversation amidst this urgent dash for survival, so neither felt any pressure to elaborate either way.

The rumble of acceleration swelled, then suddenly, jarringly escalated into something much more seismic – a shuddering roar that slammed through every inch of the crippled frigate's hull. Alarms shrieked through the dimmed lighting as warning indicators began to dance across monitor screens like maddened fireflies while the ship lurched harder to stay level. At the same moment, the inertial dampeners strained to offset the sudden savage change in speed and stress, their efforts apparent in the creaking groans emanating from the superstructure.

"What the hell—?" Astrid half-turned from the helm, her voice cut off abruptly by another titanic pulse of thunder that struck the Starlight Marauder from starboard, throwing her and Leo brutally against their restraints as the ship heeled over and shields strobed with repeated energetic blasts. An emergency light flickered to life above them, casting harsh shadows across faces set in grimaces of pain and shock as aftershocks reverberated.

"Weapons status?!" Astrid barked with teeth clenched against the anguish of being tossed against her harness. Sweat beaded on her forehead and ran in rivulets down temples and cheekbones slick with fear.

Leo forced himself upright again, eyes scanning the readouts with desperation despite the world seeming to tilt sickeningly beneath his boots. His heart stuttered in his chest as awareness dawned — the enemy had matched their evasive maneuvers, closing the gap with ruthless efficiency until mere tens of kilometers separated their wildly spiraling ships now.

"And... shit—" Leo winced, jaw tightening. "...we've got a critical failure in the dorsal port warp nozzle vent, likely shredded by debris kicked up during their opening salvo. I don't know how long we can keep running like this before we start shedding major sections."

The Starlight Marauder canted drunkenly beneath them, its trajectory erratic now due to the damaged thrust assembly. Another blast ripped past too close, sending up a shower of sparks from the scorched foredeck. Astrid's gaze snapped to him, eyes narrowing as realization crept in along with dread.

"Time to get real creative then, huh?" She gritted her words as the vessel rocked again, this time from an internal blowout somewhere midships. Steam hissed forth from ruptured pipes as the crew compartment flashed red with emergency lighting.

"Nothing like leaving it to the last minute," Leo quipped weakly, managing gallows humor despite the growing sense of impending doom that knotted his stomach into an impossibly tight coil as the distant speck on their tail grew larger with alarming rapidity.

✦ ✦ ✦

The frantic roar of escaping atmosphere gushed against their eardrums through the shattered viewport as the Starlight Marauder lurched, plumes of superheated plasma geysers erupting from breaches scattered across its hull like macabre fireworks. Shattered fragments and molten debris whizzed past in a deadly hail, the piercing shriek of stressed metal cutting through the chaos. Alarms clamored frantically, a dissonant cacophony blaring through the intercom speakers as damage-control teams scrambled to contain the carnage.

Leo and Astrid struggled to maintain footing amidst the lurching motions, grasping for handholds on the bulkheads as they fought to stay oriented within the cockpit, a nightmarish kaleidoscope of flashing warning lights, flickering instrument panels, and smoke-blackened console displays reflecting their anguished faces. They exchanged grim glances, the unspoken understanding palpable – every breath counted, every decision paramount if they were to survive this infernal gauntlet.

A strident alert pierced the din, pulling their attention to the forward main screen. A stunning vista unfolded before their awestruck eyes, a titanic vortex of roiling purple clouds, turbulent tendrils of ionized plasma stretching miles into the distance. At its heart churned an eye-like void of relative calm, dark and foreboding. Even as they watched, a crackling wave of electromagnetic radiation burst from the core in a blinding flash, bathing the approaching pirate vessel in eerie, violet light.

"G-Gamma-ray flare," stammered one of the junior tactical officers, voice trembling with barely constrained fear. "Pulsar wind nebula... core is either about to go supernova, or we're seeing radiation from an extreme energy event!"

Leo processed the dire implications in seconds flat, grim resolve settling over him like a cloak. They had been driven toward catastrophe, forced into this desperate pincer move, but perhaps fortune could still smile upon them. This wondrous spectacle was also a harbinger – a chance for escape, if only they could seize it.

"We'll ride out the pulse!" Leo commanded, throwing his weight behind the navigation controls. The ship groaned in response, compensating for the erratic input as the thrusters fought to maintain course. The star-drive field began to flicker erratically as the distortions around them mounted, yet held sway for now thanks to Astrid's earlier patchwork magic.

"And get our people prepped!" he ordered sharply. "We pivot into that pulsar light as quickly as possible once the event hits. It might be our only way clear of these sons-of-bitches!"

As if to punctuate his decree, the Marauder juddered severely on its axes, tossing everyone back into their restraints as another colossal explosion tore open the aft of the ship, blooming an incandescent fireball that consumed entire decks almost instantly. Smoke billowed forth, obscuring view of the bridge, and the klaxons reached a deafening pitch.

Cursing under her breath, Astrid barked out orders at her people even as she glanced back at Leo with haunted eyes. "Hope your genius plan plays nice in reality, Earth boy... We're gonna need every scrap of luck just to draw another breathe come what may next."

Before he could respond, the shockwave from the burgeoning gamma-ray storm slammed into them, hammering the pirate frigate like a celestial fist. In that instant of transcendent chaos, the Starlight Marauder hung poised, teetering on an unforgiving razor's edge...
