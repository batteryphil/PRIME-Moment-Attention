# Chapter 12: Gunfight on the Gantry

With Mercury's gravity already pulling at their heels, Leo and Astrid barely registered the jolt as the distressed Starlight Marauder's hull bucked beneath them, a reverberation more felt than heard in this vacuum. Yet even as aftershocks dissipated, they both immediately turned toward the ominous glare of impending doom now less than a parsec ahead of their vessel--too distant to discern details from this remove, but close enough for the eerie, pulsing brightness to scorch retinas and blind without UV filters.

It couldn't be natural. Neither Celestials nor quantum anomalies broadcast such power unfiltered. This was something else altogether, and if the Marauder's shields were compromised... One thing about pirates, their tech generally worked despite questionable priorities over safety standards. Without shields protecting them, that searing light alone would incinerate anything caught in its cone.

"What a fine mess we're in," Astrid quipped dryly, voice laced with the humor born of dire circumstances to stave off true panic. She swiftly scanned the main plotting display, her eyes widening at the rapid closure rate with the accreting celestial horror. "Looks like our ticket home just got jammed in high orbit beyond reach."

Leo nodded curtly, attention laser-focused on gauging escape vectors under gravity and propulsion constraints. No chance to sprint along the gantry outside in normal-Earth gee; they'd never keep up in this low-grav environment with acceleration dead opposite Mercury's pull. His hand involuntarily tightened around the grip of his silenced railcarbine as analysis ran through neural nets. One option presented itself amid the dire predictions: venting the Marauder's cargo holds to shift mass-center to reduce exposure during descent.

"You'd better prep our exit pods, Chief-- "

Astrid gestured emphatically before he could finish suggesting those escape vessels designed for one-shot hyperspace jumps, built with crash-and-abort protocols already engaged given the unfolding catastrophe. The corsair captain's urgency translated visually through clenched fists beating against thighs; frustration, fear, and acceptance wrestled for dominance across her battle-scarred visage.

"Escape pods aren't going anywhere, not with our engines offline either," she explained grimly. "Our only remaining option is EVA, suit up--"

Leo jerked a nod, intuition screaming for him to prioritize immediate action. He started toward the airlock, hand drifting back to tuck a spare energy cell into a pocket of his flight suit for emergency power. Astrid fell seamlessly into lockstep behind, her imposing presence making him acutely aware of every labored breath, every tiny tremor of expectation carrying the weight of imminent destruction. Even in silence, death loomed a mere second away.

The airlock hissed open, allowing them to clamber inside before the cycle started and the inner door sealed. On the other side awaited the cold dark of spacerock's surface, littered with chunks of asteroid and ice, waiting to skewer or crush them with equal indifference. Leo felt the hairs on his nape prickle yet steeled himself mentally for the punishing ordeal to come.

"...and pray there's solid ground close enough once we hit atmosphere," Astrid finished succinctly as the outer hatch cycled, the warning sirens dying along with the last dregs of life support back on their besieged vessel.

In reality, reaching terra firma on Mercury with standard 1G equipment wasn't feasible. Even discounted suits boasted grav-manipulation, far short of orbital-level inertia.

His ears rang from the alarm blasts and a metallic cacophony of screeching hull plating under assault. The Starlight Marauder seemed to scream around him, rebelling against its violent torments.

Leo's disoriented gaze tracked the streaking impact craters across astrogation holo-realities flickering to life within the cramped bridge, each one an unseen killing stroke delivered by unknown assailants cloaked in Mercury's shadowy depths. A fresh surge of crimson alarms pierced eardrums already stretched taut.

"We're taking too much damage for a routine boarding," Astrid intoned over comms, voice laced with clinical detachment belying the storm churning beneath. Her calloused fingers danced across console interfaces in a blur of diagnostics and fire control overrides. "Defensive measures are spent. Our only hope lies on the rock outside."

The abyss of exposure called hungrily from just beyond the lock, promising brutal unforgiving cold and merciless pressure that would liquefy living tissue given seconds without protection.

"Get suited up, commander." Cold calculation seeped into Astrid's edgy command, steeling around her eyes like nightfall over some unyielding precipice. With laser precision, Leo lunged for the airlock, fighting gravity and his own racing heart in a dizzying spiral toward salvation. Behind him, Astrid followed like a black panther, fluid and deadly, her eyes locked firmly on escape as her thoughts no doubt teemed with escape strategies and contingency plans – after all, an ill-timed misstep outside might spell disaster just as surely as the hail of projectiles bearing down upon them now...

The airlock cycled open with a sudden rush of sub-zero slush - airless, motionless darkness that yawned wider than the Grand Canyon awaiting those bold enough to step into its maw. In this final instant before the vacuum claimed them, trapped between the abyss and the last shreds of life-sustaining air within their battered spacecraft, Astrid fixed Leo with an expression that was equal parts a dare and a prophecy:

Into Mercury's Scorching Abyss

Astrid grabbed Leo's gloved hand, securing it tightly against hers even as she braced the other against the durasteel bulkhead to steady herself. They were pressed chest-to-chest in the cramped airlock, facing away from the hissing hatch seals and towards the yawning void. Each held their breath, hearts pounding in unison against the confines of their pressure suits.

Outside, stars blazed like diamonds scattered across velvet. The planet Mercury loomed larger with every passing second—a searing metal orb consumed by reflected solar fury. Astrid's gaze never wavered from their target, the tiny, fleeing dot representing their crippled ship rapidly dwindling beneath the horizon.

Leo checked their tether, giving a curt nod of confirmation before he switched his helmet comms to the external frequency. "Starlight Marauder, priority distress signal transmission—this is Commander Mercer and Captain Ross. We are about to attempt an emergency rendezvous at orbit twelve point niner." 

The pirate vessel's AI responded immediately, smooth contralto tones undeterred by the distance between ships: "Receiving, Commander. Initiate your approach vector now."

With a fierce twist of Astrid's wrist, they detached themselves from the station. Weightlessness enveloped them instantly, and for a brief moment, she allowed herself to savor the surreal beauty surrounding them before zeroing back in on their imperative mission. Astrid propelled herself and Leo through the void, each precise movement calculated to maintain precious momentum while conserving energy.

As Mercury loomed nearer, its blistering heat painted the inner surfaces of their helmets with sanguine hues and turned the dark expanse around them a hazy, hellish orange. Astrid knew time was running out—their brief window to connect with the Marauder was closing fast.

She initiated a final course correction, pushing off expertly to intercept their ship just shy of grazing Mercury's surface. Alarms began to screech warnings as Leo took the helm, working in tandem with Astrid despite the chasmic distances and centuries separating their origins.

Their ships collided with a jarring metallic crash that sent bone-jarring seismic shockwaves pulsating through the hull—dampened but still violent thanks to the superior engineering of the marauder class. Smoke wafted, and sparks erupted from compromised power conduits, yet both vessels remained structurally intact.

"Well played, Captain," Leo radioed over the ship-to-ship channel, grinning wearily as Astrid helped wrest him through the gaping breach.

"We're not out of this yet, Commander," Astrid shot back, already assessing the damage and plotting their next steps. Her voice carried an edge, both exhaustion-fueled and battle-hardened—a testament to the unforgiving nature of their odyssey so far. "There's still debris and possible hostile craft in the area..."

Leo merely nodded sagely, his visor tinting crimson as he inspected the ruinous interior of what once was their cockpit. The charnel house of twisted steel framing, shattered consoles, and scorched upholstery spoke volumes about the hellfires that had ravaged their stolen Kestrel transport before its desperate plunge back into deep space to meet Astrid's rescue gamble head-on.

"I know," he rasped before trailing off, struggling visibly to keep the despair behind his masks. Even now, with death's dark shadow finally held at bay due to Astrid and the Starlight's intervention, grief could come later—and would likely hit harder when the adrenaline receded.

Leo cradled his ruined right forearm, a grimace tugging at his lips. Though not actively bleeding, the charred skin cracked open whenever he tried to flex, stoking waves of pain against his will. He squinted toward the ruptured viewport, where starlight struggled to infiltrate the clouded plenum of gas and ionized dust that surrounded them—a thin, ethereal blanket of interstellar haze drifting on solar wind across billions of miles of empty cosmos.

"Well, Captain Vex..." His voice croaked like sandpaper on bone. "...looks like we're stuck. No navigational data left to steer by...no fuel to make a break for it either. And it'll take weeks if not months just to scrounge up replacement parts—if you can find a black market deal maker with access to Type-2A shields and maneuvering thrusters." 

The ship creaked around them, settling into her final rest like a bird losing flight feathers, unable to take wing ever again. Astrid nodded soberly, eyes narrowed into glints of appraisal within her silver mask—as though sizing up any potential weaknesses she might exploit in her own predicament, trapped alongside the alien warlord who had brought this on herself. For good reason, Leo knew—that same ruthlessness had earned her title as a corsair legend, making deals with demons and ghosts where most sane beings would draw red lines.

"The local stars don't show much promise for repairs anyway," Astrid allowed after a long pause, voice echoing quietly from her suit comm system. "No significant gravity wells, and definitely no jump gates out here this side of the Orion Spur." Her gloved hand found his injured forearm, giving it a reassuring squeeze despite her own evident weariness etched into every angle and line of her posture. A surprising gesture, given their tumultuous history—but then again it was only fitting of a woman who defied borders and allegiances where loyalty was a currency paid in blood. Still, he felt a fleeting spark of gratitude in spite of himself.

"What does one do after a crash, then?" Leo muttered bleakly, blinking away vision distortions. At his query, the viewport suddenly flared bright enough to eclipse his pupils momentarily, bathing the gantry in stark relief as they shielded their eyes against the searing light.

"No idea," Astrid admitted as the flare faded slowly, casting everything in a diffuse luminosity that seemed almost surreal amidst the wreckage. In this sterile realm without echoes where vacuum's chill darkness clung to each surface with unyielding persistence—soundless, unscented, utterly alien—the pair stood frozen, staring at the glowing portal now visible beyond the jagged opening previously concealed by hull plating.

An unmistakable wormhole signature—it pulsed rhythmically as though alive, swirling with aurora-shimmering quantum foam in impossible hues that danced across spectrums normally seen by human eyes. Without bothering to consult charts or manuals, an inexplicable sense told Leo they were witnessing some arcane phenomenon well outside the purview of even their star-mapped universe—and yet...

"...according to all known models, this configuration suggests a stable gateway connecting two regions possessing vastly different energies in space-time." Astrid murmured softly once her gaze adjusted to focus past the mesmerizing display, tone tinged with a mix of amazement and foreboding. Whatever lurked on the other end of this tunnel piercing dimensions unseen—be it paradise, apocalypse, or something far more sinister beneath classification—Leo understood implicitly that no decision taken here would remain purely their own.
