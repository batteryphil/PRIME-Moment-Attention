# Chapter 13: Breaching the High Security Block

Commander Leo Mercer strained against the massive shock-mounted servos securing the gantry to his rig, watching as Astrid writhed atop a tangled mass of severed power conduits. Flames licked at the frayed edge of his flight suit, heat radiating through the carbon-fiber mesh to scorch his thighs.

"Hold steady!" she panted between gritted teeth, reaching for the blow torch strapped to his thigh.

Leo couldn't spare breath for reply. His core muscles screamed with sustained effort as he tried to keep the servos locked fast. The wormhole's light pulse thrummed a staccato beat in harmony with his pounding heart, filling his vision with an otherworldly glow. Sweat poured down his face, but evaporated instantly in the sub-zero climate before he could feel its warmth.

"Astrid—" he choked out warningly, noticing her gaze had drifted back towards the pulsating vortex swallowing half the viewport. In a sudden burst of insight, Leo realized her eyes glowed with a feverish brightness, reflections of the celestial anomalies dancing within.

In a fluid motion, he activated the built-in hydraulic overrides, allowing the servos to retract while maintaining control. As the restraints eased, Leo lunged forward, grabbing Astrid's wrist to halt her aim at the portal. Her blowtorch hissed out a futile plume of sparks into a void devoid of oxygen.

"Don't risk firing that near the wormhole," Leo gritted. "We don't know how it'll interact with our tech or radiation shielding!"

Astrid jerked her arm free, but he caught hold of her other hand in a vice-like grip. She met his gaze with fiery defiance, lips curled in a snarl. A current of raw electricity crackled through their joined bodies under the starscraper's artificial ambient field—a potent mixture of attraction and resentment.

"You always do this, Leo," Astrid snapped. "Micro-manage until you stifle every creative thought or daring impulse I have! Just because your primitive linear mentality can't grasp the implications"

"You think I'm restraining innovation?" Leo shot back fiercely. "I'm trying not to get us killed off in a catastrophic experiment we're woefully unprepared for! When it comes to your instincts and hunches, I trust them. But this is beyond either of our paygrades. We need information and strategy before—"

The wormhole flickered violently, casting an eerie strobe effect over the duo and their ravaged surroundings. It made Astrid's enhanced irises seem to swallow whole clusters of the chromatic fire raging inside the singularity. For a moment, Leo swore his vision blurred as if distorted by some unfathomable curvature of space. Astrid's features warped too, reflected in the plasma dance as if they both existed outside the three-dimensional bounds of reality in those chaotic instants.

They stared at each other transfixed, neither fully present in the momentary confluence of temporal planes and energy flux. Then, in the next cycle of the wormhole's rhythmic spasms, Leo shook his head to regain focus. Their gazes finally broke apart as if electrified from a charged arc.

"...we breach the adjacent compartment," Astro finished lowly, pulling herself up with trembling arms onto the crumbled husk of the airlock door. It creaked alarmingly as she put weight upon it. The metallic groans echoed strangely in the emptiness of space, magnified by the acoustic dampeners long since destroyed. "Find whatever hardware and data you can salvage to improve our chances of manipulating this thing safely."

Leo watched, heart thrumming, as Astrid climbed atop the disintegrating airlock hatch. Her stance was precarious, knees bent beneath her, one hand anchoring while the other grasped for purchase anywhere in the mangled metal. The wormhole continued its frenzied pulsing beyond, casting shifting hues across their haggard bodies like some cosmic, maddening disco light show. The silence outside the Marauder was absolute, void of even the comforting hiss of life support - the abyssal vacuum embracing them without respite.

"How...sturdy do ya think that'll hold?" Leo ventured, voice barely above a whisper against the sonic wasteland stretching out from the ship. His eyes remained fixed on Astrid's lithe form perched precariously above the fraying threshold. Even through her skintight spacesuit, he could sense her physical strain, the labored breathing and tensing muscles as she sought to stabilize herself. He understood that desperation-fueled resilience all too well.

Astrid flashed him a grim smile over her shoulder, not breaking eye contact. "Guess I should've brought my trusty ladder, eh? Just gotta hope enough of this junk holds together." She paused, considering the implications. "Considering we got dropped into here unprepped and still recovering from the fall... might be our best bet so far."

"Falls ain't exactly my strong suit," Leo muttered ruefully, running a nervous hand through his wind-tousled hair. But there was no time for self-pity. Any second the entire structure around Astrid could plummet like a dying warbird into the hungry jaws of the singularity below. Against such unimaginable forces, a few more tons of scrap steel didn't mean much.

"Astro—"
"What?" Her gaze snapped back to meet his, amber orbs alight with a primal mix of grit and fear.

"This feels...bad. Like tempting fate or something." He held her stare, willing her to understand the old-fashioned gut check happening amidst the stars. "We're already skating damn close to certain doom out here. Letting you balance on top of a broken airlock feels like inviting a little extra catastrophe..."

His words trailed off as realization struck him like a plasma pulse to the chest. What was he even saying? That they should just give up, retreat back to the relative safety of their crippled vessel and pray fate went easy on them? No, hell no. He couldn't let those cowardly thoughts take root. Not when Astrid was out there risking everything because one crazy risk mattered more than staying alive. A feeling akin to shame gnawed at his resolve.

"It's okay, Commander. I know what I'm doing here," Astrid said firmly, as if sensing his growing inner turmoil. Those honeyed words carried the subtle cadence of command rather than camaraderie this time. Beneath her tone lay an undercurrent of 'back the hell off,' letting Leo know he'd tread a fine line as her second-in-command aboard the Marauder.

The heavy silence that followed was oppressive, straining with the weight of expectations and raw, unspoken emotions bubbling just beneath the surface. The wormhole's erratic flashes seemed to pulse in sync with the racing beats of their hearts. Finally, unable to bear any longer the seething storm of frustration, resentment, and admiration battling within, Leo pushed himself upright, hands bracing against his thighs.

"Good. Because we've got a job to finish and an Empire to bring to its goddamn knees," he managed through clenched teeth, the old military cadence breaking free despite his better judgment. "Let's get moving."

The clanging clangor of rapid hatches announced their entry into the heavily fortified sector of Kyros Orbital's research hub. Lieutenant Saez, eyes wide with a mix of apprehension and excitement, fumbled with his plasma rifle, trying not to get trampled in the chaotic advance of the Marauder's boarding party.

"We're in deep now," Leo muttered, voice barely audible over the rattling of armor plates and shouting commands. His gut twisted like a corkscrew, but he steeled himself to face whatever terrors awaited beyond the airlock. The flickering blue lights cast eerie shadows along the corridor's sealed metal bulkheads, as if some malevolent entity lurked in the darkness, waiting to claim souls for the ages.

Behind him, Astrid's boots clicked sharply against the decking, each deliberate step exuding confidence born from years of defying death. Her scent, a heady blend of cool sweat, sharp ozone, and an unseen essence uniquely hers, tantalized Leo's senses, a fleeting reminder of the perilous game they played so intimately entangled.

Time stretched, distorting the rhythm of pulse and breath. Each labored inhale was bitter, metallic – recycled air thickened by fear, adrenaline, and the acrid tang of ion-scraped metal. Every exhale shook the mask covering Leo's face, a frail barrier against the choking void outside. The silence was oppressive, punctuated only by muffled alarms issuing from distant nodes and the heavy rasp of their breathing.

Leo's hands trembled slightly, betraying the turmoil threatening to unravel his composure. Memories crashed anew – his first spacewalk, the horrific loss of the Challenger, Gagarin's triumphant orbit... all jumbled into a sense-deadening cacophony. It was almost too late when Astrid grabbed his arm, hauling him forward.

"Hold up, Lieutenant!" she snapped, her voice crisp and commanding amidst the chaos. They'd reached an armored bulkhead marked with imperial crests and warning placards. A pulsing energy field seared across the surface, humming with the promise of instantaneous disintegration for any daredevil foolish enough to breach the defenses uninvited.

Astrid's fingers danced deftly across a small holographic control panel mounted nearest Leo's shoulder. Vectors converged, calculations sped along hidden data streams, and an oscillating resonance began to build within the shielding emitters. The energy mesh danced wildly, sputtering weakly as Astrid bypassed the security protocols.

She glanced back over her shoulder, eyes blazing with triumph and something else, darker still beneath, that made Leo's heart thud erratically against his ribcage. "Ready?" Without giving him a chance to respond, she triggered the overload resonance, sending an avalanche of turbulent energy crashing through the shield wall.

In a burst of strobing light, crackling atmosphere, and the smell of scorched micro-circuits, they squeezed through the breach. On the other side, they found themselves in a narrow corridor lined with closed blast doors, pulsing life support conduits, and towering storage pods loaded with top-secret project crates.

"Well, damn," Leo breathed, still squinting against the afterglow of their incursion, when a sudden, piercing scream shattered the momentary hush. It echoed down the passageway, a chilling howl that turned Leo's blood to ice. Whatever had made that unearthly noise was close, fast approaching – and far from friendly.

Astrid's gaze locked onto Leo's with an implicit understanding. Their mission had just acquired a whole lot Deadlier.

CHAPTER 13: BREACHING THE HIGH SECURITY BLOCK

The concussive force of their transit through the shield door hurled Leo forward, slamming him against the wall. Pain ignited across his knuckles where he'd instinctively braced his impact suit. He slid down, catching himself on his heels, and stared wide-eyed at the chaotic corridor beyond the smoking breach. Emergency lighting flickered fitfully, casting long shadows across the narrow passageway.

Leo's breath sounded harsh in his ears amid the echoing thrum of damaged shielding and suppressed alarm klaxons. Glancing over, he saw Astrid step out of the glow, already scanning their surroundings with cold predatory interest. Her black hair rippled like nightfall beneath the pale light. Her expression - lean-lippered focus - spoke of steel-sharpened reflexes and intellect honed by decades of predation in the stars.

"Well, damn," he murmured, struggling to regain his bearings amidst the disorienting kaleidoscope of alien technology. Their daring penetration of the secret block already felt like walking the razor's edge of danger.

Stroking back sweat-slick hair, Leo pointed toward the open storage pods lining the corridor. Each crate was clearly labeled Project: Orion-Skyguard. Classified intel and advanced weapons perhaps? He wondered what nefarious plans the Emissaries might be constructing behind these sealed doors.

Just then, Astrid's head whipped around sharply, features contorting in a grimace of alarm. Following her gaze, Leo caught sight of movement further down the passage - faint outlines darting between towering tech cylinders.

"What in–?"

A gut-wrenching scream tore the air, the cry of something primal and tormented. It sent icy pricks racing up Leo's spine as the echoes reverberated off the metal deck. That awful noise wasn't machinery malfunctioning - it was definitely alive...

And headed straight for them.

Astrid's lips flattened to a ruthless line. Her rifle snapped free from its holder as she edged to the corridor's center, pressing into the shadowy bulk of a supply pod. The howls grew louder, closer, until Leo could discern frantic, bestial panting punctuated by snarls born from unutterable pain and fury.

He fumbled for his own pulse carbine, finger hovering reluctantly above the trigger. Whatever beast this was, they were defenseless in the cramped, exposed corridor without cover. He prayed it was just some deranged research subject; but the unnatural shrieks kindled a sickening suspicion that the creatures hiding among the high-security assets might have designs more malignant than mere experimentation gone sour...

Astral's fiery amber gaze met Leo's terrified blue stare. Though not a word passed between them, the message transmitted in those searing seconds was unmistakable - we're in for the fight of our lives. The floor trembled under claw-beats, warning them barely a second before a massive, lopsided creature burst into view around the next bend, preceded by a halo of reflected emergency lights...
