# Chapter 10: Stealing a Relic


Commander Leo Mercer materialized on the dimly lit impound bay floor in disorienting free-fall. The chill air nipped at his exposed skin as he drifted downward past the hangar cranes and docking ports of the Imperial vessel, Manticore's Fury. He snagged a loose strut to arrest his momentum, then braced for the painful strain as his body mass reoriented under artificial gravity. 

Swallowing dryly, he surveyed the sleek lines of expensive spacecraft arrayed around him – gossamer-hulled light skirmishers and heavily armored dreadnoughts awaiting their masters' return. The metallic tang of ozone filled his nostrils, mixing uncomfortably with the acrid residue of spent ion thrusters. Every breath of air recycled through the atmosphere processors tasted stale as forgotten regrets. This place had been designed to efficiently warehouse, service, and secure Imperial assets, not hum with warmth and purpose.

Leo made haste for a solitary fighter slumbering against the far wall – a prototype Xarlan Strike-3 interceptor, its angular surfaces a latticework of composite materials punctuated by weapons bays and redundant engine nozzles. Rumblings of approaching footsteps in the corridor prompted him to focus intently on remembering every detail of the ship's security systems and access codes gleaned from hijacked databases. Hacking and disabling defenses, however sophisticated, required methodical skill applied rapidly under duress and the very wrong circumstances.

His gloved hands hovered over the fighter's sleek hull, tracing the line where matte black armor met polished chrome canard. As he found the correct seam hiding magnetically secured panels, Leo steeled himself for what would surely follow. He just hoped an alert patrolling officer wouldn't spot his intrusion before he could get airborne and make good his escape.

With a quiet hiss of released seals and sliding components, the fighter's portside panel swung open, flooding the hangar with the harsh glare of instruments and warning lights. Leo plunged into the frigid, humid cocoon within and got to work preflighting the jet to minimum delay standards, fingers moving deftly over switches, grips, and readouts set into raised handrests as he felt his way past wires, hydraulic lines, and various maintenance modules toward his seat behind the cockpit canopy.

The aircraft's automated navigation systems were currently off-line, relying upon a combination of manual inputs tied to astro charts stored onboard. Leo mentally converted galactic coordinates to local star frame references used by Imperial pilots, calculating estimated inertial accelerations based on delta-v reserve stores and flight path efficiency. Once satisfied they left no obvious breadcrumbs or error margins in their wake, he fired up the engines and settled back into the seat, grasping the throttle grip lightly but firmly.

Cold metal and ceramic touched bare flesh across multiple points along his torso beneath torn uniform shirt – faceplate ridges, armrest controls, lever switches, the thin frame of the ejector harness cinching tight around his hips just above the crotch ring. Each surface served both functional and ergonomic purposes sculpted by decades-spanning traditions honoring the unyielding physical boundaries of space travel.

Leo took a final moment to steady himself against the mounting heart rate signaled by his watch monitor. With a deep breath, he cycled the cockpit canopy shut, sealing them together in claustrophobic heat-trapping intimacy. The jolting thrusters ignited, hurling the commandeered Imperial fighter forward at blistering speed. Leo's seat harness held fast as gees pressed mercilessly into him from multiple angles. Behind closed blast shutters, stars streaked past in kaleidoscopic diagonals—the universe a blurred rainbow hellhole hurtling itself towards infinity.

Leo grunted, straining against the crushing forces bearing down upon him. His chest plate and helmet compressors struggled to maintain a sustainable airflow. Aural warnings shrieked in his ears like tortured animals. Sweat beaded on his forehead, dripping into the sealed visor. The cockpit reeked of ozone, hot plastic, and scorched metal—intoxicating, yet acrid, the olfactory cocktail characteristic of Imperial military craftsmanship.

At last, after an agonizing minute, the thrust died away as the fighter hit orbit proper, climbing steeply away from the planet's crust on fumes and inertia alone. Exhaustions washed through Leo like tidal waves as he felt the gee force gradually slacken. Hands shaking, he slowly disengaged the harness, letting his limbs flutter free in the near weightlessness within the cramped chamber.

Discharge crackled across the starboard canopy as the Marauder banked hard in pursuit of an escaping escort frigate. Leo fought the unresponsive yoke with grim tenacity born from countless hours mastering antique spacecraft controls in virtual simulations.

Astrid's voice cut through background noise with icy precision as she guided her co-pilot. "Leo. Engage main thrusters. Now."

Ignoring her curt tone didn't spare him from a sharp elbow to the ribs. Leo grunted, doubling down on manual control stick inputs amidst the rumble and vibration of straining engines.

The bridge deck lurched, throwing them both against safety harnesses. Alarms shrieked through sealed cabin walls while an ominous geyser of flame erupted outside the portside viewer—likely shredded armor plating spewing forth vaporized alloy and lubricants.

"Helm hit bad!" shouted his tac sergeant over comlink hiss. "Rolling lateral stabilizers—"

An abrupt drop pitched the ship downward as if caught in a gravitational well, hurling Leo sideways into Astrid. Her hip jammed against his, shoulder blades pressed together, their faces a hairbreadth apart amidst the cacophony and disorienting turbulence.

Cabin lights strobed wildly, casting the narrow confines in a dizzying dance of crimson and violet. In split seconds, they found themselves locked together, struggling to maintain balance on the precarious shifting of reverb-dampened floorplates beneath their feet.

Leo smelled ozone and heated metal leaking past seals, alongside a jarring whiff of musk from their closely confined bodies. The scent brought back memories of cramped cockpits, stale air recycling, and the intimate contact no matter how brief those high-risk missions were.

Over the din, Astrid growled a cursory instruction. "Re-target thruster vents on the frigate—a single shot—give us our opening."

Nimble fingers danced expertly across control surfaces even as her chest pressed flush against his. Leo's hand drifted involuntarily to the curve of her hip, the touch so fleeting yet electric in the chaos. Then Astrid lunged away from him at a dead run toward the navigation console.

He watched her disappear into the aisle, barely registering the sudden warmth suffusing his cheeks—an unfamiliar sensation beneath the cold stoicism of prolonged survival stress. For a fleeting moment, Leo allowed himself to imagine what lay hidden beneath all that abrasive bravado—

Only to be snapped out as Astrid hurled herself against the tactical nav array, hands racing to configure targeting parameters and fire solutions on the desperate flyby of a vulnerable target. 

Time seemed to stretch then, elastic in its compressive gravity. The air crackled once again, static electricity arcing as ship hull systems scrambled to compensate for stresses inflicted upon them from without and within.

Mercifully, the crisis passed, leaving in its wake nothing but the ringing shockwave of spent energy reverberating through stressed alloys and rattling the bones of any unfortunate soul still standing upright. 

Chest heaving from exertion, Astrid straightened and turned to face him, battle-lashed gaze smoldering with the aftermath of near-death urgency combined with lingering resentment, her flushed cheeks matching the rosy glow reflecting off sweat-beaded skin from shared strain and heat.

Leo met her look head-on, his heart hammering against ribcage confinement like a trapped bird seeking escape, the intimate memory of her body's press against his now burning brightly in his mind despite the clamor of surrounding noise.

No words exchanged in the charged moment, communication reduced solely to the searing intensity piercing their respective gazes and the stubborn refusal to yield even an inch in the face.

Leo stood transfixed by Astrid's scorching stare, caught in the eye of a tempestuous vortex swirling with adrenaline-fueled desire, lingering fear, simmering rivalry, and an undeniable attraction building like an approaching supernova. In this suspended moment, no thought could pierce the cocoon of raw sensation enveloping him—a palpable electric current pulsing wherever astral bodies might touch, sparking neural synapses into overdrive.

Then, to sever the molten thread connecting them, a staccato rhythm punctuated the air—warning klaxons announcing an external contact acquisition. The proximity alarm, once a distant threat indicator, had closed in to seconds. Astrid's warrior instincts sprang back into focus, her expression sharpening into one of pure, unfiltered intent as she whirled toward the main viewscreen.

Mercer followed in her wake, pupils dilating as vision corrected from afterimages of electromagnetic chaos to reveal the celestial canvas unfolding before them. Looming at the edges of navigational sensors was an otherworldly spectacle—stellar nurseries threaded along filaments of dark matter, luminous gas clouds sculpted into celestial tableaux by ancient stellar winds. At the center of it all floated an impossibly massive, luminous artifact—the long-sought relic Astrid had risked everything to steal.

A shock of primal awe tinged with trepidation rippled through Leo, the enormity of discovery hitting him with the force of a gravitational wave—he and Astrid now possessed the key to unlocking civilization-spanning secrets...and the Empire would stop at nothing to reclaim it.

Time no longer stretched under this monumental revelation; it snapped taut as a bowstring. Every second counted, every decision made or contemplated harbored galaxy-altering consequences. With the relic hanging tantalizingly close, a maelstrom of impulses churned within Astrid—desire to claim her prize, dread of failure, wrath toward the oppressors driving humanity to desperation.

She wheeled around, eyes flashing as they alighted upon Leo's visage, searching, demanding answers without voice. Mercer took a bracing breath, the antithesis of her fiery passion settling cool and steady within his chest—a beacon of rational thinking amidst the tempestuous emotional currents surrounding them both.

In that pivotal instant, a bond was forged—it wasn't about love or loyalty, though those too soon ran strong—first and foremost it was an alliance forged from a shared awareness of the weighty burden now resting on their shoulders. Together, they vowed to harness the power bestowed upon them, steering humanity toward freedom rather than ruin. And, in doing so, forge a truth out of the lie that had defined their lives thus far.

Astrid jerked her chin, the single flick of her head a silent command—a call to arms echoing throughout the ship's holds, rousing sleepy warriors, engineers, hackers all into alert status. "We get what we came for," she growled, the promise ripe with unspoken menace. "Now let's go get the damn thing."
