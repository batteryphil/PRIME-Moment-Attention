# Chapter 11: Blackout at Sector 7


Leo materialized in the dimly glowing bridge, straining against inertial dampeners designed to prevent even the slightest motion during hyperspace jumps. He landed heavily amidst the low hum of systems powering back up, disoriented by the sudden shift to conventional propulsion. The Marauder eased into its sleek profile, gleaming alloys painted pitch black under the artificial illumination of instrument panels and control interfaces.

"It's like a tomb in here," Astrid muttered from where she gripped the pilot's seat. Her gloved hand cradled the disruptor pistol holstered across her knee, ready to unleash kinetic death if need be. The air felt heavy, recycled and slightly stale, a reminder that the Starlight's exterior shell was breached. A faint tang of ozone mingled with the coppery scent of spilled blood—and his own sweat, the telltale clamminess not entirely due to the lingering microgravity. His heart pounded in anticipation of the unknown dangers lying ahead.

"Alright, let's check the situation," Leo said over the intercom as he slid into the copilot's chair. His fingers danced nimbly across the holographic controls springing to life before him. He winced when the nav display revealed their location—a godforsaken pocket of space on the rim of inhabited zones. Just where Orlo had promised them the data relay would be hiding in plain sight. "Looks like our friend made good on that threat."

On nearby monitors, grainy feeds showed armed patrols closing in on the Marauder from multiple angles—their sleek craft adorned with crimson Imperial eagles. Merciless starboard-facing laser cannons winked open along the freighter's flanks in preparation for intercept.

"Too much firepower to outrun outright," Astrid agreed grimly. "Better make peace with the prospect of an intimate dance with our adversaries." She tapped commands into the main screen, bringing external cameras online. Thermal signatures blazed across the void like distant suns as the patrol ships opened fire on range, ion blasts churning the darkness and sending fragments of melted metal spinning away from burning hulks.

"Helluva welcome party," Leo grumbled, eyeing the devastation unfold on screen. Unshielded hull plating couldn't resist the onslaught, seams splitting open under relentless barrage—hollow echoes of tortured metal as their opponents tore the Marauder apart. "EMP must've fried most of our comm gear, too. Can't raise anyone on the station or sector wide net." No way to hail for backup, stranded alone amongst hostile craft... This really was just him, Vex, and whoever else still drew breath onboard against overwhelming odds. A pit formed in his stomach as the tactical display updated to show three cruisers and half a dozen fighters pincer-maneuvering to surround and cut off any potential escape.

Astrid bit back a curse while scanning navigation options, her face a mask of serene focus betrayed only by the tight white line of her lips and the slight flex of clenched fists against thighs. Sweat trickled down the sides of her face, evaporating instantly in the chilled confines. When she finally spoke, her voice carried the calm, controlled edge of battle-hardened veterans everywhere: "All right, we'll improvise."

Leo leaned forward in his command seat, hands clasped behind his head as he studied the tactical map scrolling across the main viewer. The situation was grim – five heavy cruisers and eight fighter interceptors moving in to hem them in. All it would take was one lucky hit to cripple the Marauder.

"Astrid, we need to-" His words caught in his throat as the pirate commander suddenly launched herself across the width of the cockpit, slamming a cylindrical object into a port nestled just above the control arrays. A shower of sparks erupted from the panel as she crouched over it with fierce intensity. Even without touching, Leo could feel the deck reverberate from the impact.

"What the devil was that?" he asked, genuine disbelief dripping from each word. The last he'd seen, they were running blind after taking severe damage from hostile fire.

Astrid straightened up, wiping sweat from her brow with the sleeve of her tactical jacket. Her dark hair stuck out wildly in every direction from a crash helmet she'd somehow neglected.

"Hacking equipment you so thoughtfully provided. Trying to bring up some functioning comms." She scowled, tapping a gloved finger against another console. "Shame about those patrol boats cutting our external antennae, eh? Made my job rather more difficult."

Leo blinked rapidly, struggling to reconcile her casual nonchalance with the adrenaline-pumping chaos unfolding outside. They were literally under attack and this woman seemed almost amused by her own technical acrobatics.

"You have a very... unconventional coping mechanism," he managed to say. "Isn't there something called 'remaining calm under pressure' back where you're from? Because if I didn't know better, I'd think you just enjoyed seeing our ship tear itself to pieces to spite me."

That earned him raised eyebrows and an incredulous snort from Astrid. "Oh please, Commander Mercer. Let's cut the cute routine, shall we? You aren't exactly Mr. Chill yourself, despite that rosy-cheeked 'ooh what a fine mess we're in' act you pulled aboard." She cocked her hip and fixed him with an unsettlingly level stare, arms crossed over her chest. "We both know your heart's racing about as fast as mine and yours isn't exactly concealed under several inches of armor-plated muscle."

Leo felt his cheeks flush hot at that pointed assessment. When had she gotten so perceptive in just a handful of days'? He shifted uncomfortably under her gaze before settling on a defiant grin, deciding to call her bluff.

"Oh don't give me credit for introspection I clearly don't possess. You seem fixated on getting under people's skin today." He waved around at the shattered viewports and flickering systems warning screens. "Care to tell me how much longer we can keep playing this game before someone actually bites the big one? Preferably not us, given you seem to prefer us alive for reasons unknown."

"I like a good challenge," Astrid retorted simply, meeting his impish smile with equal arrogance. "Besides," she added, her tone turning speculative, "seems like you're adapting quickly enough considering you started out so far removed from everything I do for a living."

Despite himself, Leo found his annoyance simmering closer to fascination as Astrid's enigmatic presence filled the small command room. There was something captivating about the pirate's unflappable confidence bordering on recklessness.

"Just how long have you been out here pirating in interstellar no man's land, anyway?" he posed aloud, genuinely curious now. Any normal human being would've fallen apart months ago facing threats like these on a daily basis.

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo's challenging question hung heavy in the thin air of the command chamber, echoing slightly off its metal walls lined with circuitboards and control consoles in disarray from yet another chaotic system failure. Yet as Astrid absorbed his probing scrutiny through hooded eyes, those eerie irises seemed to swirl with stardust rather than betray any sign of unease.

"A century and change by your measurement," she replied calmly, her voice muffled slightly by the respirator mask clutched tightly to her face as wisps of cold, acrid smoke wafted from the vent ducts high overhead. She leaned back into her chair now, stretching the taut leather of her flight suit across corded muscles barely covered by her singed cape.

"Time travels different out in the expanse where stars birth worlds anew each moment," Astrid continued, gazing upwards towards the darkened main viewport, its fragmented remains allowing splinters of sector light to bleed crimson against the inky blackness beyond the wrecked hull plating.

"Moon cycles, market prices of xenon fuel... even personal lifespans can span generations depending on which side of a wormhole jump you find yourself while crossing realities."

Her words painted an awe-inspiring, unfathomable tapestry of lives lived beyond the scope of Earth's limited understanding, civilizations born, thrived and crumbled among the swirling chaos of interstellar frontier.

"You adapted remarkably fast considering," Astrid remarked, shifting her gaze back down to meet Leo's startled stare. Her features softened infinitesimally beneath the harsh glow of the emergency lighting—something akin to genuine admiration glimmered within the depths of those otherworldly eyes.

"You humans may be slow to evolve, but you have remarkable tenacity once prodded. Quite the contrast from my kin..." She let that last observation trail off on a thoughtful whisper, almost to herself before focusing sharply back on him.

"Flying ships is more than just muscle memory and brute reaction, Commander. It takes intellect, cunning, intuition—and knowing when someone's full attention isn't solely on the controls, not just in critical engagements." With a deft motion, Asterid tossed aside the shattered remnants of the comms headset that had once kept them linked with their stranded comrades. Its brittle plastics cracked and spun lazily amidst the growing drift of flakes and scraps.

In a heart-wrenching gesture of solidarity, she pulled off her own oxygen mask then carefully placed it in his waiting hand. Then again with deliberate, measured slowness, Astrid unlaced and shrugged free her respirator as well, exhaling heavily as if finally breathing the pure, thin vacuum of liberated freedom.

The faintest blush touched both cheeks beneath weathered faces half-obscured by visored helmets—a fleeting recognition kindled in the depth of shared desperation, a silent pact forged in the crucible of a dying starship crumbling into the void.

When at last they connected eye to eye across the gap of mere inches once spanning vast divides of culture, species and time... it felt less like two warriors poised on the brink of a battle won—as if instead they held the fragile, vulnerable heart of their strange new alliance captive between them. Each inhale became a communion, lungs filling with frigid night air until stars burned bright behind fluttering eyelids. Every exhalation whispered ancient truths across language barriers yet still managed to convey the raw depth of emotion pulsing between newfound allies brought together by fate's terrible whims.

Chapter 11: Blackout at Sector 7 
Scene 4:

Astrid's eyes remained locked on Mercer's own, pupils dilating like twin black holes swallowing stars against retinas shimmering with stardust. No audio needed for comprehension anymore—only those breaths, warm currents of air mingling with each exchange, carrying entire conversations that transcended tongues and civilizations.

The drifting clouds of wreckage cast an eerie glow across cracked and smoldering hull plating. Flares sparked periodically where exposed wiring arced, throwing jagged shadows across the duo's forms silhouetted against the infinite velvet blackness. Amidst the chaos of their crippled vessel listing limply beneath them, somehow this fragile moment—these tentative exchanges of gaze and gentle sharing of precious breaths—evoked a profound peace.

Their helmets hung uselessly from fingers limp at sides—trophies of victories long past become burdens grown meaningless in the face of extinction. In their place, skin prickled with gooseflesh as temperatures plummeted, making Astrid and Mercer acutely aware of every degree they strayed from Earth-normal. And yet, none of it mattered, could never matter again... not out here among unfathomable galaxies where worlds were but flickering embers.

"This...isn't much longer," Astrid whispered, voice thin and raspy due to reduced airflow. Even those syllables required hergreatest efforts—tongue stiffening against dry lips, throat convulsing with exertION.

Mercer nodded solemnly despite knowing she couldn't see him do so. "We did all we could, Vex." His chest rattled with a cough he quickly suppressed before continuing in a low murmur meant for only her ears—"But even combined humanity's brightest minds...can't defy gravity itself."

There was no need for further words between them after that. Instead, they simply basked in the radiant heat of the dying star already beginning its violent implosion behind them, casting mesmerizing ripples across surrounding spacetime with each concussive wave. It was almost hypnotic—the way light twisted and warped around them—forcing acknowledgment of scales utterly incomprehensible by human standards. Entire ecosystems existed within these fiery storms...cosmologies born from entropy...

Abruptly, a sudden alert buzzed through the derelict bridge, mercifully sparing them from lapsing into philosophical meandering under the star's thrall. Warning lights flashed like a strobe across control panels reactivating spasmodically one-by-one.

"What in nine hells..." Astrid trailed off, narrowing her squinted eyes towards the darkness ahead.

A faint glow appeared near the limit of their unaided sight—a pinprick of luminescence that rapidly expanded into a blinding corona, outshining even the dying behemoth just behind them. Mercer instinctively shielded his vision but maintained steady footwork amidst trembling ship components.

"By the Throne," Astrid murmured, her mind struggling to fathom what could possibly radiate such intensity short of another stellar ignition.

As Mercer squinted, attempting to make sense of the searing phenomenon bearing down upon their crippled hulk, realization bloomed behind sun-dazzled vision like a shooting star blazing across the firmament—

The Starlight Marauder's distress beacon.

Forced offline upon first contact with the anomaly now visible, it had remained dormant until atmospheric decompression severed any hope of salvage...until Vex and Mercer stood poised at the precipice gazing upon total annihilation.
