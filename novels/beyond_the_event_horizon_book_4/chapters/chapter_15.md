# Chapter 15: The Launch Bay Ejection

Shudders rippled through the Starlight Marauder's deckplating as it jolted free from the dying colony ship, inertial dampeners already straining against the sudden disparity in mass ratios. Gravity generators hummed ominously, compensatory fields warping into existence around the fleeing pirate frigate to counteract the loss of Centaur's ponderous pull. 

Leo felt the violent lurch first through seat restraints digging painful lines into his torso, helmet clamped down tightly over eyes squeezing shut lest stray photons pierce retinas. Then the cacophony of battle outside the hangar doors erupted, an impromptu symphony of laser fire screeching along the spectrum, explosive fragmentation pummeling in earsplitting drumroll, metallic shrieks protesting overstressed structural integrity. The Marauder's shields hissed like smoldering brakes fighting to ablate incoming ordinance and protect vulnerable internals.

"All hands, strap in!" Astrid's gravelly command clawed its way through Leo's helmet, echoing in auditory implants linked to comms circuitry. Though not fully cognizant yet, even slumbering nerves and reflex-hardened muscles reacted instinctively to crisis, limbs knotting together in tight ball as if trying to contain the primal fear gnawing at gut. Not fear specifically of death per se – they both had lived through far worse – but dread born from a nameless existential horror lurking just beyond perception.

Outside, the abyss rushed in, eager to reclaim the vanishing metal husk of humanity's failed last stand. Inky vastness stretched to infinity in every direction, devoid of stars save the solitary maroon hulk of Centaur eclipsing what little sunken light struggled through atmosphere. The absence of background noise was equally unsettling – no soft hiss of breathing or gentle hum of engines but only the acrid tang of burnt metals and ozone dancing on the chill of liquid helium thrusters exhaling their chemical waste into the frozen emptiness.

Inch by inch, yard by agonizing yard, the freighter shrank in the viewscreen, its fading angular silhouette receding towards pointlessness. A momentary respite came amidst the mayhem, giving the battered marauder crew short-lived reprieve to glance up, catch their breaths even as ragged gasps wheezed in oxygen masks. But reprieve soon turned bittersweet when stark reality revealed themselves reflected back in polished armor plating strewn across viewport.

Flanking the Marauder like menacing piranhas darting up the flank of a wounded whale were six gleaming cruisers of unknown origin with sleek, obsidian hulls bristling razor-sharp spines and arrays of pulsing weapons modules. Even the limited resolution granted by passive sensors could detect no human presence on these vessels – whatever occupied them, it moved with alien fluidity and purposeful intent, as chilling as any nightmare. 

"We cannot outrun," Astrid breathed, speaking so low that Leo almost thought the words were his own private paranoia. Fumbling fingers danced nimbly over the navigation console, seeking optimal trajectory through clouds of debris strewn across the horizon like confetti after a bloodied riot while fighters continued to probe like sharks hunting survivors from doomed escape pods.

"S-soon as we clear—" another jagged roar rocked the starboard flank and Leo heard panicked cry cut off in splintered screams over local comms feed as a plasma bolt pierced engine housing spraying molten shrapnel through compartment —

Leonidas 'Leo' Mercer shook off fragmented images of burning flesh with effort similar to forcing open rusted safe deposit box, focusing once more on maintaining situational awareness though the clammy grip of adrenaline.

Chapter 15: The Launch Bay Ejection

"Damn you, damn me, damn this hellhole!" Commander Mercer snarled, voice cracking between gritted teeth as he clung desperately to the wreckage-strewn exterior of the hijacked liner. Explosions blossomed across his panoramic view in retina-searing lurches of scarlet and incandescent violet, each brighter than Mars at zenith. The deafening cacophony assaulting his senses—shattering impacts, blistering heat pulses, shrieks of tortured metal—struggled for relevance amidst the eerie stillness of the vacuum. Not a whisper of wind, not the faintest crackle of electricity, only the brutal force of projectiles hammering the cerulean darkness.

Mercer twisted half-turned along the struts supporting the ruptured boarding airlock, searching frantically for an optical signal from their captors. Astrid had warned him of the pirates' ruthless tactics—their penchant for abandoning even valuable prisoners adrift as space junk—but he couldn't shake the compulsion rising within him. Unspoken code of command, ingrained since childhood in starry-eyed cadet fantasies. Duty required him to know what hit them before making plans to dodge or destroy...

A sudden tug yanked his arms up beside his head, hurling him into a wild spin down a pockmarked tunnel of torn metal girders toward the distant terminus where escaping gas vented in shimmering pearls into the abyss. The g-forces pinned him rigid against the strut, breath ragged and ears popping as pressure dropped precipitously outward—hurtling him toward oblivion at several thousand meters per second. His bodyweight shifted as the ship's gravity died away, flinging him forward now...now...

Suddenly a massive hand clamped on the back of his flight suit just above the collar bones, halting his tumble mid-air. A momentary daze washed over him at the shocking contact, eyes bulging involuntarily behind transparent shield goggles...then understanding kicked in. He'd nearly forgotten—his captain, his partner, his rescuer—had done similarly impossible acrobatics aboard the liner itself earlier, leaping vast distances and defying physics with every desperate stride through burning corridors as they fled their enemies. What once seemed superhuman now felt all too grounded: this woman was capable of anything—had proved it already under dures he could hardly comprehend.

"What?" Astrid demanded between clenched teeth via the neural implant link embedded beneath his tongue. "Can it, Cowboy! I ain't got time nor patience left to play hero damsel today."

He forced reluctant laughter past his dry throat, managing a gruff chuckle even as bone-jarring turbulence buffeted them mercilessly between grasping claws of fractured hull plates. "Thought maybe I was too much meat for ya pirates to enjoy at leisure on your kill tally board," he retorted sarcastically even as an explosive fireball lit her faceplate almost supernaturally bright. For one breathless instant Mercer swore he saw the glint of tears lurking in those storm-gray eyes behind the tinted polymer.

"Bullshit," she spat. Just that. Two vicious syllables, but they packed more vehemence to sting into his eardrum than any rebuke ever hurled from superior officer or ex-lover. There it was plain and simple—a reminder of who owned his loyalty, at least in this precarious hell-hole beyond the reaches of Empire law—and she was right, goddamn it, they didn't have time. Mercer's blood ran cold as a sudden jolt shook the airframe. Pressure gauges flared crimson across the dashboard's augmented display, warning them their failing suit seals wouldn't hold long against the freezing void. Astrid's voice snapped like a whip through the comms, tone sharp as razors:

"Hulls breached starboard side! Multiple structural collapses in progress—"
A deafening whine drowned out the rest as the Marauder's repulsor core surged to maximum thrust in an emergency ascent maneuver. Searing heat blistered Mercer's cheekbones—the acceleration pressing him hard back into the console, stars blurring into blue-white streaks outside. But there was no respite; they must've hit some unknown debris field en route, judging by the violent lurches and groans wrenching their battered ship from side to side.

A burst of searing ozone stung his nostrils. Alarms chirped frantic warnings amidst crackles and groans echoing through the lifelines and struts. Mercer's heart threatened to seize up in his chest as the numbers scrolled relentlessly downward on two key systems—

Main Fuel Reserve: 
78% → 62% → 46%

Starboard Gyro Stabilizers:

Operational → Reducing Power → Failure Imminent

Grim recognition sank in—theirs might well be a suicide run, catapulting a fuel-starved spacecraft without functional stabilizers straight into open space. Unless...

The idea seized him like a vision from the pits of madness—or maybe salvation offered by the chaos itself. Mercer jerked his gaze to Vex, mind racing; he needed convincing, needed her onside. And quick—because if not soon... 

The Marauder bucked savagely under them once more then again, throwing the two of them off balance before slamming them face-first against restraints. Astrid roared something incoherent over the howl of tortured metal, silver hair whipping past Mercer's strained eyes. She kicked free of the harness with savage force, ripping herself toward the cockpit doorway just ahead in a flurry of leather, cloth, and flex-weave armor.

With a snarl of frustration mixed with grim determination, Mercer fumbled after hers, heedless of his ripped uniform flapping wildly about his legs and forearms now unmoored within the centrifugal forces. Pain from strained ligaments in his shoulders only sharpened focus—and desperation. A few vital seconds lost meant an early grave trapped behind dead inertial dampeners. It was down to raw muscle and sheer nerve next.

Outside the cockpit, stars swirled in sickening spirals, their constellations bleeding towards infinity within distorted arcs of shattered atmosphere. Mercer had to think, had to plan despite the screaming need in his gut urging immediate action. But what choice did they have besides a catastrophic exit? No escape this way... unless—

His gaze snapped around to lock onto a nearby hulk barely perceptible in the darkness beside them—the burnt skeleton of some other craft, likely vaporized in the chaotic crossfire. Then everything clicked into place.

Grabbing onto a severed strut jutting out from the wreckage, Mercer struggled to haul himself closer to the mangled hull, feet scrabbling clumsily against the outer skin covered in slick hydraulic coolant residue. This was insane—it felt mad. Astrid watched his antics warily, lips peeled back in a furious grimace etched upon pale cheeks, fingers curled tight around her pulse rifle.

"The damned thing could implode on us and spray us to atoms even if we manage to rip free..."

With agonizing effort, Mercer managed to pull himself hand over scraping elbow along the disintegrating hull, using every fiber to resist Earth's brutal gravitational tug. Coolant dripped like icy tears across his face as he wrenched himself over a jagged metal ridge to clamber atop an exposed section of fuselage frame.

Stellar pinpricks still whirred like pirouetting dervishes against inky blackness beyond broken viewport seals. But for a fleeting moment, his vantage revealed something far more shocking than the war-torn void outside—a pinpoint of radiant light hanging steady just off their starboard bow. Mercer squinted against the glare until his brain reluctantly processed it. That wasn't any known comet or shooting star: it was a ship, or at least its engine flare cutting cleanly through emptiness like a laser scalpel.

"Astro—look!" He jabbed a triumphant finger before he second-guessed the urgency of their situation. "Maybe if we—"

"Oh, holy fuck," Astrid breathed, spotting the incoming vessel alongside him and instantly grasping its implications. Her features twisted into a mix of disbelief and dread. Even through her exosuit, she felt an involuntary shiver crawl like frost up her spinal column.

They were less than 200 kilometers away, well within missile range even without thrusting flares painting the darkness. A larger ship by at least three-fold compared to theirs, it resolved into sleek lines and angular armor plating as it surged relentlessly forward. Not military hardware by the looks—the streamlined design betrayed corsairs or privateers instead, built for speed rather than heavy fire. But Mercer knew better than to underestimate raiders who could afford such premium technology.

"It's a heavy-hitter alright..." he muttered, scouring its deadly profile inch by deadly inch. "Looks like they're heading straight for us, intent on investigating whatever drew their notice."

Astrid bit her lower lip hard enough to taste blood, fingers twitching hungrily over her pulse rifle stock. If this raider class had the firepower to carve open the derelict...

"We aren't going to outrun that if they decide we're a threat." Dread slithered through Mercer's chest at the prospect, heavier than the gravity pulling at him. "Not in our crippled state. You ready for company, Vex?"

She snorted. "God help me, I was born for chaos. This should be fun." Her eyes burned with feral intensity as she leveled the weapon, scanning for any sign of movement amidst the approaching ship's shadows. "If you can wriggle us closer to that wreck without getting our asses blown to Kingdom Come, do it fast. We might yet salvage something—" 

Their gazes met and held across mere meters, an instant of wordless understanding passing between strangers bound together by dire circumstance. Mercer nodded curtly and began hauling himself onward once again, muscles straining beneath his own suit. In a deadly game of cosmic hide-and-seek, they'd need to improvise...and pray like Hell this audacious gamble paid off before they became its most tragic casualties.
