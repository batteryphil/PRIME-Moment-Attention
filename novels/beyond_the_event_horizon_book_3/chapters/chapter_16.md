# Chapter 16: Blind Navigation


The starfield beyond the damaged viewport spun a mad whirligig of light and shadow—Leo clung to the chair, stomach roiling as he watched the constellations blur past at horrific speed. Inertial compensators struggled valiantly but the g-force exerted by Marauder's desperate dive pressed mercilessly down upon his skull. Sweat beaded his forehead, eyes stinging from the relentless pressure. He blinked away the stars, focused exclusively on the faint holographic display projecting ship schematics and navigational data within the confines of his helmet.

Their battered vessel pirouetted within a hair's breadth of catastrophe, hurtling toward the unseen bulk of a magnetar—its gravitational well would quickly turn Marauder into a smoldering wreck. Yet the very gravity wave that threatened destruction also held the slim hope for escape. By embracing its embrace he could potentially slingshot around the ancient corpse and gain enough velocity to break free...

Provided they didn't collide first or the pirates decided to play grim reaper during what must surely be the longest, loneliest seconds of their lives. Leo's vision flickered around the edges, oxygen-rich air recirculated by the life support system tasting strange on his tongue. Each shallow breath expanded his chest, a constant reminder of the unforgiving atmosphere locked away behind the transparent, sealed dome of his envirosuit.

He risked a glance at Astrid, noting the captain's head tilted back against the cushions, her face slack and pale against the ruddy gleam of warning lights painting every surface in hellish hues. Even in this surreal freefall he remained perfectly composed, movements economical despite the obvious strain bearing down upon her like the weight of a collapsing skyscraper. Astrid was a force of nature personified—and it frightened Leo just how attuned he'd become to every subtle nuance of her presence, every ripple in an ocean far stranger than either of them had ever known.

Her eyes snapped open then, drilling into his as if she possessed psychic sight. Leo felt her probing gaze like a lance of pure energy searing into his very soul—a fleeting connection only possible after surviving such shared horrors together. "Navigation check," Astrid commanded in a flat monotone devoid of warmth, yet somehow more terrifying for its emotional detachment. Her hands moved across the battered console with practiced ease, bypassing damaged systems and hotwiring temporary workarounds as needed.

In mere minutes, her fingers danced across complex displays—the dim blue glow of holographs illuminating the rugged lines of her face like moonlight bathing in the depths of some otherworldly sea. "Estimating sixty-eight...sixty-nine...seventy seconds to event horizon insertion..." Astrid muttered, eyes never leaving the shifting calculations. "If current acceleration holds, we should begin feeling effects soon—" She trailed off abruptly, expression tightening into a grotesque parody of a smile that sent chills racing down Leo's spine.

Outside the ruined cockpit, an eerie purple haze swirled into existence on the curved metal shell—the telltale sign magnetar's gravitational influence began enveloping them whole. Involuntarily, Leo flinched at the sight—but maintained his focus, knowing any distraction could spell disaster. Already distortions rippled through local spacetime, the fabric of reality unraveling in visible waves.

"We've got company," he warned unnecessarily, thumb sliding across the comms interface built into his left glove. The response came immediately, each voice dripping with malice and anticipation: "Starlight Marauder— surrender yourself and maybe we let you live long enough for a proper interrogation..."

Leo's hand froze on the comms control, muscles tensing as Astrid's gaze locked onto him from over her shoulder. Dark amusement danced in her eyes, tempered by a cold calculation his instincts couldn't decipher.

"Company?" she repeated mockingly, voice barely loud enough to cut through the hum filling the cabin. "How quaint." With a flick of her wrist, another interface lit up—the Marauder's weapons grids. "Looks like our maggot-brained friends have sent a little welcoming committee."

On the main display, mercifully shielded from direct line-of-sight, five mismatched vessels emerged from the swirling purple mists—the telltale signature of warp drive activation. Hulks of salvaged merchant vessels converted for piracy, sleek strike craft from forgotten colonial conflicts, even what appeared to be a repurposed mass driver cannon mounted onto a makeshift frigate. They danced around their prey in tantalizing maneuvers—a predator's chorus celebrating its imminent feast.

"Four heavy point-defense mounts," Leo murmured, running mental calculations. "Probably railguns, judging by the size. And those smaller craft—most likely swarmers, packing flechette volleys or directed EMP."

Astrid snorted, waving away his analysis. "All I care about right now is how much bang they can deliver before we dust 'em. Don't waste breath on tactical minutiae, soldier-boy."

Leo's hackles rose at the dismissive tone, years of ingrained discipline clashing against the brazen disregard for collateral damage displayed by these modern pirates. The chasm between his world view and Astrid's grew wider with each passing second, yet the thrill of the impending battle stirred his blood in ways he'd thought long extinguished—igniting a primal competitiveness that defied logic.

"We need a plan," he reminded her gruffly, struggling to rein in the adrenaline-fueled bravado threatening to override cooler judgment. Every fiber of his being screamed warning signals—that they were outnumbered, outgunned, and hopelessly entrenched in someone else's star system thanks to Astrid's reckless ambition. But pride would not permit defeat, not so close to achieving their goal.

Astrid rolled her shoulders, a fluid grace belying her unyielding resolve. "Plan? Feh—a waste o' time. Just prepare t'vearn some scars, because I ain't letting those scum take my ship without a fight."

Leo bit back the retort hovering on the tip of his tongue, realizing this was Astrid's core strength—and potentially her greatest weakness. Unwavering conviction and instinct had seen her through the unforgiving streets of the underworld, but the cosmos was different animal altogether. He recalled Kaelin's words on departure, how a lack of strategic depth might ultimately prove Astrid's downfall. Was he witnessing the seeds of that downfall in stark relief?

Before he could dwell further on such bleak musings, the bridge bucked once, twice, forcing him to clutch the nearest stanchion for balance. The inertial dampeners whined in protest as the Marauder surged forward again, chasing its unlikely gambit toward the event horizon.

Outside, warped star-space heaved and convulsed, reality itself trembling on the knife-edge between order and chaos. Gravity's fist tightened its relentless grip, energy surging through the bridge's hull like living lightning. The pirate crew screamed and clung to whatever bulkhead offered respite from the mounting tidal forces—except Astrid.

Stoic visage hardened into granite, she stood immobile, hands poised above auxiliary controls. Waiting. Watching. For something only she knew.

✦ ✦ ✦

The cloying reek of ozone scorched nostrils as solar flares danced in strobe-lit bursts across the viewport's darkened armor-glass. Spikes of crimson light seared retinas as starlight distorted through the swirling perturbations of spacetime.

"Time to earn those scars," Astrid growled, her voice a low rumble beneath the incessant groan of tortured metal and shrieking generators.

Leo gripped the edge of the command chair, knuckles white against the polished alloy as he struggled to suppress the roiling sensation of panic rising in his gut. Her unwavering resolve, forged in the crucible of the underworld's violence, both awed and terrified him. They were sailing into the heart of a maelstrom without a definitive plan or reliable backup—it was the very antithesis of anything his military training or even his years flight-testing advanced fighter prototypes had prepared him for.

As if reading his thoughts, Astrid's gaze caught and held his, piercing depths in a moment of unspoken understanding. For an instant, the hellish turmoil around them dropped away, and it was just the two of them locked in an orbit of anticipation and trepidation. Then, the Marauder lurched again, throwing him off-balance as the inertial compensators fought to maintain a semblance of equilibrium in the face of the encroaching singularity. 

"Astrid!" he bellowed over the cacophony, straining to be heard. "Give me—

Silenced by the crashing boom of a thunderously loud alarm, their exchange disintegrated into fragmented gestures as they each scrambled to diagnose the new threat threatening their precarious stability. Critical systems flashed red and amber as automated warnings filled the air like a thousand screaming ravens:

"Cooling failure, Deck 4! Decompression imminent!"

"Hull breach, Frame 37! Sealing initiates NOW!"

"Momentum dump system offline!"

Leo frantically tapped at the main console, fingers dancing across a keyboard where keys shuddered from the impact of pounding vibration. His heart slammed in his chest as the abyssal math of propulsion and maneuvering scrolled past in a dizzying blur, the calculations screaming impossible odds. Even his prodigious skillset couldn't reconcile this mess fast enough to chart a successful egress. Panic clawed at his throat, demanding answers to questions no one could possibly provide in time...

Then, Astrid moved, fluid grace belied by the raw power coiled within her limbs. She planted her palms atop the commander's station, fingers tapping out a command sequence so rapid even his trained eyes could barely track its frenetic arc.

"No brakes," she declared, features pinched in concentration. "No thrusters either. We dive, baby. Burn till we can't... then hope our shielding holds."

"What?" Horror colored the single word as comprehension bloomed, cold dread spreading from gut to bones as realization crashed down upon him. This was reckless bordering on suicidal—a desperate bid to outrun the unstoppable maw of an eater-hole without propulsion or adequate protection. Yet even as terror coiled its icy fangs around every coherent thought, Leo saw the logic behind her decision—perhaps the sole avenue open to them given the dire state of their crippled vessel. It was mad, but he grasped its audacity. More than that, a twisted echo of Astrid's steely resolve began to resonate within him...

Beneath quaking deckplates—boards etched with age and grime that would have sparked derision from any seasoned spacer Leo had crossed paths with—a low thrum built, building pressure and heat until the bridge became a furnace box.

✦ ✦ ✦

The vortex swallowed them whole. 

A singular point of infinity, an abyss unutterably vast, opened its maw and engulfed Starlight Marauder. One moment they plunged headlong into darkness, next they were racing through the heart of creation itself.

Light-years contracted to inches, the cosmos compressing into an immersive hologram wrapping around the stricken ship. Streaks of luminescence flared past the viewport like brushstrokes across a celestial canvas - supernovae, nebulae, blazing black holes. Distant galaxies tumbled, whirled and careened by in dizzying profusion while the very fabric of spacetime undulated like an ethereal tide.

Inside the vessel, Astrid fought gravity to keep her footing as the hull groaned under unprecedented stresses. Every strut and beam sang a symphony of protest, deck plates shuddering violently enough to rip handholds from the walls. Alarms screamed in cacophonous discord, status boards bathing the bridge in a strobing nightmare of red and yellow.

Leo clung to his seat, vision locking to the aft screen as if mesmerized by an apocalyptic ballet unfolding outside. Marauder's engines cast a searing glare across the void, ion plasma trails streaming away from her drive mounts like spectral comet tails. Heat shields flared orange, glowing like embers beneath siege-fire assault, while inertial dampeners hissed on overdrive to stave off the lethal g-forces shredding systems and crew alike.

Around them, the universe imploded into chaotic glory - stars bursting, worlds colliding, quantum fluctuations sparking forth like fireflies in the void. Time lost meaning, seconds stretching to eternity and back again as they hurtled onwards in relativistic torment. A silent, deafening scream echoed in Leo's mind as his world narrowed to the blistering engines and the warrior woman riding shotgun to madness.

Then, star-bright, leviathan-shaped monstrosity loomed ahead. Astrid jolted upright at the sudden contact alert, eyes snapping to the forward sensors. Heart thudding in her chest, she watched as the monstrous apparition filled the viewports - the hulking form of some ancient derelict drifting lazily across the path of their doomed plunge.

"What?" Leo gasped beside her, incredulous and horrified as behemoth ruins swelled to fill the field of view. Asteroid-forged bulkheads, labyrinths of conduits and driveshells, great halls and chambers festively strewn with relics beyond counting and ken.

"Hold on..." Astrid breathed, reaching for the master control yoke. With no other escape, no last resort left undone Marauder's final collision course was the only option remaining.

"Astrid--" Leo protested weakly as she punched in the settings. The helm trembled in her grasp as she angled for interception.

"Too close," she whispered to herself, calculating odds that would leave a bookmaking asteroid miner queasy with doubt. The dying cruiser plowed onward as if hell-bent on transpiration into that alien hull. 

Impact was inevitable. And even the Star-Eater's titanic corpse might not survive the encounter.
