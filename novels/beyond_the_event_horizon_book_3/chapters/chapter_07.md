# Chapter 7: The Corsair Council


Captain Astrid "Vex" Ross knelt atop a metal bench, eyes downcast as she coiled the last locking lever taut, locking it flush against the hull plate. Her boots grated across the floor, displacing particles that swirled lazily in low gravity. Nearby, mercenary pilot Leo Mercer peered over her shoulder at the cryptic schematics projected onto durasteel bulkheads. This converted cargo bay was makeshift command center, reconfigured haphazardly to suit Vex's needs—hence the spare seats mounted by jury-rigged chains, the mottled haze of recirculated oxygen wafting off the rust-spattered decking plates.

"Magnetic latches should keep hold till we hit relativistic speeds," Vex said matter-of-factly, glancing up for only a instant before refocusing intently on her task.

"What's the threshold velocity you're aiming for?" Mercer asked, genuine curiosity vying with nervous apprehension about the risks being taken.

"Hundred kilos per second, easy. Needs to outstrip any interception attempt by Dominions. If nothing else, we'll give 'em a hell of a tale to tell once our trail goes cold."

Leo nodded slowly, processing the implications. With current propulsion chemistries, any ship attempting to achieve such speeds would be utterly unequipped for safe re-entry back into atmosphere—a final sacrifice thrown to the void. Yet if success meant evading pursuit indefinitely—

Just then, an audible shudder racked the ship, causing Vex to stumble minutely and lose her grip on the locking lever. A chorus of clatters and rattles echoed throughout as loose tech trinkets slid out from their moorings, drifting listlessly through the small compartment until they smacked softly against walls or settled on the decking with empty metallic thuds.

"What in Neptune's--?"

Before either could query, Vex unholstered an older style flip-comm unit worn around her neck on a simple leather thong like something out an antique holofilm. Clipping it to her ear, a crackling noise filled the air—static, followed shortly thereafter by the tinny voice of Soren, her chief engineer, filtered through faint bursts of interference.

"...Astrid, get clearance to shut down life support in sectors D-F for the duration of recalibration!"

"No! We can't risk exposing crew to those frigid temps!" replied another unfamiliar female voice, perhaps Veshal 'Darkwing' Khatra, Vex's head technician and right-hand.

"Temps be damned, Anika, we might've ruptured one of the quantum dampeners during this hull reshaping. Without proper stabilization, it could catastrophic stress readings rip right across—"

"You've been warning us for weeks about 'catastrophes,' and every single damn time, your panic turns into nothing more than routine maintenance. Don't lecture on hypothetical disaster scenarios anymore," quarked Anika tartly, not masking irritation creeping into her tone.

"...Fair...but just humor me and let's run these diagnostics first, okay? Safety protocols—”

"—ain't our priority here tonight! Astrid, permission was granted!" cut in Soren sternly, overriding further discussion.

"Astra-dammit..." Vex groaned audibly, fisting a gloved hand upon encountering a particularly stubborn screwhead on the hull plate. After a few exasperated grunts, she finally worked loose the offending widget and tossed it aside—"This whole vessel feels ready to shake apart from my boots upward!"

Commander Leo Mercer winced at the curt exchange spilling out from his captor, Captain Astrid, as their illicit repair work progressed. Inwardly, he seethed with frustration over being unable to intervene. His own skills were largely wasted on such makeshift chores. But Astrid had made it crystal clear: in her realm, they flew under her unforgiving radar, no exceptions. 

Leo gritted his teeth, yanking a particularly recalcitrant rivet back into position with hands slickened by exertion and the chilly atmosphere's dryness. The acrid scent of ozone and singed metal hung heavy as he surveyed his handiwork alongside Astrid.

"What are you two arguing about?" he prodded bluntly, leaning against the bulkhead to catch his breath while the starboard plasma injector whined back to life.

Astrid shot him an irate flick of her emerald eyes before replying tersely: "Just Darkwing running some paranoid precautionary checks, but she knows as well as I do—these repairs top urgent after our last battle."

Leo frowned, unease tightening his chest. From what he gathered, Captain Astrid commanded an advanced warship that should have shrugged off even devastating damage. His mind raced, searching for logical explanations amidst the veil of secrets surrounding this era's technology. Astrid likely knew of his lingering questions, an expression of suspicion momentarily flashing across his weather-lined face caught her glare.

Shrugging off the implied challenge, she turned her attention back to Leo. "Speaking of which... Mercer's proven himself handy enough. Maybe it's time for him to tackle the real task at hand – getting us clear of Tarenis space unnoticed."

He flinched inwardly at the implication but forced a neutral smile, knowing better than to argue specifics that lay beyond his pay grade. "If that's what you need me for, I'll work whatever hours required."

"I figured as much. You're coming with me to the Corsair Council on Rhenara Prime once these repairs reach sufficient quality." Her tone edged with reluctance tinged by underlying command.

The pit of Leo's stomach dropped as the words registered. He was no politician, far less versed in piracy etiquette compared to Astrid's obvious experience. Still, an opportunity too promising to pass up presented itself—and that chance alone bolstered his nerve to press for clarification:

"The Corsair Council? What exactly does that entail?"

Astrid raised a challenging eyebrow, assessing him as if daring him to back down from pursuing knowledge of her world. When Leo held steady beneath her gaze, she gave a curt nod of acknowledgment.

"It means I present you to the assembled corsairs—those who control nearly three-quarters of the Helix League's smuggling routes—as proof of our partnership agreement. If they greenlight my proposals, we gain their protection...and considerable resources to modernize my ship for the next phase of my vendetta."

Each word fell like deliberate weights straining an already taut relationship. Leo acknowledged these developments signaled a turning point for both himself and Astrid. No sooner did they secure clearance to leave Tarenis territory than a maelstrom of dangerous schemes would unfold around them—all hinging upon navigating the lawless underworld of pirate alliances. His stomach churned at the magnitude of such trust, yet for reasons he struggled to articulate, keeping his bond with Astrid intact took precedence. Perhaps it stemmed from the spark of intrigue, forged in the crucible of shared adversity, between a man born centuries too early to this era—and a woman utterly defined by its ruthless realities.

CHAPTER 7: THE CORSAIR COUNCIL

Scene 3: Combustion Point

Astrid stood firm beside Leo, her gaze riveted to the viewscreen where the pirated cargo vessel hung crippled before them—their unwitting pawn in negotiating passage through the notorious Pirate's Perch of Andromeda's Spiral Arm. With the Starlight Marauder's sensors trained upon the helpless freighter, Astrid's fingers danced across the command console, weaving a complex pattern to intercept potential escapes.

"You see, they won't stand idly while one of their own ships becomes the spoil—hence the need for quick and permanent resolution," she stated matter-of-factly, voice dripping with grim expectation. "Once they detect us drawing near, the pirates aboard will scuttle the vessel to prevent capture. Then the fun begins."

Leo gripped the back of Astrid's chair, heart pounding a staccato rhythm he could only imagine echoed hers as their proximity closed. He couldn't see her face in profile, but the rigid set of shoulders and sharp angle of the jaw betrayed her concentration.

"I think it's wise to be ready for anything," he said cautiously, recalling the chaotic nature of close quarter battles involving multiple hostile vessels in low-orbit conditions. The risk of catastrophic collision posed as much danger as weapons fire or boarding actions—though pirates weren't known for their finesse when faced with an opportunity to plunder valuable cargo.

Within minutes of entering the system, transmission warnings flashed, signifying the Pirates' Guild had locked onto their signatures. Astrid let out a short laugh, devoid of amusement—a low, tight vibration beneath Leo's hand.

"And here they come—four warships on intercept vectors," she reported coolly, eyes scouring the tactical display for optimal positioning options. Her fingers worked deftly against the keys, initiating long-range probing scans and firing off cryptic distress signals intended to spoof rival pirate factions they didn't wish to entangle with during these maneuverings.

On cue, red diamond symbols erupted across the viewscreen, converging into a deadly V-pattern. Each sleek silhouette bristled with armaments—including railgun emplacements capable of launching hypervelocity projectiles exceeding the speed of light.

Leo swallowed hard, mind racing over the variables. In theory, their newer faster-alloy hull offered superior protection against kinetic bombardment, combined with state-of-the-art ECM shielding to disrupt targeting systems—but against four opponents, they'd quickly become outnumbered.

"We'll hold our fire initially unless provoked." Astrid's instructions were clipped and authoritative, a tone reserved for situations calling for precision and strength beneath pressure. Her words whispered warm against Leo's ear, stirring residual heat along his neck despite the clammy coolness of the cockpit's atmosphere recirculated and purified.

"It might help knowing their call signs—" he started to suggest, though Astrid waved off his offer.

"No time for courtesies. They know who we are—they're just testing how eager we are to establish terms."

Through sheer force of will, Astrid coaxed a hair's breadth more acceleration from their already stressed engines, carefully angling the pursuit to keep the crippled merchant in range but avoid triggering the pirates' trigger-happy instincts.

The freighter crumbled beneath barrage fire, becoming a smoldering hulk within seconds, as planned. At 300 meters, warning alarms shrieked through the bridge and emergency clamps kicked into place—just as railgun rounds whipped past, vaporizing chunks of shielding that flared away like meteors.

The Starlight Marauder shuddered violently as the hail of cannonfire chewed through her battle-damaged hull plates. Alert klaxons wailed incessantly, strobing across console displays while damage reports flickered through on priority feeds. Behind the command couches where Leo and Astrid crouched in their crash seats, blast-proof composite walls reverberated from the furious pounding outside. Each explosive impact vibrated up through deck grating, stinging feet through thick boots.

Leo gritted his teeth, white knuckles straining the leather straps of his restraints under the relentless stress. As starfighters swooped in close range to riddle the Star-Eater with armor-piercing projectiles, the larger vessel bucked and lurched unpredictably. His stomach swooped as he fought against centrifugal forces trying to fling him out of his seat during each near-miss pass. 

Through it all, Astrid remained perfectly centered, every muscle taut yet supple beneath her tactical webbing. Her dark eyes didn't leave the primary viewscreen. Its fractured image showed shattered skyglow filtering through gaps in defenses: scattered stars wheeling crazily amid clouds of superheated gas and debris belched from weapon hits to the ship itself. A flaming gash bisected one viewscreen quadrant—the work of at least two penetrating munitions, based on scorch patterns alone.

Leo tore his gaze from the horror show to share a glance with Astrid. Despite the harrowing chaos, her face was eerily calm—as unshaken as polished obsidian, lit only by the hellish ambient glow. She met Leo's concerned stare head-on before returning her focus to sensors with an almost predatory intensity.

Rearming themselves for the inevitable next wave of attacks, both of them knew they couldn't rely indefinitely on their relatively fragile shields and limited ammo reserves. Even if they managed to fend off these would-be boarders long enough to reach safe distance or negotiate capitulation, severe structural breaches made prolonged orbit impossible without serious repairs.

"We can't afford another salvo without some backup," Leo voiced aloud, though not expecting a response amidst all the constant alert buzz. Abruptly, a faint anomaly piqued his attention—a minute variation in sensor readings, almost imperceptible unless you were deliberately scanning for hidden presences at the edge of perception...

The hair stood up on the back of his neck as intuition flared. Before he could even process his discovery, Astrid seized on it instantly—and slammed their shared tactical display onto maximum sensitivity settings. The results defied understanding...and filled them both with dread.

On the screen, overlapping sensor pulses converged on a lone object approaching the fray from unknown origins, its mass undetermined but size large compared to the attacking ships. As a proximity countdown accelerated toward minutes and then seconds, this mystery entity began glowing with an ethereal radiance, so bright it threatened to burn holes straight through the viewscreens—

Then the world around them erupted into pure blinding-white oblivion as the newcomer unleashed an unimaginable celestial cataclysm that consumed everything nearby, obliterations echoing across space-time itself in waves of searing plasma...
