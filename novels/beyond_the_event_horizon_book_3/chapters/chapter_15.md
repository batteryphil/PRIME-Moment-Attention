# Chapter 15: The Magnetar Flare

Captain Astrid Vex Ross's eyes flashed open, unclouded even by the afterimages of the proximity alarm strobes that had stabbed through her pupils half a breath prior. Reflexively, she snapped her gaze to the viewports spanning the curved forward bulkhead, instantly seeking confirmation or disavowal of the danger that had spurred her to react with such swift, instinctive violence. Her mind and body, honed over centuries by countless battles fought under a dozen solar systems, performed the rapid threat assessment as smoothly and reflexively as the hum of her cybernetic limbs.

At first glance, nothing seemed amiss. No missiles flared on infrared scanning grids or streaked trails marred the abyssal blackness outside. But then Astrid's trained awareness picked up the cues — the sudden absence of warmth radiation emanating from the crippled destroyer hovering in the distance, its shattered hull glistening like a vast shard of splintered jade against the stars; the eerie stillness that settled over the battlefield when all sound waves were leeched by the void; and, worst of all, the flatline silence of her starboard radar display, once a dancing tapestry of friendlies, foes, and neutrals, now merely a cold, inert slate devoid of any echoes, pulsing signals, or warning markers.

Astrid's hand shot out, clamping onto the wrist of her tactical officer, Lieutenant Zara Krieghof. The young woman, despite sharing her commanding officer's shock at this sudden information blackout, maintained professional composure and met Astrid's gaze straight on. "Radar's gone dark, ma'am. So has long-range scanner and the sensor lattice. Looks like the magnetic field of that nearby blue giant surged big-time."

"We're blind, then," Astrid grated through clenched teeth. She watched as Zara frantically worked her console, firing frantic pulses towards their closest allies on the far horizon, silently praying that some of the signals would pierce whatever electromagnetic tempest now besieged their instruments. Astrid could feel the crew's tension mounting, a palpable presence heavy enough to settle in her lungs, but she suppressed any flicker of panic. Experience had taught her that in situations like these, calm decisive action was required, not a commander's screams and threats. 

Her attention was then drawn to the main viewscreen where Leo Mercer stood rigidly, his boots planted squarely on the deck amidships as he gripped the handholds, features taut with concern. They'd suffered severe damage during the recent clash and were drifting in a damaged pattern that would carry them ever closer to the magnetar they'd only recently begun surveying.

Astrid stepped over, deliberately slowing her approach so as not to alarm him, careful to project solidity, reassurance. Mercifully, the ship still lurched sluggishly along its current course thanks to its residual thrust injectors. If luck held, they'd narrowly miss the blue giant's inferno before the Marauder finally succumbed to its deadly gravity. "Leo, I think—"

Before she could utter another word, the cruiser bucked and heaved so violently that both of them stumbled, crashing together harder than intended upon the decking plates. Astrid snatched a glimpse sideways past Leo; through the viewport, she witnessed the awful sight: the burgeoning flame of a megaflare erupting from the heart of the magnetar, a solar cataclysm hundreds of times more powerful than anything known to sustain human habitation or navigateable stellar bodies. That searing vortex of ionized plasma and X-rays would engulf worlds and incinerate everything within its path.

Scene 2: Tactical Dispute and Collision Course

Astrid righted herself by sheer force of will, using the momentum to pull Leo back into stability against the shuddering hull. His fingers clenched the nearby handrails as if the very fate of their battered ship depended on the strength of his grip— which wasn't entirely inaccurate.

"They're really going to throw all that raw energy out there?" Leo gasped, eyes wide with disbelief etched across his storm-gray face. Sweat glistened darkly on his furrowed brow in the dimmed cabin light. "Could this be..." He glanced around as if seeking reassurance from any material surface. 

"Incorrect," Astrid countered calmly through gritted teeth. "That ain't energy being 'thrown.' It's a colossal explosion of energy released as matter gets superheated in magnetic reconnection events." Her gaze transfixed the raging stormfront before them, an otherworldly aurora dancing on subatomic scales as countless tons of electromagnetic plasma charged toward oblivion. "The flare itself might last mere minutes but it'll scorch the light-years. We need to get this boat turned around pronto!"

"Why? So we can roast in the magnetar's outer corona like a pair of tardigrade toast, you mean?" Leaning to one side, Leo peered at the navigation display as it sketched out their spiraling trajectory with merciless precision. In mere hours, they would cross the event horizon into the star's belly if no course correction took place. Yet as desperate as their predicament seemed, neither option offered a pleasant outcome should their current vector persist.

"No way in hell should you let your own vessel become space junk by willingly diving headfirst into that furnace for nothing!" Leo insisted, hands balling into furious fists at his sides. "Think, damn it! Our mission was to survey. Not commit suicide!" Tension thrummed through each word uttered, a powder keg of pent-up frustration sparking just beneath his skin.

"How dare you question my judgment out of the blue, milksop," Astrid retorted bitterly, voice dripping disdain. She jabbed a calloused finger toward the maelstrom swirling before them. "This isn't some routine recon job anymore, not with that beast about to eat us whole! When pushed between the proverbial rock and fire, choices gotta be made." Bitterness coated every syllable and her stormy gaze bored into Leo like a drill bit scraping metal.

The bridge fell silent save for the mournful creaks and groans accompanying each tremor running through their beleaguered craft. Astrid kept her back arched defiantly, jaw set in stubborn resolve while Leo's features worked to process the harsh realities. It was clear these two seasoned warriors would have to reconcile vastly different philosophies if hope existed of salvaging themselves from this precarious predicament.

The magnetar's pulsating core glowed incandescent blue-white against the abyssal black horizon, casting sickly illumination upon their terrified faces. 

Astrid's knuckles whitened as she gripped the shattered control yoke, her eyes wild and pleading to avoid collision while Leo's stolid expression belied the turmoil brewing behind the façade of military composure.

"Astrid, I'll admit what you've done so far has been...impulsive," Leo began carefully, "but pulling off a Hail Mary burn like this without thorough prep or checks puts both our lives needlessly on the line!"

"Don't pretend you have any better ideas, genius boy!" Astrid snapped back acidly, the name-calling barely masking desperate fear driving her actions. "We were dead in the water unless—"

"I've analyzed the approaching flux storm," interjected Leo hastily, cutting through the recrimination. "It'd consume us instantly. We simply don't have enough thrust to—"

"You think you know more?" Astrid interrupted again, voice quavering despite the bravado. "How can a relic from another century have any notion of facing this? This wasn't in the textbooks, Lieutenant!"

Leo bristled at the slight, his tone icy as he shot back, "Neither were the tech tricks you pulled getting us this far. We're an imperfect, mismatched duo trying to survive the uncharted expanse. Let's focus."

Their voices locked in heated deadlock as they hurtled closer to an unyielding destiny, stars streaking past like laser slashes across infinity. The blistering tempest grew exponentially more menacing, its tendrils lashing outward like vengeful tentacles seeking prey.

Suddenly, a spine-wrenching jolt slammed the command center. The Marauder lurched drunkenly side to side as multiple alarm klaxons screamed cacophonous warnings. Overwhelming scents of burning insulation and superheated fluids assaulted them simultaneously.

"We need an immediate fix or we—" began Astrid futilely as structural failures echoed outside. Debris peppered the bridge transparent dome, cracking it spider-web-pattern wise despite reinforcement spells.

"Now, Mercer!" she bellowed, pain-ringed eyes flashing with resolute determination.

Leo's hands flashed over console touchscreens as he initiated a priority override on key systems. The faint hum of compensators kicking online was a soothing counterpoint to the chaos around them. His brow furrowed intensely as he fought temporal distortions creeping in, the star eater's extreme gravitational influence warping spacetime itself.

Cyan displays lit up in urgent warning sequences yet still Leo pressed on, trusting his knowledge of orbital principles and the ship dynamics calculations racing within his computational mind. Sweat slickened the bridge floor as tremors intensified while he strained to recalibrate and reprioritize, one catastrophic failure too many spiking blood pressure to the brink of hypovolemic shock.

With a symphony of grinding servos, a hiss of pressurized hydraulic fluid, and a bone-jarring clunk, the crippled Marauder finally swung perpendicular to its death spiral trajectory, a single massive throttle venturi firing crimson-hot plasma into tiny nozzles spaced along the hull flanks. Centripetal force flattened Astrid into her seat as Leo grimaced through teeth ground white with tension, body straining forward toward the control console.

Outside, ionized plasma flames curled serpent-like from the retropropulsion vents, fighting valiantly to reverse momentum. For a stomach-churning infinity, the outcome hung uncertain, each second a ticking clock toward incineration by stellar winds and searing magnetar radiation.

Leo's fingers danced across tactile controls, eyes never leaving main displays where failing hull integrity flashed amber alongside climbing temperature gauges. Sweaty palms left slippery trails against synthetic grips as Astrid's curses echoed in his ear. "Pitiful, Mercer, just pitiful! The marauder's guts torn out like a freshly gutted targ at market!"

Her bitter tirade spurred him to focus tighter, pouring every ounce of years-killed skill into coaxing life-saving maneuver from wreckage of their once proud starship. Sweat, tears, and recaptured sanity dripped from Leo's chin as crimson fire vomited from the superheated thrusters. The Marauder groaned pitifully, protesting every attempt to right herself after her violent tumble into hellhole's gravity well. 

Outside, the battle-scarred hulk seemed to writhe under the onslaught of stellar winds screaming at hundreds of thousands of kilometers per hour, lashing with vicious energy until every inch of charred, heat-crumpled metal smoked and reeked of seared ferrocrete. Solar gusts howled with fury enough to pulverize any lesser vessel stone dead. And waiting like some eldritch celestial monstrosity, the magnetar's bloated mass pulsed ominously, poised to unleash wrath of untold mega-stocks upon those brave few who dared this abyss.

Slowly, agonizingly slowly, the Marauder responded to frantic efforts, struggling towards stability like a faltering drunken stag, each jolt sending a shockwave through the bucking bridge deck. Finally, the tortured hull began shifting sideways, away from the abyssal maw that had nearly swallowed them whole. 

Exhilarated relief surged though him but it would not last. Already the monitor lights flickered with new warning - they were not out of danger. At all. Rising in defiance from the depths were telltale wisps of stardust, coalescing rapidly into unmistakable form... 

An enemy fleet, far larger than their numbers led them to believe. And those ships bore no markings of legitimate traffic control nor enforcement. Instead they displayed garish finery that screamed plunder and piracy. Merciless black void ships bristling with weaponry, their angular profiles cutting through the void like razors driven by dark wills.

"Astrid, I make visual contact with unknown hostiles," Leo gasped aloud. "Confirmed pirate vessels. Six, possibly eight of them." Horror gripped his heart as he realized just how badly their chances now soured like unclean spirits on the worst night ever...

Astrid spared the barest glance from her intent scrutiny of damage readouts before fixing him with a look riddled with equal parts murderous rage and bleak despair. Only then did she deign to respond, voice dripping venom through gritted teeth. 

"...and you have the gall to inform me? Do I look fucking clueless, commander?" She hissed the final word like the venomous strike of a hungry viper, lean fingers drumming agitated patterns on the shattered console, tracing broken circuitry like caressing the corpse of a friend.

"Not so," Leo replied coolly, jaw clenched to hide the quake threatening to betray him. "I thought that given our precarious situation and near-dead status, your strategic preferences might alter."

He shifted slightly in his seat, ignoring the residual tingles from overexerted muscles screaming their protests anew as he continued steadily, tone even and measured despite racing heart beneath his ribs. 

"But the cold facts won't change even for my tactful suggestions. Our options are limited and the approaching pirates do us no favors."
