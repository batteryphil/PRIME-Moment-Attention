# Chapter 5: Stolen Breaths

Mercer strode down the starboard corridor, senses heightened by desperation and time's relentless march. Every passing moment brought the ominous rumble of approaching star-fleet dreadnoughts closer. His skin prickled; that familiar metallic tang of blood and ozone permeated the air, the ship's lifeblood seeping through fissures in scorched plating.

A distant wail—worryingly close—attracted his attention to damage control displays flickering with red-tinged schematics. External repair drones clustered around hull breaches like vultures attending a mortally wounded beast. Damage assessments painted a dire portrait: limited lifeboats still functional, propulsion crippled, defenses stalwart yet tenuous against impending bombardment.

As he processed vital intel, Vex materialized on his right, features ablaze with fierce determination despite the haphazard dressing covering half her face. "Commander, our shield coherence just dropped to seventy percent—"

"...hold onto your gravity boots," Leo finished brusquely as a nearby blast wave from some distant skirmish rocked the crippled vessel violently, hurling him into astride the corsair captain. They crashed to the deck together, entwined, her weight momentarily a pleasant offset to inertia in the zero-gee tumult. 

Regaining their footing via frantic scramble, Mercer seized the opportunity, cupping Vex's scarred cheek—now leathery against his palm—with reverent care for the woman who had become his lodestar in the void. For an epoch of heartbeat-less seconds they locked gazes, the fiery intensity of desire and devotion smoldering across an abyss of desperation and death.

Before either could process this raw display of emotion, an urgent comms alert shrieked through the corridors, echoing from every console. Mercer winced at the clamor piercing the charged silence.

"What've we got, op?" he barked toward the source of the distress signal, eyes never leaving Vex's.

"Sir! Starfleet forces are en route fast approach, estimated arrival two dozen minutes," Ops replied calmly against the background din of emergency beacons and crackling energy discharges elsewhere throughout the ship. "Warden-class cruisers led the vanguard. Request command decisions on our response vector..."

Leo swallowed hard, turning to share a knowing glance with Astrid—their lifelines, now and forever entwined against the merciless infinity outside. Decision time loomed like a capital punishment galleon closing for boarding. Hyperjump risked collision at the edge of FTL transit points. Surrender signaled defeat, the extinguishing of starfaring autonomy. All-out defense threatened to vaporize Marauder's already severely compromised frame...

Only one truth cut through chaos like the icy breath upon exposed skin – He'd suffer annihilation at Vex’s side, if only to spare the fiery spirit dwelling within those calloused, beautiful fingers curling around his. In this, the briefest window of existential purgatory existing between destruction and possible salvation, there was no contemplation, merely irrevocable acceptance tempered by the most unwavering loyalty imaginable...

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo's gaze snapped back to the ops hologram hovering before him, the officer's stoic face a stark contradiction to the frenetic chaos erupting behind him in the Marauder's control room. "Vector them off," he ordered curtly, his voice cutting through the cacophony like a razor's edge. "We're not engaging Warden-class at close range."

Astrid snorted derisively, her piercing emerald eyes never faltering from his own. "Bullshit, flyboy. We stand by ourselves – or die alone. Ain't happening." Her grip on his forearm tightened warningly as she started to rise...

But Leo forestalled her motion with a firm hand on her shoulder, the gesture sending a jolt racing up his spine where touch flayed raw nerves. "Not tonight, Vex. If you don't care about living another orbit—"

"I do!" she shot back hotly, her expression twisting into a sneer. "More than you know, soldier boy. I won't rot on some penal moon while Warden scum gut my prize hulk for scrap..." 

His jaw tensed. She was baiting him, trying to goad past his professional filters straight to whatever primal, defensive reaction lay lurking beneath the polished veneer of his training and instincts, forged in a crucible of discipline and sacrifice...

Damn it.

Leo pushed himself fully to standing, using every ounce of resolve to maintain eye contact even while releasing her grip on his arm. "Then stay out of my way until these Warden dogs pass us by – before they decide to turn your precious Marauder into confetti."

"You'll see me right at your six. Where else would pirate Captain Vex want to be but reaping revenge on a fresh scalp?" Astrid flashed that maddening, infuriating smirk – daring, taunting, and yet strangely endearing to him despite everything. "Now drop the sanctimony and get this bucket turned around. My hull doesn't fix itself."

"Might could have spared me this shitshow on my watch if you'd actually kept your wares under wraps—" 

"And what, expected me to surrender years' worth of scavenged tech for a chance at a sweet Alliance payout? Fat fucking chance. Not happening on this ship, when everyone still owes their very lives to Captain Vex's genius and audacity!"

The Marauder lurched beneath their feet, a shivery resonance screaming through decks and bulkhead as hyperdrive engagement finally roared to life. Warning klaxons flared to strobing red alerts amid the sudden, almost physical compression of realspace folding in close proximity – each pulse of subspace turbulence threatening to rend the fragile peace binding them to their fates together...

Leo exhaled hoarsely, the sheer force of their situation overwhelming pride and petty squabbles alike – his 21st century mind unable to wrap around the implications of pirating 32nd century tech, operating on solar winds and scraps alone, pursued by authorities hell bent on crushing such defiance without mercy... The odds were astronomically stacked, far more insurmountable than any dogfight over Earth or Mars.

No wonder she'd chosen him – a man who once flew wingtip to wingtip with angels and devils in the death throes of humanity's birth pains. Astrid needed someone broken enough to understand how far beyond redemption the line stretched into a universe devoid of quarter, charity or grace. And yet in reaching across the yawning chasm of half millennia to claim him, she'd also made herself utterly dependent – setting the two of them upon a collision course that promised explosive outcomes in every conceivable manner...

✦ ✦ ✦

In the moment before Vex initiated the next transit jump, her gaze locked onto Leo's. The blue depths of her eyes swirled like vortex gates opening onto infinity, and he felt himself drowning in them again against his will. Her lips parted fractionally, perhaps to speak, but no words emerged. The heavy quiet hung precariously between them amidst the thrumming energy of the ship's ready lights, straining like live wires waiting for closure.

Then Vex pressed the firing sequence, sending urgent pulses down the fibre lines. Alarms shrieked an ear-piercing requiem to spacetime's imminent betrayal as reality frayed along fault lines of artificial gravity fields, relativistic velocities, and exotic matter drives wrestling dominion over dimensions unknown since creation. 

Leo's heart thundered in his chest just off rhythm with the ship's labored breathing - every inhalation a ravenous gasp for the stolen oxygen they carried exclusively between them against the crushing void outside. Each minute they eked out together was one less step toward oblivion – a desperate truce amidst eternal chaos.

He watched transfixed as her hand hovered near his, poised to key in an emergency quarantine lockdown that would seal them in separate airlocks. Would she press it if something went wrong during transit? As crazy as their partnership already was, even surrendering what remained of his solitary freedom felt safer than facing the alternative abyss alone again...

The dread certainty of impending transition slammed home in a brutal tidal wave, extinguishing thoughts and wrenching breaths away until he gulped desperately at the recycled air mingling so inconspicuously with the tantalizing musk he associated irrevocably with the woman beside him. An instinctive craving for human touch flared within his core - longing for her strong fingers entwined with his once more...

Just then, status displays erupted around them in riotous color-codes, each flashing panel like a dire judgement condemning their foolhardy endeavor. Leo barely registered the warning klaxon as it pierced his eardrums, its cacophony dwarfed by the psychic scream echoing in his skull.

Hyperdrive had failed.

They drifted defenseless in the abyss, suspended motionless while countless parsecs ticked by according to relativistic standards, yet mere seconds in reality. His eyes darted wildly over the readouts, trying and failing to decipher their cryptic gibberish in the rapidly descending panic clawing at his throat.

Somehow, amidst the mounting certainty of their inevitable demise, the cold pragmatism that had twice before nearly cost him everything kicked back into gear. He forced his frazzled nerves to focus on assessing immediate needs: shields, armor plating, remaining fuel reserves, and most crucially the dwindling oxygen supply. All were critical vulnerabilities playing directly into the hands of any hungry leviathan within weapon range.

"What do we have?" Leo demanded, the hoarse rasp of urgency grating through clenched teeth. "Time estimates, V-ex?"

Her gaze snapped from the viewport to meet his, her own features etched with a grim resolve echoing the desperation clawing at his stomach. In this moment, stripped raw by unforgiving circumstance, there was only one person on this entire ship he trusted explicitly to answer that question...

Astrid's voice cut through the din like a crystalline bell against tinny pandemonium. "We've got thirteen minutes of O2 left," she stated flatly, meeting his searching eyes beneath a cloak of steely control. "If we can stabilize at a low orbit and send out an SOS before that, our chance survives."

✦ ✦ ✦

Leo's ears still rang from his frantic query echoing off the bulkheads as Astrid delivered her dire report. Thirteen minutes - hardly enough time for a distress beacon to find its mark let alone draw aid. And yet amid the creeping doom, her eyes remained unflinching, hard-carved granite beneath the flickering lights. 

Without a word, he punched up the external sensors, scouring the starfield for anything resembling potential refuge or rescue options. Beyond streaks of burnt plasma and debris from their ill-fated chase, a kaleidoscope of vibrant hues danced across the viewscreen - swirling nebulae in shades of crimson and indigo, wispy filaments of interstellar gas glowing like luminescent cobwebs against the inky dark. Insects drifting through the cosmos, unaware of the desperate drama playing out amidst their ethereal beauty.

With a jolt, his attention snagged on a pinpoint of light hovering near the edge of frame - not a star, its steadiness too erratic, but something else altogether. Growing bolder, it pulsed with tantalizing frequency, casting a mesmerizing dance of shadow and reflected brilliance upon nearby dust particles. An artificial source. A beamed transmission, fainter than a dying ember, yet persistent in its presence, crying out in vain across impossible light-years. Whoever or whatever its origins, they were no more. This lone message, a ghost echo of forgotten voices, drifted toward them like a lonely seashell on solar winds. And instinct whispered that they must have it. This, above all else, was their singular purpose in facing annihilation.

"Astral signal!" Leo called, urgency spiking his voice until he realized it needed none. His words hung heavy in the air, each pulse of those distant transmissions synchronizing with Astrid's gaze as if by unseen force. Together, they stared transfixed at the viewport as the ghostly call grew marginally stronger, heralding secrets of a bygone era waiting to be unearthed amidst the endless starfields.

"No coincidences in the void," Astrid murmured finally, her voice as still as the gravityless chamber surrounding them. Though time still ticked mercilessly closer to suffocation, neither dared break eye contact, lost instead in the ancient mystery unfolding before their very eyes. For now, survival became secondary - mere semantics against an unspoken truth echoing ever clearer through the cosmos' vast silence. They had crossed uncharted boundaries with every desperate second, bridging epochs and realities alike to reach this appointed instant. And in doing so, perhaps beyond even fleeing extinction, they had become something far greater: harbingers for a new frontier stretching infinitely beyond their wildest imaginings. A reality whose dawn, though treacherously close, would require the swift reckoning of their next actions.
