# Chapter 2: The Magnetar's Pulse

Leo's heart hammered against his sternum as Astrid gripped his hand tight enough to threaten bruising. Their tiny ship shuddered violently under the onslaught of particles and electromagnetic barrage unleashed by the magnetar. The viewport screen flashed warnings red-hot before mercifully fading to amber as system redundancies kicked in. 

Thermal radiation painted both pilots in an iridescent sheen that would have been cool to the touch in normal conditions. In near-vacuum, every exposed centimeter of skin felt seared, hair standing on end as electrons danced in wild eddies around each atom's core. Air circulators struggled just to maintain breathable pockets within the cramped cockpit. Somewhere beneath the maelstrom, metal groaned ominously under stress.

"This close to Sol," Astrid gritted out through gritted teeth, voice barely audible over cavitating hiss of failing air seals, "you'd think it'd be a bit more— predictable." She gestured vaguely at the chaotic storm raging outside their shields. "Next magnetar burst is going to scorch us like a cigarette butt dropped into a bonfire."

Leo nodded jerkily in agreement, attention flitting between instrument panels and the blood-red glow licking at the edges of their ship. "According to my calculations, we've got about... four-point-five minutes until impact—"

"No need for specifics," Astrid cut him off bluntly. As if sensing his rising dread, her grip tightened on his hand. Inexplicably, the action sent a strange thrill through Leo's gut—a mix of anxiety, excitement, and something deeper that he couldn't quite name. "Point being, our window for evasive maneuvers is about to slingshot shut."

She glanced back at Leo before refocusing sharply forward, squinting against the stinging reflection of magnetar energy bathing the cockpit. Outside, stars whorled madly as ship and asteroid tumbled in death-dance around the searing vortex left in the magnetar's wake.

"We're riding an 80-kilometer boulder right into the face of God," Astrid stated, voice taking on a surreal tone of detached amusement. "In real life, they never prepare you for that kind of raw, uncut physics. Just... adrenaline and prayer." A hint of irony tinged her voice.

Leo could only stare, transfixed by Astrid's eerie calm amidst chaos. She piloted the battered ship with an intuitive ferocity, every muscle poised in anticipation of the inevitable crash, yet somehow finding solace in movement rather than stasis.

Suddenly, their rear-quarter viewscreen flared to life with incoming transmission. Symbols scrolled across the bottom edge too quickly for either pilot to follow, accompanied by a shrill klaxton of warning tones.

"What in—?" Leo interrupted himself, gaze snapped wide-eyed toward the display. Whatever language those hieroglyphics represented, one image stood crystal clear at center: the schematized profile of a massive vessel, sleek dark hull undulating with tendrils of exotic matter thrust.

Astrid swore in two languages as realization settled like an anvil onto their shoulders. "That can only mean..." She trailed off, shaking her head in grudging acceptance. "Time's run out. Looks like I'm trading one set of owners for another."

Her words hung heavy in the charged air as magnetar particles pummeled their ship ruthlessly, making every joint ache with simulated pressure. Yet for some reason—some fool notion born of desperation and shared danger—those words carried an almost hopeful subtext.

Captain Astrid Vex Ross slammed the Starlight Marauder into evasive maneuvers as the distant magnetars erupted, their searing pulses slamming against the hull like cosmic fists. Alarms shrieked and console screens flickered, spewing raw data that Leo Mercer struggled to process through pounding adrenaline.

"What now, Cap?" he asked, a rasping whisper against his helmet's comms pickup. Even amid utter bedlam, Leo couldn't help but marvel at Vex's serene focus, each rapid keystroke or subtle tug on the yoke a testament to grit and skill honed over centuries beyond his comprehension.

"Just... adrenaline and prayer," she replied, a wry note threading through her husky voice. For a moment, Leo saw past the scars and calloused hands to catch a glimpse of Astrid Ross's essence—the roiling sea beneath the stoic veneer, desperate for calm, yet forever locked in tempestuous dance with the cosmos.

Her reply barely registered before the battered freighter lurched again, compensating for the ship's frantic dives and banks. Onscreen, distant stars streaked across the viewspace like meteors, while within the cramped cockpit, the artificial gravity simulation made every joint throb with stress-simulated pressure. Sweat trickled down Leo's neck, leaving shimmering trails in the faint light cast by their status displays.

The Starlight Marauder groaned under the relentless assault as the outer solar system tore itself apart around them. Then, like a gout of bloody mercury injected into the chaos, a massive foreign form strobed starkly across the aft quarterscreen. Leo instinctively braced, ready to take evasive action, even as his mind struggled to make sense of what he was seeing—a towering saucer-hulled warship, her sleek obsidian hide threaded with strange, ethereal tentacles that seemed to writhe of their own accord.

His incredulous gaze flicked to Astrid, a mirror of her shock etched on his own face. "What in—?" he started, trailing off as understanding crashed down upon them both like a celestial anvil.

Astrid's expression slowly settled into grim resignation as she met Leo's dawning comprehension. "Those hieroglyphs... they don't look local. Hell, I'd bet my black heart we still have no idea how to decipher half their meaning."

She exhaled sharply, muscles rippling beneath the stretched canvas of her flight suit. "Looks like we're trading one lot of owners for another. And right about now...

"Let me rephrase that," Astrid continued quietly, turning to fix Leo with her most piercing glare yet. In that instant, a startling glimmer passed between them—a fleeting spark of something akin to hope, born perhaps from the sheer audacity of facing an unknown enemy together. "We'll sell our souls again if it means surviving this frakking nightmare."

In the oppressive silence left behind, Leo felt the true import of their fate settle heavily between them, mingling with acrid smells of ozone and scorched wiring, painting their skin with an inescapable veneer of desperation—and maybe, just maybe—something closer to purpose.

✦ ✦ ✦

Astrid punched the comm button viciously, barking into the open frequency, 'Starlight Marauder, diverting orbits! Initiating evasive burn and plotting new intercept course. Someone, get me navigation!' The ship lurched as secondary engines roared to life, and warning klaxons screamed through her helmet speakers, making her ears ache.

Leo gripped the arms of his seat tightly enough to turn his knuckles white, fighting the centrifugal force that pressed him back into the cushion. Sweat beaded on his forehead despite the cold, sterile air circulating through the helm.

'Navigation?' Astrid demanded more insistently as the vessel shuddered around them, metal creaking ominously. No response. Frustration and fear twisted her features into a knot. They were alone, unarmed, outgunned, and hurtling toward the hellish maw of an implacable enemy they could barely comprehend. The only people who should have known where they were headed, or what lurked there, were stone dead.

She turned a blistering glare on Leo, her voice low and deadly. 'Time we took matters into our own hands.' Swift, precise movements guided her hand across a glowing touchscreen. 'Target that power nexus.' A holographic projection of the approaching world spun to superimpose targeting reticles over critical infrastructure. 'Once we disable those weapons—

Screech-sharp, the warning bleat cut her off—the ship's core had lost primary feed, plunging them into darkness save for emergency red panels that bathed the cockpit in an eerie, oppressive glow.

'Hull breach at Frame 42!' bellowed an AI voice. 'Pressure dropping fast! Estimated ten minutes to reach depressurization threshold.'

The sudden loss rattled the Marauder like a tin can struck by lightning. Alarms yowled, servos whined, and warning lights strobed red against the bulkhead plates. Astrid slammed her palm against the armrest, fighting to swallow her panic.

Behind the dark visor, Leo's eyes widened as he took in the dire situation unfolding before them. He met Astrid's intent stare, reading the unspoken challenge there. This was no time for hesitation.

Craning back in his chair, Leo seized the opportunity while Astrid focused on main systems checks. One fluid motion clipped his harness to the frame, then he launched himself across the space dividing them, slamming against her sternum with bruising force but holding fast. Her head snapped back against the seat headrest with a muted thud.

'Leo!' she hissed, shocked and furious. Still pinned in place by his body, she made a grab for her pistol holstered within easy reach. He shifted, twisting the weapon from its strap and bringing it around to press the barrel to her temple.

'I need you at full functionality,' he rasped against her ear, voice shaking harder than the starboard plates. 'In case things go sideways in mid-burn.'

For a heartbeat that threatened to stretch forever, Astrid froze, poised between fury and a strange, thrumming jolt that seemed to vibrate up her spine and set her nerve ends alight. Then her fingers closed around his wrist in a vice-like grip, squeezing until he gritted his teeth.

'Shit, Mercer...' she whispered, exhaling slowly. 'Next time consult. It's not exactly the most subtle way to establish trust.' But even as she spoke, her pulse raced with the wild, disorienting thrill of that brief, electrifying contact. She couldn't quite meet his eyes behind his mask.

✦ ✦ ✦

Astrid registered Mercer's rigid posture and burning gaze beyond the clear visor, still reeling from the collision of their bodies. His breath punched hot against her skin, mingling with the acrid tang of sweat and burned-on sunscreen. She struggled to catch her breath, heart thudding against her ribs like a trapped animal.

The silence stretched taut between them, charged with unspoken words and a tension that snapped the air around them like static. Astrid could feel the heat radiating off Mercer's form, a wall of lean muscle and explosive power. His firm restraint of her struggling limbs sent jolts of electricity down her nerves, making her skin prickle and flushed beneath her enviro-suit.

Reluctantly releasing her grip, Astrid exhaled shakily and met his burning gaze, those emerald eyes sparking with intensity and barely concealed fear. "Alright, you had me," she admitted grudgingly, the admission costing her something. "Now we good?"

Mercer's fingers flexed, maintaining the pressure of the gun to her temple as if reluctant to relinquish control. But after a long moment, he nodded briskly, stepping back and removing the muzzle from her skull. "Let's get this burn finished."

As the magnetic clamps released him from the harness, Astrid seized the opportunity to scramble into her seat once more. Hands trembling slightly, she restarted the engines roaring to life, spewing fire-blue light across the dark void.

"Starlight's responding," she reported, feeding power to the fusion drives. Alarms quieted as systems stabilized. Within seconds, the small vessel began to accelerate, climbing out of the planet's weakening gravity well.

Outside, the universe blurred past in a kaleidoscope of star-streaked indigo, white-gold flame etching the darkness. G-forces pushed them back into their seats, a relentless press forcing laughter-chinned faces taut under transparent helmets. Sweat beaded on Astrid's brow, clinging in tendrils that streaked down her cheeks like tears frozen in crystal droplets.

"Soak it in while you can, Vex," Mercer called over the roar of rushing vacuum. His words carried on the hiss of recycled air, tinged with a note of somber reverence. "We're entering the magnetar's radio horizon."

Astrid tracked their velocity and trajectory on the dashboard displays, marveling at the incredible energies unleashed by the pulsing star they pursued. Solar flares cast flickering shadows on the gleaming black surfaces of the Starlight Marauder, each dazzling burst an inferno of hot plasma racing across hundreds of lightyears.

"That's incredible..." She breathed, awestruck despite years of confronting the awe-inspiring and often terrifying phenomena of the cosmos. Astrid had witnessed supernovas that birthed new stars, neutron stars pirouetting through gamma-fire storms, and black holes vomiting forth relativistic jets that carved through galaxy clusters – each leaving her humbled before nature's majesty. Still, the magnetar eclipsed them all in raw seething power.

"We're getting a lot of neutrino readings," Mercer said, scrutinizing data streams with an expression tight with focused concentration. "If we calculate correctly, it may—"

His warning came a fraction too late. A burst of exotic particles slammed into the ship's shielding, causing the helm console to wobble and flicker with discharged energy. Alarms shrieked anew, demanding attention.

"Hull damage!" Astrid barked, fingers flying over controls to divert power and reroute systems.
