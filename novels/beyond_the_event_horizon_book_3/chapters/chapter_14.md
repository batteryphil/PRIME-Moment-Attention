# Chapter 14: Spacewalk Under Fire

Commander Leo Mercer gripped the padded edges of the extravehicular lock hatch with trembling fingers, trying not to look at the looming singularity devouring billions of kilometers of star-strewn wilderness outside the viewing portals. The white noise static of the life support systems masked most other sounds, but beneath that eerie blankness he heard—faintly—the highpitched whine of pumps charging his EVA suit to nominal operating pressure. It was an indistinguishable hum among the cacophony of life signs beeping and flashing indicator lights, a constant reminder that only a thin layer of superstrength polymer and pressurized air separated him from venturing out into suffocating darkness with no return.

"You sure about this, Astrid?"

The skipper flashed him a smirk from where she stood rigidly opposite, mirrored visor reflecting the frantic activity within his helmet like the polished convex surface of sunglasses. "Not just sure – excited. Who gets to play space chicken with the mother of all black holes everyday? Hell, I might even get my first close-up glimpse of those spooky accretion disk winds. What do you say, Leo - ready to dip our toes in eternity?"

Leo barely managed a snort through thinned lips. Ready as he'd ever be to stare Armageddon in the face... He slid the outer seal closed with a reassuring thunk, feeling the chill vacuum seep through the sealant as the interior atmosphere equalized at a mere few percent oxygen partial pressure.

"Don't let me forget to send some prayers to whichever deity you worship," he muttered wryly.

"Sarcasm noted," Astrid replied dryly, tapping her temple – supposedly a neural link to something resembling a deity according to Corsair lore. Whether genuine faith or cleverly crafted bluff remained anyone's guess. All that mattered was whether whatever force granted her crew such audacious luck held sway for them once more. If so...

"We've got fifteen minutes to defuse this fire," Astrid said, cutting the tension and gesture at the critical status display flashing red amidst a sea of amber warnings illuminating the cockpit control panels.

As if on cue, the bridge erupted in a symphony of klaxons and flashing lights - the ship itself shrieking its distress amid mounting heat and approaching critical failure. With only seconds left on the countdown timer, Astrid slammed open the inner hatch, pulling both of them out onto the exposed walkways surrounding the central command axis. She reached down, seized a cylindrical maintenance pod with one gloved hand, and hurled it at an angle across the bridge, watching with hawk-like focus as it tumbled forward in zero gravity before striking hard against the edge of an emergency shuttle bay door.

Leo watched in rapt attention as Astrid grabbed another pod from storage, spinning effortlessly on one toe as she prepared for launch herself. In this environment, without the artificial gravity fields that sustained human physiology on board ships, every movement required meticulous calculation and precision—lest one become the ship's worst enemy.

"Time's running out," she growled over the comms system, voice muffled slightly by the snug fit of her helmet and the background din of hissing valves and warbling alarm sirens. "Need you to cover my back on this one, commander. We're headed into the reactor compartment."

Mercer nodded curtly within the confines of his own helmet, hands working swiftly along the arm of his suit to unfold the grappling tether coiled there. Its thin yet nearly indestructible carbon fiber strands could withstand tens of thousands of gees' acceleration stress without failure—and likely far more under vacuum conditions if needed.

Scene 2: Spacewalk Under Fire

The bridge erupted in chaos around them, strobing blue light casting eerie shadows as laser blasts sizzled through the thin hull plating. Astrid sprinted toward the gaping hole torn by Leo's earlier explosive decompression, propulsive muscles straining beneath her skintight spacesuit as she battled planetfall gravity while hauling twice her body weight in a portable air cylinder slung from her belt.

Leo scrambled after her, boots clomping heavily against the grated catwalks despite the best effort of his microgravity dampeners to approximate Earthlike traction. Ropes snagged behind him, trailing like a macramé snake, as he released his grip on the exposed structural members and lunged into the void beside Astrid. A gout of escaping gas belched past their heads, stinging eyes shut. They fell together, arms instinctively wrapping in a tight embrace as they drifted toward the damaged bulkhead.

Astrid shoved off a nearby surface just in time, her booted foot knocking the breath from Leo as she angled herself away from the jagged hole now yawning wide mere meters behind. Alarms wailed on and on—structural warnings, depressurization alerts, damage reports tumbling in from throughout the crippled hulk of the starship.

"What the fuck were you thinking, pilot boy?" Astrid snarled, her words distorted by crackling static in the suit comms. "You couldn't have waited five damn minutes?"

The anger in her tone jarred, a rare frison amidst the numbing terror and adrenaline pumping through Leo's veins. He glanced sidelong at Vex, noting the feral intensity burning in those icy blue eyes beneath a mask of carbon fiber faceplate—a look reserved only for battle situations where survival truly hung in the balance.

"Suffocated civilians wasn't enough?"
he retorted hotly.

A blast of searing wind buffeted them hard from above, almost ripping the safety line tethers still clinging desperately to their wrists. Together they pirouetted, limbs flailing wildly as they struggled to regain control in near-weightlessness.

Once stabilized again, Leo shot an accusing glare her way, hand gripping the rope like a taloned fist. "Your call, captain. You want to play hero and risk us both to secure that reactor core access? Fine. I'll cover your back, but don't expect me to follow orders blindly ever again under fire, understand? Not even yours."

Astrid paused, the faint red glow of reentry heat suffusing her suit as the ship grew hotter and hotter. After a moment's consideration, her features softened into a grim yet respectful expression—the kind reserved for an adversary acknowledged worthy, even in extremis.

"You did what you had to do back there," she allowed grudgingly beneath the helmet's metallic reverberations. "I wouldn't have hesitated either. Doesn't change the facts though, commander... we're not exactly on the same page when planetary politics collide with personal morals. It's gonna be real fucking interesting trying to navigate the moral implications of that explosive situation once we get to the bottom of who hired these bastards."

Leo bit back a savage retort threatening to tear loose in response, realizing with a jolt of trepidation that not only was this a struggle to align tactical goals, but also a battle being waged within Astrid herself—and potentially within himself, as well, given their vastly different backgrounds and ethics.

With new resolve, he faced forward again, squinting against the glare of distant starfires bleeding over the curvature of Kaida Prime below as the atmospheric shell thinned to a blue haze.

Leo Mercer's heart thundered as the tether rattled ominously, jerking him heedlessly closer to the Marauder's spinning hull. Sweat trickled beneath his goggles, stinging eyes already constricted by the solar radiance blazing mercilessly at near-orbital velocities. Just behind them, the disintegrating junk freighter flared into a brief, incandescent cinder before collapsing into streaks of ionized plasma.

Astrid Vex's voice cut through the screeching static as she struggled valiantly to restrain their wayward lifeline amidst the tempest raged by the rapidly thinning upper atmosphere:

"Damn it, damn it, hang on, I almost—got it! Fuck, that was too close!"

The momentum still sent both spacecraft spiraling uncontrollably as Astrid hastily locked off their tether reel, forcing Leo to anchor with hand-over-hand tugs down the haphazard rope whipping erratically around the asteroid field. His gloved fingers ached with each brutal grab amid the lacerating grit and razor splinters scoured raw from their impact crater.

"You good out there?" Astrid asked tightly while wrestling with the ship at his end, her words clipped from sheer effort over comms lost in the howling maelstrom between them.

Leo risked a glance toward the tumbling freighter's skeletal remains and turned away from the searing light of Kaidaprine's ascending sun blazing like a miniature supernova. The planet's cobalt seas and emerald forests shimmered tantalizingly visible beyond the translucent veil of atmosphere already consumed by superheated gas and molten rock.

"Not great," Leo replied hoarsely, grunting as another particularly vicious jolt tore his grip nearly loose before locking down again on the frayed line above him. "But I'm here. What about the ship? Still on course?"

"Yeah, barely. Thrusters are shot, navigation's wonky, and I've got structural stress mounts climbing through the red zones," Astrid ground out. "But at least we managed to stay clear of whatever nukes they were planning to drop after us. Once we stabilize and reboot the AI, I'll start scouting repairs...if we don't get ourselves snared in this damned debris first."

She paused, exhaling a deep breath that puffed out mistily in the vacuum before continuing:

"If not for that lucky spin from your emergency jettison—sorry, couldn't resist—damn straight I owe you one now too. Maybe we should work on our team coordination sometime, huh?"

Leo resisted a chuckle despite the terror still gripping his guts. This woman really knew how to make an old flyboy's heart skip a beat during the most improbable flight of his life. When they finally managed to wrest free of the chaotic asteroids, limp exhausted against the airlock's magnetic catch after another nerve-wracking hour, he realized just how intimately entangled they'd become in more than just metal lines—and how much it pained him to realize their roles would be worlds apart someday soon...

Because regardless of their budding trust, or whatever other secrets they might cautiously reveal, one thing remained as certain as the unforgiving laws of gravity themselves—Ideals like theirs could never reconcile upon the kill-or-be-killed playing fields of the Rim's illicit fringes.

The airlock released its magnetic hold as Leo drifted back into the Starlight Marauder under his own dwindling momentum. As he tumbled slowly toward the main compartment, lights reactivated across the ship's battered hull, casting strobing shadows across asteroid-scorched plates.

"Astrid? You okay?" Leo called over the intercom as he pressed himself against the outer hull, arms spread, and let his centrifugal motion wind down.

"We're...still coherent," Vex replied, her voice laced with exhaustion's brittle edge, echoing from the helm. A moment later, the hatch sealed shut behind him, its inner door sliding open with a pneumatic hiss.

Leo pushed off the cold surface, catching himself before colliding with his fellow survivor amidst the cramped airlock chamber. They locked eyes, gazes lingering briefly in a silent acknowledgment beyond spoken words - a shared understanding of the perils they'd faced and escaped, bound by threads thicker than any mere human connection ought to sustain.

Yet even then, beyond the confines of their beleaguered vessel, amidst the wreckage of what had once been stable orbits and star-spanning ambitions, Leo sensed the vast expanse stretching around them like an inscrutable titan awakening...

He glanced past Vex at the forward main viewer and gasped. Through the scratched acrylic, the distant spiral arms of the Whirlpool Galaxy pierced the blackness, galaxies, stars, and glowing nebulas sweeping across their viewport like spectral brushstrokes. Jupiter-sized crimson pulsars flanked the massive galaxy's core, beaming eerie cosmic rays and magnetically anchored radiation storms.

"Holy shit..." Leo breathed, his hands drifting involuntarily upward towards the unseen reaches of the universe without truly connecting. The breathtaking vista dwarfed every memory, accomplishment, or dream he'd ever had. Before this instant, his grasp of infinite scales was limited to theoretical equations and abstract simulations. Now, the very fabric of creation unspooled before his awestruck gaze.

"Try the sensors after we get the shields up," he suggested, reluctantly tearing his attention away to face reality, to salvage what bits they could manage here, there. There'll be no triumphant victory parades awaiting them back home...if they made it back at all. Only cold data to analyze, damaged pride to heal, and haunting thoughts of what might've been if fate hadn't cruelly jerked them both across lightyears to this unforgiving rim of civilization.

As the klaxons subsided from the last stages of post-impact diagnostics, quiet fell across the bridge. Astrid broke the oppressive silence:

"Looks like our long-range transponder took a direct hit. Anything we beam back will be pinging for days until someone finds the distress frequency buried amongst the noise..."

Leo nodded grimly, knowing full well what those long, agonizing days would mean if rescued. Even with all the medical marvels back Earthside, the toll of prolonged hibernation and the mysteries they ported in from the future - whether cursed alien nanotech or temporal anomalies that ate entire fleets, leaving only whisper-thin ripples of quantum decay - they likely would never step foot again on the soil of Sol-Three. Any hope of rekindling lives already lived on Terra, of reconnecting bonds with families who must have mourned their loss eons ago, slipped further away with each passing orbit of the dead world below.

Their journey would forge them anew, perhaps into legends whispered in hushed bars on forgotten colony worlds, but at the cost of forever being cut off not only from loved ones but from everything humanity held dear about itself - history woven from myth and reality, laughter in sun-d.
