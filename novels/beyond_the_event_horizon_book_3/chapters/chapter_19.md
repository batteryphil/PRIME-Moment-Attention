# Chapter 19: The Gateway Discovered

Chapters 19: The Gateway Discovered 1

Cold darkness surrounded Starlight Marauder like a shroud - chillier even than the sterile vacuum at the heart of Groom Lake's missile silos back in Leo's own century. Though shields and armor had absorbed what shockwaves could reach this far out from Pyrax's ruins, the ship shuddered ominously as internal casualties racked up a deadly tally. Pressure alarms screamed in Leo's skull until he pinched his ears shut, muffling the auditory assault but leaving the chilling whine of stressed hull materials ringing in his mind.

He stared into Captain Ross's pale reflection etched beside him, her troubled gaze mirrored in his own, as they floated among debris strewn across the ruined command deck. Scorch marks singed the ceiling plating overhead; severed wires crawled across console surfaces like charred arachnids gone mad. And yet, no matter how catastrophic the damage inflicted on their battered vessel, it paled compared to the existential crisis looming beyond the viewport.

At first, Leo took it for another hallucination born of fatigue - though not one nearly so pretty as the vision of Astri leaning close earlier. The ancient Jump Ring drifting serenely in the starfield seemed to pulse with bioluminescent energy, its maw agape open in a permanent scream as if some cataclysmic event had sundered its structure in two.

"We're moving..." Astrid whispered, turning in slow-motion to peer aft where the wreckage of Pyrax continued its lethal dance behind them. Leo frowned, following her gaze - and understood then what must be happening despite instruments insisting otherwise: the crippled Marauder, bleeding mass into space, was gradually falling under the inexorable pull of nearby worldlets or planetoids... which left one terrible implication:

They were no longer within the habitable realm of their original destination or intended route. No nearby rescue or resupply mission would intercept them here while lost amidst the vast emptiness of interstellar gulfs and nomadic asteroid clouds. Their chances of being found, especially amid these hazardous star systems teeming with unstable megastructures and aggressive alien species, stood around zero.

Captain Ross spun fully around, pale gaze raking the smoldering wreckage littering consoles - then fixed upon Leo with steely determination he'd come to associate with times of extreme stress or decision making. "Mercer," she barked authoritatively, every syllable crisp and clear as polished obsidian cutting the air between them, "you know star navigation better than anyone else aboard. I want you to pinpoint our current location relative to those Jump Rings." She jerked a thumb over her shoulder at the monolithic gateway now steadily growing larger in the viewport.

"Understood, Cap'n," Leo replied crisply, hands flying across the navigation console with practiced precision as he plotted their position from available debris field data and residual sensor readings. His brow furrowed in concentration, eyes squinting slightly against the bright starlight reflecting off the Marauder's shattered viewscreen.

In mere seconds, astronomic projections scrolled across the display, rendering his grim assessment in stark visual terms. Leo swiveled the screen for Vex to examine, fingers hovering instinctively over comm controls.

Their vessel had drifted too far along the wrong vectors, pulled by celestial bodies in some nearby system that bore little relation to their intended course toward the Tauran Spiral. He felt her appraising stare boring into him as she studied the nav data.

"There, see?" he said gravely, hooking a thumb at the screen. "Too many parsecs off-track for any rescue or relief crew to reach us. Current trajectory suggests we'll intersect the outermost orbit of a gas giant sometime tomorrow K-day, but then it's a one-way trip to the inner system on our present fuel state..." His voice trailed off, the unspoken implication heavy and grim: The Starlight Marauder was doomed to crash and burn like all the other wreckages they'd encountered out here among the derelict ruins.

Captain Astrid 'Vex' Ross let the silence hang, expression unreadable beneath her helm. When at last she spoke, her words carried the sting of criticism aimed at a subordinate:

"Should've seen this sooner, Lieutenant."

Leo flashed a quick, wry grin, bristling slightly at her tone despite understanding the pressure circumstances dictated. "Had my eyes up on nav plots nonstop since the hit, ma'am. Just didn't anticipate getting tugged into orbit by unknown gravity wells until it was too late."

"This isn't about blame or second-guessing your watchkeeping skills, Mercer," Vex cut him off firmly. "It's about facts versus fiction in a universe full of uncertainties. Face the music – we're running low on options here with the damage, our fuel reserves, and the Jump Rings looming ahead. What's your honest assessment?"

Leo considered the question, mind racing. Inwardly, he knew he should urge caution, perhaps even advocate for hailing the mysterious entities inhabiting those ancient structures - at least to learn if such beings could render aid rather than posing threats. But his gut told him otherwise. In this harsh, unforgiving expanse of cosmic space, survival meant adaptability and swift decisions, sometimes walking the razor's edge between prudence and peril.

"Honestly?" He shrugged. "I reckon our best shot lies with finding a way past those Jump Rings, Cap'n. I know the risks look steep, but it beats crashing into whatever hostile hellhole orbits ahead. And if the Rings are still operational or can provide a stable enough slipstream effect, maybe it'd grant us enough delta-v to alter course significantly before exiting or give us time to make repairs before landing."

He finished speaking, anticipating the scathing retort he usually faced whenever someone proposed deviating from established flight plans or resorting to desperate measures involving uncharted phenomena. Yet instead, a fleeting glimmer of intrigue flickered in the captain's eyes - just long enough to make him wonder if there wasn't more to Vex than met the eye in her tough-as-nails exterior.

After a tense moment, Captain Ross let out a short, bitter chuckle. "Looks like you've decided to trade your pilot's wings for a foolhardy daredevil's cape, huh, Mercer?"

✦ ✦ ✦

The mariner's smirk playing on her lips, Captain Astrid 'Vex' Ross leaned back in her chair, arms folded behind her head as she met Commander Mercer's gaze, unblinking and unwavering. His insubordinate suggestion hung heavy in the air, weighing upon them both like the oppressive gravity of some distant gas giant mere light-years away.

"Well now... seems the boy genius has caught a case of hubris," she drawled, a note of playful malice threading beneath the lazy cadence of her words. With languid slowness, she extended a long, pale finger, tapping Mercer's chest above the rumpled tactical suit. "Begs the question, Lieutenant Commander Maverick here..."

Her voice drifted off, letting the rank title roll around it like a curse, emphasizing the audacity of his dare. She narrowed her eyes, the pupils pinpricks of intense focus against the icy blue of her irises. The scar above her right eyebrow, souvenir of a bygone smuggling run gone wrong, stood stark and livid against milky-white skin.

"You really think we should recklessly barrel through some derelict space station's relic defenses, blind hope alone guiding us?" Her sneer turned sarcastic. "Or perhaps you imagine it'll be a joyride akin to an arcade game—shoot the asteroids, grab the power-ups!" 

Mercer bristled at the mocking tone, yet couldn't help his own quickened pulse, thrill and fear mingling equal parts within him. "That's not exactly what I said, Cap—" He cut himself off when Vex raised an elegant, silver-tipped brow in challenge. "Alright, alright. You're right, it's madness. Crashing into some godforsaken orbiting doom sounds peachy after trying to sneak past Jump Rings. Maybe we can even find a shiny bonus treasure along the way! Just what the doctor ordered before facing my upcoming court-martial."

Despite the biting sarcasm that left his lips, he felt his face heat up ever so slightly—the flush of adrenaline mixed with a lingering undertow of awareness whenever Vex was near. There was something undeniably captivating about the infamous corsair captain that went beyond mere dangerous allure. It was the unpredictability, the raw intelligence shimmering behind those piercing eyes, as well as an underlying sense of vulnerability barely concealed beneath the gruff and impenetrable façade.

Vex laughed again, but this time without mockery, a husky appreciative chuckle tinged with amusement. It drew attention to the tantalizing curve of the captain's mouth, inviting and sensual when those lips were not set in a stern line. A flicker of understanding glinted in her gaze, seeming to speak volumes about their shared experiences navigating treacherous terrains where one misstep could spell disaster.

"Aye commander, Captain Braveheart here sure knows a thing or two about courting recklessness," she quipped, a ghost of a smile tracing the porcelain perfection of her cheekbones. "Perhaps, with any luck, the detour will give me a chance to redeem my reputation...or have it further tarnished! Either way, I get to see if Lieutenant Maverick actually lives up to his heroic billing—" 

She paused, her attention dropping to scan the holo displays flanking the command console once more, eyes calculating distances and trajectories with lightning-fast speed. When she looked up again, it was directly at Mercer, her expression intense and uncompromising—a dare in itself, silently challenging him to meet her unyielding stare.

"But first things first, hotshot."

Chapter 19, Scene 4:

With a nod acknowledging the captain's declaration of intent, Commander Leo Mercer rose from the navigator station. He stretched lethargically, back cracking protestingly after hours cooped in the cramped cockpit. "First things first indeed," he agreed dryly, heading for the hatch. As he grasped the lever, he turned to face his unlikely confdant once more.

His eyes met Vex's piercing blue orbs, searching them for the threads of bravado and doubt that wove the intricate tapestry of her psyche. Something resonated between them—an acknowledgment of shared scars, accumulated mileage etched on both body and soul in irrevocable ink. They'd danced perilously close to the abyss together these past few days—that suffocating darkness where certainties crumbled and only willpower remained intact between you and oblivion—and emerged relatively whole.

Leo smiled cryptically, the corners of his mouth flexing in a gesture equal parts camaraderie and warning born of bitter experience. Understanding flashed between them—the knowledge that even now, they sailed a thin wire stretched over an unforgiving void, balancing recklessness against survival. But damn if it wasn't exhilarating.

"Astrography reports a promising anomaly just beyond our projected turnaround point," Vex said, voice smooth as polished steel as she steered their discussion away from sentimentality back toward practical objectives. "Appears to be an undiscovered gateway—a wormhole potentially connecting us to other star systems within a reasonable distance."

Mercer's head snapped around, eyes widening at the revelation. Even in the sterile confines of their spacecraft, the sheer implications electrified the air. His palms grew sweaty despite the temperature control, pulse quickening in anticipation. 

"That changes everything," he breathed, awe washing over him like a tsunami as the vast possibilities unfolded in his mind. The thought that countless worlds, civilizations, could be mere astronomical stones' throws away yanked the bottom out of his stomach in a stomach-churning plunge of cosmic scope and scale.

Vex nodded grimly, a calculating look settling over her features. "Indeed. If we can manipulate the gateway's geometry right—for a stable hop through, that is—this could be our ticket out of this war zone, a shortcut home and reconnection with the fleet." 

As her words sank in, Mercer slowly turned back to grasp the hatch release mechanism. Before pushing it open to expose the narrow access corridor, he glanced sideways at the commander of the Starlight Marauder. Her focus had once more narrowed to the holographic astrogation display, fingers dancing across the controls with the practiced ease of long habit.

"We might be trading one hell for another," Mercury cautioned quiet as a knife's edge. "A wormhole that could spit us out god-knew-where..."

"In a nutshell," Vex rejoined equally soft, flashing him a feral grin laced with defiance. "Welcome to my life aboard the Starlight Marauder. Now stop worrying and start flying, because we're about to dive into the unknown again."
