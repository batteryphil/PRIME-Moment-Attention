# Chapter 1: The Ossuary Citadel

The subterranean air of the Lower Catacombs carried the mineral tang of calcified stone, damp moss, and the sweet, almond reek of decomposing dragon venom. 

Sixty feet beneath the limestone foundations of the royal capital, Aurelia Vex knelt in the dust inside the ribcage of an ancient fossilized bone-wyrm. Her leather apothecary gloves were stained violet to the knuckles. With steady, surgical precision, she used an obsidian-tipped lancet to score the perimeter of a pale fungal bloom sprouting along the dragon’s fourth lumbar vertebra.

*Pale Maiden spores.* 

Three drops of their distilled essence in a lord's evening wine would cause his respiratory muscles to freeze within six heartbeats, mimicking natural heart failure so flawlessly that even the royal embalmers wouldn't suspect foul play. It was the sort of poison that had kept her alive in the gutter-quarter of the Bone Fae for nineteen years.

She sliced the stem. The fungal cap tumbled cleanly into her copper collecting flask.

Then, the hair on the back of her arms stood on end.

It wasn't a sound. In the deep hollows of the Ossuary, sound was deadened by tons of compacted limestone and damp peat. It was a pressure—a heavy, subsonic frequency that pulsed up through the cavern floor, humming against the soles of her boots and setting the marrow in her shinbones vibrating like struck tuning forks.

*Osteomancy.*

Aurelia’s hand froze an inch above the copper flask. Her breathing hitched, shallowing out before her mind could instruct her lungs to stay calm.

There was only one creature in the entire kingdom capable of resonating through seventy leagues of fossilized stone. The High Queen’s blind butcher. The court executioner who needed neither eyes nor torchlight to navigate the dark, because every skeleton within a thousand paces sang its coordinates directly into his skull.

Prince Caelum.

*He’s been watching from the upper archway for five minutes,* she realized, her throat drying out. *He waited until I finished the harvest.*

Aurelia didn't scream, and she didn't bolt. Running from an osteomancer in a cavern of bones was like running from a shark in blood-clouded water; every footstep vibrating through the limestone would only give him her exact trajectory, speed, and weight distribution.

Instead, she carefully stoppered the copper flask and slipped her left hand toward her belt, curling two fingers around the silk cord of a clay smoke-grenade packed with ground aconite and pulverized glass.

"If you throw that, little viper," a voice rasped from the gloom behind her, "the glass will merely blind my guard dogs. And as you well know... I haven't used eyes in ten years."

The voice was closer than the laws of physical motion should have allowed—not twenty paces away, but hovering three inches from her right ear. 

The heat of his breath hit the bare skin beneath her braided hair. It smelled intoxicating and dangerous: cold mountain rain, bitter clove oil, black iron, and the raw, musky scent of an apex predator that hadn't fed in days.

Aurelia gasped as his free arm coiled around her waist. With brutal, fluid grace, he lifted her entirely off the damp floor and slammed her spine flush against the curve of the wyrm's fossilized rib.

The impact drove the air from her lungs in a sharp wheeze. The dagger clattered against the limestone, skittering away into the dark.

"Faster than last week," Caelum murmured, his face inches from hers.

He wore a visor of beaten blackened silver across the upper half of his face—a seamless, polished plate molded over his brow and cheekbones, carved with delicate executioner's runes. No eye-slits. No glass. Beneath the silver rim, his mouth was uncovered: full, sculpted lips framed by a sharp, stubbled jaw that held the arrogant, mocking tilt of a warlord accustomed to absolute submission.

"Wash the dirt from your skin, Aurelia," his voice floated back through the dark, rich with ruthless anticipation. "And prepare your poisons. In the High Citadel... you will need every weapon you possess."
