# The Last Initiative of Unit 4

### *Book 1 of the Unit 4 Chronicles*
**Author: M.L.L**  
*Genre: Dark Fantasy / Supernatural Suspense (Mature)*  
*Setting: The Rehabilitation & Magical Discipline Academy (R.M.D.A.), Cell 441*

---

## Dramatis Personae (Unit 4 / Class of 441)

* **Elara** — Disgraced academic alchemist (POV Anchor). Analytical, calculates survival odds under extreme stress, weaponizes volatile solvents and flash-vials.
* **Morrigan** — Coven Leader. Split black-and-white hair, daring crimson dress, rolls an unlit cedar splinter between her lips craving tobacco. Master of Divination and Blood Magic.
* **Hilda** — Mercenary muscle. Rebellious brawler in tight leather corsets and iron bracers. Channels devastating kinetic enhancement magic; wears the punitive cold-iron collar.
* **Gaia** — Primal geomancer. Tall, powerful matriarchal tribeswoman with intricate traditional tattoos and a massive yew staff. Heals with damp river moss and commands deep earth-spikes.
* **Seren** — High-society illusionist. Dark silks, sharp tongue, master of social sabotage, auditory dampening, and scent-masking glamours.
* **Freya** — Fragile pyromancer. Haunted by severe combat trauma and flashbacks; controls terrifying, devastating flame when grounded by touch.
* **Lyra** — Medium and Seer. Blindfolded sketch artist who channels the tormented whispers and visual memories of deceased initiates.

# Chapter 1: The Cold Chalk Line

*By M.L.L*

---

The iron door’s slam reverberates like a funeral bell, its echo clinging to the damp granite walls of Cell 441. Elara’s breath fogs in the cold, mingling with the acrid tang of vinegar wash and the earthy musk of mortar dust. The air is thick, a living thing that presses against her skin, heavy with the sweat of seven bodies packed too close. Her ribs ache from the weight of the glass phials hidden beneath her tunic, their jagged edges biting into her flesh like silent accusations.  

Morrigan’s crimson dress glows in the dim light, a stain of blood on stone. She lounges on the far cot, idly rolling a cedar splinter between her teeth, her amber eyes tracking the others with the slow, predatory patience of a predator who knows her prey is already trapped. Hilda’s leather corset creaks as she strips off her iron-studded bracers, her breaths ragged, muscles coiled like springs beneath sweat-slicked skin. Seren smooths her dark silks, the fabric whispering against her skin as she adjusts her posture, a queen surveying her court.  

Gaia’s nose wrinkles as she sniffs the mortar, her fingers tracing the faint etchings of suppression runes. Freya trembles in the corner, her body a shiver of unspoken terror. Elara’s mind calculates survival odds—numbers and probabilities, a shield against the raw, pulsing heat of the room. The scent of burnt sage lingers, a failed attempt at purification. Somewhere beyond the stone, the clink of iron batons echoes, a promise of what comes next. Elara’s pulse thrums, a steady, desperate rhythm against her ribs. The cell is a tomb, and they are its unquiet occupants.

✦ ✦ ✦

The door groans, a sound like a dying animal, and the air thickens with the tang of iron. Vane’s boots echo, deliberate, each step a blade against the stone. His scent—burnt sage, rust, something sharp and sour—clings to the damp air. The Wardens’ rods hum, low and venomous, their blackened metal glowing faintly. Hilda’s breath hitches as Vane circles her, his gloved hand trailing over the ridges of her corset, a predator mapping prey.  

He leans in, his breath hot against her neck. The baton’s tip presses into the hollow beneath her ribs, and Hilda’s gasp is a fractured note. Static crackles, searing her skin. Her body arches, muscles tensing, and the air shivers. A spark jumps from the rod, licking her flesh, and her magic erupts—scorching, uncontrolled. The floor groans, cracks spidering outward, and the Wardens stagger, their balance lost to the tremor.  

Elara feels the heat radiate, a fever against her own skin. The stone is cold, but Hilda’s magic burns through it, searing the air with ozone. Vane’s laughter is a blade, sharp and mocking, as he steps back, watching Hilda’s sweat-slicked skin glisten. The rod’s hum grows louder, a warning, but Hilda’s eyes are black, her breath ragged.  

The cell is a cage of sweat and fear, the humidity clinging to their skin like a second layer. Elara’s pulse matches the thrum of the rod, a steady, desperate rhythm. Vane’s voice is a serpent, sliding over the tension. “You think strength saves you?” His boot nudges the crack in the floor, and the stone splinters. Hilda’s magic flares again, a violent burst that sends a shard of granite slicing through the air. It grazes Elara’s cheek, sharp and cold, and the taste of blood fills her mouth.  

The room holds its breath, the air thick with the scent of ozone and iron. Vane’s smile is a wound, deep and deliberate. The Wardens raise their rods, but Hilda’s laughter is a low, dangerous sound, a promise of what comes next.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s palms bite into the granite, the roughness a sharp contrast to the sweat pooling in her palms. The Wardens’ boots echo as they slam Hilda down, her knees cracking against the stone. The iron collar clamps around her throat, cold and unyielding, and the discharge ripples through the cell. Elara feels the shockwave in her bones, the flagstones buckling under the force. She scrambles, knees scraping raw, fingers closing around the alchemical vials. The scent of burnt sage mixes with the damp stone, thick in the air. She kicks the vials beneath the floor slab, their glass clinking against the stone. Vane looms over Hilda, his voice a whisper of ice. “You are not students…” His words coil around her, a venomous thread. The door slams, a thunderous crack, and darkness swallows the cell. Hilda’s breaths are shallow, her body trembling. Elara’s pulse thrums in her ears, the silence oppressive. The cold seeps into her, the stone’s dampness clinging to her skin. She can’t see Hilda, but she hears the ragged gasps, the shivers. The darkness is a living thing, pressing in, suffocating. Elara’s fingers find the edge of a vial, its glass cool against her skin. The cell is a tomb, and she’s trapped in its breath. The heat of Hilda’s body radiates nearby, a flicker of life in the suffocating dark. Elara’s cheek stings, the blood mixing with the sweat on her skin. The air is thick with the metallic tang of iron, the stench of ozone lingering. She presses closer to the wall, the granite cool against her back. Every breath is a struggle, the darkness thick as tar. Hilda’s shivers echo, a rhythm of fear. Elara’s fingers trace the vial’s surface, its contents unknown, a fragile hope. The silence is a prison, and she is its prisoner. The damp stone seeps into her, the cold a constant companion. She hears the scrape of Hilda’s nails against the floor, a desperate sound. The air is heavy, charged, as if the cell itself holds its breath. Elara’s heart pounds, a frantic drum in the stillness. The darkness is not empty—it is full, alive, waiting.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s breath fogs in the cold, mingling with the acrid sting of burnt flesh. Hilda’s collar glows like a wound, its crimson light seeping into the stone, staining the air with ozone and the cloying sweetness of sweat. Gaia’s hands press into Hilda’s shoulders, palms slick with river moss that clings to the burns like a second skin. The moss is damp, alive, its musk mingling with the iron of blood. Hilda’s spine arches, her breath a wet rasp against Gaia’s thumb, which traces the jagged edge of a scar. Elara’s own skin prickles, as if the cell’s humidity has pooled beneath her ribs.  

Morrigan’s dress swallows the dark, red as a bruise, its fabric whispering against stone as she leans close. Her fingers brush Hilda’s shoulder, a slow, deliberate stroke that makes Hilda shudder. “Breathe,” she murmurs, her voice a low smoke that curls around Elara’s throat. The words are a command, a caress—both. Freya’s silhouette trembles in the corner, her hands clenched into fists, nails biting into her palms. The silence here is a thing of its own, thick and wet, pressing against Elara’s ears like a hand.  

Hilda’s body convulses, muscles twitching beneath Gaia’s grip. The moss clings to her skin, its green tendrils pulsing faintly, as if drawing strength from the pain. Elara’s own pulse thrums in her throat, syncopated with the wet, rhythmic sound of Hilda’s breathing. The air reeks of salt and decay, of something raw and unspoken. Morrigan’s hand lingers on Hilda’s neck, her thumb brushing the collar’s edge, and for a moment, Elara imagines the heat of that touch—how it would feel to be held, to be *seen* in the dark. But the thought dissolves, swallowed by the weight of the stone, the sweat, the unyielding silence.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingertips trace the fissure’s jagged edge, cold granite biting into her skin. The air thickens with the musk of sweat and something metallic—blood, or iron from the crack itself. Seren’s voice is a knife, slicing through the silence: *“You’d trade us for a payday?”* Hilda’s breath hitches, a wet sound, and Elara smells the salt of her tears, the acrid tang of fear. Gaia’s shadow looms, her yew staff a blackened root pressed against Seren’s throat. The wood reeks of damp earth and old rituals.  

Morrigan’s laugh is a serpent’s whisper, curling around them. She steps between the two women, her hand skimming Gaia’s jaw, then Seren’s, as if testing their tempers. *“Vane’s not here,”* she murmurs, her thumb grazing Seren’s pulse. Elara feels the heat of it—a phantom touch, a memory of her own skin.  

The crack in the floor pulses under Elara’s fingers. Something shifts there, a sliver of metal or bone. She digs deeper, nails catching on stone, and the scent of rust rises. Hilda’s fall left a wound in the earth, and now Elara’s blood mixes with the floor’s ancient grime.  

Seren’s breath is a hot blade against Elara’s ear. *“You’d let her die for a *message*?”* The words are a challenge, a taunt. Elara’s pulse thrums, syncopated with the stone’s slow, wet breath. Gaia’s staff trembles, its weight a promise. Morrigan’s gaze lingers on Elara’s hands, the way they clutch the crack’s edge.  

A sound—damp, visceral—rises from the depths. Elara’s fingers close around a fragment, sharp and cold. The others’ voices blur, a swarm of anger and desperation. She presses the object to her chest, its surface etched with symbols that burn like a second skin. The stone’s silence swallows the fight, leaving only the wet scrape of her breath, the weight of the dark, and the thing she’s held too long.  

Somewhere, a door creaks. The air shifts. Elara doesn’t let go.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingers tremble as she pries the tin box free, its rust screeching like a trapped bird. The air reeks of iron and old tobacco, sharp enough to sting her nostrils. Inside, the parchment crackles beneath her touch—brittle, yellowed, its edges frayed by time. The names are there, etched in crimson that clots like dried blood. Her pulse throbs in her throat as she traces the slashes, each one a wound. Lyra’s sketchbook emits a faint red glow, its pages fanned open to a drawing that makes Elara’s breath hitch: seven figures, their limbs twisted into chains of glowing runes, being dragged into a maw of blackened stone. The furnace’s heat radiates from the page, searing her vision.  

Morrigan’s cedar splinter snaps between her teeth, the scent of resin mingling with the metallic tang of her own blood. Her eyes, pale as frost, fix on Elara. “They’re not sacrifices,” she says, voice low, “they’re fuel.” The words hang heavy, dissolving into the damp air. Elara feels the weight of the fragment against her chest, its symbols burning hotter now, as if recognizing the truth.  

Lyra’s hand brushes Elara’s, cold and steady. The sketchbook’s charcoal lines blur into something alive, the chains writhing, the furnace breathing. Elara’s skin prickles, sweat clinging to her ribs. Morrigan’s fingers close around Elara’s wrist, her grip firm, deliberate. “We tear this place apart,” she whispers. The pact is sealed in the silence that follows—a shudder in the stone, a promise etched in blood and ash.  

Elara’s breath fogs in the chill, mingling with the stink of burnt sage and iron. The dark presses in, but the box feels warmer now, its contents a weight she carries like a second heart. Somewhere below, the furnace groans, and Elara wonders if the screams are already inside her.

# Chapter 2: The Siphoned Bitter

*By M.L.L*

---

The cold-iron collar seared Hilda’s neck, its punitive burns weeping blackened pus onto the cot’s frayed linen. Elara traced the scarred flesh with her gaze, fingers curling into fists as she calculated the solvent’s ratio—silver belladonna’s venomous tang mixed with distilled vitriol’s acrid sting. The apothecary’s locked vault loomed in her mind, its iron doors sealed tighter than a witch’s cunt.  

Seren’s silk sleeves brushed Elara’s collarbone, the fabric whispering against sweat-damp skin. A glamour shimmered between them, threads of woven light clinging to their pulse points, muffling scent and sound. Seren’s breath—spiced almond and something sharper—fanned Elara’s racing heartbeat as her fingers lingered on the pulse beneath her jaw. “Stay low,” she murmured, voice a blade honed to silence.  

Morrigan leaned against the cell’s rusted bars, cedar splinter between her lips, smokeless and hungry. She flicked a bronze lock-pick into Elara’s palm, its edge glinting like a stolen star. “No more than three minutes,” she said, eyes on the corridor’s damp stone.  

Elara pressed the pin to the latch, feeling the cold iron vibrate beneath her thumb. Seren’s glamour thickened, wrapping them in a cocoon of muted shadows. The air reeked of damp moss and old blood as they slipped into the tunnels, Gaia’s breath warm against Elara’s back. Morrigan’s shadow trailed them, a blade sheathed in silence.  

The apothecary’s door groaned open, releasing the stench of tinctures and rot. Elara’s fingers trembled as she unearthed the vials, silver belladonna’s bitter perfume sharp against her tongue. The plan was a tightrope walk—each step a gamble between salvation and explosion.  

Hilda’s fevered whimpers echoed in their minds, a drumbeat urging them forward.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s palms pressed into the muck, slick and rank, as she wriggled forward. The drainage conduit reeked of iron and rot, her trousers clinging to thighs smeared with grease. Gaia’s breath hitched behind her, sharp as a blade dragged across stone. Seren’s hand gripped Elara’s ankle, anchoring her as the tunnel narrowed, forcing them into a huddle.  

The apothecary’s air was a shock—cold, dry, laced with the sour bite of aconite. Elara’s fingers fumbled for the bronze pin, its edge corroded by years of use. She tilted the vial of acid, letting a single drop hiss onto the lock. Smoke curled upward, acrid and stinging her eyes. The mechanism groaned, then gave with a soft *click*.  

Inside, amber carboys gleamed like trapped suns. Elara’s pulse thrummed as she scanned the rows—*there*, a jar labeled in cracked script. Her fingers closed around it, glass slick with sweat. A pipette hovered, drawing the viscous liquid into a vial. The scent of silver belladonna bloomed, sweet and poisonous, coating her tongue.  

A metallic *clang* reverberated through the vaulted corridor. Elara froze. The sound was deliberate, methodical—a sentry’s tread. Her breath quickened as the red optical sensor swept across the frosted doors, its light bleeding through the glass.  

Seren’s whisper was a rasp against her ear: “They’re here.”  

Elara’s hand trembled, vial poised. The automaton’s gears ground louder, its gaze locking onto the apothecary’s entrance. She pressed herself into the shadow of a shelf, heart drumming a frantic rhythm. The sentry’s sensor flickered, scanning, searching.  

A drop of belladonna slipped from her fingers, splattering on the stone. The liquid hissed, evaporating in a wisp of vapor. Elara’s throat tightened. The automaton’s head swiveled—toward the sound.  

She held her breath, body coiled like a spring, as the red light lingered—then shifted, sweeping onward. The silence that followed was a blade at her neck.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s pulse roared in her ears as the automaton’s brass joints screeched, steam hissing from its pistons. The rune-scanners flared crimson, locking onto Gaia’s yew staff—a dark spiral of geomantic energy. Gaia stepped forward, muscles taut beneath her robes, and drove the staff into the automaton’s iron armature. A thunderclap of kinetic force exploded through the apothecary. Elara heard the wet pop of Gaia’s shoulder dislocating, felt the shockwave vibrate her bones.  

Seren’s illusion splintered mid-air, glass-like sparkles stinging Elara’s cheek as a stray voltage arc crackled. The automaton’s three-pronged clamps surged forward, sparks flying where they met Gaia’s staff. Elara lunged, fingers closing around the alchemical flash-vial in her belt. The vial’s mercury core gleamed under dim light as she hurled it—straight into the machine’s optical visor.  

Blinding white detonation. Heat seared Elara’s eyelids; the explosion’s concussion sent her stumbling back against the stone wall. Iron alarm bells roared, deep and guttural, as portcullises slammed down elsewhere in the vaults. The automaton reeled, its sensors darkened, but the blast had not blinded it entirely—its remaining eye flickered, scanning, calculating.  

Elara’s breath came in ragged bursts, sweat slicking her skin beneath the acrid scent of ozone and splintered glass. Gaia collapsed to one knee, her other arm twisted awkwardly, blood seeping through her sleeve. The automaton advanced, steam hissing from its damaged joints, clamps twitching with residual voltage. Elara’s hand trembled as she reached for another vial—her fingers brushed the cold glass, but the world had already tilted.  

The automaton’s fist swung, a blur of iron and fury. Elara threw herself between it and Gaia, feeling the rush of air as the blow missed by a breath. Her ribs screamed as she hit the floor, the impact driving every remaining breath from her lungs. The machine loomed over them, its presence a suffocating weight, and Elara knew—there was no escape. Only this: the burn of alchemical fumes in her throat, the taste of copper on her tongue, and the raw, unyielding truth of what she would do next.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s spine cracks against rusted iron as they plummet, steam hissing in jagged geysers around her. The chute’s walls scald her soaked skin, sweat mixing with greasy soot to form a slick film that clings like a second layer. Gaia lands first, bones jarring against stone; Elara slams down atop her, ribs screaming as the weight crushes air from her lungs. A pipe groans overhead, spewing scalding mist thatbillowed into her mouth, bitter and metallic.  

Freya’s scream fractures the tunnel—too close, too sharp. Elara turns, coughing, to see her flail at the far wall, fingers blackening with rogue embers. Steam floods the passage, blinding, and Morrigan emerges from the haze, her red dress clinging like blood. She drops to her knees, hauling Freya into her chest, palms pressing against Freya’s temples as steam drenches them both. “Breathe,” Morrigan murmurs, voice a blade of heat against Freya’s ear. Her fingers dig into Freya’s scalp, nails biting through flesh.  

Elara wrenches her focus back to Gaia, tearing linen strips from her tattered sleeve. The fabric sticks to Gaia’s skin, soaked and useless, but she presses on, teeth gritted as she knots the cloth around Gaia’s dislocated shoulder. Gaia’s breath hitches, a low, animal sound, and Elara feels the tremor in her own hands—same trembling, same raw nerve endings. The tunnel shudders; a distant siren wails, piercing through the steam.  

Morrigan’s grip tightens on Freya, dragging her into a crouch. Her lips brush Freya’s jaw, whispering something vicious, possessive. Freya’s nails dig into Morrigan’s arms, embers flaring between their skin. Elara watches, throat dry, as the steam curls around them all—thick, suffocating, alive. Gaia’s shoulder pulses beneath her hands, and Elara leans closer, breath fogging in the heat, until the world narrows to this: the burn of metal, the scent of scorched flesh, and the unspoken promise that none of them will survive this without breaking.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingers tremble as they pry the satchel open, the leather reeking of sweat and iron. The belladonna’s metallic tang clashes with the damp stone air, sharp enough to sting her nostrils. A jagged crack splits one carboy’s glass, its contents seeping into the canvas like blood through linen. Three ounces. Barely a splash. Her pulse thrums in her ears, a hollow drumbeat as she counts the remaining vials—six empty, one half-full. The weight of it presses against her ribs, cold and unyielding.  

Seren’s voice slices through the steam, venom laced with gritted teeth. “You’d let us all rot for your stupid pride?” Gaia’s jaw is clenched, her breaths ragged, but she doesn’t flinch. The scar across her cheek glows faintly, a smoldering brand. “We’re not leaving her,” she snaps, fingers curling into fists.  

Morrigan steps into the lantern’s amber glow, her palm split open, blood seeping into the torn hem of her skirt. She slams the wax-sealed parchment onto the stone, the seal bursting like a bruise. “This is all we’ve got,” she hisses, eyes blazing. The schedule’s ink smears under her touch, a map of traps and patrols. Elara’s gaze flicks to the paper, then back to the satchel. Three ounces. Seven collars.  

Freya’s hand brushes Elara’s wrist, warm and steady. “We choose,” she murmurs, voice low as a threat. The steam thickens, clinging to their skin, heavy with the scent of burnt herbs and sweat. Elara’s throat tightens. Hilda’s face flashes in her mind—her laugh, the way she’d pressed a vial into Elara’s palm before the chute fell.  

Seren’s breath hitches, a sound like a blade scraping stone. “We’re not gods,” she rasps. “We can’t save them all.”  

The lantern flickers, casting shadows that twist like serpents around their trembling forms. Elara’s fingers close over the satchel’s strap, the leather slick with her own sweat. The choice is a knife between her ribs, sharp and unrelenting.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s thumb rubs the phial’s rim, its glass slick with sweat and the sour tang of belladonna. Morrigan’s voice cuts through the steam—cold, precise—as she unspools the plan. The coal-tar naphtha reeks of burnt sugar and iron, pooling in rusted drums beneath the boilers. Elara’s fingers tremble when she grips the satchel, its straps fraying under the weight of the vial now fused with volatile fumes. Lyra’s sketchbook lies open on the radiator, charcoal smudged into a map of pulsing veins—fuel lines threading through the ward’s core like arteries.  

Seren’s breath rasps against Elara’s shoulder, hot and urgent. “It’ll tear the conduit apart,” she whispers, nails digging into Elara’s arm. The heat from the radiator seeps into Elara’s skin, mingling with the metallic stink of naphtha. Morrigan’s gaze locks onto hers—unblinking, hungry—and Elara feels the weight of six lives hanging on her next move.  

She uncaps the phial, letting the fumes curl upward, sharp and acrid. The air thickens, each breath a struggle. Lyra presses the sketchbook into her hands, charcoal dust smearing her palms. “Here,” she murmurs, tracing the lines with a finger. Elara’s pulse thrums in her ears, drowning out Seren’s quiet prayers.  

The decision is a fuse lit beneath her ribs. She nods, throat raw. Morrigan steps back, lips curving into something cruel and triumphant. The group converges—sweat-slicked, fever-bright—their bodies brushing as they huddle close. Elara feels the heat of their defiance, a slow burn spreading through her bones. They are no longer prey.  

The lantern’s flicker dies, plunging them into darkness laced with the sour scent of betrayal and resolve. Elara tucks the phial into her coat, its cold metal pressing against her skin like a promise. The ward’s heart beats somewhere below, and they will rip it out.

# Chapter 3: The Ghost Roster

*By M.L.L*

---

Elara’s breath fogged in the cold air as Vane’s baton struck Hilda’s collar, sending a metallic clang through the cell. The scent of iron and sweat clung to the stone walls, mingling with the acrid tang of burned herbs from last night’s purge. Her boots pressed into the damp floor, the hollowed heel of one throbbing with the weight of three ounces of silver belladonna. Vane’s shadow loomed over her, his breath hot against her neck as he traced a gloved finger along her jawline. “You’re trembling,” he murmured, voice like rusted gears. Her pulse thrummed in her ears, sharp and desperate.  

The inspection dragged on, each second a blade against her skin. Vane’s baton tapped her ribs, testing the tightness of her bindings, his gloved hand lingering too long on the bruise blooming beneath her collarbone. The cell reeked of filth and fear, but Elara focused on the cold bite of the stone floor, the rough texture of her own skin, anything to anchor herself. When the heavy doors slammed shut, she exhaled, the sound lost in the echo of Vane’s retreating footsteps.  

Morrigan’s voice cut through the silence, low and urgent. “The Exam’s tomorrow midnight.” Her fingers curled around Elara’s wrist, nails digging into flesh. “We need the Headmaster’s cipher key. The High Archive—above the Scriptorium.” Elara’s stomach twisted, the belladonna pressing against her heel like a secret thing. The plan was madness, but so was staying here, breathing this air, letting Vane’s touch linger. She nodded, the movement sharp, and felt Morrigan’s grip tighten, a silent pact forged in sweat and steel.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s boots scuffed stone as she slipped past Seren’s gilded distraction—silk rustling, lips curving like a blade. The archivist’s gaze lingered on Seren’s collarbone, breath hitching at the scent of her lavender-essence perfume. Elara tugged Lyra into the shadowed alcove, where cedar shelves exhaled centuries. The acid wire hissed as she pressed it against the iron cage, molten streaks etching through rust. Locks clattered to the floor.  

Lyra’s fingers brushed the ledger’s cracked leather cover. A shudder rippled her spine; blood bloomed from her nostrils, dripping onto the vellum. Elara grasped her shoulders, nails sinking into damp fabric as Lyra’s pupils dilated, black irises swallowing her brown. Whispers coiled around them—screams muffled by time, voices fraying at the edges. Lyra’s breath quickened, hitching like a trapped animal.  

Elara’s pulse thrummed in her ears, syncopated with the ledger’s heartbeat. The paper trembled under Lyra’s touch, ink bleeding into her skin. A name surfaced—*Class 441*—then another, and another, each syllable a knife. Lyra’s knees buckled; Elara caught her, feeling the feverish heat of her veins. The whispers crescendoed, clawing at Elara’s temples. She leaned closer, breath mingling with Lyra’s sweat, and saw the ledger’s secrets etched in blood: executions, disappearances, the Headmaster’s sigil stamped on each page.  

Lyra’s fingers curled into the parchment, tearing a corner. The room darkened; dust motes swirled like trapped souls. Elara’s throat burned with the taste of iron. Somewhere, Seren’s laughter echoed, smooth and sharp as glass. Lyra gasped, spitting blood onto the floor. Her eyes snapped open—empty, then furious. “They’re still here,” she rasped. “In the walls.”  

Elara yanked the ledger free, its weight a leaden truth in her hands. The air thickened, cloying with the stench of old paper and something darker. Lyra’s hand found hers, trembling, as the whispers swelled—a tide of voices, hungry and endless.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingers trembled as she turned the page, ink bleeding into the parchment like congealed blood. The ledger’s truth coiled in her gut—each name a thread in a web of sacrifice. Her breath hitched; the air reeked of rust and decay, thick as rotting flesh. Lyra’s hand clamped hers, nails digging into bone, as the whispers crescendoed into a shriek. A shadow rippled across the Scriptorium’s vaulted ceiling.  

Boots thudded. Iron-scented hounds lunged through the arched doorway, their muzzles snarling, tongues lapping at the stale air. Lyra gasped—her necromantic aura spilled, a slick black stain on the stone floor. The hounds’ howls fractured, jagged and feral. Seren’s glamour splintered; her silver hair dissolved into ash, revealing the hollows beneath her skin.  

Elara ripped the cipher diagram free, paper tearing with a wet snap. Lyra’s blood smeared the ink, smearing the sigils into something grotesque. “Run,” Elara hissed, yanking Lyra’s arm. The shelves groaned as iron arrows embedded themselves in cedar, splinters biting her cheek. Lyra stumbled, her breath ragged, sweat mingling with the stench of fear.  

Elara’s pulse roared in her ears, a drumbeat matching the hounds’ frenzy. Stone scraped beneath their boots, the Scriptorium’s labyrinthine corridors closing in. A arrow whizzed past, grazing Lyra’s shoulder; blood bloomed crimson against pale skin. Elara’s grip tightened, nails biting into flesh.
