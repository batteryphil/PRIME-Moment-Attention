# Chapter 3: The Ghost Roster

*By M.L.L*

---

Elara’s breath fogged in the cold air as Vane’s baton struck Hilda’s collar, sending a metallic clang through the cell. The scent of iron and sweat clung to the stone walls, mingling with the acrid tang of burned herbs from last night’s purge. Her boots pressed into the damp floor, the hollowed heel of one throbbing with the weight of three ounces of silver belladonna. Vane’s shadow loomed over her, his breath hot against her neck as he traced a gloved finger along her jawline. “You’re trembling,” he murmured, voice like rusted gears. Her pulse thrummed in her ears, sharp and desperate.  

The inspection dragged on, each second a blade against her skin. Vane’s baton tapped her ribs, testing the tightness of her bindings, his gloved hand lingering too long on the bruise blooming beneath her collarbone. The cell reeked of filth and fear, but Elara focused on the cold bite of the stone floor, the rough texture of her own skin, anything to anchor herself. When the heavy doors slammed shut, she exhaled, the sound lost in the echo of Vane’s retreating footsteps.  

Morrigan’s voice cut through the silence, low and urgent. “The Exam’s tomorrow midnight.” Her fingers curled around Elara’s wrist, nails digging into flesh. “We need the Headmaster’s cipher key. The High Archive—above the Scriptorium.” Elara’s stomach twisted, the belladonna pressing against her heel like a secret thing. The plan was madness, but so was staying here, breathing this air, letting Vane’s touch linger. She nodded, the movement sharp, and felt Morrigan’s grip tighten, a silent pact forged in sweat and steel.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s boots scuffed stone as she slipped past Seren’s gilded distraction—silk rustling, lips curving like a blade. The archivist’s gaze lingered on Seren’s collarbone, breath hitching at the scent of her lavender-essence perfume. Elara tugged Lyra into the shadowed alcove, where cedar shelves exhaled centuries. The acid wire hissed as she pressed it against the iron cage, molten streaks etching through rust. Locks clattered to the floor.  

Lyra’s fingers brushed the ledger’s cracked leather cover. A shudder rippled her spine; blood bloomed from her nostrils, dripping onto the vellum. Elara grasped her shoulders, nails sinking into damp fabric as Lyra’s pupils dilated, black irises swallowing her brown. Whispers coiled around them—screams muffled by time, voices fraying at the edges. Lyra’s breath quickened, hitching like a trapped animal.  

Elara’s pulse thrummed in her ears, syncopated with the ledger’s heartbeat. The paper trembled under Lyra’s touch, ink bleeding into her skin. A name surfaced—*Class 441*—then another, and another, each syllable a knife. Lyra’s knees buckled; Elara caught her, feeling the feverish heat of her veins. The whispers crescendoed, clawing at Elara’s temples. She leaned closer, breath mingling with Lyra’s sweat, and saw the ledger’s secrets etched in blood: executions, disappearances, the Headmaster’s sigil stamped on each page.  

Lyra’s fingers curled into the parchment, tearing a corner. The room darkened; dust motes swirled like trapped souls. Elara’s throat burned with the taste of iron. Somewhere, Seren’s laughter echoed, smooth and sharp as glass. Lyra gasped, spitting blood onto the floor. Her eyes snapped open—empty, then furious. “They’re still here,” she rasped. “In the walls.”  

Elara yanked the ledger free, its weight a leaden truth in her hands. The air thickened, cloying with the stench of old paper and something darker. Lyra’s hand found hers, trembling, as the whispers swelled—a tide of voices, hungry and endless.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingers trembled as she turned the page, ink bleeding into the parchment like congealed blood. The ledger’s truth coiled in her gut—each name a thread in a web of sacrifice. Her breath hitched; the air reeked of rust and decay, thick as rotting flesh. Lyra’s hand clamped hers, nails digging into bone, as the whispers crescendoed into a shriek. A shadow rippled across the Scriptorium’s vaulted ceiling.  

Boots thudded. Iron-scented hounds lunged through the arched doorway, their muzzles snarling, tongues lapping at the stale air. Lyra gasped—her necromantic aura spilled, a slick black stain on the stone floor. The hounds’ howls fractured, jagged and feral. Seren’s glamour splintered; her silver hair dissolved into ash, revealing the hollows beneath her skin.  

Elara ripped the cipher diagram free, paper tearing with a wet snap. Lyra’s blood smeared the ink, smearing the sigils into something grotesque. “Run,” Elara hissed, yanking Lyra’s arm. The shelves groaned as iron arrows embedded themselves in cedar, splinters biting her cheek. Lyra stumbled, her breath ragged, sweat mingling with the stench of fear.  

Elara’s pulse roared in her ears, a drumbeat matching the hounds’ frenzy. Stone scraped beneath their boots, the Scriptorium’s labyrinthine corridors closing in. A arrow whizzed past, grazing Lyra’s shoulder; blood bloomed crimson against pale skin. Elara’s grip tightened, nails biting into flesh. The air reeked of iron and ozone, the scent of impending violence.  

Lyra’s breath hitched, her body trembling like a leaf in a storm. “They’ll burn us,” she rasped, voice raw. Elara didn’t answer. The ledger’s weight burned against her ribs, its secrets a brand. Somewhere ahead, a door creaked open—exit or trap? The hounds’ snarls echoed, close now. Elara lunged, dragging Lyra into the darkness, the cipher clutched like a weapon.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s palms skidded on rain-slick stone as she hauled Lyra into the cell. The door groaned shut behind them, sealing in the damp rot of old mortar. Lyra collapsed onto the cot, her ribs heaving like a trapped bird’s. Elara knelt, fingers digging into Lyra’s shoulders, feeling the tremors ripple through her spine. “Stop,” she hissed, but Lyra’s voice was already fracturing—whispers of names, of bodies in the vaults, of hands that never let go.  

Freya’s breath ghosted Elara’s neck as she wrapped around Lyra from behind, bare skin pressing to shivering flesh. The warmth of Freya’s chest against Lyra’s back, the scent of her sweat mingling with rain. Elara felt the tension in Freya’s jaw, the way her fingers curled into Lyra’s shoulders as if anchoring herself.  

Hilda stood at the cell’s edge, nails raking raw lines across her own neck. The burns there pulsed like coals, a brand no different from the iron collars they’d all worn since birth. She spat, the sound sharp enough to cut stone. “They think we’re livestock,” she growled, voice hoarse. “But we’re not dead yet.”  

Elara’s own ribs screamed as she straightened, the ledger clutched to her chest like a shield. The cell reeked of mildew and fear, but here—here was something else. Lyra’s breath hitched, then stilled against Freya’s pulse. Hilda’s fingers curled into fists, knuckles white. Elara leaned close, her own skin damp with rain and sweat, and pressed her cheek to Lyra’s trembling shoulder. The girl’s heartbeat thudded against her collarbone, erratic, desperate.  

Somewhere above, a bell tolled. The lock clicked. Darkness pooled in the corners, but Elara didn’t move. Not yet. The ledger’s weight was nothing compared to the fire in her veins, the raw, unspoken truth of them all—alive, breathing, *unbroken*.

✦ ✦ ✦

Seren dropped to the stone floor, shaking rain from her dark hair. Water slicked her skin, pooling at her hips as she hunched, breath ragged. The cell groaned under her weight. “He’s isolating us,” she hissed, voice raw. “Salt-cells. Tomorrow morning.” Her fingers dug into the flagstones, knuckles bleeding.  

Gaia surged forward, boots slapping wet stone. “You *heard*?” Her fist struck Seren’s shoulder, a brutal knock. “Your little games—”  

“Don’t,” Elara cut in, stepping between them. Her palm pressed against Gaia’s chest, feeling the erratic thud of her heart through coarse wool. The air reeked of damp linen and iron. Lyra’s breath hitched somewhere behind them, a tremor in the dark.  

Seren laughed, sharp and wet. “He’ll offer the Pardon to the first one who talks.” Her gaze flicked to Hilda, who’d curled into herself, fists clenched so tight her nails left crescents in her palms. “To the *traitor*.”  

The word hung, thick as rot. Elara’s pulse roared in her ears. She smelled fear—sour sweat, burnt copper—and something older, darker: the tang of salt from the cells, the rusted iron of chains. Lyra’s shoulder pressed against hers, trembling.  

“Who?” Hilda whispered. Her voice cracked. “Who’s the *traitor*?”  

No one answered. The silence was a living thing, slithering through the cracks in the walls. Elara’s fingers curled into her palms, nails biting flesh. She tasted blood, metallic and raw. Somewhere above, the bell tolled again, slow and deliberate.  

Seren’s breath hitched. “I didn’t—”  

“Liar,” Gaia snarled.  

Elara stepped back, crowding into Seren’s space. The heat of her body, the sharp scent of rain and smoke. “We’re *alive*,” she growled, voice low, dangerous. “And we’re not breaking.”  

The cell shuddered with their breaths—rough, ragged, alive.

✦ ✦ ✦

Morrigan’s blade bit into her palm before Elara could react. The silver gleamed like a serpent’s fang, slicing through skin with a wet *snap*. Blood bloomed dark, dripping onto the tin box’s rusted lid. Morrigan didn’t flinch. Her eyes—black as spilled ink—locked onto Seren’s trembling form. “Nobody buys Vane’s lie,” she hissed. The words curled like smoke, sharp and lethal.  

Elara’s pulse roared in her ears as Morrigan pressed their palms together. The wound seeped into Elara’s skin, hot and metallic. A shudder rippled through her—pain, then something else. The others followed: Hilda’s calloused hand, Gaia’s scarred knuckles, Seren’s pale fingers, Freya’s jagged nails, Lyra’s trembling palm. Their blood mingled, thick and coppery, pooling in the box’s grooves.  

A hum vibrated in Elara’s bones. Seven magics tangled—raw, electric, *alive*. The air thickened, heavy with iron and desperation. She tasted it on her tongue, felt it seep into her marrow. Fear still clawed at her ribs, but now it was anchored, sharpened. Morrigan’s voice cut through the haze. “We burn together or we rise together.”  

The box groaned under their grip. Elara’s fingers curled around its edges, nails biting into cold metal. The scent of blood and sweat hung thick, mingling with the tang of iron. Somewhere above, the bell tolled again—slower now, almost reverent.  

She looked up, meeting Morrigan’s gaze. The cipher was there, etched in their shared blood: *Tomorrow night*. The Discipline Exam would be a blade turned inward, a reckoning. Elara’s breath steadied. The pact hummed in her veins, a second heartbeat.  

The cell held its breath. Their palms remained fused, a mosaic of wounds and resolve. The dark pooled between them, a promise and a threat. Elara didn’t flinch when Morrigan’s blood dripped onto her wrist. It was warm, like liquid fire.
