# Chapter 4: The Crack in the Circle

*By M.L.L*

---

Elara’s wrists crack as the chains bite into bone, cold iron seeping through her sleeves. The salt-circle vault reeks of brine and burnt sage, the air thick with the metallic tang of blood she can’t see. Vane’s boots echo against stone, his shadow swallowing the room. He slides a parchment across the table—gold wax glints like dried marrow. “Restoration,” he says, voice smooth as a blade dragged over flint. “Immunity. Give me Morrigan’s name, and you walk free.”  

Her pulse thrums in her throat, steady. Vane’s baton rests at his side, its iron surface etched with runes that hum faintly, pulsing like a second heartbeat. She watches the light dance across them, noting how they shift when he moves. His eyes are too still, pupils dilated as if he’s already inside her head.  

“You’d trade me for a whisper?” she murmurs, fingers flexing against the chains. The iron tastes of rust and desperation.  

Vane leans forward, breath warm with cloves and ash. “You’re out of options, Elara.” His hand hovers over the parchment, ink still wet. “The belladonna’s gone. You know what happens when the Academy’s secrets bleed.”  

She smells his lie—sweat beneath the perfume, a faint sulfur note. The vault’s chill seeps into her bones, but her skin prickles with something else: the baton’s resonance, a low vibration she feels in her molars. Vane’s gaze flicks to her wrists, then back up. A twitch in his jaw.  

“Morrigan?” Elara tilts her head, lips curving. “She’s already dead.”  

The runes on his baton flare, sharp and sudden, as if the iron itself recoils. Vane’s breath hitches. The salt-circle hums, alive with something older than either of them.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s pulse thrums in her throat as Vane’s baton hums against her jaw, a live wire grazing her skin. She lets her head loll, feigning docility, but the iron tang of her own blood floods her mouth—she’d bitten through her tongue, sharp and deliberate. “The belladonna,” she whispers, voice trembling, “it was ruined. The drainage… it seeped into the roots.” Her fingers curl into the stone floor, nails splitting against rough grit.  

Vane’s breath is a hot blade at her ear, tobacco and ash. He presses the baton lower, tracing her collarbone with a flicker of static that makes her muscles convulse. “Lies,” he murmurs, but his grip on her wrist tightens, testing. She doesn’t flinch. The salt-circle’s hum grows louder, vibrating in her bones.  

A guard yanks her up, hauling her through the corridor. Her knees scrape stone, and she lets out a soft whimper, letting the fear pool in her voice. Cell 441’s iron door slams behind her. The air reeks of mildew and old sweat. Then—movement. A flicker of fabric.  

Seren is gone.  

Elara’s breath hitches. Footsteps echo down the hall, muffled by thick walls. Gaia’s pacing is a relentless drumbeat, boots striking stone like a heartbeat. “That coward,” she hisses, voice raw. “She’ll tell him everything. The wards, the glyphs—” Her fist slams against the cell wall, sending a shiver through Elara’s spine.  

Elara sinks to her knees, pressing her forehead to the cold floor. The scent of Seren’s lingering perfume—a faint sulfur note—clings to the air. She imagines Vane’s baton plunging into flesh, the sickening *snap* of bone. Her own hands tremble, not from fear, but the weight of what she’ll do when the next interrogation begins.  

Somewhere, a door creaks open.

✦ ✦ ✦

The iron door groans, its rusted hinges weeping as Seren’s boots scrape against the stone. Hilda’s breath is a snarl, her fingers curling around Seren’s throat before she can breathe. The cell reeks of sweat and damp wool as Seren’s back slams into the wall, her silk bodice tearing. Blood blooms pink on her collarbone, mingling with the sulfur of her perfume.  

Hilda’s knuckles bruise Seren’s skin, muscles rippling under taut flesh. “You sold us out!” she hisses, jaw clenched. Seren chokes, fingers scrabbling at Hilda’s wrist, tears smudging her makeup into smears of ash. Her other hand darts to her sleeve—slow, deliberate—and pulls free a dagger. Silver, curved, dripping black oil that glints like tar under the cell’s flickering light.  

Elara’s pulse thrums in her ears as Seren raises the blade, its weight trembling in her palm. The oil smells of rot and iron, a sickly sweetness that clings to the air. “He told me to poison Morrigan’s water,” Seren rasps, voice raw. Her nails dig into her own palm, leaving crescents in the skin. “To save myself.”  

Hilda recoils, breath ragged. The dagger slips from Seren’s grip, clattering onto the floor. Elara’s stomach twists—this is no pardon, no mercy. Vane’s handiwork: a blade meant to carve through flesh, not free them. The oil pools beneath Seren’s feet, seeping into cracks in the stone.  

Seren collapses, knees buckling. Her chest heaves, each breath a battle. Elara steps closer, the scent of her fear sharp as broken glass. Somewhere beyond the cell, boots echo—orders being barked, chains clinking. The air thickens, heavy with the promise of what’s coming. Elara’s own hands itch, longing for the feel of steel, the weight of a choice that won’t bleed.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s pulse thrums as Hilda’s grip releases Seren, who crumples like a broken thing, her trembling ribs scraping against the cold stone. Morrigan’s fingers close around the dagger—silver, slick with black residue—and Elara smells it before she sees it: the acrid tang of corpse-nettle extract, sour and sweet as rotting fruit. Morrigan’s lips part, a slow smile stretching over her teeth as she tilts the blade, letting the oil drip in viscous ribbons down her wrist.  

Elara’s breath catches. The poison is refined, its volatility coiled tight beneath the surface—dangerous, alive. Her own skin prickles, sweat dampening the fabric of her sleeves as memory floods her: Vane’s hand pressing a vial into hers, his voice low, *“This isn’t just fire. This is* breaking *.”* The coal-tar naphtha they’d stolen from the armory, the silver belladonna crushed into dust—combined, they’d been missing something. A spark.  

Morrigan’s gaze flicks to Elara, sharp as the dagger’s edge. “A gift,” she murmurs, her voice a rasp of amusement and something darker. The blade glints, its black coating thickening where it meets Morrigan’s skin, bubbling like oil on water. Elara steps closer, her boots echoing in the hollow chamber, and the air between them thrums—hot, charged, a current of possibility.  

She reaches out, fingers brushing Morrigan’s wrist, and the poison seeps into her palm, bitter and warm. Her mind unravels, calculations flashing behind her eyes: the naphtha’s viscosity, the belladonna’s latency. This is it. The catalyst. The moment they’d bled for.  

Somewhere above, a door slams. Boots thunder down the corridor. Elara’s pulse syncs with the rhythm of distant screams, her body alight with the weight of what they’ve made. Morrigan’s breath fogs against her cheek, sweet and metallic, as she leans in, whispering, “Let them come.”  

Elara’s hand curls around the dagger, its edge biting into her palm. The poison is a promise, a wound, a weapon. And this—this is how they burn the world open.

✦ ✦ ✦

Freya’s knees press into the cold stone, her palms sweating against the glass beaker. Elara’s fingers bracket Freya’s wrist, grounding her as the vapor curls upward in thin silver threads. The air reeks of burnt copper and damp earth; seven bodies huddle like spoons in a drawer, their breaths fogging the darkness. Freya’s pulse thrums beneath Elara’s touch, erratic, trembling—Elara presses harder, anchoring her with the weight of her palm, the heat of her skin.  

The solution pulses, thick and violet, then fractures into clarity. A mercury-gold sheen spreads across the beaker’s interior, slick as oil on water. Freya’s breath hitches; Elara feels it vibrate through their joined hands. “Breathe,” Elara murmurs, her voice a rasp against Freya’s ear. The words dissolve into the stench of sweat and fear.  

Somewhere above, boots scrape stone. A distant shout fractures the air. Elara doesn’t flinch. Her other hand grips the dagger, its edge slick with blood or poison—she can’t tell anymore. Freya’s palms blister against the glass, her pyromancy a fragile ember. Elara shifts, pressing her chest to Freya’s back, channeling her own heat into the space between them. The vapor thickens, humming.  

A tremor ripples through Freya. Elara’s fingers dig into her wrist, holding steady. “Stay,” she whispers. The beaker glows brighter, its contents rippling like liquid starlight. Freya’s breath hitches again, but this time it’s a sob, raw and trembling. Elara leans closer, her lips brushing Freya’s temple. The heat between them is a living thing, searing, desperate.  

The bell looms. Elara feels its echo in her bones. The solution shimmers, perfect now—clear as glass, heavy with promise. She releases Freya’s wrist, sliding her hand down to clasp Freya’s fingers, interlacing their palms. The vapor rises, steady, unbroken. Somewhere above, the door groans. Elara doesn’t look up. The world is this: heat, breath, the weight of a thousand stolen moments compressed into a single, trembling second.

✦ ✦ ✦

Elara’s fingers tremble as she presses the vial into the iron pendant, its cold bite sharp against her palm. Hilda’s gloved hand closes over it, sliding the relic beneath leather laces that dig into her ribs. The air reeks of rust and sweat. The bell’s toll vibrates in Elara’s molars, a guttural groan that shakes the stone walls. Freya’s breath fans her neck, hot and staccato, while Morrigan’s cedar splinter bites into her lip, bitter pulp smearing her chin. Gaia’s staff thuds once against the floor—solid, final.  

Unit 4 locks arms, muscles taut beneath woven straps and scarred skin. Elara feels the press of Lyra’s blindfold against her forearm, the silk gritty with dust. Seren’s silks whisper as she adjusts them, a sound like dying embers. Freya’s eyes blaze, twin coals in the gloom, and Elara leans into that heat, anchoring herself. The Wardens’ boots echo down the corridor—thudding, deliberate, each step a hammer stroke.  

Elara’s pulse syncs with the bell’s rhythm. Her throat is raw from holding her breath. The pendant bites into Hilda’s ribs, a secret weight. She imagines it burning through flesh, molten and hungry. Morrigan spits cedar slivers onto the floor, their scent sharp as broken bone. Gaia raises her staff, its wood gnarled with age, and Elara feels the shift in the air—tightening, electric.  

Freya’s hand grips hers, knuckles white. The Wardens’ voices rise, muffled by distance, but the sound of their chains is a razor across Elara’s nerves. She tastes iron on her tongue. This is it. The Crucible’s door looms ahead, its hinges weeping rust. Unit 4 steps forward, bodies aligned like blades in a sheath. Elara’s feet drag, but the others pull her, their grip unyielding.  

The bell tolls again—deeper this time, reverberating in her bones. She doesn’t look back. The executioners are coming. Let them. Elara’s fingers curl into Freya’s palm, and she smiles, sharp as a blade.
