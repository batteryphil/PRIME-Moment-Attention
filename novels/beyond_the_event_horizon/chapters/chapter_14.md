# Chapter 14: The Pirate King's Summons

Captain Astrid "Vex" Ross floated before the viewport, her piercing violet gaze fixated upon the enigmatic crystal latticework sprawling endlessly beyond. Her fingers brushed the icy metal rim as if seeking reassurance that this surreal vista was no mere hallucination born from the harrowing resonance echoing still within their minds. Nearby, Commander Leo Mercer drifted awkwardly, struggling against the relentless drag of inertia now that they had left the artificial gravity field of their escape pod behind. His skin gleamed with dew beads collected during the wild, weightless tumble through open space, leaving glistening trails in the faint illumination spilling from within.

"You really plan to stand up to Thane Blackwood?" Leo asked, his tone edged with a grudging admiration for his piratical companion's temerity in the face of such daunting odds.

Astrid's jaw set, her lips compressing into a thin line. "Blackwood and the rest of the Syndicate can rot in the void," she spat, the venom in her words unmistakable. "Giving you to them would make me nothing but a traitor in our own ranks." Vex turned to face him fully, her eyes blazing with an inner fire that belied the chilling numbness creeping into her fingertips. "Besides, there's something I need us both to uncover. And the first step is gaining access to whatever knowledge Blackwood's hoarding in those crystal spires."

Beneath their discussion, the metallic exterior of the pod hummed softly—a soothing drone masking the stark silence permeating the starlit expanse. Even with the hatch sealed once more, wisps of frozen air continued to ooze inside, carrying whispers of outer space that sent a ticklish shiver down both captives' spines. Each ragged breath fogged within the cramped confines, condensation streaking their faces before rapidly subliming away in the frigid environment.

Leo swiped his palm across the chilled inner wall, feeling the biting chill soak through to his bones. He shivered and pulled his jacket tighter around himself, fingers curling into fists to stanch the growing tremors. "I don't know what kind of game you're hoping to play at Blackwood's court, but navigating their intrigue could spell doom for both of us."

Vex crossed her arms, posture relaxed yet radiating an aura of implacable determination. She fixed her gaze on the glittering cerulean plane looming before them, and whispered, "Doom awaits anyway, if we stay hidden and wait for the Syndicate's patrol ships to scour the void. Might as well spit in Fate's eye while we have the chance."

Just then, the pod's communication system crackled to life with jarring intensity, nearly startling them both into involuntary flinches. A melodic voice echoed through the speakers, smooth as silk and colder than the absolute zero they were accelerating toward at tens of thousands of kilometers per hour—"Captains Mercer and Ross, please prepare to interface for customs inspection." The transmission ended on that singularly unnerving note, leaving an abyssal silence in its wake that underscored the sheer scale of the challenge ahead.

Without a word, Astrid extended a hand toward the darkened console nestled against the curved bulkhead. Her fingers danced across hidden sensors and tactile pads, coaxing the hulking vessel into submission much like a feral cat being leashed. At her prompting, the outer hatch hissed wide again, admitting a flood of biting cold that threatened to freeze oxygen from the escaping air.

Leo grabbed the edge of his seat as the frigid wind swept past him, a howling banshee from the depths of the abyss beyond their fragile sanctuary. He resisted the urge to shield his face, knowing full well such futile actions would only accelerate the inevitable freezing of exposed skin. They were mere specks launched at impossible speeds by forces he hardly comprehended, and the vacuum snarled around them with malevolent glee.

Across from him, Astrid stood impassive to the assault, stooping slightly to maintain traction amidst the drifting flakes of ice. Her black hair whipped about her head, a wild shock of night sky contrasting starkly against the pallor of her cheeks and the eerie blue glow suffusing her eyes. In this alien tableau, she exuded an unnatural stillness, a creature carved from shards of starlight and forged in the crucible of a billion-year-old void.

"What the hell kind of welcome is that?" Leo spat, voice emerging tinny and strained through the com implant's audio enhancers. His words vanished into the roaring expanse as quickly as they had escaped his lips—lost, fleeting whispers in the cataclysmic symphony of space itself. He clenched his jaw, swallowing back a bitter laugh at the irony. After all these eons traversing an emptiness vast enough to make the Earth seem a mere splinter, why should it be anything other than a frozen wasteland?

Astrid spared him a glance, her expression unreadable save for a flicker of amusement in those eerie orbs. "The infamous Ice Queen has graciously deigned to inspect her new playthings," she remarked dryly, gesturing toward a pair of glowing skiffs rising from beneath the ice-swept surface. Sleek and deadly, they mirrored each other with identical matte-black hulls emblazoned by garish crimson runes that pulsed like miniature suns. Their arrival sent rippling waves of distorted light across the cavern, as if reality itself recoiled before their imposing presence.

Leo felt a chill, not entirely from the environs. These ships... he'd faced his share of pirates and smugglers in days gone by, but there was something chillingly inhuman about their design—the angular, aberrant geometry that defied any rational blueprint or observed aerodynamic principle. Were these vessels born from some lost civilization, their secrets crumbling into cosmic dust alongside their creators? Or did Astrid design them herself? Somehow, he rather suspected the latter. There was far too much raw, unbridled violence coiled in every supple line and jagged plane for such artistry to belong to anyone else.

In silence, the two craft bore down upon them like predatory insects lured by a wounded creature's cry for help... though help they sought not. As they closed, Leo detected subtle variations in their acceleration patterns despite their eerie uniformity of movement. Some primal instinct, honed over decades bracing himself against unrelenting forces, told him these ships were more than just advanced fighters—every one an asymmetrical weapons platform designed to shred its prey apart atom by atom.

Their trajectory altered abruptly as the lead skiff flashed past Leo with a proximity closer than he cared to contemplate. It banked at incredible velocity, swinging around to flank them as a secondary vessel matched its course. Astrid held up a gloved hand, savoring the fleeting touch of frigid air against her palm before raising her arm to trigger whatever dark resonance technology lurked within her suit.

The world around them erupted into a kaleidoscope of soundlessness—flashbulbs of luminescent force blossoming along the skiffs' leading edges that painted the void in vivid.

The pirate king's summons came without warning—a spectral transmission flickering across the holographic displays embedded in the darkness-drowned cabin of Lieutenant Commander Leo Mercer's trusty starfighter, Dauntless, a veteran of countless hotdog loops and barrel rolls during his days as NASA's hottest new test pilot. Those days seemed ancient history now; relics buried beneath layers of scorching light, corrosive vacuum, and desperate cunning forced to mature in only a few pitiful years.

This summoning did not come in vain; Astrid 'Vex' Ross herself materialized before him a moment later, striding onto the lowered boarding ramp with practiced grace and bearing the lethal radiance of forty trillion stars condensed onto human skin, each atom screaming vengeance across the cosmos. At her side strode a towering giant, twice her height, clad in armor plated with dull silver and black enameled scales.

"Captain Vex," Leo managed to greet her, barely suppressing the tremble of anticipation and fear that seized his voice as those obsidian eyes burned directly into his very soul. "I thought we had a deal."

"You know precisely what I crave." Astrid's words danced in mid-air like a hypnotist's mesmerizing spiral, drawing Leo deeper into her thrall. The subsonic rumble of her suit's life-support systems, a haunting symphony of servos whining and hydrazine venting in thin plumes, harmonized with the hiss of oxygen recycling and the sterile beeps emanating from his own survival gear. "Consider this your initiation into my legion—your first taste of true battle."

Leo swallowed hard against the desert dryness encrusting his throat. "What do you want me to do?"

Her lips curled into a cruel smile that somehow looked beautiful adorning those chiseled angular features. "Fly with us tonight, Leo Mercer. Fight like hell, die if need be—I don't particularly care which—but give me everything you've got and no quarter will be asked in return. These creatures"—she gestured outward towards the icy depths beyond the flickering lights of Dauntless—"are something other-worldly, aberrant lifeforms we call shadow-wraiths. They preyed upon our kind, so it naturally follows..." Her gaze bored back into him, relentless as a surgical laser. "...we retaliate most thoroughly."

"Right. Got it." Though Leo barely acknowledged the gravity of committing an act of war against an unknown alien threat, the primordial thrill of being sent out to hunt and destroy still resonated deep inside him, old instincts never truly dormant.

As Vex and her massive companion disappeared once more into shadows beyond the threshold, beckoning him forward with raised arms, Leo found himself following them into darkness—the promise of violence hanging heavy in the damp air of his helmet like the scent of ozone and oil.

And so it began anew—for Leo Mercer, a man torn between centuries and allegiances. Tonight, there would be blood spilt in distant starlight, and mercy left behind on some lonely fragment of ice drifting lost within the infinite expanse of cold, uncaring nothingness that called itself the void.

The blackness was total, devoid of even starlight, as Leo stepped off the ramp onto Dauntless's open docking bay door—a precipice overlooking uncountable miles of crystalline horror stretching to the event horizon of Deneb X. In the absence of any visible guidance, he kept pace beside Vex, trusting her familiarity with the ship's cavernous interior to steer them clear of obstacles.

Behind their helmets, both captains' eyes adjusted slowly to the faint diffuse illumination suffusing the bowels of the Starlight Marauder from the various instrument boards and status displays along the corridors they traversed. The soft hiss of atmospheric fans, the quiet thrum of power relays, and the metallic clank of mechanical systems operating in harmony served as comforting beacons amidst the oppressive nothingness looming outside.

As they descended an inclined walkway into the heart of the vessel, Vex paused near a bulky console displaying schematics of the outer ship. She glanced over at Leo, concern evident behind her mirrored visor. "Keep your suit powered through realspace transition—won't get caught sleeping at the switch while playing explorer." He nodded, acknowledging the sensible caution, but made no promises about staying close to his station. The allure of peering out across unfathomable gulfs of astral emptiness to the orbiting ruins overhead already burned insistently within him, an irresistible draw like a moth to flame.

Beyond another sealed hatch waited yet another descent via a spiral bulkhead ladder, each step bringing Leo deeper into the belly of Leviathan. His gloved hand met chilled steel again and again as he followed, until finally reaching a lower deck and passing through a thick blast-door into a cramped command center hivemind.

Flickering holographic starscape projections mapped across several corners of the chamber cast swirling rainbow patterns across the faces of the crew attending workstations, while Vex strode purposefully for an empty chair reserved just before her own captain's command throne. "Captain. Report," she said crisply, not bothering to turn around as she seated herself facing rearward consoles displaying real-time sensor data and navigational readouts.

A young woman in a close-fitting black leather flight suit leaned back from her tactical display, helmet tucked under one arm, to meet Vex's gaze. "Shadow-wraith activity has increased sixfold during our approach, Captain. We're pushing a thin layer of asteroid dust clouding our long-range imagery, but no signs of anything bigger than boulders on those fringes as far as scanners can penetrate."

Leo wandered closer, drawn by morbid curiosity, to stand behind the pirate queen contemplating the unfolding battlemap. His gloved fingers absently drummed against his thigh in anticipation, an itch he dearly hoped soon to scratch. The hum of the ship's propulsion plant reverberated through the decking beneath his boots, a steady heartbeat in sync with the pulse of a warrior nation united under its fearless ruler. Even without explicit orders, crew movements throughout the room bespoke an expectation of coming action ripe as freshly picked fruit.

"What's our ETA to engage the main field?" Vex continued calmly, her mind entirely focused. "Assuming current rates persist, we should slip past the wraith perimeter's rear edge within fifteen minutes."

"All systems hot and ready, ma'am—it's just a matter of timing," the officer replied smoothly.

Vex shifted in her seat to look more squarely at the projected depths of space beyond her ship—and beyond Leo too, as he involuntarily shared the scope of her sights. A vast, glittering sweep of spiral galaxies stretched in every direction, so distant the closest stars resolved only into pinpoint brightnesses amid profound darkness.
